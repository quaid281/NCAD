## 1. Why Boxed Grid Tables Fail Peer Review

Default spreadsheet exports and introductory LaTeX tutorials encourage boxed grids:
```latex
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
...
```
This fails typographically for three reasons:
1. **Boxed prisons interrupt horizontal reading**: A reader compares values by scanning horizontally across a row or vertically down a decimal column. Vertical pipes (`|`) act as visual stop signs between every column.
2. **`\hline` has zero vertical clearance**: Standard `\hline` places the horizontal rule flush against the ascenders (`b`, `d`, `h`, `k`) of the row below and descenders (`g`, `p`, `q`, `y`) of the row above.
3. **Rule intersections (`+|+`) look broken**: When `\hline` meets `|` alongside `\doublerulesep`, small gaps or awkward crosshairs appear at every corner.

### The `booktabs` Solution
`booktabs` defines three distinct rule weights and injects asymmetric vertical whitespace (`\aboverulesep` and `\belowrulesep`):
- `\toprule` (`\heavyrulewidth = 0.08em`)
- `\midrule` (`\lightrulewidth = 0.05em`)
- `\bottomrule` (`\heavyrulewidth = 0.08em`)
- `\cmidrule(lr){a-b}` (`\cmidrulewidth = 0.03em`, trimmed on left and right by `\cmidrulekern`)

## 2. How to Eliminate `\resizebox` Without Overflowing `IEEEtran` Columns

In `IEEEtran` journals:
- Single column (`\columnwidth`): **`3.5 in`** (`252.0 pt`)
- Double column (`\textwidth`): **`7.16 in`** (`516.0 pt`)
- Standard `IEEEtran` table text size: **`\footnotesize`** (`8 pt` on `9 pt` leading)

When a table overflows `\columnwidth` or `\textwidth`, apply these five steps in order instead of reaching for `\resizebox`:

1. **Switch explicitly to `\footnotesize`** (or `\scriptsize` = `7 pt` for dense wide tables).
2. **Hoist repeated units and strip redundant text**: Move `%`, `ms`, `GB`, and `s` out of data cells into the column header. Replace verbose inline strings like `100.0% (33/33)` with two clean subcolumns (`Count` and `%`) under a `\cmidrule(lr)` spanner.
3. **Wrap multi-word headers with `\makecell` or two header rows**: A column whose data is `98.4` (`4 em` wide) should never be stretched to `12 em` just because its header says `Total Coverage, mean (range)`.
4. **Use `tabular*` with `@{\extracolsep{\fill}}` and `\setlength{\tabcolsep}{0pt}`**:
   ```latex
   \setlength{\tabcolsep}{0pt}
   \begin{tabular*}{\columnwidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}} l S[table-format=2.1] S[table-format=2.1] @{}}
   ```
   LaTeX automatically computes the exact uniform horizontal gap between columns so the table spans `\columnwidth` to the exact point (`pt`) with zero scaling distortion.
5. **Transpose the matrix** if you have more columns than rows.

## 3. Formatting Point Estimates + Confidence Intervals (CIs) or Standard Deviations

Scientific tables frequently report a point estimate alongside a 95% confidence interval or $\pm$ standard deviation. Three clean patterns from TeX.SE #112343 and #681429 prevent ragged alignment:

### Pattern A: Dedicated `Est.` and `95% CI` Sub-Columns (Recommended for `table*`)
```latex
\toprule
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Retention, $S_r$ (\%)}} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-3}
\textbf{Method} & {\textbf{Est.}} & \textbf{95\% CI} \\
\midrule
Baseline & 63.3 & {[54.1, 71.8]} \\
Proposed & \bfseries 88.3 & {[81.2, 93.1]} \\
\bottomrule
```
*Note*: Wrapping `[81.2, 93.1]` in `{[81.2, 93.1]}` prevents LaTeX's `\\` newline command from misinterpreting `[81.2, 93.1]` as an optional vertical-space argument if it ever appears at the start of a line.

### Pattern B: `siunitx` $\pm$ Uncertainty Alignment (`separate-uncertainty`)
When reporting $\text{mean} \pm \text{SD}$:
```latex
\sisetup{separate-uncertainty = true}
\begin{tabular}{@{} l S[table-format=2.1(2)] @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & {\textbf{Accuracy (\%)}} \\
\midrule
Baseline & 84.2 \pm 3.1 \\
Proposed & 94.7 \pm 1.2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
```
`siunitx` aligns both the decimal point and the $\pm$ symbol vertically!

## 4. Modern Alternative: `tabularray` (`tblr`) from TeX.SE #681429

If a project's TeX Live installation includes `tabularray` (2021+), the `tblr` environment integrates `booktabs` and `siunitx` with cleaner separation of content and style:

```latex
\usepackage{tabularray}
\UseTblrLibrary{booktabs, siunitx}

\begin{table}[!t]
  \centering
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Modern \texttt{tabularray} Table with Guarded Headers}
  \label{tab:tblr_example}
  \begin{tblr}{
    width = \columnwidth,
    colspec = {
      @{}
      X[l]
      Q[c, si={table-format=2.1}]
      Q[c, si={table-format=4.0}]
      @{}
    },
    row{1} = {guard, font=\bfseries},
  }
    \toprule
    Scenario & Coverage (\%) & Latency (ms) \\
    \midrule
    GPU Attacker     & 98.4 & 1739 \\
    Web Exploitation & 98.3 & 1177 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tblr}
\end{table}
```
*Compatibility Note*: For IEEE ScholarOne / PDF eXpress submissions where older TeX Live engines may run on the publisher's server, classic `tabular*` + `booktabs` + `siunitx` remains the safest universal choice.
