\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We study how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space under distribution shift, alongside an Operator-Entropy variant (OpEntropy JEPA) combining a linear Koopman operator, spectral entropy and shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier terms, coordinate saliency gating, and a multiscale dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, our primary finding is relative false-alarm suppression rather than general detection superiority or exact attainment of the 0.1\% nominal design risk: latent scoring reduces false-positive rates three- to four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (7.4\% to 10.1\% versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.1\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%), while point F1 and precision-recall ranking remain comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled ablations isolate two mechanisms: LayerNorm bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, while learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. On a fixed Koopman backbone, Two One-Sided Tests confirm that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank without altering point F1 within a 0.01 to 0.03 margin, while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to unconstrained TS-JEPA. Static thresholding still overshoots its 0.1\% design target out of sample (1.5\% to 2.0\% stream-macro causal and 5.5\% to 6.4\% buffered across 33 streams, or 15- to 64-fold); a three-stage refitted permutation decomposition estimates that inter-block shift accounts for 57\% to 64\% and serial dependence for 29\% to 35\% of excess causal overshoot.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments (NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT, and biometric arrays), dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, where faults must be identified without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains ambient sensor jitter and channel-scale disparities, observation-space residuals exhibit severe excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with automated extreme-value thresholding such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), these heavy-tailed residuals suffer severe calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} To suppress false-alarm flooding without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}, we center our primary investigation on how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space \cite{lecun2022path} under distribution shift, and formulate an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) transition architecture regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a shifted Bregman energy barrier. Rather than claiming that physics-inspired regularizers (operator entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) drive anomaly detection accuracy, we evaluate them strictly as algebraic conditioning priors on abstract latent coordinates. Our experiments separate three operational objectives: (i)~relative false-alarm suppression under automated calibration versus exact attainment of the nominal $0.1\%$ risk target, (ii)~discriminative detection across operating points and fault classes, and (iii)~score-emission latency versus fault-detection delay.

\subsection{Contributions}
This study makes four focused contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Primary Mechanism Isolation (Compression vs.\ Drift):} Across 12 controlled backbone arms on all 19 non-GHL streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)), adding a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck to an observation forecaster (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}) compresses calibration kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$ causal) and halves causal FPR ($0.247 \to 0.117$), while learned latent projection in TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA halves calibration-to-holdout KS drift ($0.376 \to 0.214$, $p{=}0.0010$; $0.376 \to 0.209$, $p{=}0.0004$) and lowers causal FPR to $0.009\text{--}0.028$.
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Suppression vs.\ Detection Limits:} Across 33 streams from seven datasets, latent scoring lowers relative held-out FPR three- to four-fold buffered ($7.4\%\text{--}10.1\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$, $p \le 0.006$) and $8\times\text{--}20\times$ causally ($1.1\%\text{--}2.8\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). Separating deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$) from post-hoc matched realized test-FPR curves ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)) shows higher low-FPR causal event recall ($0.395\text{--}0.431$ vs.\ $0.217$ at $f^*{=}0.01$), whereas Point-F1, PR-AUC, and buffered recall match reconstructors and quiet flatlines remain weakly detected ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ recall).
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy Conditioning and Narrow-Margin TOST Bounds:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that at finite $\epsilon > 0$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$ and non-zero cross-covariance ($\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$) excludes zero stationarity, while as $\epsilon \to 0^+$ the barrier diverges as $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$. The OpEntropy transition architecture cuts buffered detection delay from $110.3$ to $79.5$ steps ($127.0 \to 58.2$ causal), while fixed-backbone TOST ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ control) confirms Point-F1 equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.00074$) and down to $\pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$) and $\pm 0.010$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0088$), isolating $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}$ as a conditioning constraint rather than a detection driver.
    \item \textbf{Operational Calibration Gap and Refitted Permutation Audit:} Static SPOT misses its nominal $q{=}10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) target out of sample ($1.51\%\text{--}1.95\%$ stream-macro causal and $5.54\%\text{--}6.38\%$ buffered FPR across 33 streams for JEPAs, or $15\text{--}20\times$ and $55\text{--}64\times$ overshoot vs.\ $142\text{--}222\times$ for reconstructors, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Our three-stage refitted permutation protocol (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}(b)) estimates that inter-block shift accounts for $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess causal overshoot for JEPAs ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ for reconstructors) and serial dependence for $29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$ ($4.7\%\text{--}19.4\%$), alongside quantile, $W{=}320$ isolation-gap, and kurtosis audits.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Dynamical Systems, Reconstruction, and Forecasting Detectors:} Multi-sensor telemetry models system dynamics as state trajectories $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize velocity fields in raw observation space, where integrating through high-frequency sensor noise induces numerical stiffness. Koopman theory and dynamic mode decomposition \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn linear operators on observable embeddings, identifying disruptions via spectral decoherence; our proposed Operator-Entropy JEPA architecture transfers this principle to a latent transition operator regularized by spectral entropy.

Reconstructive detectors model nominal patterns via observation reconstruction: LSTM forecasting with dynamic thresholding for spacecraft telemetry \cite{Hundman2018b}, association-discrepancy attention in Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, 2D FFT-periodicity variation in TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and adversarial sub-series attention in TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}. These share a vulnerability: point-level losses bias models toward stochastic noise, accumulating residual variance across irrelevant channels in high-dimensional arrays.

\noindent\textbf{Threshold Calibration, Contrastive, and Self-Supervised Detection:} Unsupervised monitoring requires automated rules to map residual scores to binary alarms without labels. Non-parametric thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} and Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fit Generalized Pareto tails under design risk $q$; however, EVT asymptotic guarantees assume i.i.d.\ samples, which temporal autocorrelation and operational drift violate.

To avoid reconstructing noise, contrastive methods train discriminative representations: NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} injects synthetic anomalies, yielding lower calibrated point detection than predictive models ($0.058$ vs.\ $0.160$ macro Point-F1 across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} maximizes dual-attention consistency ($0.070$ macro Point-F1). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target representations in latent space with non-contrastive collapse prevention \cite{zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening, bardes2021vicreg}. Time-series extensions \cite{yue2022ts2vec} evaluate ranking or post-hoc threshold sweeps without studying how latent residuals behave under automated tail calibration.

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Priors and Conceptual Positioning:} PINNs \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian/Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} embed physical conservation laws as rigid differential constraints; their stiff competing gradients \cite{wang2021understanding} and known-PDE requirements fit poorly to uncalibrated multi-sensor telemetry. We strictly distinguish \emph{physics-inspired} algebraic regularizers (where latent coordinates are abstract learned features, not physical state variables) from \emph{physics-informed} conservation constraints; \emph{causal data access} from \emph{score-emission latency}; and \emph{operator non-degeneracy} from \emph{encoder representation diversity} and \emph{anomaly separability}.

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the UCI Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP and MSL entities, with two short-nominal MSL streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet FoG streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 GHL gasoil heating-loop streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample threshold tuning, each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Stream inclusion in the primary multi-seed protocol required: (i)~$N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows after splitting, and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed runtime. Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (6 Daphnet, $K{=}9$; 14 GHL, $K{=}16$; 4 MSL, $K{=}55$; 6 SMAP, $K{=}25$; Genesis, $K{=}18$; Metro, $K{=}5$; SWAN, $K{=}38$). The remaining 12 streams (CalIt2, $K{=}2$; two Room-Occupancy, $K{=}5$; two short-nominal MSL; GECCO, $K{=}9$; six 72-channel CICIDS flows) appear in operational incident case studies and a secondary 11-dataset, 130-stream single-seed baseline cross-check (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)); the dyadic front-end ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)) includes SMD \texttt{machine-1-2} ($K{=}38$).

Two external-validity caveats bound interpretation: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented mislabeled segments and point anomalies \cite{wu2023flawed}, so per-dataset scores represent upper bounds on operational detectability; and (ii)~because streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware and environmental characteristics, streams within a dataset are not independent domains ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report unweighted Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed SDs, leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), restricting conclusions strictly to the evaluated entities and sampling regimes.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a multivariate stream partitioned into context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize pointwise error $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}\| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2$, which can weight channels with different noise scales unequally; whether this consumes model capacity is not tested here. We instead adopt the JEPA framework \cite{lecun2022path}: a context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an EMA momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}}$, with $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$, $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$. The objective forecasts target representations in latent space to filter out stochastic sensor noise and condition prediction residuals for automated tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We formulate Operator-Entropy JEPA as our primary architecture, and evaluate it alongside an unconstrained baseline (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and two comparative physical formulations (Reynolds-Stress and Potential-Flow):

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, where nonlinear trajectories are mapped via linear operators on observable functions, we parameterize the predictive latent transition via a learned operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent collapse onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or to zero (energy collapse), the regularizer decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ eigenvalues of $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^\top$, and $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ total energy. To ensure collapsing modes ($\lambda_i \to 0^+$) receive strictly negative gradients ($\partial \Delta\mathcal{H}/\partial \lambda_i < 0$) without sign reversal, we form the smoothed modal distribution $p_i = (\lambda_i + \epsilon_{\log}) / \sum_{j=1}^D (\lambda_j + \epsilon_{\log})$ ($\epsilon_{\log} = 10^{-12}$) and Von Neumann spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D p_i \log p_i$. The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} measures Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while rank-1 collapse yields $\Delta\mathcal{H} \approx \log D$. Minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ drives $\mathbf{K}$ toward isometric modal equipartition rather than low-rank truncation, acting directly on learned embeddings.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) without explosive gain or zero-gradient clamping, we impose a smooth shifted Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log\bigl(r(\mathbf{K}) + \epsilon\bigr) + \log(1 + \epsilon) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $r(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / (\gamma D)$ and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. This barrier is $C^\infty$ smooth for $r \ge 0$, vanishes at $r = 1$, caps zero-energy penalty at $\approx 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$, and has radial derivative $\partial\Phi_{\text{energy}}/\partial r|_{r=0} = 1 - 1/\epsilon < 0$. With $\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$, $r \approx 1$ and initial barrier penalty vanishes. The composite regularizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
and the training objective minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b}\| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{Lyap}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ ($\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0, \lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5, \beta_{\text{Lyap}} = 0.02$). The optional discrete Lyapunov defect aligns trace-normalized batch covariances on the transition operator, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}} = \frac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} / s_{\text{ctx}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{tgt}} / s_{\text{tgt}}\|_F^2$ ($s_m = \operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_m/D + 10^{-6}$, $m \in \{\text{ctx},\text{tgt}\}$), where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{tgt}}$ are the empirical covariances of $\mathbf{K}\operatorname{sg}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$ over the batch. Shared representation stages are fixed across controls. $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ act on $\mathbf{K}$ only; encoder collapse is mitigated empirically by EMA target dynamics and stop-gradient operations \cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Triviality Under Finite-$\epsilon$ and Singular Barriers]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}}, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, target batch energy $E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$, and empirical cross-covariance $\mathbf{M} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top$:
(i)~\emph{Finite-$\epsilon$ smooth regime ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$):} $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{0}) \approx 12.8$ is finite and $\partial\Phi_{\text{energy}}/\partial r|_{r=0} < 0$, but the matrix gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$ vanishes because $r(\mathbf{K}) \propto \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2$. The barrier alone produces no repulsive gradient at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$; instead, whenever $\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$ excludes $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ as a stationary point or minimizer.
(ii)~\emph{Singular-barrier limit ($\epsilon \to 0^+$):} For unshifted $\mathcal{L}_0(\mathbf{K})$, $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$ and $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}_0(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$ unconditionally, independent of $\mathbf{M}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) For $\epsilon > 0$, $\Phi_{\text{energy}}$ and $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ are smooth. By the chain rule, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{2}{\gamma D}\Phi'_{\text{energy}}(r)\,\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$. At $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$, $\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{I}/D$ minimizes the nonnegative smooth entropy deficiency $\Delta\mathcal{H}$, so $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$. Differentiating the prediction loss gives $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M}$; if $\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ is non-stationary. (ii) For $\epsilon = 0$, $\Phi_{\text{energy}} = r - 1 - \log r \to +\infty$ as $r \to 0^+$; nonnegativity of the other terms yields $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0}\mathcal{L}_0(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Scope, Cross-Covariance Verification, and Non-Collapse):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} guarantees operator non-degeneracy, not representation diversity or anomaly detection. Unlike representation regularizers (VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}) that penalize covariance collapse of encoder outputs $z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}}$, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ constrains only the transition spectrum. If $E_\theta$ maps all inputs to a constant, $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ attains zero loss; conversely, an isometric operator ($\kappa(\mathbf{K}) \approx 1$) is compatible with representations in a low-dimensional subspace ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}}){\approx}5.3$, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}){\approx}11.6$ of $32$; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). Empirically, target variance ($E_{\text{tgt}} \in [0.09, 5.88]$) and coupling ($\|\mathbf{M}\|_F > 0$) hold across all 195 primary runs because adjacent windows share LayerNorm-standardized features under cosine EMA $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap}.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary Quadratic Second-Moment Head}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Inspired by Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence, which separates mean flow from velocity fluctuations, we evaluate an auxiliary quadratic second-moment head in latent space. The observed matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$ ($d_s = 16$, via projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$) is an instantaneous rank-1 projected outer product plus ridge penalty, not a physical turbulent covariance. A closure head outputs lower-triangular $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ reconstructing predicted covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}$. The objective minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}})$. At test time, scoring adds the second-moment discrepancy: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$.

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory and Flow Matching)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition a smooth velocity field splits into a curl-free gradient and a solenoidal part. We parameterize $\hat{v} = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi$ via a residual MLP conditioned on state $z_t$, flow time $t \in [0,1]$, context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a regime projection selected among $K_r = 4$ orthonormal subspaces ($d{=}8, D{=}32$) with tangent projection $v_\perp = v - \langle u, v\rangle u$, $u = z_t/\|z_t\|_2$. Training uses conditional flow matching along linear paths from noise $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ to $z_{\text{tgt}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \| \hat{v} - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2$, and inference queries the field at the trajectory midpoint, $\mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}}, 0.5, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - z_{\text{tgt}}$. The curl-free bias is hypothesized to penalize cyclic nominal orbits and degrade detection on periodic kinematics (Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors}). 

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed at stride 1. For TS-JEPA and Potential-Flow, the score gates the residual $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ (or whitened $\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{1/2}(\mathbf{e}(t) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_r)$) via $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}): $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$. Because final LayerNorm projects embeddings to radius $\sqrt{D}$, Operator-Entropy JEPA tracks pre-LayerNorm radial energy scaled by window RMS ($e_{\text{ctx}}, e_{\text{tgt}}$) with $e_{\text{pred}} = e_{\text{ctx}}\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}\|_2 / (\|z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2 + 10^{-6})$, scoring $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t)) + 0.2\,|e_{\text{tgt}}(t) - e_{\text{pred}}(t)| / (e_{\text{tgt}}(t) + e_{\text{pred}}(t) + 10^{-6})$. For Reynolds-Stress, $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t)) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. Shared stages are fixed across controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
To resolve the methodological confounding between data availability, decision latency, and evaluation attribution, we formalize detection under three operational criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$, the model observes only history $\mathbf{X}_{:\tau} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:\tau, :]$. Neither encoder accesses future observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because suspect representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$ encodes the horizon $\mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :]$, window score $s_t$ cannot be computed until timestep $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}(a)). This imposes a structural score-emission latency of $S - 1 = 63$ steps: $0.98\,\text{s}$ on Daphnet ($64\,\text{Hz}$), $10.5\,\text{min}$ on SMAP, MSL, and GHL under standard 10-s ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$) sampling \cite{Hundman2018b, zhou2026mtsbench}, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO ($1\,\text{sample/min}$), and $63\,\text{h}$ on Metro ($1\,\text{sample/h}$). We strictly distinguish this structural emission lag from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$, which measures the elapsed steps from ground-truth anomaly onset $t^*$ to the first timestamp $t_{\text{alarm}} \in [t^*, t_{\text{end}}]$ where the attributed score exceeds threshold $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ (under causal trailing attribution, an alarm at decision time $\tau = t_{\text{alarm}}$ utilizes context and target observations $\mathbf{X}[t_{\text{alarm}}-W+1 : t_{\text{alarm}}+1]$ covering the first $\Delta t + 1$ steps of the anomaly). Batch scoring over $S{=}64$ fits supervisory health monitoring and persistent subsystem faults rather than sub-second emergency tripwires on low-rate telemetry; Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency} quantifies how shortening $S \in \{1,4,16,32,64\}$ eliminates emission lag.
    \item \textbf{Alarm attribution convention:} We distinguish two attribution regimes: (1)~\emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, where score $s_t$ is emitted and evaluated strictly at $\tau$, providing zero-lookahead online monitoring; and (2)~\emph{retrospective buffered smear attribution}, where $s_t$ is attributed across the entire suspect window $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$. Under unadjusted point evaluation, buffered alarms in $[t^*-S, t^*]$ register as false alarms: buffering provides fixed-lag detection, not early warning. The two regimes represent distinct operational realities and are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}




\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, the latent JEPA architectures incorporate shared inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and the Auxiliary Channel-Gate Variant}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults frequently originate in a localized subsystem while the remaining channels exhibit ambient noise, diluting localized anomalies in isotropic representations. The deployed detector mitigates this in latent space via an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e})$ on error vectors $\mathbf{e} = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \\
    w_d &= \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)}, \nonumber
\end{align}
amplifying sparse coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$.  We fix blend parameter $\alpha = 0.5$ and softmax temperature $\tau = 0.5$ across all latent predictors. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) evaluate unweighted observation errors and do not use the gate. This is the discrepancy gate used in every latent experiment: input channels are encoded unweighted, and the gate acts only on the $D$-dimensional predictive residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary Input-Channel Gate (Evaluated Variant)}
A strictly causal gate weights each channel by the sigmoid of its standardized context-window variance share, $g_k = \sigma\!\left((s_k - \bar{s})/(\sigma_s + \epsilon)\right)$, applied symmetrically to both encoders so that $\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$ (identity at $K{=}1$). It cannot isolate quiet faults (recall $0.02$--$0.07$ under every gating regime, Section~\ref{sec:ablations}); the deployed pipeline weights inputs uniformly.



\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Anomalies span temporal scales from sensor spikes to thermal drifts; the encoder front-end therefore splits each channel into $J{=}4$ smooth dyadic shells on an $N_{\text{fft}}=256$ rFFT grid, normalized bin-wise into an exact amplitude partition of unity, $\sum_j \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ (measured deviation $< 1.2\times10^{-7}$). Each shell feeds a causal 1D convolution ($k \in \{15,9,5,3\}$ coarse-to-fine), recombined by learned scale routing. Because the shells overlap smoothly at band edges, the front-end is \emph{less} frequency-localized than standard wavelet banks. We therefore describe it as a Fourier-domain multiscale front-end, not an exact Littlewood--Paley octave decomposition, and measure its empirical contribution by matched ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}: excesses over a high threshold $u$ (98th percentile) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To test whether sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals can compensate for pre-asymptotic tail heaviness, we evaluate two heuristic adjustments: (i)~scaling the total threshold from zero, $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau [ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) ]$, and (ii)~scaling strictly the tail excess above $u$, $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) ] \ge u$.

These multipliers are engineering heuristics for pre-asymptotic tails, not EVT theorems. The primary pipeline uses unadjusted SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and evaluates both multipliers as sensitivity arms (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}). Because $64$-step buffered smoothing produces tightly concentrated, platykurtic scores ($\gamma_2 < 0$) on $31.9\%$ of TS-JEPA cells, scaling total $\tau$ pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below $u$ into the bulk distribution ($6.40\% \to 16.30\%$ buffered FPR), whereas scaling only the excess $(\tau - u)$ preserves the $u=p_{98}$ floor ($6.33\%$ buffered FPR).

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted via a multiscale dyadic front-end and causal dilated TCN (6 residual blocks, $F{=}48$, kernel $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$); multi-statistic pooling (terminal, mean, max, and channel statistics) projects the $C{=}256$ context through a linear bottleneck recording pre-LayerNorm radial energy before LayerNorm and dropout ($p{=}0.20$). The momentum target encoder uses cosine EMA $m_{\text{base}}{=}0.996 \to m_{\text{max}}{=}0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Window Construction}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}}]$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}}]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}(a)). Standardization ($\bm{\mu}, \bm{\sigma} \ge 10^{-2}$) and model weights ($E_\theta, E_\phi, \mathbf{K}$) are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}[j : j+W]$) alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$. Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = C + S = 320$ are constructed at stride $1$ strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$: window $i \in \{0, \dots, N_{\text{val}} - W\}$ uses context $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}[i : i+C]$ and target $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}[i+C : i+W]$. Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations serve as context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}}]$ emitted at $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}[i + W - 1]$. Splitting $v$ at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H]$ (fitting GPD parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}p_{98}$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$ and $\gamma_2$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}}]$, excluding $S{=}64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S]$. Because suspect targets have length $S{=}64$, the last calibration target $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}[H-1+C : H-1+W]$ and first evaluation target $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}[H+S+C : H+S+W]$ are $100\%$ disjoint; the first $C{=}256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}[H+S : H-1+W]$ with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. A stricter full-window gap check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320]$ so passive context is also disjoint across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) leaves stream-macro causal FPR virtually unchanged: $0.0207 \to 0.0218$ (OpEntropy) and $0.0302 \to 0.0324$ (TS-JEPA) vs.\ $0.1215 \to 0.1397$ (TimesNet) and $0.0905 \to 0.0973$ (TranAD). Thresholds remain fixed during test evaluation; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}.

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment}
All experiments use standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier) on held-out nominal splits, except the rolling-SPOT incident analysis and the calibrator comparison itself (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}); no table mixes calibrators across models.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Equivalence Testing}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams run five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) and ablation controls run three seeds (five on the two smallest GHL streams). We report held-out nominal FPR, Point-F1, and PR-AUC as distinct outcomes; nonsignificant differences are not evidence of parity. Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, moving-block bootstrap ($L{=}64$) per-cell FPR intervals, stream-resampled paired CIs (2{,}000 iterations, seeds averaged within stream, $n{=}19$ or $n{=}33$), dataset-clustered checks ($N{=}7$), and Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($q_{\text{FDR}}{=}0.05$). For physical regularizer ablations, we pair two-sided tests with Two One-Sided Tests (TOST): $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$; $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}})$) alongside $90\%$ ($1{-}2\alpha$ at $\alpha{=}0.05$) and $95\%$ CIs. We prespecify $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ Point-F1 for fixed-backbone controls ($\approx 13\%$ of baseline F1, $<0.09\times$ between-stream SD) and $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ for cross-architecture contrasts ($\approx 25\%$ of baseline F1, $<0.15\times$ between-stream SD, corresponding at prevalence $\pi \approx 0.15$ to an event-recall shift of $\le 0.04$ or $\approx 15\text{--}20$ false alarms per $1{,}000$ nominal steps), and evaluate narrow-margin TOST sensitivity down to $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.010, \pm 0.015, \pm 0.020, \pm 0.030\}$ alongside TOST on held-out FPR and event recall (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Equivalence requires $p_{\text{TOST}} < 0.05$ and the $90\%$ CI strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures, Fairness Checks, and Cohort Structure}
All models share a common streaming pipeline ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, stride $1$, AdamW $\eta{=}10^{-3} \to 10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch $32$, $20$ epochs, SPOT $u{=}p_{98}, q{=}10^{-3}$). We separate primary multi-seed conclusions (Cohorts 1--2: 33-stream and 19-stream 5-seed benchmarks across 12 neural, linear, and non-parametric detectors including PCA, Isolation Forest, $k$-NN, MAD, and Z-score baselines) from secondary single-seed screens. Three fairness checks bound this protocol: (i)~\emph{Training-budget sensitivity} (7 streams $\times$ 3 seeds): doubling optimization from $10$ to $20$ epochs raises JEPA mean F1 from $0.038$ to $0.048$ (OpEntropy $0.029 \to 0.065$) while reconstructor F1 is stable ($0.158 \to 0.154$; TimesNet $0.154 \to 0.151$, TranAD $0.161 \to 0.158$) and held-out FPR stays $0.087\text{--}0.092$ vs.\ $0.333$. (ii)~\emph{Native published windows} (5 streams): at native windows (TranAD $W{=}10$, TimesNet $W{=}96$), F1 rises ($0.164$, $0.218$), but observation residuals remain leptokurtic ($\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and $98.14$ vs.\ $3.14$ for TS-JEPA) with high nominal FPR ($28.4\%$, $44.1\%$). (iii)~\emph{Published-score and secondary 130-stream single-seed cross-check}: baselines replicate published PA-F1 on SMD/MSL, and Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) provides a secondary 130-stream, 11-dataset single-seed screen against NCAD ($149{,}634$ parameters at $K{=}25$), DCdetector ($298{,}400$), and Anomaly Transformer ($4{,}785{,}120$).

\noindent\textbf{Cohort Summary and Reproducibility Specification:} Five cohorts address distinct questions: (1)~\emph{33-stream primary multi-seed suite} (19 non-GHL + 14 GHL, 3--5 seeds, Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)--(c), \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)); (2)~\emph{19-stream non-GHL multi-seed mechanism/operating suite} (12 arms, 5 seeds, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(e), Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b), Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}); (3)~\emph{6-stream factorial and fault suite} (3 seeds, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c),(d)); (4)~\emph{5-stream horizon and front-end panels} (3 seeds, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a)); and (5)~\emph{secondary 130-stream 11-dataset single-seed screen} (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). Parameter counts at $K{=}25$: OpEntropy $285{,}701$, TS-JEPA $289{,}061$, Reynolds $296{,}037$, Potential-Flow $338{,}209$, TimesNet $648{,}553$, TranAD $205{,}746$. Non-finite values are zero-imputed ($\bm{\sigma} \ge 10^{-2}$); SPOT fits GPD $(\xi, \sigma)$ above $u{=}p_{98}$ via Grimshaw MLE with L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10], \sigma \ge 10^{-6}$). All 195 primary runs converged on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (RTX 5090, $0.60$--$0.84$~ms/window, $183.8$--$185.3$~MB VRAM); manifests, thresholds, and code are at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we report complementary evaluation tiers: (i)~\emph{operating characteristics}: event recall over deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$) and matched realized-test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in [0.01,0.50]$, interpolated within stream--seed curves); and (ii)~\emph{secondary endpoints}: Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (unweighted across datasets so 14-stream GHL does not dominate), channel-macro Point-F1, PR-AUC (threshold-free average precision), event recall ($\ge 1$ alarm per contiguous anomaly segment), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ (onset-to-first-alarm lag over detected events), the constant-alarm reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$, pooled micro-F1, and contiguous/test-sweep PA-F1.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone. Because controls run three seeds against the five-seed principal runs, the paired fixed-backbone means below are computed on the shared three-seed subset ($n{=}19$ streams) with $90\%$ (TOST $\alpha{=}0.05$) and $95\%$ bootstrap CIs and individual one-sided Wilcoxon TOST $p$-values ($p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}$; $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}})$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs.\ $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ (fixed Koopman backbone):} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.232 \pm 0.039$ vs.\ $0.235 \pm 0.038$; difference $-0.004$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.011, +0.003]$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.014, +0.004]$, two-sided Wilcoxon $p{=}0.89$; PR-AUC $0.365$ vs.\ $0.365$), confirming formal TOST equivalence within prespecified $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ ($p_{\text{lower}} = 0.00074, p_{\text{upper}} = 6.6\times 10^{-5}, p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.00074$) and $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 7.7\times 10^{-5}$). Narrow-margin sensitivity checks confirm that fixed-backbone equivalence holds down to $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0015$), $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$, with $90\%$ CI $[-0.0112, +0.0029]$ strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$), and $\Delta = \pm 0.010$ by Wilcoxon signed-rank ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0088$), as well as within $\pm 0.010$ PR-AUC ($90\%$ CI $[-0.0053, +0.0035]$) and $\pm 0.025$ event recall ($90\%$ CI $[-0.0220, +0.0027]$).
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs.\ $\alpha_{\text{stress}}{=}0$ (fixed backbone):} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.195 \pm 0.029$ vs.\ $0.182 \pm 0.036$ ($+0.013$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.001, +0.030]$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.003, +0.034]$, two-sided $p{=}0.59$), confirming TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ ($p_{\text{lower}} = 6.6\times 10^{-5}, p_{\text{upper}} = 0.0019, p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$) and down to $\Delta = \pm 0.035$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0031$).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs.\ no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing yields $0.135 \pm 0.062$ vs.\ $0.189 \pm 0.024$ ($-0.054$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.131, +0.012]$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.148, +0.022]$, two-sided $p{=}0.86$), failing equivalence at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ ($p_{\text{lower}} = 0.17, p_{\text{upper}} = 0.0090, p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.17$).
\end{enumerate}
We strictly distinguish these fixed-backbone regularizer controls from cross-architecture comparisons against unconstrained TS-JEPA (which uses an MLP predictor without radial energy scoring or $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$): on the 19 non-GHL subset, OpEntropy JEPA vs.\ TS-JEPA yields $+0.028$ Point-F1 ($90\%$ CI $[-0.018, +0.074]$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.026, +0.085]$, two-sided $p{=}0.39$), which is statistically inconclusive at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ because the upper bound exceeds $+0.05$ ($p_{\text{lower}} = 0.0027, p_{\text{upper}} = 0.11, p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.11$; Reynolds vs.\ TS-JEPA $-0.015$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.056, +0.026]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.11$), whereas across all 33 primary streams OpEntropy JEPA vs.\ TS-JEPA ($+0.016$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.009, +0.045]$) and Reynolds vs.\ TS-JEPA ($-0.008$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.031, +0.016]$) satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$ and $0.0017$; OpEntropy vs.\ TS-JEPA also satisfies $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.030)$ by Wilcoxon at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0077$ and $\pm 0.015$ causal FPR at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.00011$).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Factorial Isolation of the Entropy Term and Energy Barrier}
\label{subsec:factorial}
A single on/off control cannot separate the spectral-entropy term from the energy barrier or show how either scales. Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) crosses the two terms (neither, entropy only, barrier only, both) and sweeps $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00\}$ with the barrier on, on six streams at three seeds with a $D{=}32$ operator. Each arm reports operator rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K})$, condition number $\kappa(\mathbf{K})$, representation rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}})/\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$, the coefficient of variation $\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}$ of the training loss as a stability proxy, and held-out calibration and detection outcomes in both attribution regimes.

Three observations follow. First, the unconstrained operator is already near-isotropic ($\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}){=}31.53$ of $32$, $\kappa{=}1.437$) because $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near the identity; the regularizers tighten conditioning monotonically with $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$ ($31.85$ and $\kappa{=}1.255$ at the deployed $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0.50$, $31.95$ and $\kappa{=}1.154$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}2.00$) and lower $\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}$ from $0.565$ to $0.545$. Second, because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ detaches $z_{\text{ctx}}$, the encoder representation is unaffected by $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$ and $\beta_{\text{norm}}$: $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ stays within $5.16\text{--}5.54$ and $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ within $11.54\text{--}11.58$ of $32$ in every arm. Operator non-degeneracy, representation diversity, and anomaly separability are therefore separate properties, and only the first moves systematically. Third, no arm or strength produces a large detection shift: buffered Point-F1 spans $0.032\text{--}0.047$ and causal Point-F1 $0.004\text{--}0.011$ with buffered FPR tightly grouped in $0.070\text{--}0.074$ and causal FPR in $0.016\text{--}0.017$, while the deployed setting ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0.50, \beta_{\text{norm}}{=}1$) attains the highest causal event recall ($0.444$ vs.\ $0.369$ unconstrained). The entropy term is a well-posed constraint that preserves operator conditioning without inflating false alarms.

\noindent\textbf{Decoupling.} On an eight-stream exploratory panel (two seeds, strictly causal trailing), coupling the detached trace-normalized Lyapunov defect ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}{=}0.02$) with the smooth Bregman barrier ($\alpha_Z{=}0$) cuts mean detection delay from $80.2$ steps (historical one-sided clamp, $\beta_{\text{Lyap}}{=}0$) and $127.0$ steps (unconstrained TS-JEPA) to $58.2$ steps, while raising causal Point-F1 from $0.044\text{--}0.046$ to $0.094$, PR-AUC from $0.153\text{--}0.157$ to $0.190$, and event recall from $0.206\text{--}0.250$ to $0.258$ at causal FPR $0.013$ (buffered Point-F1 $0.116$ vs.\ $0.063$ for TS-JEPA). Adding explicit representation-covariance entropy ($\alpha_Z{=}0.005, \beta_{\text{Lyap}}{=}0.02$) yields intermediate Point-F1 ($0.068$) and delay ($60.7$ steps), confirming that detaching $z_{\text{ctx}}$ in $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ aligns the operator without distorting encoder representations.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating and Quiet-Fault Reference Monitors}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling, a non-causal target-only leaky control, and independent context/target gating under an identical TS-JEPA configuration (seed 42, four streams). Macro Point-F1 ranks uniform weighting ($0.118$) above context gating ($0.077$), with the leaky control at $0.128$ and independent gating weakest ($0.071$); the effect is stream-dependent in sign (MSL \texttt{M-1}: $0.047 \to 0.227$; SMAP \texttt{P-3}: $0.280 \to 0.019$). The gate is an evaluated variant; the deployed pipeline retains uniform input weighting.

Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d) injects six single-channel fault classes into six multivariate streams. The variance gate roughly doubles point recall relative to uniform pooling (macro $0.045$ vs.\ $0.023$) at about $3.6\times$ the nominal FPR ($0.069$ vs.\ $0.019$), and a median-absolute-deviation variant is marginally stronger ($0.060$ recall). Quiet faults (flatline, stuck value, dropout) stay weakly detected under every latent gating regime (point recall $0.02\text{--}0.07$). Two observation-space reference monitors test whether a complementary score can cover this blind spot: a rolling channel-variance floor (\texttt{lowvar}) and a maximum channel $z$-deviation (\texttt{dev\_maxz}). The variance floor reaches point recall $0.18$ ($0.32$ on mid-variance flatlines) and the $z$-deviation monitor $0.09\text{--}0.11$, but at nominal FPR of $0.14\text{--}0.16$, roughly eight times that of the latent detector. A complementary variance monitor recovers part of the quiet-fault gap only at a substantial alarm cost, so quiet-fault coverage remains an open limitation.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells (including the corrected Nyquist plateau shell carrying $40.5\%$ of broadband white-noise energy and $76.0\%$ ideal-band mass) plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a mixed, stream-dependent trade-off (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)): across a five-stream exploratory panel under kurtosis-adaptive SPOT, macro PR-AUC rises from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD $+0.193$ and GECCO $+0.121$) and validation residual kurtosis drops from $1.409$ to $1.069$, while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ ($-0.040$) due to threshold tightening on SMAP \texttt{P-3} ($0.301 \to 0.115$ as FPR drops $0.043 \to 0.012$). The block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters and shell overlap prevents strict octave isolation, so it acts as a ranking-conditioning front-end rather than a uniform F1 gain.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
To isolate the operational mechanisms governing tail conditioning and threshold transfer across all 19 non-GHL streams (five seeds, SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e) and Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a) compare 12 controlled backbone arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} ($C{=}256, S{=}64, D{=}32$) alongside linear and non-parametric controls:
(1)~\emph{Unnormalized observation forecasting (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred}):} Raw observation residuals exhibit high causal kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.10$), severe calibration-to-holdout KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$), and $0.247$ causal FPR.
(2)~\emph{Observation forecasting with LayerNorm (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}):} Adding a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck mechanically curtails excursions, compressing kurtosis to $\gamma_2 \approx 0.91$ and halving causal FPR to $0.117$ (buffered $0.193$), but leaves drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$). Bounded geometry alone halves false alarms via score compression without resolving temporal drift.
(3)~\emph{Latent projection (\texttt{TS-JEPA}, \texttt{OpEntropy}):} Predicting in learned latent coordinates halves KS drift ($0.376 \to 0.214$, paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.0010$, $\gamma_2 = 1.33$; $0.376 \to 0.209$, $p = 0.0004$ for \texttt{OpEntropy}) and suppresses causal FPR five- to eight-fold ($0.117 \to 0.024$, event recall $0.487$).
(4)~\emph{Foresight vs.\ geometric compression (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}):} Autoencoding context embeddings without temporal foresight matches this drift reduction ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, $\gamma_2 = 2.59$, causal FPR $0.017$, event recall $0.542$), showing that latent coordinate compression rather than temporal extrapolation drives tail stability.
(5)~\emph{Linear vs.\ non-parametric controls:} Linear projection forecasting (\texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN}) achieves $\gamma_2 = 0.95$, $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.233$, causal FPR $0.021$, and recall $0.452$, whereas \texttt{IsoForest} collapses in recall ($0.149$ at SPOT; $0.317$ at $f^*{=}0.01$). With $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$ occupying $5.6\text{--}6.1$ and $8.5\text{--}14.0$ of 32 dimensions, LayerNorm bounds kurtosis while learned latent projection halves distribution drift to reach the $0.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$ causal FPR regime.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$), with buffered (a) and strictly causal (b) attribution reported separately. The principal finding is relative false-alarm suppression; the evidence for general detection superiority is inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Relative False-Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} Latent predictive scoring via Operator-Entropy JEPA and TS-JEPA improves relative false-alarm control under static calibration: on 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.074\text{--}0.101$ ($95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs: TS-JEPA $0.093$ $[0.048, 0.145]$, OpEntropy $0.101$ $[0.043, 0.180]$) versus $0.300$ $[0.162, 0.446]$ for TimesNet and $0.307$ $[0.177, 0.445]$ for TranAD (three- to four-fold reduction buffered, and $8\text{--}20\times$ causal: $0.011\text{--}0.028$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.243$). Across all 33 streams the paired buffered reduction is significant: OpEntropy JEPA achieves $-0.144$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.241, -0.065]$, $p=0.006$) and $-0.142$ vs.\ TranAD ($p=0.006$); TS-JEPA yields $-0.153$ ($p=0.002$) and $-0.151$ ($p=0.003$). Dataset-clustering ($N=7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$, and leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) folds hold macro buffered FPR to $0.038\text{--}0.070$ (OpEntropy) and $0.038\text{--}0.061$ (TS-JEPA) vs.\ $0.084\text{--}0.183$ (reconstructors). On MSL, TimesNet records lower buffered FPR than OpEntropy ($0.133$ vs.\ $0.177$), while on Genesis, Metro, and SWAN reconstructors record zero FPR with zero F1 due to inactivity.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1, Event Recall, and Ranking Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not establish universal detection superiority. On the 19 non-GHL streams, buffered Point-F1 ($95\%$ CI) and event recall ($95\%$ CI) are $0.206$ $[0.113, 0.319]$ and $0.477$ $[0.331, 0.629]$ for TS-JEPA, and $0.233$ $[0.131, 0.355]$ and $0.550$ $[0.398, 0.700]$ for OpEntropy, versus $0.197$ $[0.098, 0.315]$ and $0.583$ $[0.383, 0.781]$ for TimesNet, and $0.130$ $[0.053, 0.232]$ and $0.490$ $[0.295, 0.679]$ for TranAD. Across all 33 streams paired differences remain inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$). Between-stream SDs match the means (SMAP $0.379 \pm 0.361$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $0.289 \pm 0.334$ for TimesNet). Only SMAP exceeds the constant-alarm reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}{=}0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.396$); MSL, Daphnet, and SWAN fall below it, while PR-AUC leads over prevalence on SMAP ($0.504$/$0.525$ at $\pi{=}0.150$) and MSL ($0.434$/$0.422$ at $\pi{=}0.255$).

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing across fault classes, domain families, and sampling regimes defines where latent predictive scoring succeeds and where it remains inadequate: (i)~\emph{favorable operating regime}: multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement; (ii)~\emph{latency-constrained regime}: low-rate telemetry ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ spacecraft or $1\,\text{sample/h}$ Metro) where a $63$-step score-emission lag at $S{=}64$ ($10.5\,\text{min}$ to $63\,\text{h}$) is prohibitive for immediate tripwires, requiring short horizons $S \in \{1,4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}); (iii)~\emph{quiet single-channel faults}: flatlines, stuck sensors, and dropouts ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ recall, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d)), which LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background and which require auxiliary variance monitors at higher alarm cost ($14\%\text{--}16\%$ FPR); and (iv)~\emph{continuous non-stationary process ramps} (GHL, Metro), which defeat all tested static and rolling detectors ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1).

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
What does the Operator-Entropy formulation contribute beyond unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? Isolating the spectral regularizer on the fixed Koopman backbone ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ control) confirms strict TOST equivalence within prespecified $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ Point-F1 ($-0.004$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.011, +0.003]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.00074$) and down to $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$) and $\pm 0.010$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0088$, Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}), while the OpEntropy transition architecture (Koopman $\mathbf{K}$, detached Lyapunov alignment, and pre-LayerNorm relative radial energy scoring) cuts buffered detection delay from $110.3$ steps ($95\%$ CI $[70.3, 151.9]$) in TS-JEPA to $79.5$ steps ($[47.9, 113.2]$; causal delay $127.0 \to 58.2$ on the decoupling panel). Across all 33 streams, cross-architecture Point-F1 differences against TS-JEPA satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ for OpEntropy ($+0.016$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.009, +0.045]$, two-sided $p{=}0.23$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$; $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.03)$ at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0077$) and Reynolds-Stress ($-0.008$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.031, +0.016]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0017$), whereas on the 19-stream non-GHL subset the $+0.028$ OpEntropy difference ($90\%$ CI $[-0.018, +0.074]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.11$) is inconclusive and Potential-Flow degrades Point-F1 ($-0.081$, $p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Physical regularizers therefore act as structural conditioning constraints rather than detection drivers.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Strictly causal results provide the operational evaluation; buffered results are retrospective. Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b) reports strictly causal streaming. On 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.024$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.007, 0.046]$; OpEntropy $0.028$ $[0.007, 0.063]$, Reynolds $0.011$ $[0.006, 0.017]$) versus $0.243$ $[0.123, 0.375]$ (TimesNet) and $0.222$ $[0.119, 0.337]$ (TranAD). Causal Point-F1 is $0.161$ $[0.063, 0.287]$ (TS-JEPA) and $0.153$ $[0.070, 0.247]$ (OpEntropy) versus $0.209$ $[0.109, 0.326]$ (TimesNet), with causal event recall at $0.487$ $[0.345, 0.627]$ and $0.517$ $[0.367, 0.661]$ vs.\ $0.588$ $[0.394, 0.783]$ (TimesNet). Buffered detection delay is $79.5$ $[47.9, 113.2]$ steps for OpEntropy vs.\ $110.3$ $[70.3, 151.9]$ for TS-JEPA, $234.4$ $[31.0, 603.1]$ for TranAD, and $48.5$ $[20.0, 85.1]$ for TimesNet (causal delay $112.5\text{--}124.0$ vs.\ $62.2$ steps): without lookahead buffers, latent scoring operates as a conservative filter trading raw recall and onset speed for $8\text{--}20\times$ false-alarm suppression. Dynamic SPOT on five incident streams suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ under drift.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
At $S{=}64$, window scores emit $S-1=63$ steps after window opening ($10.5$~min on 10-s telemetry; $63$~h on hourly Metro data), distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$ measured after fault onset (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}(a)). Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models at $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S{=}1$ (zero emission lag), OpEntropy holds causal FPR at $0.005$ (TimesNet $0.025$) with event recall $0.729$ and detection delay $\Delta t = 76.3$ steps (TimesNet $0.895$). Causal FPR for reconstructors rises with horizon ($0.025 \to 0.213$), while latent models stay at $0.003\text{--}0.020$ (buffered $0.005 \to 0.099$). Where rapid response is required, $S \in \{1,4\}$ eliminates emission lag while preserving relative false-alarm suppression.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We strictly separate two operating evaluations: (i)~\emph{deployable calibration quantiles} ($q^* \in [10^{-4}, 0.9]$, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)), selected using nominal calibration data alone, and (ii)~\emph{post-hoc matched realized test-FPR curves} ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)), which set oracle thresholds on test nominal scores to compare intrinsic receiver operating separation at equalized alarm rates. Under deployable calibration quantile $q^*{=}0.05$ causally, TS-JEPA achieves event recall $0.730$ at $177.3$ false alarms per 1{,}000 nominal steps (precision $0.251$) and OpEntropy reaches $0.752$ at $202.8$ alarms ($0.259$) versus $0.618$ at $262.3$ alarms ($0.221$) for TimesNet; buffered at $q^*{=}0.001$, TS-JEPA attains recall $0.487$ (OpEntropy $0.559$) vs.\ $0.583$ for TimesNet at higher precision ($0.236\text{--}0.242$ vs.\ $0.194$). Because anomalies span hundreds of steps (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), alarming at $30\%$ FPR intersects $>95\%$ of segments by chance ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$). Under post-hoc matched realized test FPR ($f^* = 0.01$ strictly causal), TS-JEPA achieves event recall $0.395$ (latent models $0.371\text{--}0.431$, reaching $0.431$ for \texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}) versus $0.217$ for TimesNet and $0.149$ for TranAD. At relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$ causal and buffered), recall differences narrow (over $f^* \in [0.10, 0.50]$, causal TS-JEPA vs.\ TimesNet is $+0.061$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.153, +0.329]$, $p = 0.625$): latent scoring improves sensitivity specifically at stringent low-FPR budgets ($f^* \le 0.01$), while achieving those budgets prospectively remains bounded by calibration drift.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does static extreme-value thresholding achieve its nominal design risk $q{=}10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample? It does not. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a), where Overshoot $>q$ is the share of cells whose $95\%$ CI lower bound exceeds $q$), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro (cell-weighted) causal FPR of $1.51\%$ ($1.71\%$) for TS-JEPA and $1.95\%$ ($2.12\%$) for OpEntropy ($15\text{--}20\times$ the $0.1\%$ target) and buffered stream-macro (cell-weighted) FPR of $5.54\%$ ($6.40\%$) and $6.38\%$ ($7.25\%$), or $55\text{--}64\times$ ($64\text{--}73\times$) the nominal target. While this represents a substantial relative improvement over TimesNet ($16.11\%$ stream-macro causal, $19.85\%$ buffered) and TranAD ($14.23\%$, $19.63\%$), a $1.5\%\text{--}6.4\%$ nuisance-alarm rate still misses operational $0.1\%$ targets in continuous monitoring. Closing this remaining $15\text{--}64\times$ calibration gap requires pairing latent residuals with block-declustered GPD fitting (replacing raw exceedances $N_u$ with cluster maxima at Ferro--Segers extremal index $\hat{\theta}$), online conformal or rolling quantile updates gated by prediction-error variance under discrete regime shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), or state-conditioned threshold tables indexed by telemetry operating mode. Three diagnostic findings explain the static overshoot:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Multi-risk breakdown and quantile/length sensitivity:} Across $q \in \{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), static thresholding overshoots nominal risk for all models: TS-JEPA median causal FPR is $0.0001$, $0.0047$, $0.0106$, and $0.0585$ vs.\ $0.1360$, $0.1360$, $0.1603$, and $0.2889$ for TimesNet. On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u{=}p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u{=}28$ (IQR $20\text{--}31$) causally and $21$ ($16\text{--}32$) buffered, with $0\%$ numerical fallback and median bootstrap threshold uncertainty $\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal ($0.8\%\text{--}1.1\%$ buffered). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi{=}-0.90$) in $4.4\%$ of TS-JEPA and OpEntropy cells ($\text{median }\xi{=}-0.27, \sigma{=}0.11$) vs.\ $34.4\%$ (TimesNet) and $54.4\%$ (TranAD); buffered $64$-step smoothing raises boundary hits to $26.3\%\text{--}31.6\%$. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR invariant ($0.0241\text{--}0.0242$ for TS-JEPA; $0.0278$ for OpEntropy), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ vs.\ $100\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) lowers median $N_u$ to $20$ and raises causal FPR from $0.0242$ to $0.0372$ (TS-JEPA) and $0.0278$ to $0.0413$ (OpEntropy).
    \item \textbf{Mechanism of the kurtosis-multiplier failure:} Code verification confirms $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ to $<10^{-15}$. On leptokurtic buffered cells ($\gamma_2 \ge 0$, $68.1\%$ of TS-JEPA cells), $\tau_{\text{eff}} \ge \tau$ lowers buffered FPR from $0.0449$ to $0.0111$ (OpEntropy $0.0634 \to 0.0386$). However, $64$-step smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of buffered cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$) with tight dispersion ($\sigma_{\text{cal}}/\mu_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.02\text{--}0.05$): scaling total $\tau$ toward zero pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below $p_{99.5}$ in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating FPR on $\gamma_2 < 0$ cells from $0.1048$ to $0.4870$ ($0.0640 \to 0.1630$ overall). Scaling strictly the tail excess above $u$, $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$, prevents threshold collapse below $u$ and holds 19-stream buffered FPR to $0.0919$ vs.\ $0.0929$ unadjusted for TS-JEPA ($0.1008$ vs.\ $0.1011$ for OpEntropy; $0.0240$ vs.\ $0.0242$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition of drift vs.\ temporal dependence:} Permuting held-out scores against a fixed threshold cannot alter exceedance rates; instead, our three-stage protocol pools $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{cal}} + N_{\text{hold}}}$ and \emph{refits} SPOT ($u{=}p_{98}, q{=}10^{-3}$) from scratch on each permuted calibration split (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}(b)). Stage~1 (chronological) fits $\tau_{\text{chrono}}$ on $v_{\text{cal}}$ and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} = \operatorname{mean}(v_{\text{hold}} > \tau_{\text{chrono}})$. Stage~2 ($L{=}64$ block permutation) partitions $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}}$ into $\lceil (N_{\text{cal}}+N_{\text{hold}})/64\rceil$ contiguous $64$-step blocks, randomly permutes blocks, splits into $v_{\text{cal}}^{(\text{blk})}$ ($N_{\text{cal}}$ points) and $v_{\text{hold}}^{(\text{blk})}$ ($N_{\text{hold}}$ points), \emph{refits} $\tau_{\text{block}}$ on $v_{\text{cal}}^{(\text{blk})}$, and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{block}} = \operatorname{mean}(v_{\text{hold}}^{(\text{blk})} > \tau_{\text{block}})$, equalizing block distributions while preserving intra-block autocorrelation. Stage~3 (pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation) uniformly shuffles $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}}$, splits into $v_{\text{cal}}^{(\text{pt})}$ and $v_{\text{hold}}^{(\text{pt})}$, \emph{refits} $\tau_{\text{point}}$ on $v_{\text{cal}}^{(\text{pt})}$, and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{point}} = \operatorname{mean}(v_{\text{hold}}^{(\text{pt})} > \tau_{\text{point}})$. Across the 18 streams with $N_{\text{cal}}, N_{\text{hold}} \ge 100$, decomposing excess overshoot $\Delta\text{FPR}_{\text{tot}} = \text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} - q$ yields estimated contributions for TS-JEPA ($0.0121 \to 0.0058 \to 0.0019$) of $+0.0063$ ($95\%$ stream-bootstrap CI $[-0.0054, +0.0194]$, $56.6\%$ of mean excess) for inter-block shift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$), $+0.0039$ ($[+0.0001, +0.0103]$, $35.3\%$) for serial dependence ($\text{FPR}_{\text{block}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$), and $+0.0009$ ($[-0.0006, +0.0027]$, $8.1\%$) for i.i.d.\ tail error ($\text{FPR}_{\text{point}} - q$). For OpEntropy ($0.0185 \to 0.0073 \to 0.0022$), estimated shares are $64.1\%$ ($+0.0112$, CI $[-0.0015, +0.0336]$), $29.1\%$ ($+0.0051$, CI $[-0.0005, +0.0146]$), and $6.9\%$; for TimesNet ($0.0872 \to 0.0204 \to 0.0037$) and TranAD ($0.0741 \to 0.0067 \to 0.0032$), inter-block shift accounts for $77.5\%$ (CI $[+0.0310, +0.1082]$) and $92.3\%$ (CI $[+0.0354, +0.1062]$), and serial dependence for $19.4\%$ and $4.7\%$. Because latent predictors achieve $\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} = 0$ on 11 of 18 streams (skewing stream-level differences onto the seven drifting streams so the TS-JEPA and OpEntropy inter-block CIs span zero), these percentages represent estimated shares of aggregate excess risk rather than invariant per-stream causal proportions. Across $N{=}1{,}080$ cells, KS drift correlates with realized FPR ($\rho{=}+0.377$, CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$). In Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), rolling calibrators hold risk to $1\%\text{--}7\%$ under discrete shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33\text{--}0.39$ FPR).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 telemetry streams under a strict held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Mechanism Isolation and TOST Bounds:} Contrasting 12 backbones decouples score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks bound kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$) and halve FPR, while learned latent projection halves calibration-to-holdout drift ($0.376 \to 0.209\text{--}0.214$) and lowers causal FPR to $0.009\text{--}0.028$. On a fixed Koopman backbone, the spectral regularizer satisfies TOST equivalence down to $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ Point-F1 ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$) and $\pm 0.010$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0088$; Reynolds within $\pm 0.035$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0031$), acting as an operator conditioning mechanism while the OpEntropy transition architecture cuts buffered delay from $110.3$ to $79.5$ steps ($127.0 \to 58.2$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Control vs.\ Detection:} Latent scoring reduces held-out false alarms $3\text{--}4\times$ buffered ($7.4\%\text{--}10.1\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$) and $8\text{--}20\times$ causally ($1.1\%\text{--}2.8\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$) and raises low-FPR causal event recall ($0.371\text{--}0.431$ vs.\ $0.149\text{--}0.217$ at $f^*{=}0.01$), while Point-F1 and PR-AUC match reconstructors.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Gap, Refitted Permutation, and Boundaries:} Static SPOT misses its $q{=}0.1\%$ target out of sample ($1.51\%\text{--}1.95\%$ causal and $5.54\%\text{--}6.38\%$ buffered FPR for JEPAs, or $15\text{--}64\times$ overshoot); refitted permutations estimate that inter-block shift accounts for $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ and serial dependence for $29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$ of excess causal risk. Closing this gap requires declustered GPD fitting or online conformal updates, while $S{=}1$ eliminates the $63$-step emission lag ($S{=}64$) and quiet flatlines ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ recall) remain open limits.
\end{enumerate}
