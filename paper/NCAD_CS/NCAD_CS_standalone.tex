\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs sum every coordinate into one number, letting drifting coordinates inflate the threshold. We replace that scalar score with per-coordinate circuit breakers: across target horizons, each coordinate's position error and horizon-mean drift receive healthy-fitted tripwires, and an alarm fires when a quorum trips, with the quorum fitted to correlated trip counts on healthy data. The operational detector, Laminar-JEPA-Lite, uses 128 circuit breakers across three prediction horizons. On 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets under a disjoint five-block protocol, it raises live event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA across all nine datasets and nearly halves detection delay (69.0 versus 133.5 steps), trading higher mean held-out false alarms (6.4 versus 4.4 percent; median 0.93 percent). Because healthy regime shifts drive upper-tail false alarms and slow ramps remain undetected, autonomous deployment requires a held-out acceptance check and live drift monitoring.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Anomaly detection, Multivariate time series, Representation learning, Self-supervised learning, Statistical process control.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream readings from dozens of sensors at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A detector watching this telemetry has two jobs: raise an alarm soon after something breaks, and stay quiet the rest of the time. Faults are rare and seldom labeled, so the detector must learn what normal looks like from healthy history alone. Operators lose trust quickly when a monitor alarms several times a day on healthy data, and an ignored monitor catches nothing.

The most common approach trains a network to reproduce raw sensor signals and raises an alarm when the reproduction is poor; TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} follow this recipe. In practice, much of raw telemetry cannot be copied at all. Random sensor jitter leaves an irreducible error, and benign thermal drift or duty-cycle changes degrade the copy even when nothing is broken. Summing errors across 25--55 channels measured in different units allows one noisy channel to dominate, producing heavy-tailed spikes on healthy data.

These spikes defeat threshold calibration. Setting thresholds on healthy windows with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} still yields $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) for reconstruction detectors on later healthy data across our 19 design-stage development streams, roughly one step in four. Because faults span many steps, alarming at random $24\%$ of the time already hits $60.0\%$ of fault episodes; TimesNet catches $58.8\%$, reflecting frequent alarms rather than selective fault recognition.

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoids copying raw signals. An encoder compresses each window into a 32-number summary, and a predictor forecasts the summary of the next window. Discarding unpredictable jitter yields lighter error tails and cuts healthy false alarms on those development streams by an order of magnitude (Contribution~3).

Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse the 32 prediction errors into one scalar score and one threshold. When one or two summary features drift slightly as a machine warms up or switches load, their growing errors push the shared threshold upward. A fault that shifts the remaining 30 features moderately then stays below that raised threshold and goes missed. We call these drifting features \emph{hijacker dials}. On our development streams, single-score JEPAs reach causal Point-F1 (step-level alarm accuracy) of only $0.050\text{--}0.055$ and need $138\text{--}154$ steps to flag a fault.

Two evaluation shortcuts also inflate published numbers: point adjustment credits an entire fault segment if one step inside it is flagged \cite{kim2022towards}, and thresholds tuned with test labels or calibrated on adaptation data understate false alarms. We score every step without point adjustment, fit thresholds on healthy data only, separate model fitting, checkpoint selection, adaptation, and calibration into disjoint healthy blocks on our primary archive, and reserve 118 streams never inspected during design.

We replace the single scalar alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, like a building breaker box where a short in one circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across target prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, each of the 32 coordinates receives separate \emph{dials} tracking horizon error size and persistent drift, each with its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and the system alarms when a quorum of dials trips together. Drifting dials widen only their own thresholds instead of blinding the rest of the panel. Unlike a bank of independent detectors, the dials are structured views of one error matrix (per-horizon error size and persistent cross-horizon displacement), and the quorum is fitted to the joint trip counts seen on healthy data, so dial correlation is measured, not assumed away. Targeting abrupt to medium-onset faults (within roughly one $256$-step context window), we study two variants (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, our preferred operational architecture, predicts $M=3$ horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $(3+1)\times 32=128$ dials ($96$ horizon positions plus $32$ horizon-mean drifts) under one quorum ($120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ parameters).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, a 224-dial diagnostic reference sharing the exact same streamlined encoder and parallel predictor, scores four error views across $M=4$ horizons plus 32 onset relays to isolate which views aid live causal versus buffered attribution; more dials do not imply a better operational detector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Thirty-three development streams (where adaptation and thresholds share calibration data) provide exploratory design-stage evidence, while 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets (of 141 streams across 12 datasets) under a disjoint five-block protocol provide primary evidence of generalization and threshold behavior (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}). We report live \emph{causal} streaming separately from \emph{buffered} after-the-fact attribution:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Live event coverage and delay:} On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA across all $9$ datasets (sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay ($69.0$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps), trading higher mean causal held-out FPR ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$). Pooled causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ (Wilcoxon $p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$; cluster confidence interval (CI) $[-0.026,+0.069]$) and more than doubles from $0.040$ to $0.085$ when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ calibration windows exist ($p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Operating point, false-alarm tail, and deployment gate:} At matched healthy alarm rates, the panel performs on par with Fisher, top-$k$, and max-$z$ pooling of the same residuals, and at matched test FPR it shows no ranking advantage over the scalar baseline, so its value lies in where its label-free quorum sets the operating point. Causal held-out FPR has median $0.93\%$ ($88.1\%$ of streams at or below $5\%$) but $95$th percentile $64.7\%$ because healthy regime shifts, rather than calibration length alone, govern operational reliability, motivating a formal five-step acceptance and drift-monitoring protocol.
    \item \emph{Design-stage comparison with reconstructors:} On 19 development streams, latent prediction holds causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors, and at a matched $1\%$ test FPR the multi-horizon JEPA backbone catches $56\%\text{--}63\%$ of events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet.
    \item \emph{Explicit limits:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} gives geometric bounds whose scalar score reweighting is null; most streams have too few effective calibration samples to estimate a $99.5\%$ quorum quantile precisely; an optional channel branch helps synthetic single-sensor faults only at a higher FPR ($6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$); LayerNorm and centering effects are design-stage findings; and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including all GHL streams) and natural sensor failures remain out of reach.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$: autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly; TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches (temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}; we instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate alternatives such as univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case in this paper is a \emph{stream}: one recording from one system, with $K$ sensor channels sampled over time. Every stream has a healthy (nominal) history, used for training and calibration, followed by a labeled test segment that contains the faults. We use two collections of streams. A 45-stream development suite drawn from 11 datasets guided every design decision and supplies the main development benchmark. A larger 141-stream archive drawn from 12 datasets supplies the final evaluation, and 118 of its streams were never inspected during design.

\noindent\emph{Development suite:} The 45 streams come from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. They cover three kinds of systems with channel counts from 2 to 72 and very different fault types:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry:} six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$). NASA labels each SMAP and MSL fault as either an out-of-range \emph{point} fault, where a value leaves its normal range, or an in-range \emph{contextual} fault, where values stay in range but behave abnormally together \cite{Hundman2018b}.
    \item \emph{Wearable motion sensing:} six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} recorded at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes), so the $S{=}64$-step target window spans one second.
    \item \emph{Industrial processes and infrastructure:} 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$) whose $26$ faults are slow ramps, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three of these streams form the development benchmark: 19 non-GHL streams and the 14 GHL streams, spanning 7 datasets. Inclusion required enough healthy data for calibration ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ windows), at most 55 channels, and at most $1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). The other 12 streams are reserved: five have very short healthy histories ($764$--$1{,}492$ steps; CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), the six CICIDS streams exceed the channel limit, and GECCO is evaluated in the archive instead.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive contains every qualifying stream from 12 datasets, requiring at least $100$ calibration windows and at least one labeled anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, and 13 OPPORTUNITY streams, plus single streams from GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN. Healthy histories range from $1{,}920$ to $132{,}481$ steps and test segments from $2{,}158$ to $114{,}001$ steps. Removing the 23 streams used to tune the method leaves 118 untouched streams from 9 datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{How each stream is split:} Every healthy history is divided in time order. The first $65\%$ fits the model, and its first part also sets the per-channel standardization ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$). The next $17.5\%$ calibrates alarm thresholds ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), and after a 64-step gap the last $17.5\%$ measures false alarms on healthy data the thresholds have never seen ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$). Archive runs further divide the model-fitting block (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams from the same archive often share hardware, so they are not fully independent. We therefore also treat whole datasets as units of analysis (7 for the development benchmark, 9 for the untouched streams, 12 for the full archive), using Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and leave-one-dataset-out audits (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.77\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{Four-stage pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. \textbf{(1)}~A sliding window ($C{=}256$ context steps, $S{\le}64$ target steps) feeds online and target encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) built on a shared four-block TCN with depthwise frequency shells for $K{\ge}25$, hook-free multi-depth pooling, barycenter centering, and LayerNorm. \textbf{(2)}~A parallel MLP predictor forecasts $M$ nested target prefixes ($M{=}3$ for \emph{Lite}; $M{=}4$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}), and errors are whitened into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 32}$. \textbf{(3)}~The errors expand into $(3+1) \times 32 = 128$ position-plus-drift dials (\emph{Lite}) or $7 \times 32 = 224$ dials (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}). \textbf{(4)}~Each dial has its own tripwire, and a quorum of trips raises a causal ($\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$) or buffered alarm.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-5pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} shows the pipeline from raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($K$ channels over $T$ steps) to alarms. In plain terms, it has four stages: (1)~encode each window of recent data into a short summary vector; (2)~predict the summaries of the next few stretches of data; (3)~turn the prediction errors into a set of \emph{dials}, each measuring one aspect of the error; and (4)~give every dial its own threshold and raise an alarm when enough dials trip together. The networks are trained on healthy data only; stabilization weights and alarm thresholds are then fitted on later healthy blocks and frozen.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time. At each position $t$ it splits into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of the past $C = 256$ steps and a \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the next $S = 64$ steps, which ends at the decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features are split into four smooth frequency bands (dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose band weights sum to one at every frequency), filtered by per-shell depthwise causal convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) with dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ \cite{bai2018empirical}. Lower-channel streams ($K < 25$) skip the band split and feed directly into the TCN.

The encoders follow the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}: instead of reconstructing raw waveforms, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and a target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$ channels) pool temporal (last step, max, mean $\in\mathbb{R}^{144}$) and statistical (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, mean absolute first difference $\in\mathbb{R}^{144}$) descriptors through $D=32$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads and a linear bottleneck to $D=32$. $E_\phi$ is an exponential moving average (EMA) of $E_\theta$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D archive controls) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Two native encoder mechanisms preserve fast spikes and center offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping (keeps fast transients):} Both encoders read three intermediate feature maps directly inside the forward pass: the front-end output, block $1$, and final block $3$. Concatenated mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{288}$) pass through a bias-free $288\to 32\to 32$ GELU head with affine-free LayerNorm and blend with the deep output via $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering (removes common offsets):} During training, a running mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, copied into $E_\phi$ at every EMA step) is subtracted from context and target summaries before an affine-free terminal LayerNorm. Centering removes training offsets without guaranteeing invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The simplest JEPA detector, the single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA}, uses a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to predict one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the whole 64-step target window, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant penalty ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Summarizing all 64 steps in one vector can average away a short fault. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} therefore predict $M$ nested prefixes of the target window: $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$ steps) in \emph{Lite}, and $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ steps) in \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. Starting from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ plus learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a single parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP predicts all $M$ summaries $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$ behind one final LayerNorm. The loss gives the full window weight $w_M=1/2$ and each shorter prefix $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ ($1/4$ in Lite, $1/6$ in 224-Bit), and adds a penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$ that keeps the summaries from collapsing to a constant:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. The penalty acts on each batch of predicted summaries and does two jobs: it keeps every coordinate varying, and it discourages coordinates from copying one another. Unlike log-determinant penalties, which need batches larger than $D$, it works at our batch size $B=16<D=32$. The diagonal entries of $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$, push each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma = 0.75$; the off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize the sample covariance (divisor $B-1$). Hence
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 .
\end{equation}
The penalty raises the effective rank of the target summaries, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}, from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first passed through a \emph{damped whitening} step fitted on healthy training windows, which decorrelates them and brings well-estimated directions close to unit variance. We flatten $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($128$ in full, $96$ in Lite), accumulate its \texttt{float32} sample mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and unbiased covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = (N_{\text{fit}}-1)^{-1}\sum_t (\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})(\cdot)^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$, eigendecompose $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$, clamp rank-deficient or numerical negative modes via $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$, and form $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})$ by the symmetric square root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ whose training covariance has eigenvalues $\lambda_k^2/(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2$: close to $1$ for well-estimated directions, while rank-deficient or near-zero modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$) are attenuated toward $0$ rather than amplified. The whitening is therefore approximate, with damping $10^{-3}$ fixed across all cohorts.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$, never on the block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ that later sets alarm thresholds (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), and blend them toward a simpler per-coordinate scaling, the unwhitened error divided by its root-mean-square on $\mathbf{X}_q$, $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=e_{m,c}(t)/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} An earlier six-block sequential variant also tested gradient-free editing of predictor rows (Steps Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$; because it yielded no reliable archive benefit (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}), the unified backbone omits weight editing entirely.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

The proposition below states two exact properties of this split. Part~(i) says that drift and fluctuation measure non-overlapping parts of the position energy, and that the endpoint dial cannot exceed a multiple of the fluctuation dial. Part~(ii) says the fluctuation pattern lives in at most $M-1$ directions and defines $R_{\text{LT}}$, which is $1$ when one pattern dominates and $1/(M-1)$ when the energy is spread evenly. These are geometric facts about the representation, not accuracy guarantees.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Instead of adding all 224 dials into one score, Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) gives each dial its own threshold, learned from the healthy calibration block. A dial \emph{trips} when it exceeds its threshold, and an alarm fires when enough dials trip at once. Because all dials come from the same $4 \times 32$ error matrix, their trips are correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort; the variance of the trip count is $7.2\times$ that of independent dials). We therefore do not assume independence; the alarm quorums are set from the trip counts observed on healthy calibration data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded directly at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 onset relays:} When a sudden fault first enters the right edge of the target window, the short prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover healthy data, so few position dials trip. To catch this moment, each coordinate $c$ has stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($q_{0.993}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$) and fires a relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when its full-window position dial ($m=4$) trips together with its endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$ dial.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, the trips from window $[t,t+S)$ are reported at its right end $\tau=t+S-1$, the earliest moment all of its data exist; this fixes a reporting lag of $S-1$ steps, separate from the detection delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$ after fault onset $t^*$. Under \emph{buffered attribution}, used for after-the-fact review, each trip is assigned to the steps it describes: position trips to their prefix $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation trips to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint trips to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. $N_{224}(\tau)$, the position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and the relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. Ranking metrics use the plain count $N_{224}(\tau)$ directly. The binary alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with quorums set on calibration counts: $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$. All quantiles use linear interpolation, the safety factor is applied before the floor, quorums stay real-valued, and both trips and alarms use strict inequalities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both tiers share the exact $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$-parameter backbone ($120.0\text{k}$ at $K < 25$ and $127.7\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$, down from $295.8\text{k}$ in the legacy six-block sequential model) and differ only in horizon count and dial expansion:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Shared streamlined backbone:} Four adaptive TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $D$-wide pooling and native hook-free tap heads, depthwise rFFT shells gated at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbf{u}_m = z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{32} \to \text{Linear}(32,64) \to \text{GELU} \to \text{LayerNorm}(64) \to \text{Linear}(64,32)$, returning $\operatorname{LN}_{\text{no-aff}}(\mathbf{u}_m + \Delta_m)$).
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and keeps only the $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ under a single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$, removing fluctuation/endpoint dials and onset relays.
    \item \emph{Diagnostic $7D=224$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and expands errors into all $224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA (224 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, attribution mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Per-timestep binary alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 1: Disjoint Training, Whitening \& Adaptation ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q$)}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$ and standardize channels using $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ alone.
\State Set nested prefixes $S_{1:3}=\{16,32,64\}$ (\text{Lite}) or $S_{1:4}=\{16,32,48,64\}$ (\text{224-Bit}) with $K\ge 25$ depthwise rFFT shells.
\For{stride-$16$ training batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ and targets $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$ with hook-free multi-depth pooling and barycenter shift $-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$.
    \State Predict all $M$ prefixes in parallel behind one LayerNorm; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (or fixed epochs) and fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_+^{MD})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\State Fit and freeze stabilization scales and blending weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 2: Untouched Circuit-Breaker Calibration on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$}
\State Compute frozen stabilized residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ via (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).
\If{tier $=$ 224-Bit}
    \State Expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ into $7D=224$ dials (position, drift, fluctuation, endpoint) and $32$ right-edge onset relays.
    \State Fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\Else \Comment{Operational tier: Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
    \State Extract $(M+1)D=128$ dials ($96$ positions $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}|$ + $32$ drifts $|\bar{e}_c|$), fit $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and set $K_{\text{Lite}}=\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 3: Online Inference on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$}
    \State Compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \to \mathbf{d}(t)$ and binary trips $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(d_j(t)>\tau_j)$ (plus relays $r_c(t)$ if 224-Bit).
    \State Attribute active counts to right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ (\text{causal}) or prefix spans $[t,t+S_m)$ (\text{buffered}).
    \State Emit $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ when active counts exceed $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) or $K_{\text{Lite}}$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} To measure what the breaker panel adds over a single score on the same backbone (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), the 1D controls pool a residual vector into one number: an equal blend of its $\ell_2$ norm and a softmax-weighted coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$). Single-horizon TS-JEPA pools $\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$; 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA combines the pooled full-tensor and peak-horizon scores by root-mean-square (with optional $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$). Both are thresholded with SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) into a model-fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a held-out nominal suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint weight fitting, checkpoint selection, and adaptation):} Reusing calibration windows for early stopping, blending (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), or quenching can make nominal thresholds optimistic. Archive benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) therefore use a disjoint \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$) inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$. First, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits standardization, network weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$. Next, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the checkpoint. Finally, $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$ stride-1 windows isolated from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ by $S=64$ steps) fits and freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and optional quenching (Steps Q1--Q4) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched. Fixed-epoch development runs (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) omit $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$, and the 19-stream cache skips (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and quenching. A four-block check ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) on the 19 development streams yields causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ of $0.96\%$ for \emph{Lite} and $1.05\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, matching Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, though the smaller $50\%$ fitting block lowers causal F1 ($0.133$ to $0.071$, and $0.144$ to $0.076$). On short histories, \emph{two-fold cross-fitting} swaps the halves of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ and requires $\hat{y}^{(1)}\wedge\hat{y}^{(2)}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched calibration and boundary isolation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Stride-1 windows of length $W = 320$ in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ give $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ indices. Splitting at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step gap $[H : H+S)$ so target observations never overlap (a $G=320$ gap shifts causal FPR by $<0.25$ points). On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone, the frozen model fits all dial scales, guards, tripwires (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$. We separate raw count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective sample size} $N_{\text{eff},s} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int},s}$ via Sokal estimators ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Statistical Inference}
We report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Dataset-clustered and stream-level inference:} \emph{Dataset-clustered inference} ($95\%$ cluster-bootstrap CIs and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip permutation tests) is emphasized alongside stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs ($2{,}000$ resamples). Four focal causal contrasts on the 118 untouched streams compare \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} with Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) and with TS-JEPA on Point-F1 and ER. Their Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) \cite{benjamini1995controlling} adjusted $p$-values are $0.00088$ and $3.2\times 10^{-6}$ for stream-level Point-F1, and $0.0078$ and $0.016$ for dataset sign-flip ER (positive on $9/9$ and $7/9$ datasets). Other quoted $p$-values are raw and descriptive, and non-Exathlon subsets ($n=101$, $8$ datasets) check sensitivity to archive composition.
(2)~\emph{12-fold leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) audit:} Across all 12 LODO folds of the 141-stream archive, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps a positive gain on causal ER ($+0.104$ to $+0.145$ vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D), buffered Point-F1, and causal Point-F1 vs.\ TS-JEPA ($\max p=1.2\times 10^{-5}$); its causal Point-F1 gain over Multi-Horizon 1D holds in $12/12$ folds.

\subsection{Cohorts and Baselines}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} omits Steps Q1--Q4. Causal and buffered modes share identical splits per cohort (except a single-horizon 130-stream buffered screen in Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}):
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-stage, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}):} Because these streams were inspected during design and the 33-stream suite shares $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for stabilization and thresholds, they compare design choices rather than out-of-sample calibration. The 19-stream cache ($6$ datasets) tests the dial ladder, panel components, and conformal/SPC banks on shared unquenched residuals (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b), Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}); the 33-stream benchmark ($7$ datasets: $19$ non-GHL in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) plus $14$ GHL in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) compares all baselines. Tripwire and quench constants were fixed on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched held-out cohorts (primary evidence):} Removing those $23$ tuning streams from the $141$-stream archive ($12$ datasets, five-block protocol) leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($101$ non-Exathlon across $8$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), plus a single-seed Q1--Q4 isolation audit (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\emph{Three baseline tiers:} (i)~\emph{Matched-backbone controls} (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D)) fix the representation and test one SPOT score against the panel. (ii)~\emph{System-level aggregators} fix the whitened residuals and replace the panel with maximum $z$, top-$k$ voting, $\ell_2^2$ conformal, Fisher or Stouffer $p$-pooling, multiplicity-controlled conformal banks, or SPC charts, calibrated to the panel's $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rate unless noted (Tables~\ref{tab:matched_alert}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). (iii)~\emph{External baselines} (TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, PCA Forecaster, Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) share splits, training-only scaling, gaps, and SPOT ($u=p_{98}$, $q=10^{-3}$), using $206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ parameters while panels add none. On the archive, every JEPA backbone receives the same training budget: seeds $\{42,123,456\}$ reset all random number generators before each backbone, and each seed trains independently with AdamW ($10^{-3}$ learning rate, $10^{-4}$ weight decay, cosine schedule) for up to $12$ epochs with early stopping on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (patience $4$). Autocorrelation $\tau_{\text{int}}$ is estimated separately on each calibrated statistic's $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ series using Sokal's automatic window ($c=5$), giving $N_{\text{eff}}(1-q)$ expected effective exceedances for a $q$-quantile. All runs used PyTorch 2.4 with CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090, where \emph{Lite} takes $0.154\,\text{ms}$/window versus $0.375\,\text{ms}$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($2.43\times$ speedup; $188\,\text{MB}$ peak); code: \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Every alarm $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$ is scored step by step against $y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}. \emph{Causal} numbers show what an operator sees live at step $\tau$; \emph{buffered} numbers reflect retrospective review after the target window finishes and are not live performance. Headline values average seeds per stream before averaging across streams (or unweighted across datasets in dataset-macro Buffered Point-F1, $\text{DS-F1}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{False-alarm rates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$):} Fraction of healthy steps flagged on the untouched pre-test check block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ ($[H+S:M_{\text{val}})$) and on healthy test steps ($y(\tau)=0$). Because a few drifting streams skew the mean, we also report stream medians and $90$th percentiles ($\text{p90}$).
    \item \emph{Step-level accuracy (Point-F1, precision-recall (PR) AUC):} Point-F1 (Pt-F1) is the harmonic mean of step precision (fraction of alarm steps labeled faulty) and step recall (fraction of faulty steps flagged). Without point adjustment, Pt-F1 is naturally modest ($0.05\text{--}0.25$, not $0.90$): residuals peak near regime changes rather than across every step of a long fault, and when faults occupy a small fraction $\pi$ of the stream, even a $1\%$ false-alarm rate adds many false-positive steps (alarming at every step scores $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}=2\pi/(1+\pi)$). PR-AUC (Buf.\ PR when buffered) scores ranking quality across all thresholds without inflation from healthy steps.
    \item \emph{Episode catch rate and speed (ER, Delay $\Delta t$):} Event recall (ER) is the fraction of contiguous faulty episodes ($y=1$ runs) with at least one alarm; it must be read with FPR because random alarms at rate $p$ still hit long episodes at rate $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$. Detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ counts steps from fault onset $t^*$ to the first in-segment alarm over \emph{detected} episodes (distinct from the $S-1$ causal lag), so catching an extra late-developing fault can raise mean $\Delta t$.
    \item \emph{Budget controls and score diagnostics:} \emph{Auto.}\ uses the deployed healthy-only threshold (quorums or SPOT), $q^*=0.05$ sets thresholds at the $95$th $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ percentile ($q_{1-q^*}$), and diagnostic controls $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$ use test labels post-hoc to match a $1\%$ or $5\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$. On nominal scores, excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ ($\gamma_2=0$ for Gaussian tails) measures $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ spike heaviness, and the two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) distance $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0,1]$ ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) measures healthy drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
We keep experimental regimes separate: Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert} score one fixed $19$-stream $M=4$ cache; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) retrains each single-factor control ($3$ seeds, disjoint four-block protocol); Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) is a five-seed development run; and Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates the untouched $118$-stream archive:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cached dial ladder vs.\ why \emph{Lite} uses $128$ rather than $160$ dials:} On the fixed $M=4$ sequential cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding from $1$ to $224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$; buffered $+0.033$, $p=0.53$). Pruning fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to $160$ position-plus-drift dials reaches $0.098$ causal Point-F1 at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (removing relays gives $0.068$, a single $224$-dial quorum gives $0.063$, uniform smearing raises buffered delay to $115.4$ steps, and unweighted $N_{224}$ shifts archive PR-AUC by at most $0.0011$ with identical alarms). Retrained end-to-end on the parallel \emph{Lite} backbone (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), however, adding the fourth prefix ($S_3=48$, $160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) yields no significant Point-F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$, $\Delta\text{ER}=+0.4$ points; median causal F1 $0.026$ vs.\ $0.033$). Instead, it raises $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.82\%$ to $1.06\%$ causal; $6.2\%$ to $6.8\%$ buffered), expands $\mathbf{\Omega}$ from $96^2$ to $128^2$, and slows inference by $25.5\%$ ($0.123$ vs.\ $0.098\,\text{ms}$/window). On the $118$-stream archive rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), $128$-dial \emph{Lite} also matches or exceeds retrained $160$- and $224$-dial panels ($0.078$ causal and $0.154$ buffered F1, vs.\ $0.073$/$0.140$ and $0.074$/$0.143$).
    \item \emph{Single-factor encoder, predictor, and panel isolation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Flipping one factor from \emph{Lite} validates each simplification. Reducing \emph{depth} ($4$ vs.\ $5$ blocks) preserves F1 and ER ($p=0.98$ causal, $p=0.23$ buffered; median causal F1 $0.033$ vs.\ $0.031$) while saving $23.3\text{k}$ parameters. Narrowing \emph{pooling} ($64$D vs.\ $128$D heads) preserves F1 ($p\ge 0.45$), lifts ER by $+7.3$ causal and $+9.0$ buffered points ($63.1\%$ vs.\ $55.9\%$ causal), and saves $22.9\text{k}$ parameters. Replacing the parallel \emph{predictor} at matched $M=4$ ($160$ dials) with sequential rollout lowers mean F1 ($0.086$ to $0.077$ causal; $0.239$ to $0.195$ buffered, $p=0.078$), doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%$ to $4.0\%$), and adds $10.1$ delay steps and $4.5\text{k}$ parameters. Finally, expanding the \emph{panel} on identical $M=4$ residuals ($224$ vs.\ $160$ dials) lifts causal ER ($63.6\%$ to $69.0\%$, $p=0.0077$) and F1 ($0.086$ to $0.095$, $p=0.012$) only by inflating $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.06\%$ to $1.45\%$ causal; $6.8\%$ to $11.9\%$ buffered), and no panel removes the $S-1$ causal lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching step isolation:} Across single-seed $118$-stream \emph{Laminar-JEPA} checkpoints (unquenched causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=6.76\%$, $\text{F1}=0.0901$), Step Q1 ($k=4$) shifts unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS via 32D LayerNorm coupling ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=7.06\%$), Steps Q2--Q3 cut F1 to $0.0826$ ($6.71\%$), and Step Q4 ($39/118$ accepted) reaches $6.62\%$ ($\text{F1}=0.0904$). Ungated edits hurt $k=2$ dev F1 ($0.141\to 0.049$) and $k\in\{8,12\}$ PR-AUC ($0.292\to 0.223$), so \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate rules fail through three distinct statistical mechanisms on the cached 19-stream residuals (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Cross-coordinate false-alarm compounding:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or even at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds marginal errors across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Discrete finite-sample calibration floor:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) across 128 coordinates or BH false discovery rate (FDR, $q=0.005$) across 224 dials fails even under stationarity because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot fall below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, paralyzing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{Out-of-sample distributional instability vs.\ stable aggregators:} Calibrating the aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) bypasses the discrete floor, exposing out-of-sample instability instead: discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ from boundary ties, and recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$ under mild cross-block covariance and mean drift. Among distributionally stable aggregators on this cache, the 160-dial panel reaches Point-F1 $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, above 128-coordinate top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.068$, $0.056$, $0.072$), while Fisher pooling reaches $0.109$ (Stouffer $0.095$) at $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Independently trained archive comparisons appear in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
A frozen or flatlined sensor barely moves the latent summary because terminal LayerNorm spreads the small change across all $32$ coordinates. On \texttt{SMAP P-3}, for example, a flatline trips only $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip, and a single faulty sensor is further diluted by the $K-1$ healthy channels. Extending an initial six-stream study, we evaluate a causal channel branch across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$) without per-stream or test-label tuning.

For each channel, we compute three 64-step rolling statistics: log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, training-fit first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$. Each statistic is thresholded at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ on nominal validation windows (with a floor of $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD) and a $1.25\times$ half-split drift multiplier) behind a $24$-of-$40$-step persistence gate. We test the branch by injecting three nonoverlapping $200$-step faults per stream into clean test segments of the highest-variance active channel ($57$ events per fault class): variance collapse to the training mean, an alternating $\pm1.35\sigma$ prediction shock, sensor freezing, saturation at $\max(q_{.995},\mu+2.2\sigma)$, a linear $2.5\sigma$ degradation, and a $1.5\sigma$ bias. The variance-collapse union lifts injected-event recall for variance collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%$--$82.5\%$ to $93.0\%$--$94.7\%$, while the full channel branch lifts recall on shocks, gradual degradation, and bias from $59.6\%$--$75.4\%$ to $80.7\%$--$96.5\%$. However, on the \emph{unmodified} test streams, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union, $\text{F1}=0.070$) and $19.4\%$ (full branch, $\text{F1}=0.129$). Synthetic single-channel injections therefore do not establish detection on natural sensor failures, and the full union's $19.4\%$ false-alarm rate makes it unsuitable as a default detector.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, \texttt{GECCO}; three seeds) shows a channel-count threshold: shell splitting improves high-channel spacecraft detection ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), but lowers F1 on both $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), setting the $K \ge 25$ spectral gate in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
The 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) separate contributing factors on the development streams; they are not universal causes. Raw-observation forecasting and autoencoding share heavy tails, large drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$). A $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space cuts kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halves FPR but keeps drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$). Learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). PCA and Isolation Forest keep causal FPR low but reach only $0.017$ and $0.041$ causal Point-F1. The panels' gains do not come from lighter tails alone: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps roughly the same kurtosis as Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$).

\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark and Scope Boundaries}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports exploratory design-stage results on the 33-stream development suite (where stabilization and thresholds share $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Primary evidence is the five-block archive (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}). A separate single-seed 130-stream buffered screen gives context only and is not paired with the archive: TS-JEPA reaches Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$.

\noindent\emph{Configuration:} On the 33-stream suite \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $0.253$ buffered Point-F1 (vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}), more than halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($4.7\%$ vs.\ $11.5\%$), and leads macro Point-F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$).

\noindent\emph{Slow-ramp boundary:} A $C=256$-step encoder cannot see drift slower than its window: context and target summaries move together, yielding $0.000$ Point-F1 on all 14 GHL industrial streams and restricting our scope strictly to abrupt and intermediate-onset faults rather than general industrial coverage. Injected linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and length $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ ($15$ events per cell) map this cutoff: steep ramps ($L=64$, $a\ge 1\sigma$) reach $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ ER at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay ($5.7\text{--}7.7$ points above chance), at $L=C$ the excess falls to $0.5\text{--}2.5$ points, and for $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$) chance-corrected detection vanishes ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points).

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Live streaming is much harder than retrospective review. Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent scores degrade under causal reporting: buffered attribution averages residuals across every overlapping window covering a timestamp, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step with no averaging, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the shared SPOT threshold (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). On these development streams, a rate-matched Bernoulli alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ hits $28.1\%$ of events at $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Exploratory Horizon Sweep Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Shorter horizons trade buffered smoothing for lower live lag ($S-1$ steps). Retraining on five exploratory streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a); at $S=1$ all prefixes coincide), both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ with test FPR $\le 3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037$ and $0.050$ vs.\ $0.014$ and $0.012$) with $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ with $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER, and at $S=4$ delay falls to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps. Buffered Point-F1 instead peaks at long horizons: $0.290$ at $S=32$ and $0.255$ at $S=64$, versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot set a general rule.

\subsection{Design-Stage Operating Budgets and Fault Taxonomies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) examines the 19 development streams across budgets and fault types:
(1)~\emph{Diagnostic budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At a post-hoc matched $f^*=1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD; this ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs does not carry over to the archive (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).
(2)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point faults:} Out-of-range spikes ($>5\sigma$) are caught by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike, whereas in-range contextual faults alter cross-channel phase or velocity within normal bounds; on NASA contextual sequences \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet.

\subsection{Primary Held-Out Generalization, False-Alarm Tails, and Calibration Limits}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

\input{sections/tab_tail_audit.tex}

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:tail_audit} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c) report the disjoint five-block archive. We label each result by its operating point: \emph{autonomous} (deployed healthy-only thresholds), \emph{matched calibration rate} (every detector set to the same healthy alarm rate), \emph{post hoc} (thresholds matched on test labels, diagnostic only), and \emph{buffered} (retrospective review).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Live event coverage and delay vs.\ false-alarm trade-off (autonomous, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} Across the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, $9/9$ datasets, sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps. The full \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $69.4\%$ ER at a $54.7$-step delay, trading higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$; cluster CI for \emph{Lite} ER gain $[+3.7,+24.3]$). Pooled causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ (Wilcoxon $p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$; cluster CI $[-0.026,+0.069]$), driven by streams with $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ where Point-F1 more than doubles ($0.040$ to $0.085$, $n=59$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$) with $+20.4$ ER points. On shorter calibrations ($N_{\text{cal}}<500$), the scalar baseline leads on causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$, $p=0.81$) even though the panel catches more events ($72.1\%$ vs.\ $68.4\%$). Under \emph{buffered} review, \emph{Lite} improves ER ($59.0\%$ to $67.7\%$) and Point-F1 ($0.143$ to $0.158$, $p=0.0098$) while cutting $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $19.5\%$ to $13.3\%$ ($p=2.8\times 10^{-4}$).
    \item \emph{Where the live gain comes from (matched calibration rate and post hoc, Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), \ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)):} On the independent operating-point control rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), matching the panel's low $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ rate (median $0.25\%$) gives the panel higher causal ER ($65.0\%$) than the scalar baseline ($52.6\%$, $p=1.7\times 10^{-5}$) and Fisher, top-$k$, or max-$z$ pooling ($61.1\%$, $58.1\%$, $58.8\%$). At standard healthy alarm rates ($0.5\%\text{--}5\%$), these gaps narrow: Fisher matches or exceeds Point-F1, top-$k$ and max-$z$ match ER, and 1D trails by $3\text{--}6$ ER points. At post hoc matched test FPR, however, the panel shows no ranking advantage, and 1D leads on Point-F1 at $f=1\%$ ($0.084$ vs.\ $0.049$, $p=0.024$). Thus, the panel's gain comes from its label-free quorum operating point rather than a stronger ranking function.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tail and nominal-regime stability (autonomous, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a), Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c)):} Most streams meet standard false-alarm budgets under causal evaluation ($88.1\%$ at or below $5\%$ FPR and $56.8\%$ at or below $1\%$; median $0.93\%$). However, a severe upper tail pushes the mean to $6.4\%$ and the $95$th percentile to $64.7\%$, with $14$ streams exceeding $5\%$ FPR and $8$ exceeding $30\%$ (full threshold counts in Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)). Calibration length alone does not explain this tail ($\rho=-0.11$ with $N_{\text{cal}}$, and $\rho=-0.01$ with $N_{\text{eff}}$), as half of the ten worst streams have $N_{\text{cal}}>700$. Instead, false alarms track nominal-regime drift: the inter-block quorum-count shift $D_{\text{KS}}$ correlates strongly with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($\rho=0.63$, $p=1.6\times 10^{-6}$), with median $D_{\text{KS}}=0.55$ above $10\%$ FPR versus $0.16$ at or below $5\%$. Consequently, regime-shifting streams ($D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.5$) drive most false alarms on test healthy steps for both the panel ($17.0\%$ mean, $3.2\%$ median) and the 1D baseline ($8.8\%$ mean, $0.9\%$ median).
    \item \emph{Threshold computability, tail reliability, and formal deployment protocol:} We separate three distinct checks: (a)~\emph{threshold computability} (evaluating $q_{0.995}$ on finite $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), (b)~\emph{tail-estimation reliability} ($N_{\text{eff}}(1-q)\ge 1$, unmet on $105/118$ streams where median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$ and $N_{\text{eff}}=65$ yield $0.325$ expected exceedances), and (c)~\emph{empirical held-out acceptance} on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$. On the control rerun, replacing $q_{0.995}$ with its distribution-free $90\%$ upper confidence bound trims mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ only from $7.1\%$ to $5.8\%$ ($95$th percentile $50\%$ to $42\%$) while dropping ER from $65.3\%$ to $47.7\%$. Conservative quantiles therefore cannot cure regime shifts, and $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ is necessary (routing $N_{\text{cal}}<500$ streams to 1D lifts pooled causal Point-F1 to $0.095$) yet insufficient. Autonomous deployment therefore requires five steps: (1)~fit backbone, whitening, and stabilization on disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; (2)~compute tripwires and $K_{\text{Lite}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ with $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$; (3)~measure frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; (4)~route streams with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>\alpha_{\text{acc}}$ (e.g., $5\%$) to operator-supervised review; and (5)~monitor live quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ and recalibrate on regime shifts. Across the $50$ archive streams passing steps~(1)--(4) at $\alpha_{\text{acc}}=5\%$, \emph{Lite} outperforms 1D on causal Point-F1 ($0.076$ vs.\ $0.039$, $p=3.5\times 10^{-4}$) at mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=0.7\%$. Even then, post-hold shifts raise mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ to $12.1\%$, making step~(5) essential; on short streams ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$, $n=15$), two-fold cross-fitting cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$ at $65.9\%$ ER.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit:} On development streams, chronological versus block-permuted threshold refits attribute $71.3\%$ (TS-JEPA) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ (reconstructors) of excess causal FPR to inter-block drift, versus $18.7\%$ with centering, without ruling out tail misfit.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied multi-horizon circuit-breaker panels (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for operational deployment; 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as an ablation reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 exploratory design-stage streams and 118 primary untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event coverage and delay:} Under the five-block archive protocol, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ across all nine datasets ($p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay ($69.0$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps) at higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$), using a unified $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$-parameter backbone shared with the 224-dial reference. Pooled causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ overall ($p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$) and more than doubles when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$); on shorter histories the scalar baseline has higher Point-F1. Weight quenching gives no reliable benefit and is omitted.
    \item \emph{Operating point:} At matched healthy alarm rates the panel performs on par with simpler coordinate pooling, and at matched test FPR it does not rank faults better than the scalar baseline. Its value lies in a label-free quorum, fitted to correlated trip counts, that settles at higher episode coverage and shorter delay than scalar SPOT.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tail and deployment protocol:} Causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has mean $6.4\%$, median $0.93\%$, $90$th percentile $6.1\%$, and $95$th percentile $64.7\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), with $88.1\%$ of streams at or below $5\%$. Because healthy regime shifts ($D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.5$) rather than calibration length alone drive most false alarms, $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ is necessary but insufficient; autonomous deployment requires passing a held-out $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ acceptance gate and live drift monitoring.
    \item \emph{Limits:} An optional channel branch helps synthetic single-sensor faults only at a higher FPR, and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL at $0.000$ Point-F1) and natural sensor failures remain open.
\end{enumerate}
