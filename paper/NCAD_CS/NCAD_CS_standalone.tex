\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw sensor signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise far fewer false alarms, but they still add every summary coordinate into one score, so a few slowly drifting coordinates can raise the alarm threshold and hide faults elsewhere. We replace that single score with a panel of per-coordinate circuit breakers: each coordinate's prediction error at several future horizons receives its own threshold fitted on healthy data, and an alarm fires when enough breakers trip together. We study two versions: Laminar-JEPA-Lite, a compact 128-breaker design recommended for deployment, and Laminar-JEPA, a 224-breaker reference variant. On 118 held-out streams from nine datasets that played no role in design, with checkpoint selection, adaptation, and threshold calibration on separate blocks of healthy data, Laminar-JEPA-Lite raises causal event recall from 52.5 to 63.8 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA, improving on every dataset, and outperforms simpler per-coordinate detectors matched to the same alarm rate. On development streams, latent prediction holds causal false-positive rates near 1 percent, against 22 to 24 percent for reconstruction detectors. Slow ramps lasting four or more context windows remain undetected.
\end{abstract}

Anomaly detection, Multivariate time series, Representation learning, Self-supervised learning, Statistical process control.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream readings from dozens of sensors at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A detector watching this telemetry has two jobs: raise an alarm soon after something breaks, and stay quiet the rest of the time. Faults are rare and seldom labeled, so the detector must learn what normal looks like from healthy history alone. And operators lose trust quickly: a monitor that alarms several times a day on healthy data is soon ignored, and an ignored monitor catches nothing.

\noindent\emph{Gap 1, reconstruction detectors confuse noise with faults:} The most common approach trains a network to reproduce the raw sensor signals and raises an alarm when the reproduction is poor; TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} follow this recipe. The idea is sound: a network trained only on healthy data should copy healthy data well and faulty data badly. In practice, much of raw telemetry cannot be copied at all. Sensor jitter is random, so no network can reproduce it, and part of the error never goes away. Slow changes such as thermal drift or a switch in operating mode also degrade the copy even though nothing is broken. These errors are then added across 25--55 channels measured in different units, so a single noisy channel can dominate the total. The result is an error score with occasional large spikes on perfectly healthy data.

These spikes defeat the alarm threshold. We set each detector's threshold on a block of healthy data with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, a standard method that models the extreme tail of the score and targets a low false-alarm rate. On later healthy data from the same streams, the reconstruction detectors nevertheless alarm at $22\%\text{--}24\%$ of time steps under causal reporting, roughly one step in four or five. At that rate, their recall says little about fault recognition. Faults last many steps, so a detector that ignores the data and alarms at random $24\%$ of the time would still place at least one alarm inside $60.0\%$ of fault events. TimesNet catches $58.8\%$: its recall comes from alarming often, not from recognizing faults.

\noindent\emph{Gap 2, one anomaly score allows a few drifting features to hide a fault:} A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoids copying raw signals. An encoder compresses each window of sensor data into a short summary of 32 numbers, and a predictor learns to forecast the summary of the data that comes next. The summary keeps the structure of the signals and discards much of the unpredictable jitter, so its prediction errors are far better behaved and false alarms on healthy data fall by an order of magnitude or more (Contribution~3).

Standard JEPA detectors, however, still squash the 32 prediction errors into one number and compare it against one threshold. That threshold must sit above everything seen on healthy data. If one or two summary features drift slightly over time, as happens when a machine warms up or changes duty cycle, their errors grow and the shared threshold rises to accommodate them. A fault that shifts the other 30 features by a moderate amount then stays below the raised threshold and goes unnoticed. We call these drifting features \emph{hijacker dials}. The cost is visible in streaming use: under causal reporting, single-score JEPA detectors reach a Point-F1 (alarm accuracy scored at every time step) of only $0.050\text{--}0.055$ and take $138\text{--}154$ steps to notice a fault.

\noindent\emph{Gap 3, reported accuracy often depends on how the test is scored:} Two common evaluation shortcuts make detectors look better than they would be in service. Point adjustment counts a whole fault segment as detected if any single step inside it is flagged, which rewards frequent alarms \cite{kim2022towards}. Thresholds tuned with test labels, or calibrated on the same data that shaped the model, give optimistic false-alarm rates. We therefore score every time step without point adjustment, set all thresholds on healthy data only, keep model fitting, checkpoint selection, adaptation, and threshold calibration on separate blocks of data, and report final results on streams never inspected during design.

\noindent\emph{Our approach, one circuit breaker per dial:} We replace the single alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, much like the breaker box in a building, where a short in one room trips one switch regardless of the total load. The predictor forecasts the summary of the next 16, 32, and 64 steps, and for each of the 32 summary features we track how large its error is at each horizon and whether that error leans steadily in one direction. Each such reading is a \emph{dial} with its own threshold fitted on healthy data, and the system alarm fires when enough dials trip together (a quorum). Dials that wander on healthy data receive wider thresholds, so a hijacker widens only its own threshold and can no longer mask the rest of the panel. We target faults that appear suddenly or develop within about one context window (256 steps), and we study two versions (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, our preferred deployment configuration, predicts three horizons in parallel behind a single layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and watches $4\times32=128$ dials under one quorum. It has $35\%\text{--}52\%$ fewer parameters and runs $2.43\times$ faster.
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, the full reference variant, watches $7\times32=224$ dials covering four views of each coordinate's error, plus 32 fast onset relays and an optional weight-editing step that damps drifting coordinates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Design choices were tuned on 33 development streams. The final evidence comes from 118 untouched streams from 9 datasets (of 141 streams from 12 datasets) that played no role in design; checkpoint selection, model adaptation, and threshold calibration each use a separate block of healthy data (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}). We report two alarm modes: \emph{causal}, where each alarm uses only data available at that moment, and \emph{buffered}, where a short look-back window supports after-the-fact attribution.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{More faults caught at low false-alarm rates:} On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catches $63.8\%$ of fault events under causal reporting, against $52.5\%$ for a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. This event recall (ER) gain holds on all $9$ datasets (sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and in all $12$ leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) folds of the full archive. Causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.079$ ($p=0.014$) and buffered Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.156$ ($p=0.022$), and the panel beats simpler per-coordinate detectors tuned to the same overall alarm rate.
    \item \emph{Where the gains come from:} Controlled ablations on matched backbones show that LayerNorm compresses heavy error tails and that centering the summaries reduces drift, while spectral reweighting brings no measurable benefit.
    \item \emph{Far fewer false alarms than reconstruction:} On 19 development streams, latent prediction keeps the causal false-positive rate (FPR) at $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$, against $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstruction detectors. When every detector is allowed the same $1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ and $56.0\%$ of events, against $21.7\%$ for TimesNet.
    \item \emph{Limits:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds how concentrated the horizon errors can be, yet reweighting scores by this quantity does not help. Strong autocorrelation means that 300 effective calibration samples cannot pin down a 99.9th-percentile threshold precisely. An optional single-channel branch lifts recall of injected single-sensor faults to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$ but raises nominal causal FPR from $1.5\%$ to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$. Slow ramps lasting four or more context windows and natural sensor failures remain out of reach.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$: autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly; TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches---temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}---depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}; we instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate alternatives such as univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite spans 45 continuous telemetry streams across 11 datasets from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. Thirty-three streams enter the development protocol (19 non-GHL and 14 GHL). The other 12 are reserved, as listed below. \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry} covers six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$ channels), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$, four primary and two short-nominal), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$), where NASA \cite{Hundman2018b} labels each SMAP/MSL sequence as an out-of-range \emph{point} fault or in-range \emph{contextual} fault (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)). \emph{Wearable kinematics} covers six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals $1\,\text{s}$). \emph{Process and infrastructure telemetry} covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ slow-ramp faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry ($K{=}9$, $37$ anomalies), Metro I-94 traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$).

Each stream has a healthy (nominal) history for training and calibration ($T_{\text{nom}} \in [1{,}920, 132{,}481]$) and a labeled test segment ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized on the weight-fitting prefix ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$), and the nominal history is split chronologically into a $65\%$ model-fitting prefix (subdivided on archive benchmarks; Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), nominal threshold calibration $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ ($17.5\%$), a 64-step isolation gap, and held-out nominal evaluation $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ ($17.5\%$). Development 33-stream inclusion required $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ ($T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$), $K \le 55$, and $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2{\times}10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); the 12 reserved streams comprise five short-nominal streams ($T_{\text{nom}} \in [764, 1{,}492]$, $N_{\text{cal}} \le 101$: CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, two Room-Occupancy), six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$), and GECCO ($K{=}9$, included in archive cohorts). Archive cohorts evaluate all 141 qualifying streams across 12 datasets ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$, $\ge 1$ anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, 13 OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN; $118$ untouched by design).

Two caveats bound external validity: SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams within multi-stream archives share hardware platforms. Dataset clusters ($N \in \{7, 9, 12\}$) are therefore treated as co-primary units alongside streams via Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and LODO audits (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($K$ channels over $T$ timesteps) to binary alarm decisions. A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time, splitting each position $t$ into a historical \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of $C = 256$ steps and a future \emph{target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of $S = 64$ steps (ending at the causal decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). Before mixing channels, multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise 1D convolution ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass) and are split across four smooth dyadic frequency shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid. The shells form an amplitude partition of unity within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$ and are intended to separate slow baseline drift from high-frequency vibration before per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$) and a softmax router feed a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Rather than reconstructing raw sensor waveforms, a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path} maps context and target windows into compact $D=32$-dimensional layer-normalized summaries via dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders. Target weights track online weights via an exponential moving average (EMA) at momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on archive cohorts) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}, placing summaries near radius $\sqrt{D}$ up to the normalization epsilon. Deep dilated TCN stacks can smooth out brief onset spikes, while operating-regime shifts displace the latent mean. Both encoders address these two failure modes directly:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth hierarchy tapping:} Each encoder taps three intermediate feature maps ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow spectral stem, mid-depth TCN block, and final TCN block. Concatenating the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy of each tap, projecting to $\mathbb{R}^D$ through a two-layer GELU head, and blending with the deep TCN output through a bounded gate ($g \le 0.5$) preserves fast transients alongside long-range context.
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} During training, an online centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ updates at rate $0.05$, copies synchronously to $E_\phi$, and is subtracted from both context and target summaries prior to final LayerNorm. Centering reduces learned common-mode offsets without guaranteeing invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single 64-step forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant volume penalty ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Pooling an entire 64-step target window into one vector can average out short-lived faults, and LayerNorm bounds vector length without bounding temporal tail heaviness.

To track how prediction error evolves across the target window, Stage~2 of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} encodes $M=4$ nested target prefixes of lengths $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ at $S=64$ (clamped to at least two steps when $S>1$; at $S=1$, all four prefixes share the single target step and differ only through predictor rollout). Starting from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ with learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a recurrent sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential LayerNorm forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M-1)}$ while an anchor head $P_\theta$ predicts the full horizon $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$. Assigning half the loss weight to $m=M$ and splitting the other half equally across $m < M$, training minimizes multi-horizon MSE plus a non-collapse penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Unlike log-determinant penalties that require batch size $B > D$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ penalizes collapse directly on small batches: its diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$ enforce a per-coordinate standard-deviation floor $\gamma = 0.75$ (using batch-biased variance plus $10^{-4}$), and its off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize cross-coordinate sample covariance ($B-1$ divisor). At $B=16 < D=32$, effective latent rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ rises from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a single scalar score creates a bottleneck: if nominal drift inflates even a few coordinates between training and calibration, those coordinates dominate the scalar norm. Stacking the $M=4$ signed residuals $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ forms $\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$ ($M\!D=128$ entries). On nominal training windows, eigendecomposing the residual covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ defines a damped resolvent precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying centered residuals by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ yields whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ while suppressing near-null eigenmodes instead of amplifying them. Stage~3 then insulates coordinates against nominal calibration drift and expands the horizon trajectory:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Disjoint nominal stabilization and optional quenching on $\mathbf{X}_q$:} Estimating blending weights or weight edits on the same block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ that sets final alarm thresholds would couple model adaptation to threshold calibration. Under our disjoint adaptation protocol ($\mathbf{X}_q \perp \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), a separate nominal block $\mathbf{X}_q$ fits raw residual RMS scales and smoothly blends any coordinate whose whitened RMS exceeds $1.10$ toward its standardized raw residual $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)$, freezing all weights before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    Full \emph{Laminar-JEPA} also runs an optional gradient-free \emph{weight-space quench}: it edits the few predictor outputs that drift most on healthy data and keeps the edit only if false alarms fall. It uses only $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_q)$, before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (legacy fixed-epoch development runs without $\mathbf{X}_q$ estimate both on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}). Coordinates are ranked by a median/median-absolute-deviation (MAD) standardized instability score combining $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q$ mean/scale shifts, $\mathbf{X}_q$ half-split drift, residual autocorrelation, and a negative guard for isolated spikes. For candidate top-$k$ drifting coordinates ($k\in\{4,2\}$), four steps run sequentially: \emph{(Q1)}~damping selected predictor output rows/LayerNorm gains and shifting $\mathbf{p}_m$ toward $\mathbf{X}_q$ means (inducing unselected spillover through 32D LayerNorm, audited in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}); \emph{(Q2)}~blending residual centers $75\%$ toward post-edit $\mathbf{X}_q$ means; \emph{(Q3)}~attenuating off-diagonal precision couplings by $0.15$ and rescaling whitened $\mathbf{X}_q$ RMS; and \emph{(Q4)}~retaining the edit (preferring $k=2$ on ties) iff $\mathbf{X}_q$ half-split FPR improves while preserving $\ge 92\%$ of pre-edit quorum thresholds, freezing all parameters thereafter.
    \item \emph{Multi-horizon expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} Inspired algebraically by the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} across the $M=4$ horizons of each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$, we separate $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into its horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Taking magnitudes yields $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Removing the horizon mean splits each coordinate's squared error orthogonally into persistent offset plus within-window fluctuation, and confines the $M \times M$ horizon Gram matrix to the $(M-1)$-dimensional subspace orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}_M$ (a geometric property of the representation rather than an accuracy guarantee):

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Instead of adding all 224 dials into one global score, Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) assigns each dial its own binary circuit breaker on nominal calibration data. Because the 224 dials are derived from a single $4 \times 32$ residual tensor, their trips are correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort, with joint trip-count variance $7.2\times$ independent trials). Alarm quorums are therefore calibrated directly on the empirical distribution of simultaneous nominal trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized against its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled MAD $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into a nonnegative score $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. Two hijack guards then widen the $98.5$th-percentile tripwire $\tau_j$ of suspect dials: $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}} \ge 1$ widens a dial whose calibration tail grows over time (second-half 98th percentile above $1.35\times$ the first half), and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}} \ge 1$ widens a dial much noisier than its peers (scale above $1.875\times$ its perspective-group median):
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 right-edge onset relays:} When an abrupt fault first enters the right edge of the $S$-step target window, earlier prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover nominal data. To catch these transitions promptly, each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($\gamma^{1.25}$ guard) and fires a binary onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ whenever a terminal-prefix trip ($m=4$) coincides with an acceleration, fluctuation, or $m=3$ trip on the same coordinate.
    \item \emph{Causal vs.\ buffered attribution, score weighting, and quorum voting:} Under \emph{causal trailing attribution}, active breakers on window $[t,t+S)$ emit a real-time decision strictly at its right endpoint $\tau=t+S-1$ (structural lag $S-1$, empirical delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, position votes route to their corresponding prefixes $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation votes span $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint-difference votes route to $[t+S_3,t+S)$ (empty at $S=1$). Summing active trips yields $N_{224}(\tau)$, position sub-panel count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. Because $N_{128}$ is contained in $N_{224}$, the continuous ranking score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = N_{224}(\tau) + N_{128}(\tau) + 2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ gives sub-horizon position trips and onset relays $2\times$ weight (fixed on development streams before held-out testing; Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Binary alarms fire whenever any counter exceeds its nominal quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with nominal calibration quorums $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Compact Variant: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} makes three simplifications, each supported by the ablations in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, that cut trainable parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$ ($141.9\text{k}$ at $K < 25$; $222.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$ vs.\ $295.8\text{k}$) and accelerate windowed GPU inference by $2.43\times$ ($3.14\times$ at $K < 25$) with zero post-training weight editing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Four-block TCN and $K \ge 25$ spectral gating:} Because a fifth dilated block damps short-prefix anomaly divergence without improving effective rank, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses four TCN blocks ($d \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $64\text{D}$ pooling heads, and activates the four-shell rFFT front-end only on high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), using a direct causal projection when $K < 25$.
    \item \emph{Parallel three-horizon single-LayerNorm predictor:} Instead of sequential four-horizon unrolling and stacked predictor normalizations, a single residual two-layer GELU MLP predicts $M=3$ nested prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$ at $S=64$; $96\times 96$ precision $\mathbf{\Omega}$) in parallel from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m$ with one terminal LayerNorm, avoiding sequential lag and LayerNorm cross-coupling.
    \item \emph{128-dial position-plus-drift panel and single quorum:} Retaining only the $96$ sub-horizon magnitudes $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and $32$ horizon-mean drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ ($4D=128$ breakers calibrated via (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire})) removes fluctuation/endpoint dials, onset relays, and weight quenching, ranking steps by unweighted $N_{128}(\tau)$ and firing under one quorum $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit) and Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128-Bit)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Full},\text{Lite}\}$, attribution mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Per-timestep binary alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 1: Disjoint Training, Whitening \& Adaptation ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q$)}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$ and standardize channels using $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ alone.
\State Set nested prefixes $S_{1:4}=\{16,32,48,64\}$ (Full) or $S_{1:3}=\{16,32,64\}$ with $K\ge 25$ rFFT gate (Lite).
\For{stride-$16$ training batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ and targets $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$ with barycenter shift $-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$.
    \State Roll out sequential $\{P_{\text{sub}}, P_\theta\}$ (Full) or parallel single-LayerNorm predictor (Lite); update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (or fixed epochs) and fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_+^{MD})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\If{tier $=$ Full and $q_{\text{wsp}} = 1$}
    \State Rank drifting coordinates on $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; run Steps Q1--Q4 for $k\in\{4,2\}$ and keep edit iff $\mathbf{X}_q$ split and $0.92$-quorum gates pass.
\EndIf
\State Fit and freeze stabilization scales and blending weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 2: Untouched Circuit-Breaker Calibration on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$}
\State Compute frozen stabilized residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ via (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).
\If{tier $=$ Full}
    \State Expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ into $7D=224$ dials (position, drift, fluctuation, endpoint) and $32$ right-edge onset relays.
    \State Fit MAD scales $(m_j,s_j)$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\Else \Comment{Preferred Deployment Tier: Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
    \State Extract $4D=128$ position-plus-drift dials $(|\tilde{e}_{m,c}|, |\bar{e}_c|)$, fit $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and set single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}=\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 3: Online Inference on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$}
    \State Compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \to \mathbf{d}(t)$ and binary trips $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(z_j(t)>\tau_j)$ (plus relays $r_c(t)$ if Full).
    \State Attribute active counts to right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ (\text{causal}) or prefix spans $[t,t+S_m)$ (\text{buffered}).
    \State Emit $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ when active counts exceed $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) or $K_{\text{Lite}}$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) into a model-fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a held-out nominal suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint weight fitting, checkpoint selection, and nominal adaptation):} Reusing calibration windows for early stopping, blending weights (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), or weight quenching can make nominal thresholds optimistic. All archive benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) therefore use a disjoint \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$) inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits channel standardization, encoder/predictor weights, running barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and precision $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the early-stopping checkpoint; and $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$ stride-1 windows separated from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ by $S=64$-step target-isolation gaps) fits and freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and optional split-gated quenching (Steps Q1--Q4) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is ever touched. Fixed-epoch development suites (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) omit $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$, and the 19-stream residual cache also skips (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and quenching. A four-block check ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) on the 19 development streams gives causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ of $0.96\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and $1.05\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, matching Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, although the smaller fitting block ($50\%$ instead of $65\%$) lowers causal F1 ($0.133\to 0.071$ and $0.144\to 0.076$). On short histories, \emph{two-fold chronological cross-fitting} swaps the two halves of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ and alarms only when both folds agree ($\hat{y}^{(1)}\wedge\hat{y}^{(2)}$; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched circuit-breaker calibration and boundary isolation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Stride-1 windows of length $W = 320$ inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ yield $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window indices. Splitting at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the untouched \emph{nominal calibration block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and \emph{held-out nominal evaluation block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so calibration and evaluation share zero target observations (a full $G=320$ window gap changes causal FPR by $<0.25$ percentage points). On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone, the frozen model fits dial medians $m_j$, MAD scales $s_j$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums ($K_{\text{Lite}}$ or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ in (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}); $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$. Raw window count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ is distinguished from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective sample size} $N_{\text{eff},s} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int},s}$ via stream- and statistic-specific Sokal initial-positive-sequence estimators ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Statistical Inference}
We report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Dataset-clustered and stream-level inference:} \emph{Dataset-clustered inference} ($95\%$ cluster-bootstrap CIs and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip permutation tests) is emphasized alongside stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs ($2{,}000$ resamples). For the four focal causal contrasts on the 118 untouched streams (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) and TS-JEPA, on Point-F1 and ER), Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) \cite{benjamini1995controlling} adjusted $p$-values are $0.014$ and $3.6\times 10^{-4}$ (stream-level Point-F1) and $0.0078$ and $0.012$ (dataset sign-flip ER, positive on $9/9$ and $8/9$ datasets); other quoted $p$-values are raw and descriptive, and non-Exathlon subsets ($n=101$, $8$ datasets) verify sensitivity to archive composition.
(2)~\emph{12-fold LODO audit:} Across all 12 LODO folds of the 141-stream archive, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps a positive gain on causal ER ($+0.099$ to $+0.141$ vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D), buffered Point-F1, and causal Point-F1 vs.\ TS-JEPA ($\max p=0.0054$); its causal Point-F1 gain over Multi-Horizon 1D holds in $9/12$ folds.

\subsection{Cohorts and Baselines}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} denotes the full variant without Steps Q1--Q4. Causal and buffered attribution are paired on the same splits within each cohort (aside from a single-horizon 130-stream buffered baseline screen in Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}):
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-inspected, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}):} The 19-stream residual cache ($6$ datasets) evaluates the dial ladder, component ablations, and conformal/statistical-process-control (SPC) regimes on the same unquenched \emph{Laminar-JEPA} residuals (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b), Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}). The 33-stream development benchmark ($7$ datasets: $19$ non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) plus $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) compares both tiers against 1D JEPAs and observation-space baselines. Tripwire and quenching constants were fixed on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, and \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched held-out cohorts (primary generalization evidence):} Excluding those $23$ design streams from the $141$-stream archive ($12$ datasets, five-block protocol) leaves $118$ untouched held-out streams across $9$ datasets ($101$ non-Exathlon streams across $8$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), supplemented by a single-seed \emph{Laminar-JEPA} Q1--Q4 step-isolation audit on the same $118$ streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). None of the $118$ held-out streams was inspected during architecture or threshold design.

\noindent\emph{Standardized Baseline Fairness Protocol:} Scalar baselines (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D), TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, PCA Forecaster, Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) share the same chronological splits, training-only standardization, isolation gaps, and SPOT calibration ($u=p_{98}$, $q=10^{-3}$), whereas conformal, SPC, and simpler coordinate detector-bank baselines in Tables~\ref{tab:matched_alert} and \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} share the same multi-horizon whitened residuals and nominal calibration alert rate. External neural baselines use $206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ trainable parameters; circuit-breaker panels add none. Runs used PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090 (batched inference $0.15\,\text{ms}$/window for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and $0.38\,\text{ms}$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}; $188\,\text{MB}$ peak memory); code: \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To prevent point-adjustment inflation \cite{kim2022towards} and aggregation ambiguity, all binary false-alarm and Point-F1 metrics are computed \emph{per valid timestep $\tau$} (never point-adjusted and never per-window without timeline attribution): $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ averages $\hat{y}(\tau)$ over valid timesteps in $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ averages $\hat{y}(\tau)$ over nominal test timesteps ($y(\tau)=0$), and Point-F1, Precision, Recall, and precision-recall (PR) AUC (from $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$) score all valid test timesteps against $y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$. Event-level ER measures the fraction of contiguous $y=1$ segments with $\ge 1$ alarm, and delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ is the step lag from segment onset $t^*$ to first in-segment alarm. Headline numbers are stream-level means of within-stream seed averages unless labeled \emph{median} or \emph{Dataset-Macro}; we add stream medians for skewed FPR and use post-hoc test-FPR controls ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) for diagnosis only.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Panel Components and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate perspective expansion and panel components on the 19-stream residual cache (not paired to the separately trained run in Panel~(c)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Reference 224-dial panel vs.\ reduced position-plus-drift subsets:} We keep the 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as a reference because scoring all four views on one residual tensor shows which views help causal versus buffered reporting. Against 1D scoring, the 224 dials raise causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$; buffered $+0.033$, $p=0.53$). Pruning the fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to a 160-dial position-plus-drift subset helps causal reporting: Point-F1 rises to $0.098$, ER to $59.1\%$, delay falls from $90.1$ to $74.8$ steps, and held-out FPR drops from $0.76\%$ to $0.55\%$ with $29\%$ fewer dials. Buffered Point-F1 still favors the full panel ($0.224$ vs.\ $0.198$). This result motivates \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.
    \item \emph{Relays, PR-AUC score weighting, quorum rules, and hijack guards:} Removing the 32 onset relays lowers causal Point-F1 ($0.071 \to 0.068$), and replacing the three-counter quorum (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) with one 224-dial quorum lowers causal Point-F1 to $0.063$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0055$; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}). Replacing the double-position continuous score $N_{\text{tot}} = N_{224} + N_{128} + 2N_{\text{edge}}$ with unweighted $N_{224}+N_{\text{edge}}$ or $N_{224}$ leaves binary alarms unchanged and changes PR-AUC by at most $0.004$ on this cache and $0.0016$ on the 118 untouched streams (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses unweighted $N_{128}$). Removing hijack guards ($\gamma_j=1$) raises causal Point-F1 to $0.073$ ($70.1$-step delay) on this cache when nominal drift is mild, so the guards are a safeguard against drift rather than a source of accuracy. No panel component removes the $S-1$ reporting lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching step isolation and LayerNorm coupling:} We tested each quench step in isolation on single-seed \emph{Laminar-JEPA} checkpoints across all $118$ untouched streams (unquenched: causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 6.76\%$, $\text{F1} = 0.0901$). Damping $k=4$ predictor rows (Step Q1) shifts unselected coordinates by a median $11.1\%$ of nominal RMS through 32D LayerNorm coupling and does not lower $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($7.06\%$). Recentering and decoupling (Steps Q2--Q3) lower it to $6.71\%$ but cut causal F1 to $0.0826$. The split gate (Step Q4, accepted on $39/118$ streams) reaches $6.62\%$ with F1 unchanged ($0.0904$). Because ungated edits hurt $k=2$ causal $\text{F1}$ on $11/19$ development streams ($0.141\to 0.049$) and $k\in\{8,12\}$ cuts PR-AUC ($0.292\to 0.223$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} omits weight editing altogether.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate alarms either fire constantly without a quorum, almost never fire under multiplicity correction on short calibration sets, or lose their calibration out of sample. We compare three calibration regimes on the same 19-stream whitened residuals without test-label tuning (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Per-coordinate marginal union:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or even at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds false alarms across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Family-wise and false discovery rate (FDR) multiplicity control:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) across 128 coordinates or BH FDR ($q=0.005$) across 224 dials hits the discrete conformal $p$-value floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, paralyzing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{System-level matched nominal alarm rate:} Calibrating the aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) shows that discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ still jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ out of sample, because ties at the boundary fire whenever any coordinate exceeds its calibration maximum. Recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM charts inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$. Among stable system-matched rules, the 160-dial position-plus-drift panel ($\text{F1}=0.098$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=0.55\%$) outperforms 128-coordinate top-$k$ voting ($0.068$), continuous $\max_j z_j$ ($0.056$), and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.072$); Fisher/Stouffer pooling reaches similar F1 ($0.109/0.095$) at higher $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.05\%/0.87\%$).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
A frozen or flatlined sensor barely moves the latent summary: terminal LayerNorm spreads the small change across all $32$ coordinates, so on \texttt{SMAP P-3} a flatline trips $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip, and a single faulty sensor is further diluted by the $K-1$ healthy channels. Extending Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) ($n{=}6$), we evaluate a causal channel branch across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$) without per-stream or test-label tuning. Per channel, three 64-step rolling statistics---log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, training-fit first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$---are thresholded at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ on nominal validation windows (floor $6.0$ MAD, $1.25\times$ half-split drift multiplier) with a $24$-of-$40$-step persistence gate. On the highest-variance active channel $c^\star=\arg\max_{c:\sigma_c^{\text{tr}}>10^{-8}}\sigma_c^{\text{tr}}$, we place three nonoverlapping $200$-step faults per stream inside clean test segments ($57$ events per class). For $s\le t<s+200$ using training $(\mu_c,\sigma_c,q_{.995,c})$:
\begin{equation}
\small
x'_{t,c^\star}=
\begin{cases}
\mu_{c^\star} & \text{variance collapse},\\
\mu_{c^\star}+1.35(-1)^{t-s}\sigma_{c^\star} & \text{prediction shock},\\
x_{s-1,c^\star} & \text{sensor freezing},\\
\max(q_{.995,c^\star},\mu_{c^\star}+2.2\sigma_{c^\star}) & \text{saturation},\\
x_{t,c^\star}+2.5\sigma_{c^\star}(t-s)/199 & \text{gradual degradation},\\
x_{t,c^\star}+1.5\sigma_{c^\star} & \text{isolated bias}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:single_channel_injection}
\end{equation}
Outside $[s,s+200)$, all channels remain unchanged. The variance-collapse union lifts injected-event recall for variance collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%$--$82.5\%$ to $93.0\%$--$94.7\%$, and the full channel branch lifts recall on shocks, gradual degradation, and bias from $59.6\%$--$75.4\%$ to $80.7\%$--$96.5\%$. However, on the \emph{unmodified} test streams, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union, $\text{F1}=0.070$) and $19.4\%$ (full branch, $\text{F1}=0.129$). Synthetic single-channel injections do not establish detection on natural sensor failures, and the full union's $19.4\%$ false-alarm rate makes it unsuitable as a default detector.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, \texttt{GECCO}; three seeds) shows a channel-count threshold: shell splitting improves high-channel spacecraft detection ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), but lowers F1 on both $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), setting the $K \ge 25$ spectral gate in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 16 matched-budget arms in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five mechanisms: (1)~raw-observation forecasting and autoencoding share heavy tails ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), large drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and $\approx 25\%$ causal FPR; (2)~a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves FPR, yet raw targets keep drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$); (3)~learned $D{=}32$ latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179\text{--}0.190$ across multi-horizon JEPA tiers, $p \le 0.001$); (4)~linear PCA and Isolation Forest keep low causal FPR ($0.021$ and $0.039$) but reach only $0.017$ and $0.041$ causal Point-F1; and (5)~frozen targets and $\ell_2$ scoring retain $<2\%$ causal FPR, while latent context autoencoding lowers causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.161$ for TS-JEPA).

Following the cohort hierarchy in Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the 33-stream development benchmark under retrospective buffered attribution (Panel~(a)), causal streaming (Panel~(b)), and per-dataset stratification (Panel~(c)), while Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} reports the five-block benchmark across all 118 untouched held-out streams and the 141-stream archive. On a separate 130-stream baseline screen, TS-JEPA reaches stream-mean Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$. The observed reduction in nominal false alarms with latent scoring is specific to these protocols; spherical normalization alone does not prove a distributional guarantee.

\noindent\emph{Operational trade-offs and configuration guidance:} On the 33-stream benchmark, the 224-dial reference variant \emph{Laminar-JEPA} edges out \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} on unweighted buffered Point-F1 ($0.244$ vs.\ $0.242$) and causal ER ($67.3\%$ vs.\ $65.0\%$), whereas \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.1\%$ vs.\ $12.8\%$), and leads $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.133$). In practice, we recommend \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for streaming and buffered monitoring of abrupt and intermediate-onset faults and keep \emph{Laminar-JEPA} for diagnostic ablation. Short horizons ($S\le 4$) reduce reporting lag; raw-level variance or trend charts are needed only for single-sensor freezes or slow ramps.

\noindent\emph{Scope boundaries and controlled slow-ramp sweep:} Because the encoder sees only the last $C=256$ steps, a slow drift moves context and target summaries together, which explains the $0.000$ Point-F1 on the 14 GHL slow-ramp streams. A controlled sweep maps this boundary. We injected linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ ($15$ events per cell in clean test segments, on multi-channel and single-channel subsets). Steep multi-channel ramps ($L=64$, $a\ge 1\sigma$) are caught $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ of the time at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay, $5.7\text{--}7.7$ percentage points above the chance rate of background alarm episodes. At $L=C$ the excess falls to $0.5\text{--}2.5$ points, and for $L\ge 1024$ chance-corrected detection is indistinguishable from zero ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points) for every JEPA detector. Fixed-window JEPAs thus target abrupt to medium-onset faults rather than gradual industrial drift.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent detectors degrade under causal streaming. Buffered attribution averages residuals across the overlapping predictions that cover each timestamp, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step with no averaging, so one or two drifting coordinates can set the global SPOT threshold (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). A rate-matched Bernoulli alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, catches only $28.1\%$ of events at our $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, but $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Multi-Horizon Scaling Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ and Latency Trade-Offs}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Because a target window of length $S$ emits its causal decision at $\tau=t+S-1$, $S=64$ imposes a 63-step reporting lag. We retrain on five selected streams at $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ to examine the latency trade-off. At $S=1$ all prefixes share one target step, and at $S=4$ the shortest prefix is two steps, so these settings test the implemented detectors rather than distinct horizon views.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a), both \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every horizon with test FPR below $3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037$ and $0.050$ vs.\ $0.014$ and $0.012$) with $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ ($0.030$ and $0.036$) with $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER; at $S=4$, detection delay drops to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps. Buffered Point-F1 peaks at $0.290$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($S=32$) and $0.255$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($S=64$) versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot establish a deployment rule across sampling rates or datasets.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine detection across operating budgets and fault taxonomies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Autonomous vs.\ diagnostic operating budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Along the causal recall--FPR frontier, the autonomous points of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} occupy the $\le 1.3\%$ held-out FPR regime without test-label tuning. At post-hoc diagnostic test-FPR controls ($f^* \in \{1\%, 5\%\}$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events at $f^* = 1\%$ versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD.
    \item \emph{In-range contextual vs.\ point faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Under NASA's spacecraft taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Panel~(a)) and a $5\sigma$ amplitude split across the 19 development streams (Panel~(b)), out-of-range point spikes ($>5\sigma$) are caught by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike. In-range contextual faults instead change cross-channel phase or velocity while amplitudes stay normal. On NASA contextual sequences, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR, versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Held-Out Generalization and Calibration Limits}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and archive-wide audits establish three generalization and calibration findings:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Independent generalization under five-block non-leakage (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}):} On the 118 untouched streams (three seeds), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ over Multi-Horizon 1D, improving on all nine datasets (sign-flip $p=0.0039$), and halves causal delay ($70.8$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps). Causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.079$ ($p=0.014$) and buffered Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.156$ ($p=0.022$) at a median causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ of $0.78\%$, and the panel beats the system-matched coordinate rules (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). Without Exathlon ($n=101$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} still leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.148$ vs.\ $0.139$) and causal ER ($50.6\%\to 58.8\%$), while Multi-Horizon 1D leads causal Point-F1 ($0.073$ vs.\ $0.060$). Disjoint quenching barely moves causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($6.90\%\to 6.83\%$, sign-flip $p=0.55$) and costs $3.5$ points of ER, so \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} omits it.
    \item \emph{Autocorrelation, sample size, and cross-fitting:} Autocorrelation shrinks the effective calibration sample. Across the 19 development streams, the median Sokal integrated autocorrelation time $\tau_{\text{int}}$ is $2.51$ for dial scores $z_j$, $1.88$ for $N_{\text{edge}}$, $5.58$ for $N_{224}$ (max $206.0$), and $6.75$ for 1D scores. At median $N_{\text{cal}}=408$, each quantile is set on a different statistic ($z_j$ at $q_{0.985}$, $N_{224}$ at $q_{0.995}$, $N_{\text{edge}}$ at $q_{0.999}$), leaving median $N_{\text{eff}}$ of $154.6$, $67.1$, and $195.9$ and only $2.32$, $0.34$, and $0.20$ expected exceedances; even $N_{\text{eff}}=300$ would give $4.5$, $1.5$, and $0.3$. Block-bootstrap ($L_{\text{blk}}\in\{1..128\}$) $95\%$ CI half-widths span $12.7\%\text{--}25.0\%$, while floors $(5.0,2.5)$ hold median $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ to $0.36\%$ and $0.00\%$. On $n=118$, $N_{\text{cal}}<250$ ($n=12$) averages $16.0\%$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($17.6\%$ \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) versus $1.05\%$ ($1.60\%$) for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 2{,}500$ ($n=22$); on short histories ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$, $n=18$), two-fold cross-fitting cuts causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$ at $69.9\%$ ER, while Beta quantiles and interquartile-range extrapolation remain exploratory.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit via permutation decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits is consistent with inter-block regime drift without ruling out parametric tail misfit: $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}-\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$ represents $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors, versus $18.7\%$ in the centered model. Within-checkpoint traces of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($n{=}6$, $K\in\{9,25,55\}$) show that before tapping and centering, latent vectors sit off-center ($\|\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}\|_2 \in [4.73,5.18]$ vs.\ $\sqrt{32}\approx 5.66$) and barely separate anomalies (divergence $0.06\text{--}0.16$); tap fusion and barycenter subtraction restore divergence to $0.70\text{--}0.94$ and cut mean coordinate $D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.140$ to $0.096$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated a two-tier family of multi-horizon circuit-breaker architectures (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for deployment; \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as a 224-dial reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 development and 118 untouched held-out streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Compact vs.\ reference panels:} On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA on all nine datasets ($p=0.0039$) and improves causal and buffered Point-F1, with $35\%\text{--}52\%$ fewer parameters and $2.43\times$ faster inference than \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. On the 33-stream development benchmark the two tiers are close ($0.244$ vs.\ $0.242$ buffered Point-F1 at $1.3\%$ and $1.0\%$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), and weight-space quenching gives no reliable benefit.
    \item \emph{Mechanisms and latency:} Matched-backbone controls show LayerNorm tail compression and barycenter drift reduction, and the five-stream horizon sweep keeps $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 gains at $S\in\{1,4\}$.
    \item \emph{Calibration, single-sensor, and ramp limits:} Autocorrelation leaves too few effective calibration samples to fix extreme quantiles precisely, though two-fold cross-fitting cuts short-stream ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$) causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$. An optional channel branch helps injected single-sensor faults only at a much higher false-alarm rate, and excess causal detection vanishes on slow ramps once $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$); pairing raw-level trend charts and testing natural sensor failures remain future priorities.
\end{enumerate}
