\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry requires label-free operational thresholds, typically set via Extreme Value Theory (EVT/SPOT). We investigate whether forecasting in latent representation space via Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) conditions residuals for automated thresholding better than observation-space reconstruction. On 19 diverse telemetry streams under held-out calibration (33 in the full suite), latent predictive residuals cut nominal false-positive rates roughly four-fold ($7.6\%$ vs.\ $30\%$) with no significant change in Point-F1 or PR-AUC. Because this confounds score quality with operating point, we re-evaluate every model on a common alarm-budget grid with realized test FPR as the comparison axis. Under strictly causal scoring the latent family occupies the upper envelope of the recall--FPR curve (partial AUC $0.74$--$0.75$ versus $0.59$--$0.63$; event recall $0.77$ versus $0.62$ at design budget $0.05$), though the advantage is heterogeneous across streams and disappears under buffered attribution. A dimensionality-matched observation-space control (32-d PCA bottleneck, LayerNorm, identical gated norm) reproduces the tail conditioning ($\gamma_2{=}1.2$ vs.\ $1.2$) but not the detection (recall $0.67$): bounded low-dimensional scoring conditions tails; the learned embedding supplies separation. Static POT thresholds miss their design risk by $50$--$200\times$, a failure we attribute to inter-block drift rather than tail mis-fit. Three physics-inspired regularizers show no reliable aggregate gain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments --- NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, wearable biometric arrays --- dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
Multi-sensor telemetry reflects a physical state trajectory $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$, yet modeling dynamics in raw observation space faces four obstacles: estimating derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \Delta x / \Delta t$ on noisy discrete streams amplifies high-frequency noise; telemetry captures only a noisy projection of unmeasured states, violating full-state observability; in large arrays ($K \ge 25$) cross-channel nonlinear interactions resist rigid functional forms; and discrete operational mode transitions (orbital day/night cycling, thruster firings, gait freezing) induce continuous distribution drift.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Deep detectors face three limitations. First, generative models (autoencoders, VAEs, masked Transformers) minimize pointwise $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}, so reconstruction loss allocates capacity to high-frequency jitter rather than nominal dynamics. Second, contrastive frameworks such as NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} train on synthetic perturbations whose geometry distorts the embedding space, inducing artificial decision boundaries and false alarms. Third, reconstruction errors $\|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2$ accumulate residual variance across dozens of irrelevant channels; under automated thresholding, heavy-tailed and non-stationary residuals defeat static extreme-value calibrators and trigger false-alarm flooding --- although, as we show, inter-block drift rather than tail mass is the dominant failure mechanism.

\subsection{Investigating Latent Prediction and Inductive Priors}
We study whether latent-space prediction mitigates observation-space noise drowning without synthetic contrastive perturbations. A JEPA maps context $x_{\text{ctx}}$ into a representation space and predicts target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ directly \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}, discarding unpredictable stochastic jitter and potentially conditioning residuals for tail calibration. Alongside an unconstrained predictor (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formalize and test three physics-inspired regularizers: \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (spectral-entropy regularization of a latent Koopman transition matrix), \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} (second-moment fluctuation-covariance alignment), and \textit{Potential-Flow JEPA} (curl-free gradient dynamics $\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi$). All share a coordinate-saliency scoring gate and a dyadic frequency front-end under SPOT calibration; a strictly causal input-channel gating variant is evaluated separately in a fault-type study.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\noindent\textbf{1) Latent Predictive Residuals Shift the Operating Curve, Not Just the Operating Point:} Latent prediction produces lighter-tailed residuals and lower nominal FPR ($7.6\%$ vs.\ $30\%$). Re-evaluating all models on a common alarm-budget grid with realized test FPR as the comparison axis removes the operating-point confound: under strictly causal scoring the JEPA family occupies the upper recall--FPR envelope (partial AUC $0.74$--$0.75$ vs.\ $0.59$--$0.63$), a heterogeneous advantage that disappears under buffered attribution. A dimensionality-matched observation-space control shows that bounded low-dimensional scoring suffices for the tail conditioning, while the learned embedding supplies the separation.

\noindent\textbf{2) Rigorous Evaluation of Physics-Inspired Inductive Priors:} Across 33 streams, neither Operator-Entropy ($+0.002$ Point-F1, $p{=}0.71$) nor Reynolds-Stress ($-0.015$, $p{=}0.61$) beats unconstrained TS-JEPA. Potential-Flow is F1-weakest under SPOT ($-0.081$, $p{=}0.033$ unadjusted) yet strongest on the causal operating curve --- an operating-point artifact, not weak scores. Fixed-backbone ablations show no significant change when the physical terms are disabled.

\noindent\textbf{3) Empirical Analysis of Threshold Calibration Failures:} Static POT thresholds miss their design risk ($q{=}10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry by $50$--$200\times$. Stream-level diagnostics attribute the failure to inter-block drift (KS distance vs.\ realized FPR, Spearman $\rho{=}+0.38$), not tail mis-fit --- calibration-block kurtosis is mildly \emph{protective} ($\rho{=}-0.32$) --- and runs-declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ effective exceedances per cell.

\noindent\textbf{4) Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark:} 33 matched streams across seven datasets under a strict held-out calibration protocol (65/17.5/17.5 chronological split with a 64-step isolation gap, up to five seeds, dataset-clustered bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon tests with BH-adjusted $p$-values); all code, checkpoints, and logs released.

Supplementary material contains proofs, extended diagnostics, and case-study detail.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Dynamical Systems, Reconstruction, and Forecasting Detectors}
Physical modeling of multi-sensor telemetry frames system behavior as continuous trajectories $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize velocity fields in raw observation space, where integrating through high-frequency sensor noise induces numerical stiffness and jitter sensitivity. Koopman theory and DMD \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} instead learn linear operators on observable embeddings; physical disruptions then manifest as spectral decoherence. Our Operator-Entropy variant transfers this idea to a latent transition operator regularized by spectral entropy during training.

The predominant detection paradigm learns nominal patterns via raw-signal reconstruction: LSTM prediction with dynamic thresholding for NASA telemetry \cite{Hundman2018b}, association-discrepancy attention in Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, 2D FFT-periodicity decomposition in TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and adversarial sub-series attention in TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}. These share a vulnerability: point-level losses bias networks toward fitting stochastic noise, and sustained anomalies contaminate the context window. These errors accumulate residual variance across irrelevant channels in high-dimensional arrays.

\subsection{Threshold Calibration, Contrastive, and Self-Supervised Detection}
Unsupervised detectors require an automated rule converting residual scores to binary alarms without test labels. Non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} and Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}---fitting a Generalized Pareto tail above a high quantile under design risk $q$---are standard; however, EVT's asymptotic guarantees assume i.i.d.\ tail samples, which telemetry autocorrelation and operational drift violate.

To avoid reconstructing noise, contrastive methods learn discriminative representations: NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} trains TCNs against injected synthetic anomalies (which distort the physical manifold and miss subtle real faults), and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} maximizes cross-scale consistency via dual-attention contrasting. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead predict target representations in latent space, with non-contrastive collapse prevention \cite{zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening, bardes2021vicreg}. Time-series extensions \cite{yue2022ts2vec} typically evaluate ranking metrics or post-hoc threshold sweeps without studying how latent residuals behave under automated tail calibration---the gap this work addresses.

\subsection{Physics-Inspired Inductive Biases}
PINNs \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian/Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} embed dynamics as hard constraints; their stiff competing gradients \cite{wang2021understanding} and closed-form-PDE requirements fit poorly to uncalibrated multi-sensor telemetry. We instead test whether \emph{inspired} structures---spectral entropy, second-moment closure, conservative potential flow---improve latent residuals.

\subsection{Terminology and Positioning}
We distinguish \emph{physics-inspired} structures (exploratory regularizers; latent coordinates are not claimed to be physical state variables) from \emph{physics-informed} constraints enforcing known governing equations; \emph{causal preprocessing} (features computed strictly from history) from \emph{causal decision-time access} (alarm latency); and transition-operator non-degeneracy from representation anti-collapse. This investigation bridges joint-embedding predictive learning and multivariate anomaly detection: three physics-inspired regularizers are evaluated against unconstrained latent controls, and EVT calibration is evaluated on strictly held-out nominal telemetry to quantify the gap between design risk and realized false-alarm rates.

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the UCI Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP Earth-observation \texttt{A-1}/\texttt{A-2}/\texttt{A-7}/\texttt{D-3}/\texttt{P-3}/\texttt{E-1} power, thermal, and attitude subsystems; MSL rover \texttt{C-1}/\texttt{C-2}/\texttt{D-14}/\texttt{D-15}/\texttt{D-16}/\texttt{M-1} actuator and environmental channels, with two short-nominal streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet FoG streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 GHL gasoil heating-loop streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample calibration, each stream's nominal sequence is partitioned chronologically into training (65\%), calibration (17.5\%), and held-out evaluation (17.5\%) blocks separated by an isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). The held-out protocol spans 33 streams across seven datasets (6 Daphnet, 14 GHL, 4 MSL, 6 SMAP, Genesis, Metro, SWAN); the remaining 12 streams are reserved as operational case studies --- CalIt2, two Room-Occupancy, two short-nominal MSL, GECCO, and six CICIDS --- because their nominal blocks are too short for disjoint partitions or because multi-seed stride-1 evaluation over $10^6$ steps was prohibitive. The dyadic front-end ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b) uses a separate five-stream panel that additionally draws one SMD channel outside this manifest.

Two caveats bound interpretation. SMAP and MSL contain documented mislabeled segments and near-trivial anomalies \cite{wu2023flawed}; their per-dataset numbers are upper bounds on detectability. Entity coverage is also incomplete --- 6 of 55 SMAP and 4 of 27 MSL channels, selected by nominal-block length and stride-1 runtime constraints, not labels --- so claims apply to the evaluated entities, not the full archives.

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dataset_specs} specifies the suite: $2{,}079{,}507$ nominal training and $2{,}986{,}574$ test timesteps ($>$5.06M observations), with per-dataset domain, channel dimension $K$, sequence lengths, contamination ratio $\pi\%$, anomaly typologies, and partition protocols.


A machine-readable manifest (\texttt{reports/stream\_manifest.csv}, generated by \texttt{scripts/build\_stream\_manifest.py}) enumerates every stream's identifier, channel dimension, split lengths, held-out anomaly counts, benchmark role, and evaluated seed set as recorded in the released artifacts. All counts in this section, the abstract, and the results tables reconcile against it: 33 primary held-out streams (19 non-GHL at five seeds, 14 GHL at three to five seeds depending on size) and 12 case-study streams.

\section{Methodology: Latent Representation Forecasting and Structured Inductive Priors}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.58\textwidth]{figures/physics_jepa_triad_architecture.png}
\caption{JEPA telemetry-monitoring architecture: context and suspect windows are encoded and compared in latent space under three candidate physical regularizers, with held-out SPOT calibration.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}

This section formalizes the mathematical formulation, latent predictors, and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a multivariate stream partitioned into context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize pointwise error $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}\| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2$, where ambient jitter dominates the Frobenius norm and capacity is spent reconstructing noise (noise drowning). We instead adopt the JEPA framework \cite{lecun2022path}: a context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an EMA momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}}$, with $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$, $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$. The objective forecasts target representations in latent space, testing whether structured priors condition prediction residuals for tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
To examine whether incorporating structured dynamical inductive biases stabilizes representation forecasting, we evaluate an unconstrained latent predictor alongside three candidate formulations inspired by physical dynamical principles:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, in which nonlinear trajectories are analyzed via linear operators on observable functions, we parameterize the predictive latent transition via a learned operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent collapse onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or to zero (energy collapse), the regularizer decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote the singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T$, and $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ the total energy. For $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) > 0$, the scale-free spectral distribution over modes is $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2$, $\sum_i p_i = 1$. We omit isotropic diagonal loading from the Gram matrix before normalization --- it would give $\mathbf{K} \to \mathbf{0}$ an artificial identity spectrum with maximal entropy, spuriously rewarding collapse. The Von Neumann spectral entropy uses the convention $0 \log 0 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i: p_i > 0} p_i \log p_i.
\end{equation}
The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} quantifies the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform modal equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^T$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Here, $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while dimensional collapse to rank 1 yields maximal deficiency $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \log D$.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) and prevent explosive gain, we introduce a non-negative Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{align}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) &= r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge 0, \\
    r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) &= \max\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2}{\gamma D}, \epsilon\right), \nonumber
\end{align}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ provides a numerical floor. For the identity operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$, $\|\mathbf{I}\|_F^2 = D$, yielding $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{I}) = 0$ exactly. We emphasize that the implemented barrier is \emph{floored}: it attains a finite maximum penalty $\epsilon - 1 - \log \epsilon \approx 12.8$ (before $\beta_{\text{norm}}$ weighting) throughout $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 < \epsilon\gamma D$, rather than diverging; divergence to $+\infty$ is a property only of the unfloored idealization $\Phi_{\text{energy}}^{\ast} = r - 1 - \log r$. The operator is initialized near identity ($\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$), so $r_{\text{safe}} \approx 1$ and training starts with a vanishing energy penalty; since LayerNorm gives $\mathbb{E}[\|z\|_2^2] \approx D$, the isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ matches the natural energy of unitary transitions. In sensitivity checks across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$ the model converged stably, with $0.5$ balancing spectral spread against predictive regression. The composite spectral regularizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$. The total training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$ in all experiments. All shared representation components (saliency gating, dyadic frequency shells, LayerNorm) are held fixed across models and controls, isolating the transition-operator loss. We emphasize that $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts on the operator matrix, enforcing a non-collapsed operator spectrum; it provides no analytical guarantee against encoder representation collapse, which is mitigated empirically by the EMA target encoder and stop-gradient operations \cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Triviality Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$, and non-degenerate context--target coupling $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^T \neq \mathbf{0}$. Then (i) the zero operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is strictly not a local or global minimizer of $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K})$ under the implemented floored barrier, and (ii) under the unfloored idealization ($\epsilon \to 0^+$, $\Phi_{\text{energy}}^{\ast} = r - 1 - \log r$), additionally $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.)

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Operator Non-Degeneracy vs. Representation Collapse):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} concerns only the transition operator: it does not prevent encoder collapse. If $E_\theta$ mapped all inputs to a constant $z^{(b)} = \mathbf{c}$, then $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ would attain zero prediction error and zero spectral penalty while encoding no information; prevention of that failure mode rests empirically on the stop-gradient momentum dynamics, not on Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse}.

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Second-Moment Closure Head)}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Reynolds decomposition splits a turbulent velocity field into a mean flow and a fluctuation component, where the Reynolds stress tensor captures the second moment of velocity fluctuations. We adapt this second-moment formulation as a latent-space inductive bias; the latent coordinates are learned embeddings, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is not a physical momentum-transport tensor.

A two-layer MLP predicts the conditional mean target latent $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a learned linear projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$ ($d_s = 16$) applied to the EMA target representation yields the empirical second-moment matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^T + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}.
\end{equation}
Because $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}$ is computed from an instantaneous latent vector rather than averaged over the target horizon $S$, it forms a rank-one outer product with a ridge term enforcing membership in $\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$; statistically it is an auxiliary second-moment projection penalty. The closure head $h_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})$ outputs a lower-triangular $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ with positive diagonal $L_{ii} = \operatorname{softplus}(a_{ii}) + \epsilon$, and the predicted covariance is reconstructed via Cholesky factorization:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s},
\end{equation}
confining predictions to $\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$. The training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}).
\end{equation}
At inference time, the anomaly score combines the mean latent discrepancy and the fluctuation tensor residual via the coordinate-saliency gate (formalized in Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). The stress term enters both the training loss and the test score; its downstream contribution is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a).

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory and Flow-Matching Derivation)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, a smooth field $v \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R}^D)$ splits into a curl-free potential gradient and a solenoidal field, $v = -\nabla \Phi + \nabla \times A$. As dissipative systems relax toward local minima on potential-energy surfaces, we model latent evolution as the dissipative gradient flow $\dot{z}(\tau) = -\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z(\tau))$ for a scalar potential network $\Phi_\psi$ with smooth (SiLU) activations.

\begin{proposition}[Absence of Periodic Attractors in Gradient Flows]
\label{prop:no_limit_cycles}
Let $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$ be an autonomous gradient flow on $\mathbb{R}^D$ where $\Phi \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R})$. There exist no non-constant periodic orbits.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.)

\paragraph{Continuous Velocity Field and Multi-Subspace Regime Routing}
The velocity field is the negative gradient of a parameterized scalar potential, $\hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}})$, where $\Phi_\psi$ is a three-layer residual MLP (LayerNorm, SiLU) conditioned on state $z_t$, diffusion time $t \in [0,1]$ (sinusoidal embedding), context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a discrete regime projection $p_{\text{regime}}$; the raw gradient is tangent-projected via $v_\perp = v - \langle u, v\rangle u$, $u = z_t/\|z_t\|_2$, bounding integration curvature.

Discrete operational modes use $K_r = 4$ learnable orthonormal subspace projections $\mathbf{U}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d}$ ($d{=}8, D{=}32$); the active regime is selected by maximal captured context energy, $k^* = \arg\max_k \|\mathbf{U}_k^\top z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2^2$, $p_{\text{regime}} = \mathbf{U}_{k^*} \mathbf{U}_{k^*}^\top z_{\text{ctx}}$, with an orthogonality separation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{subspace}} = \frac{2}{K_r(K_r-1)} \sum_{i < j} \|\mathbf{U}_i^\top \mathbf{U}_j\|_F^2$. Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations} includes a single-regime control ($K_r{=}1$) with routing disabled.

\paragraph{Flow-Matching Objective, Numerical Midpoint Evaluation, and Dynamical Misspecification}
Proposition~\ref{prop:no_limit_cycles} governs the autonomous continuous flow $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$; the empirical objective instead trains $\hat{v}$ via conditional flow matching (OT-CFM) along linear paths between noise $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$: $z_t = (1-t)z_{\text{noise}} + t z_{\text{tgt}}$, $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$, with target field $v_t^* = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}$ and loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \left[ \| \hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2 \right].
\end{equation}
Along the deterministic mean trajectory ($z_{\text{noise}} = \mathbf{0}$), $z_t = t\,z_{\text{tgt}}$ and $v_t^* = z_{\text{tgt}}$ is constant.

At inference, rather than integrating an ODE over $t \in [0,1]$, the detector queries the field at the trajectory midpoint:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}\!\left(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}},\, 0.5,\, z_{\text{ctx}},\, p_{\text{regime}}\right) - z_{\text{tgt}}.
\end{equation}
Since the reference velocity along the mean path is $v_{0.5}^* = z_{\text{tgt}}$, this midpoint query directly measures the discrepancy between the learned potential gradient and the nominal target, matching the dimensionality ($D{=}32$) and scale of the unconstrained TS-JEPA residual. The anomaly score applies the saliency gate: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$.

\noindent\textbf{Dynamical Misspecification on Non-Conservative Telemetry:} A curl-free velocity field ($\nabla \times \hat{v} \equiv \mathbf{0}$) forces circulation along any closed path $\mathcal{C}$ to vanish, $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \hat{v} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$. Valid for purely dissipative equilibria, this is dynamically misspecified for the non-conservative cyclic dynamics common in telemetry (motor revolutions, diurnal GECCO consumption cycles, periodic Daphnet gait kinematics): normal circulating orbits are penalized as anomalous. This predicts degraded detection on cyclic telemetry, tested in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed at stride 1. For TS-JEPA, Operator-Entropy, and Potential-Flow, the score gates the latent discrepancy through $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}): $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t))$. For Reynolds-Stress, $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(z_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. All shared representation stages are held fixed across variants and ablation controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\textbf{Operational Distinction: Zero-Lookahead Causal Filtering vs. Buffered Attribution.}
Two regimes exist, formalized in Figure~\ref{fig:inference_timeline}: (1) strictly causal streaming (\textit{trailing} attribution), in which the score emitted at $\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_{\tau}$-measurable --- it uses only observations through window completion; and (2) buffered monitoring (\textit{smear} attribution), in which the identical window score is attributed retrospectively over the suspect region $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$, so an anomaly at $t^*$ can raise discrepancy up to $S{=}64$ steps before onset. Under strict unadjusted point-level evaluation (stride 1), alarms during $[t^*-S, t^*]$ score as false alarms: the buffered regime is detection with a fixed $S$-step buffer, not forecasting. The two regimes are \emph{not} interchangeable evidence; we report them separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}). Incident analysis (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}) also evaluates \textit{Middle} alignment ($t + S/2$); all baselines share identical windowing, horizon, and standardization.

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and the Auxiliary Channel-Gate Variant}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults frequently originate in a localized subsystem while the remaining channels exhibit ambient noise, diluting localized anomalies in isotropic representations. The deployed detector mitigates this in latent space via an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate on error vectors $\mathbf{e} = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{pred}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \\
    w_d &= \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)}, \nonumber
\end{align}
amplifying sparse coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$ while remaining exactly $\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$ under isotropic errors. This is the discrepancy gate used in every experiment: input channels are encoded unweighted, and the gate acts only on the $D$-dimensional predictive residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary Input-Channel Gate (Evaluated Variant)}
We also evaluate a strictly causal \textit{Causal Channel Saliency Gate} as an auxiliary variant (full specification, boundary-condition verification, and the fault-type study in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}): each channel is weighted by the sigmoid of its standardized context-window variance share, $g_k = \sigma\!\left((s_k - \bar{s})/(\sigma_s + \epsilon)\right)$, applied symmetrically to context and target encoders so that $\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$ (zero target leakage, with an explicit identity short-circuit at $K{=}1$). Two boundary conditions apply: quiet faults (flatlines, stuck values) cannot be isolated by variance amplification --- the fault-type study confirms they are weakly detected under \emph{every} gating regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$) --- and abrupt regime transitions transiently perturb variance shares until the context window refills.

\paragraph{Zero-Leakage Data Provenance and Temporal Isolation}
We enforce strict temporal isolation across the training, nominal-calibration, and held-out evaluation partitions (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}): standardization statistics come only from the training partition, EVT tail parameters $(u, \sigma, \xi)$ only from the calibration partition, and evaluation proceeds chronologically with frozen weights and thresholds. The auxiliary gate's weights depend only on $x_{\text{ctx}}$; the deployed pipeline weights inputs uniformly.

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Anomalies span temporal scales from sensor spikes to thermal drifts; the encoder front-end therefore splits each channel into $J{=}4$ smooth dyadic shells on an $N_{\text{fft}}=256$ rFFT grid, normalized bin-wise into an exact amplitude partition of unity, $\sum_j \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ (measured deviation $< 1.2\times10^{-7}$; construction details in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}). Each shell feeds a causal 1D convolution ($k \in \{15,9,5,3\}$ coarse-to-fine), recombined by learned scale routing. Because the shells overlap smoothly at band edges, the front-end is \emph{less} frequency-localized than standard wavelet banks: per-band in-octave spectral mass is $\{0.50, 0.33, 0.20, {\approx}1.00\}$ versus $\{0.78, 0.50, 0.55, 0.82\}$ (Haar) and $\{0.86, 0.63, 0.64, 0.87\}$ (DB4) (\texttt{reports/lp\_filterbank\_diagnostics.csv}). We therefore describe it as a Fourier-domain multiscale front-end, not an exact Littlewood--Paley octave decomposition, and measure its empirical contribution by matched ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b).

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}: excesses over a high threshold $u$ (98th percentile) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via an empirical heuristic factor governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ rises to suppress volatile-noise false alarms; on light-tailed ones it tightens for sensitivity.

We emphasize that this is an engineering heuristic for pre-asymptotic finite-sample tails, not an asymptotic EVT theorem. In the controlled calibrator ablation (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) it does not improve out-of-sample risk control: on held-out data the multiplier raises TS-JEPA nominal FPR from $5.26\%$ to $17.68\%$ with no statistically distinguishable Point-F1 change ($0.1694$ vs.\ $0.1577$).

More broadly, EVT does not eliminate threshold-calibration difficulty: under $q = 10^{-3}$ (ideal asymptotic FPR $0.1\%$), measured held-out nominal FPR settles at $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ for the JEPAs and $\approx 22\%$ for TimesNet/TranAD, owing to finite-sample variance, residual autocorrelation, and non-stationary drift between nominal training and deployment. The calibrated finding of this paper is narrower: latent predictive residuals produce substantially fewer false alarms than observation-space residuals under identical calibration, reflecting lighter tails at the cost of lower causal recall. Thresholds use static EVT on held-out nominal splits; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

The master experiment matrix enumerating all evaluation suites (stream counts, partitions, horizons, calibrators, metrics) is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:master_matrix}.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted using a causal dilated TCN (3 residual blocks, channel depth $F{=}48$, kernel $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4\}$); global temporal average pooling aggregates the full $C{=}256$ context, with LayerNorm and dropout ($p{=}0.20$). The momentum target encoder follows EMA tracking with cosine schedule $m_{\text{base}}{=}0.996 \to m_{\text{max}}{=}0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's nominal telemetry is partitioned into three disjoint chronological blocks: \emph{training} (first 65\%; representation weights only), \emph{nominal calibration} (next 17.5\%; withheld from training, used solely to fit per-stream GPD tail parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}0.98$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$, with $\gamma_2$ estimated strictly within this block), and \emph{nominal evaluation} (final 17.5\%, separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap; held-out FPR measured on data the threshold never saw). Thresholds then remain fixed during test evaluation to measure static EVT stability under drift; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}).

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment Across Experiments}
To avoid ambiguity, we specify the calibrator per experiment. \textbf{Primary held-out benchmark} (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}; all paired statistics): standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier); headline metrics are the ``SPOT'' column of Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, with calibrator variants compared on identical score streams. \textbf{Ablations and controlled studies} (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}): SPOT with the $\tanh(\gamma_2)/6$ kurtosis multiplier (the released \texttt{adaptive\_kurtosis} flag). \textbf{Buffered-attribution and EVT mechanism studies}: standard SPOT unless the calibrator is the object of comparison. \textbf{Incident metrics} (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}): rolling SPOT. No experiment mixes calibrators across models within a table.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Multiplicity Controls}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are trained across five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 large GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) use three seeds (five on the two smallest) for cost, and ablation controls use three. Two co-primary endpoints: (i) \emph{nominal FPR reduction} on held-out nominal data, testing whether latent prediction stabilizes EVT bounds; and (ii) \emph{detection parity} via unadjusted Point-F1 and PR-AUC, testing whether suppression degrades discrimination.

Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, moving-block bootstrap 95\% CIs (block $L{=}64$), and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The independent unit of analysis is the telemetry stream ($n{=}19$ non-GHL at five seeds; $n{=}33$ where GHL enters at available coverage): seeds are averaged within stream and repeated windows aggregated to one stream-level score, so nothing is double-counted. Because nonsignificant tests do not establish equivalence, we make no noninferiority claim: paired differences are reported with effect sizes and CIs for readers to judge practical margins. $p$-values across comparisons are Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted at $q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$; CIs spanning zero or adjusted $p > 0.05$ are treated as inconclusive, not as equivalence. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:test_map} maps every reported test to its endpoint, cohort, paired-stream count, seed treatment, raw and adjusted $p$, and effect-size interval.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Parameter Capacities}
To isolate algorithmic principles from external implementation discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline under identical windowing, standardization, and EVT calibration. TimesNet and TranAD are in-house re-implementations of the published architectures (rather than vendor code), configured to published depths and evaluated under the identical optimization budget (AdamW, $\eta=10^{-3}$, cosine decay, $20$ epochs); no method received per-model hyperparameter tuning beyond the shared configuration, and no threshold, early-stopping, or model-selection step accessed held-out anomaly labels. Hardware-specific throughput, batch, warm-up, and per-timestep latency measurements are reported in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}.

\noindent\textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Multi-periodic reconstructive architecture mapping 1D series to 2D variation spaces via FFT period discovery and Inception blocks ($648{,}553$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $574{,}529$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Adversarial reconstructive Transformer with two-stage self-conditioning and sub-series attention ($205{,}746$ at $K{=}25$; $201{,}090$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TS-JEPA (Unconstrained Control):} Standard JEPA predicting $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ via a 2-layer MLP ($289{,}061$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $218{,}309$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Formulations:} Operator-Entropy ($285{,}733$ at $K{=}25$, $-1.15\%$ capacity), Reynolds-Stress ($295{,}953$, $+2.4\%$), and Potential-Flow ($338{,}117$, $+17.0\%$): differences do not stem from parameter scaling.

\noindent\textbf{Linear AR Forecaster (Non-Neural Baseline):} Ridge-regularized map from flattened context to target windows, scored by observation-space prediction error --- isolating whether latent representation is necessary at all. \textbf{Training-free controls:} PCA-subspace reconstruction, per-channel max-$z$, IsolationForest, $k$NN-distance, observation-space MAD scoring, and the dimensionality-matched PCA$\to$32$\to$LayerNorm gate of Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Critical surveys \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards} document severe reporting vulnerabilities in this literature --- point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, threshold gaming --- so we report metrics across complementary tiers. \textbf{Primary endpoint:} the recall--realized-FPR operating curve and its partial AUC over the design-budget grid (Section~\ref{sec:results}). \textbf{Secondary endpoints:} \textbf{Dataset-Macro Point-F1} (unweighted mean across datasets, preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating); \textbf{channel-macro Point-F1}; \textbf{PR-AUC} (average precision, ranking quality independent of calibration); \textbf{event recall} (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments containing $\ge1$ alarm); \textbf{mean detection delay} $\Delta t$ (onset-to-first-alarm lag over detected events only); and the \textbf{trivial all-positive reference} $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at base rate $\pi$ --- models below it underperform a constant alarm. \textbf{Pooled micro-F1} exposes flood-prone models; contiguous PA-F1 and test-sweep PA-F1 quantify the residual calibration gap.

\subsection{Evaluation Regimes: Zero-Lookahead Causal vs. Buffered Inference}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered_protocol}
To keep the buffered-attribution regime separate from future-observation access, we evaluate two regimes: \textbf{strictly causal online detection} (at decision time $T$ only $\{X_\tau\}_{\tau \le T}$ is accessible; scores use trailing attribution; zero lookahead) and \textbf{buffered window detection} ($S{=}64$ future timesteps are buffered before scoring, capturing full trajectory evolution at a deliberate $64$-step latency). Both record Mean Detection Delay, Event Recall within delay budgets ($\Delta t \le 10, 64$), and false alarms per 1,000 steps ($\text{FAR}_{1\text{k}}$).

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone. Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating Variant}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling and non-causal controls under identical TS-JEPA configuration (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}, Tables~\ref{tab:gating_variants}, \ref{tab:fault_recall}). \subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}

Two caveats: the block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters, and measured shell overlap means octave isolation is not attained. The front-end is a modest contributor, not the primary driver.

\subsection{Controlled Backbone Comparison: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Does predicting in a contracted latent space condition residuals for EVT thresholding, or is the gain attributable to prediction, compression, the EMA target encoder, or merely the bounded low-dimensional score space? To isolate the mechanism, all 19 non-GHL streams were re-run under the primary held-out protocol (five seeds, identical windows, AdamW budget, and SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$ calibration) with five arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} backbone ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, three dilated TCN layers): \textbf{TCN-Obs-Recon} (observation reconstruction), \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} (observation forecasting), \textbf{JEPA-CtxRecon} (latent ``prediction'' of the EMA-encoded \emph{context} --- compression without foresight), \textbf{JEPA-NoEMA} (latent prediction with an online hard-copy target), and \textbf{TS-JEPA}. Four further controls probe the score-space confound and gate equalization directly: \textbf{PCA-LinPred-LN}, \textbf{Obs-MAD}, \textbf{IsoForest}, \textbf{KNN-Dist} (training-free observation-space scorers), and \textbf{TS-JEPA-NoGate} (identical model, ungated $\ell_2$ discrepancy). Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:residual_diagnostics} reports the per-arm residual diagnostics; the earlier 15-stream multi-SNR study remains in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:controlled_backbone}.

\noindent\textbf{Bounded, low-dimensional scoring conditions the tail:} Observation-space objectives produce heavy tails regardless of direction (TCN-Obs-Recon vs.\ TCN-Obs-Pred: $\gamma_2$ $8.31$/$8.24$, FPR $0.303$/$0.310$). Latent objectives compress tails $\approx$$8\times$ ($\gamma_2$ $1.0$--$1.6$) and cut FPR $\approx$$3\times$ (TS-JEPA $-0.230$ vs.\ TCN-Obs-Pred, $p{=}0.031$ unadjusted). This could be a mechanical consequence of scoring bounded LayerNorm-ed 32-d vectors rather than learned dynamics. Three controls isolate the role of boundedness: \textbf{PCA-LinPred-LN} (32-d PCA embeddings, ridge forecast, LayerNorm, identical gated norm --- in observation space) reproduces the conditioning almost exactly ($\gamma_2{=}1.16$, FPR $0.052$ vs.\ $0.076$), as does \textbf{IsoForest} ($\gamma_2{=}-0.24$, FPR $0.037$); \textbf{Obs-MAD} conditions only partially ($\gamma_2{=}1.30$) and still floods ($0.29$). Bounded low-dimensional scoring is therefore sufficient for tail conditioning --- the learned encoder is not its cause --- while robust normalization alone is not.

\noindent\textbf{Conditioning is not detection:} The same controls show that conditioned scores need not detect: at matched budgets PCA-LinPred-LN attains recall $0.665$ and IsoForest $0.631$ versus $0.774$ for TS-JEPA, and removing the saliency gate changes nothing ($0.768$, identical tails). The learned embedding contributes anomaly--nominal separation; boundedness contributes the conditioning.

\noindent\textbf{Compression vs.\ prediction vs.\ EMA:} JEPA-CtxRecon --- which never predicts --- retains most of the conditioning ($-0.203$ vs.\ TCN-Obs-Pred, $p{=}0.055$). Removing the EMA target changes little (JEPA-NoEMA: $-0.033$ vs.\ TS-JEPA, $p{=}0.17$); we cannot conclude equivalence, only that any effect is below our detection threshold at $n{=}19$. PR-AUC differences across the arms are inconclusive ($p \ge 0.16$).

\noindent\textbf{Native Window Horizons:} Baselines do not suffer merely from the long $W{=}320$ window: at their native horizons tail conditioning \emph{worsens} (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:native_window_baselines}). TranAD at $W{=}10$ spikes to $\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and TimesNet at $W{=}96$ reaches $\gamma_2 = 98.14$ with $44.09\%$ empirical FPR, while TS-JEPA retains $\gamma_2 = 3.14$: raw-space tail corruption is inherent, not a window-length artifact.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$). Primary findings:

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076$--$0.086$ versus $0.300$ (TimesNet) and $0.307$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired reduction is significant: TS-JEPA $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.247, -0.071]$, $p{=}0.002$) and $-0.151$ vs.\ TranAD ($p{=}0.003$); Operator-Entropy shows the same effect ($-0.144$/$-0.142$, $p{=}0.006$). The mechanism is isolated under the same-backbone control (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}).

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Parity:} False-alarm suppression does not translate into statistically distinguishable detection gains. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences are inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$).

\noindent\textbf{Event Recall and Detection Conservatism:} Lower FPR accompanies lower event recall: on non-GHL streams TimesNet captures $58.3\%$ of events and TranAD $49.0\%$, versus $48.1\%$ (TS-JEPA), $43.4\%$ (Reynolds), $30.3\%$ (Potential-Flow). Subtle onsets that do not shift dominant latent coordinates are filtered with the noise.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Delay Trade-Offs ($\Delta t$):} Mean detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) varies widely: TimesNet responds fastest ($\Delta t{=}48$ steps), TS-JEPA averages $80$, and the physics variants are slower still ($133$--$192$); TranAD is slowest ($234$). Part of this gap is a detected-set artifact --- delays are averaged over different event sets per model. On the intersection of events both models detect at $q^{*}{=}0.05$, delays are $59$ versus $57$ steps (TS-JEPA vs.\ TimesNet) and $60$ versus $51$ (vs.\ TCN-ObsPred): the raw gap mostly reflects which events each model catches, not intrinsic latency.

\noindent\textbf{Necessity of Nonlinear Representation Learning (Linear AR Control):} A ridge-regularized linear AR forecaster under identical calibration attains FPR $0.006$ but collapses to Point-F1 $0.057$: observation-space linear dynamics cannot separate faults from background variance. Two training-free references sharpen the point (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:nonparametric}): a PCA-subspace reconstructor matches TimesNet almost exactly ($0.311$/$0.198$/$0.331$ vs.\ $0.300$/$0.197$/$0.329$ FPR/F1/PR-AUC), while a per-channel max-$z$ scorer attains the best ranking ($0.374$) at the worst FPR ($0.339$): ranking quality and threshold conditioning are independent properties.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-Specific Telemetry Dynamics:} Relative performance depends on the physical regime (per-dataset breakdown in Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:all_datasets}--\ref{tab:dataset_prauc}): TimesNet leads on Daphnet periodic kinematics ($0.245$ vs.\ $0.155$ F1); latent prediction leads on spacecraft telemetry (SMAP: OpEntropy $0.591$ PR-AUC; MSL: $0.445$ vs.\ $0.159$). On GHL and Metro all models struggle ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1).

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data refute this:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. TS-JEPA:} paired difference $+0.002$ Point-F1 (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$), $+0.002$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.86$), $+0.009$ FPR ($p{=}0.75$); leads on 9 of 33 streams.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. TS-JEPA:} $-0.015$ Point-F1 ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$), $-0.007$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.13$); leads on 11 of 33.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. TS-JEPA:} worse Point-F1 under SPOT calibration ($-0.081$, $p{=}0.033$ unadjusted) yet the \emph{strongest} strictly causal operating curve of any arm (partial AUC $0.749$, recall $0.784$ at $q^{*}{=}0.05$ on realized FPR $0.11$): its concentrated scores let a quantile-matched threshold detect well while the SPOT point lands poorly --- an operating-point artifact, not weak scores.
\end{enumerate}

These findings align with the matched-capacity controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a): the physical regularizers provide no measurable detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. The dynamical diagnostics on \emph{why} (operator rank preservation without detection gain, covariance-shift insensitivity, curl-free misspecification on cyclic telemetry) are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

Event-level incident metrics, latency, and rolling-SPOT case studies are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}: rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at a recall cost, and TS-JEPA's causal false-positive rate ($4.18\%$) sits an order of magnitude below reconstructive baselines ($20.8$--$30.1\%$).


Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark} reports the same benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression; on the 14 GHL streams causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and event recall declines from $58.82\%$ to $46.55\%$: without a lookahead buffer, latent predictive scoring is an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of detection sensitivity.

\paragraph{Pre-Onset Alarm Attribution}
Buffered attribution is strictly retrospective: each window's score is painted over $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$, so no mechanism can generate a lead beyond $S{=}64$ steps. The median pre-onset ``leads'' of $180$--$204$ steps in \texttt{reports/early\_warning\_leadtime.csv} are therefore unrelated nuisance alarms at the underlying false-positive rate or gradual precursor drift, not prediction: TimesNet emits $206$ such alarms per $1{,}000$ nominal steps versus $24$ for TS-JEPA, a difference consistent with the alarm-rate gap alone. We do not claim early-warning capability.

\paragraph{Detection Quality at Matched Operating Points}
\label{par:operating_curves}
The comparisons above confound score quality with the operating point each calibrated threshold happens to reach. We therefore re-evaluate every model on a common design-budget grid $q \in [10^{-4}, 0.3]$, thresholds fit on calibration and metrics read on test, so \emph{realized} FPR is the comparison axis (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_curves}). Two consequences follow. (i)~\emph{Design risk is not realized risk}: $q^{*}{=}0.05$ thresholds realize $0.11$--$0.36$ FPR on test --- a $2$--$7\times$ inflation, largest for observation-space baselines ($0.26$--$0.30$), smallest for Potential-Flow ($0.11$) and the dimensionality-matched control below ($0.14$). (ii)~\emph{At matched budgets the causal ordering reverses}: under trailing evaluation the JEPA family occupies the upper recall--FPR envelope (partial AUC over budgets ${\le}0.10$: $0.74$--$0.75$ versus $0.59$--$0.63$ for every observation-space, nonparametric, and distance-based baseline). At $q^{*}{=}0.05$ TS-JEPA attains event recall $0.774$ versus $0.618$ (TimesNet) at lower realized FPR ($0.180$ vs.\ $0.262$): the native-threshold gap (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}) largely reflects operating point, not sensitivity. The advantage is heterogeneous --- positive on $6$--$10$ of $18$ streams (bootstrap $p{=}0.05$--$0.25$; CIs overlap zero) --- and disappears under buffered attribution. We claim a favorable shift of the \emph{strictly causal} operating curve, concentrated in a subset of streams, not a uniform improvement.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out-of-sample? Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under buffered inference. The calibrator evaluated here is the \emph{static} peaks-over-threshold step of SPOT --- a single threshold fit once on the calibration block; the streaming variant that re-fits on new peaks is evaluated in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}. Four realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Systematic design-risk overshoot:} the static POT threshold ($u{=}0.98, q{=}10^{-3}$) never achieves its design risk out-of-sample --- held-out nominal FPR averages $5.26\%$ (TS-JEPA), $6.17\%$ (OpEntropy), $22.17\%$ (TimesNet), $22.17\%$ (TranAD), a $53$--$222\times$ overshoot.
    \item \textbf{High failure rate:} 95\% bootstrap CIs exclude $q$ in $61$--$86\%$ of runs; non-stationary drift violates EVT's stationary-tail assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Parity with empirical quantiles at their own risks:} on TS-JEPA, validation-maximum gives FPR $0.0553$ / F1 $0.1591$ and empirical p99.5 gives $0.0682$ / $0.1598$ --- these encode different nominal risks than $q{=}10^{-3}$ (not risk-matched); parametric GPD tail modeling confers no benefit at comparable realized FPR.
    \item \textbf{Kurtosis-adaptive failure (sensitivity arm):} the heuristic multiplier $1+\tanh(\gamma_2)/6$ inflates TS-JEPA FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$ (CI-exclusion $61\% \to 82\%$) with indistinguishable F1 ($0.1694$ vs.\ $0.1577$). Note the direction: calibration-block excess kurtosis is non-positive in $\sim$40\% of cells (median $0.23$), so the multiplier \emph{lowers} the threshold there --- it is an ad-hoc sensitivity arm, not an EVT method, and its failure carries no implication about GPD tail modeling.
\end{enumerate}

To separate the causes of calibration failure, we recorded per-stream tail-fit diagnostics on a nine-stream non-GHL subset (\texttt{reports/evt\_tail\_diagnostics.csv}). Tail-sample scarcity is genuine --- the median exceedance count above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and the Grimshaw estimator returns the degenerate shape $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits --- yet goodness-of-fit is not the dominant failure: KS tests reject in only $6/27$ (TS-JEPA) and $8/27$ (TimesNet) cells, while realized held-out FPR varies from $0.0$ to $0.67$ across seeds on the \emph{same} stream under passing fits. Inter-block drift, not tail mis-specification, drives the overshoot (decomposition in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:evt_detail}).

This attribution is quantified stream-level across all dumped cells: the KS distance between calibration and test nominal scores predicts realized SPOT FPR (Spearman $\rho{=}+0.38$), while calibration-block excess kurtosis predicts \emph{lower} realized FPR ($\rho{=}-0.32$): heavier tails make the GPD fit more conservative, so drift --- not tail mass --- drives failure. A controlled-drift study (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:drift_calibration}) sharpens this: runs declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ \emph{effective} exceedances per cell (of a median $21$ raw), and rolling calibrators control risk to $1$--$7\%$ under step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33$--$0.39$ FPR): adaptation absorbs regime jumps, not within-block drift.

Computational profiling is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}: JEPAs use $183.8\text{--}185.3$~MB peak memory at $0.60\text{--}0.84$~ms per-window inference.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigated empirically whether JEPA-style latent prediction and physics-inspired regularizers resolve the false-alarm vulnerability of multivariate anomaly detection, evaluating 33 telemetry streams from 11 datasets under a strictly held-out calibration protocol. Three conclusions follow.

First, the latent-residual effect decomposes into two factors: bounded, low-dimensional scoring conditions residual tails (a dimensionality-matched observation-space control and IsolationForest reproduce it), while the learned embedding supplies detection --- the JEPA family dominates the strictly causal recall--FPR curve ($0.74$--$0.75$ vs.\ $0.59$--$0.63$ partial AUC), a heterogeneous advantage that disappears under buffered attribution. Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain statistically indistinguishable ($p \ge 0.13$).

Second, hypothesis testing finds no detection advantage for the physics-inspired regularizers ($p \ge 0.61$). Potential-Flow is F1-weakest under SPOT calibration ($p{=}0.033$ unadjusted) yet strongest on the causal operating curve --- an operating-point artifact, not weak scores; its curl-free explanation for cyclic-telemetry weakness remains conjecture, though the inference-path mismatch alternative is rejected experimentally.

Third, static POT thresholds systematically overshoot their design risk on non-stationary telemetry ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ for JEPAs, $\approx 22\%$ for baselines at $q{=}10^{-3}$), a failure driven by inter-block drift rather than tail mis-fit.

\textbf{Scope.} All evaluations are \emph{within-dataset}: models are trained, calibrated, and tested on disjoint chronological partitions of the same stream. We claim nothing about cross-dataset transfer or unseen fault types (cf.\ Section~\ref{subsec:gate_fault_study}). Extensions include solenoidal components via Helmholtz decomposition, memory-bounded online drift adaptation, accelerated streaming inference kernels, and integer quantization for edge deployment.
