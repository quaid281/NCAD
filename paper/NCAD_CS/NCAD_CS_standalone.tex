\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one number so drifting coordinates inflate the shared threshold. We replace that scalar score with coordinate-level circuit breakers. Across target horizons, each coordinate's error magnitude and horizon-mean drift receive healthy-fitted tripwires, and an alarm fires when a correlation-aware quorum trips on healthy-calibrated counts. Rather than improving post-hoc score ranking, this quorum shifts the label-free operating point toward higher live event coverage and faster response. On 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets under a disjoint five-block protocol, our operational detector, Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 breakers across three horizons), raises live event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent across all nine datasets over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. It also cuts mean detection delay from 133.5 to 69.0 steps at a higher mean held-out false-positive rate of 6.4 versus 4.4 percent (median 0.93 percent). Because nominal regime shifts drive upper-tail false alarms and slow ramps evade fixed-window prediction, autonomous deployment requires a held-out acceptance check and live drift monitoring.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Anomaly detection, Multivariate time series, Representation learning, Self-supervised learning, Statistical process control.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet stochastic jitter, benign thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ heterogeneous channels allows one noisy sensor to produce heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibration via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) on later healthy data across our 19 development streams. Because faults span many steps, random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes, matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next window's summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and one threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} drift during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1 and $138\text{--}154$-step delay on development streams). Published benchmarks are further inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} and shared or test-labeled threshold tuning; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint nominal blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace the single scalar alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, like a building breaker box where a short in one circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across dyadic target prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, each of the 32 coordinates receives separate \emph{dials} tracking horizon error size and persistent drift, each with its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and the system alarms when a quorum of dials trips together. Drifting dials widen only their own thresholds instead of blinding the rest of the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Unlike a bank of independent detectors, the dials are structured views of one error matrix, and the quorum is fitted directly to joint trip counts on healthy data so cross-dial correlation is measured rather than assumed away. The panel does not act as a universally superior anomaly ranking score; instead, correlation-aware coordinate thresholding improves the label-free operating-point trade-off between live event coverage and detection delay without test-label threshold optimization. Targeting abrupt to intermediate-onset faults (within roughly one $256$-step context window), we establish a clear architectural hierarchy (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (primary operational architecture): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $(3+1)\times 32=128$ position-plus-drift dials under a single quorum ($120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable parameters).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic and ablation reference): shares the exact same encoder and parallel predictor across $M=4$ horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays) solely to isolate which error views aid live causal versus retrospective buffered attribution; adding fluctuation and relay dials inflates false alarms without improving live operational F1, making \emph{Lite} the preferred detector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Thirty-three development streams guide design-stage comparisons, while 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets (within a 141-stream, 12-dataset archive) under a disjoint five-block protocol test generalization and calibration reliability (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}), separating live \emph{causal} streaming from \emph{buffered} retrospective attribution:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Theory and live operating-point gain:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} proves that per-dial tripwires and quorums stay invariant under coordinate-scale drift that inflates global scalar thresholds. On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ across all $9$ datasets ($p=0.0039$) and cuts delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps at higher mean causal FPR ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$), doubling causal Point-F1 when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Operating-point attribution and regime-shift failure modes:} Matched test-FPR controls confirm no score-ranking gain over 1D scoring; the benefit comes from where the label-free quorum sets the operating point. While $88.1\%$ of untouched streams hold causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($92\%\text{--}100\%$ across six datasets), post-calibration concept drift rather than finite-sample quantile variance drives the $64.7\%$ $95$th percentile (verified via moving-block bootstrap and healthy regime-shift stress tests), motivating a five-step acceptance and live $D_{\text{KS}}$ protocol.
    \item \emph{Baselines, interpretability, and deployment:} Latent prediction cuts development causal FPR from $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ (reconstructors) to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$; full-pipeline benchmarks verify single-core CPU streaming ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s, $\le 128\,\text{KB}$ state) and multi-horizon coordinate attribution across abrupt, contextual, and slow-ramp events.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds horizon energy (null scalar reweighting); $105/118$ streams lack independent tail support for standalone guarantees; synthetic channel-branch gains cost $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$ FPR without proving natural sensor-fault coverage; and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL) require a frozen-reference long-horizon drift monitor.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$: autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly; TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches (temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}; we instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate alternatives such as univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy history followed by a labeled test segment. Two cohorts play distinct roles. Forty-five streams across 11 datasets guided design, while 141 archive streams across 12 datasets (118 never inspected during design) provide the primary evaluation.

\noindent\emph{Development suite:} Drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench} ($K\in[2,72]$), the 45 streams span three domains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar:} six SMAP ($K{=}25$) and six MSL ($K{=}55$) streams, split by NASA into out-of-range \emph{point} and in-range \emph{contextual} faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, plus SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$).
    \item \emph{Wearables:} six $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes; $S{=}64$ spans $1\,\text{s}$).
    \item \emph{Industrial and infrastructure:} 14 slow-ramp Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), Genesis ($K{=}18$), GECCO ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three streams ($19$ non-GHL, $14$ GHL, $7$ datasets) meet $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$, $K\le 55$, and $\le 1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); the remaining $12$ ($5$ short histories of $764\text{--}1{,}492$ steps, $6$ high-channel CICIDS streams, and GECCO) are held aside or evaluated in the archive.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive contains every qualifying stream from 12 datasets with at least $100$ calibration windows and one labeled anomaly. Multi-stream cohorts comprise SMAP ($47$), Daphnet ($22$), MSL ($18$), SMD ($18$), Exathlon ($17$), and OPPORTUNITY ($13$), alongside single-stream GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN. Healthy histories span $1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ steps and test segments span $2{,}158\text{--}114{,}001$ steps; removing the $23$ tuning streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and zero-tune transfer:} Every stream is fitted independently on its own unlabeled nominal prefix, so deploying on a new physical asset requires a healthy warm-up history rather than cross-asset weight transfer. What transfers zero-tune across heterogeneous sensor counts ($K\in[2,55]$) and sampling rates is the architecture, whitening damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum rule. Chronologically, the first $65\%$ of nominal data fits weights and standardization ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$), the next $17.5\%$ calibrates thresholds ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), and after a $64$-step gap the final $17.5\%$ checks held-out false alarms ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$); archive runs subdivide the fitting prefix into disjoint selection and adaptation blocks (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams from one facility share hardware characteristics, distinguishing cross-stream generalization within an archive from cross-domain generalization to unseen sensor suites. We therefore evaluate both dataset-clustered inference ($7$ dev, $9$ untouched, and $12$ full-archive datasets) and 12-fold leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) audits (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.66\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-7pt}
\caption{Four-stage pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational, $M{=}3$, $128$ dials) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic, $M{=}4$, $224$ dials): \textbf{(1)}~sliding window ($C{=}256, S{\le}64$) into online/EMA encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) with $K{\ge}25$ rFFT shells, multi-depth taps, centering, and LayerNorm; \textbf{(2)}~parallel MLP multi-horizon prediction and $MD{\times}MD$ damped whitening; \textbf{(3)}~position-plus-drift dial expansion; and \textbf{(4)}~per-dial tripwires and correlation-aware quorum alarm.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-7pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time, splitting at $t$ into a $C = 256$-step \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and an $S = 64$-step \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ ending at causal decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features split into four smooth dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose weights sum to one at every frequency (four bands partition the Nyquist interval without over-fragmenting $256$-step windows), pass through depthwise causal convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombine via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) with dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ \cite{bai2018empirical}. Lower-channel streams ($K < 25$) feed directly into the TCN.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) pool temporal (last step, max, mean $\in\mathbb{R}^{144}$) and statistical (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, mean absolute first difference $\in\mathbb{R}^{144}$) descriptors through $D=32$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads to latent dimension $D=32$ (balancing expressivity against covariance estimation cost; $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ is ablated in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). $E_\phi$ tracks an exponential moving average (EMA) of $E_\theta$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D controls), matching a $\approx 250$-step target horizon that prevents representation collapse across $10\text{--}20$ epochs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Terminal affine-free LayerNorm projects embeddings onto the $\sqrt{D}$-sphere, curbing heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$) at a structural trade-off: rescaling by total feature variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines by $O(1/\sqrt{KD})$ across coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two native mechanisms preserve fast transients and training offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Both encoders read the front-end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, passing concatenated mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{288}$) through a bias-free $288\to 32\to 32$ GELU-LayerNorm head blended at $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} A running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, synchronized into $E_\phi$) is subtracted before terminal LayerNorm, removing static offsets without guaranteeing invariance to post-calibration regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA} predicts one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the 64-step target window via a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant term ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Because one $64$-step vector averages out brief transients, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ dyadic target prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\} = \{S/4, S/2, S\}$ steps, spanning impulse, intermediate, and sustained timescales), while the diagnostic reference \emph{Laminar-JEPA} adds $S_3=48$ ($M=4$). From $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, one parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP predicts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$ behind a single terminal LayerNorm, weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ with a batch anti-collapse penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. The penalty acts on each batch of predicted summaries and does two jobs: it keeps every coordinate varying, and it discourages coordinates from copying one another. Unlike log-determinant penalties, which need batches larger than $D$, it works at our batch size $B=16<D=32$. The diagonal entries of $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$, push each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma = 0.75$; the off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize the sample covariance (divisor $B-1$). Hence
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 .
\end{equation}
The penalty raises the effective rank of the target summaries, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}, from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first passed through a \emph{damped whitening} step fitted on healthy training windows, which decorrelates them and brings well-estimated directions close to unit variance. We flatten $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($128$ in full, $96$ in Lite), accumulate its \texttt{float32} sample mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and unbiased covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = (N_{\text{fit}}-1)^{-1}\sum_t (\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})(\cdot)^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$, eigendecompose $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$, clamp rank-deficient or numerical negative modes via $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$, and form $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})$ by the symmetric square root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ whose training covariance has eigenvalues $\lambda_k^2/(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2$: close to $1$ for well-estimated directions, while rank-deficient or near-zero modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$) are attenuated toward $0$ rather than amplified. The whitening is therefore approximate, with damping $10^{-3}$ fixed across all cohorts.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$, never on the block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ that later sets alarm thresholds (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), and blend them toward a simpler per-coordinate scaling, the unwhitened error divided by its root-mean-square on $\mathbf{X}_q$, $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=e_{m,c}(t)/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} Gradient-free predictor-row editing (Steps Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$ gave no archive gain (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}) and is omitted from the unified backbone.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} states two exact algebraic properties of this split: drift and fluctuation partition horizon energy orthogonally, and the fluctuation spectrum spans at most $M-1$ modes.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
In plain terms, multivariate residual scoring faces three design options across $J$ coordinates or dials $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$: (1)~\emph{global quadratic aggregation} $S_{\text{glob}}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$, which sums all coordinates into one threshold; (2)~\emph{marginal union} $\max_j \mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$, which compounds per-coordinate false alarms across $J$ tests; and (3)~\emph{correlation-aware coordinate quorum}, which gives every dial its own quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and sets a joint trip-count threshold $K_q$ on healthy calibration data. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes why global aggregation is vulnerable when even $k\ll J$ ``hijacker'' coordinates exhibit larger post-training variance on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, whereas per-coordinate tripwires isolate that inflation completely.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Quorum Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^J$ be a nominal residual vector on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, and let $\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}$ rescale a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k=|\mathcal{H}|<J$ drifting coordinates by $\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta\ge 0$) while leaving $j\notin\mathcal{H}$ unchanged:
(i)~under global scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-calibration quantile satisfies $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_j^2\bigr)$, growing unboundedly in $\eta^2$ so any live fault $\mathbf{u}(t^*)$ supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected when $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous tripwires $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}|)$, every binary trip $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)})$ is invariant to $\eta$ for all $\eta\ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate dependence, leaving $N_{\text{cal}}=\sum_{j=1}^J b_j(t)$ and $K_q=\kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$ unchanged and guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $\sum_{j=1}^J (u_j^{(\eta)})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_j^2$ pointwise, monotonicity and positive homogeneity of quantiles give~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ yields $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u}(t^*))=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2\le\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity gives $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}\,\tau_j^{(0)}$ for $j\in\mathcal{H}$ (and $\tau_j^{(0)}$ otherwise); dividing $|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)}$ by $\sqrt{1+\eta^2}>0$ yields $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=b_j^{(0)}(t)$ identically at every $t$, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $K_q$, while $m$ active coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ yield $N(t^*)\ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration and validity assumptions:} For $J=32$ standard normal coordinates at $\alpha=0.005$ and $\beta=0.015$, inflating just $k=2$ hijacker coordinates by $\eta=5$ raises $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$, completely masking a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault on $\mathcal{H}^c$ ($10\times 3.8^2=144.4 < 252.4$), whereas $K_q$ stays invariant at $3.5$ and fires with $10 > 3.5$ trips. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} also makes the panel's two required assumptions explicit: (1)~\emph{multi-dial fault coverage ($m > K_q$)}, so ultra-localized single-sensor flatlines diluted below $\tau_j$ fail to reach quorum; and (2)~\emph{nominal regime stationarity on at least $J-\lfloor K_q\rfloor$ dials}, because a post-calibration shift between $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and deployment that simultaneously inflates $>K_q$ dials violates calibration exchangeability and raises false alarms unless gated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and live $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) implements this correlation-aware quorum across the healthy calibration block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Because all dials view the same $M \times 32$ error matrix, their trips are strongly correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on development streams; count variance $7.2\times$ binomial independence), so quorums are fitted directly to empirical $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ trip counts rather than binomial formulas:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded directly at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 onset relays:} When a sudden fault first enters the right edge of the target window, the short prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover healthy data, so few position dials trip. To catch this moment, each coordinate $c$ has stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($q_{0.993}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$) and fires a relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when its full-window position dial ($m=4$) trips together with its endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$ dial.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, the trips from window $[t,t+S)$ are reported at its right end $\tau=t+S-1$, the earliest moment all of its data exist; this fixes a reporting lag of $S-1$ steps, separate from the detection delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$ after fault onset $t^*$. Under \emph{buffered attribution}, used for after-the-fact review, each trip is assigned to the steps it describes: position trips to their prefix $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation trips to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint trips to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. $N_{224}(\tau)$, the position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and the relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. Ranking metrics use the plain count $N_{224}(\tau)$ directly. The binary alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with quorums set on calibration counts: $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$. All quantiles use linear interpolation, the safety factor is applied before the floor, quorums stay real-valued, and both trips and alarms use strict inequalities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both tiers share the exact $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$-parameter backbone ($120.0\text{k}$ at $K < 25$ and $127.7\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$, down from $295.8\text{k}$ in the legacy six-block sequential model) and differ only in horizon count and dial expansion:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Shared streamlined backbone:} Four adaptive TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $D$-wide pooling and native hook-free tap heads, depthwise rFFT shells gated at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbf{u}_m = z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{32} \to \text{Linear}(32,64) \to \text{GELU} \to \text{LayerNorm}(64) \to \text{Linear}(64,32)$, returning $\operatorname{LN}_{\text{no-aff}}(\mathbf{u}_m + \Delta_m)$).
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and keeps only the $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ under a single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$, removing fluctuation/endpoint dials and onset relays.
    \item \emph{Diagnostic $7D=224$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and expands errors into all $224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA (224 Dials)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\State Partition $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$ or $S_{1:4}{=}\{16,32,48,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and freeze $K_{\text{Lite}}{=}\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$ (or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum})).
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t){=}\mathbb{I}(d_j(t){>}\tau_j)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (\text{causal}) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (\text{buffered}); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ when active counts exceed quorum.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (RMS of full-tensor and peak-horizon scores, $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$) blend $\ell_2$ norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is split chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}) into a fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a validation suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$, with all parameters and thresholds frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint fitting, selection, and adaptation):} Because reusing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for early stopping or stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) can make thresholds optimistic, the primary 118/141-stream archive uses a \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$): $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits standardization, network weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the checkpoint; and $S$-isolated $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$ windows) fits and freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched. Exploratory fixed-epoch suites omit $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($65\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$, unblended on the 19-stream cache and 130-stream buffered screen, or sharing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ on the 33-stream suite); a disjoint four-block check ($50\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) on the 19 development streams confirms matching causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.96\%$ for \emph{Lite}, $1.05\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}), though the smaller fitting prefix lowers causal F1 ($0.133\to 0.071$).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched calibration and autocorrelation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Splitting the $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ stride-1 windows at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step gap so target steps never overlap (a $G=320$ gap shifts causal FPR by $<0.25$ points). Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ensures at least $500(1-0.995)=2.5$ raw tail windows above $q_{0.995}$, whereas autocorrelation ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$ via Sokal's $c=5$ estimator) shrinks effective sample size $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Statistical vs.\ Practical Significance}
Seeds are averaged per stream (seed standard deviation (SD) $\le 0.008$ on archive causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER) before two-level inference. First, we report both \emph{dataset-clustered inference} ($95\%$ cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip tests) and stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests ($2{,}000$ bootstrap resamples), applying Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) \cite{benjamini1995controlling} across the four focal 118-stream causal contrasts (\emph{Lite} vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D and TS-JEPA on Point-F1 and ER: BH $p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$ and $3.2\times 10^{-6}$ for stream F1; $0.0078$ and $0.016$ for sign-flip ER). Second, we separate statistical significance from practical magnitude and regime dependence: pooled causal Point-F1 rises modestly from $0.076$ to $0.086$ ($p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$), yet its cluster CI $[-0.026,+0.069]$ spans zero because 1D SPOT achieves higher step precision on SMAP's long anomalies when $N_{\text{cal}}<500$. Practically substantial gains concentrate in live episode coverage ($+12.1$ ER points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$, positive on $9/9$ datasets), halved delay ($69.0$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps), and streams with $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ where Point-F1 more than doubles ($0.040\to 0.085$). Across all 12 LODO folds of the 141-stream archive (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(b)), \emph{Lite} keeps a strictly positive causal ER gain in $12/12$ folds ($+8.9$ to $+17.9$ points, $\max p=0.0027$) and positive mean Point-F1 gain in $10/12$ folds (Wilcoxon $p\le 0.036$ in $11/12$ folds and $p=0.055$ when dropping Exathlon).

\subsection{Cohorts, Baseline Fairness, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} omits Steps Q1--Q4. Causal and buffered modes share identical splits per cohort:
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-stage):} Because these streams guided design and the 33-stream suite shares $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for stabilization and thresholds, they test architectural factors rather than out-of-sample calibration. The 19-stream cache ($6$ datasets) tests the dial ladder and detector banks (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:matched_alert}); the 33-stream suite ($19$ non-GHL plus $14$ GHL, $7$ datasets) compares external baselines (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Hyperparameters were fixed once on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched archive (primary evidence):} Removing those $23$ streams from the $141$-stream archive ($12$ datasets) leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($101$ non-Exathlon, $8$ datasets; Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\noindent\emph{Baseline fairness across three tiers:} (i)~\emph{Matched-backbone controls} (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D)) use identical encoders, window budgets, and random-seed resets ($\{42,123,456\}$) to isolate scalar SPOT from the breaker panel. (ii)~\emph{System-level aggregators} score the exact same frozen whitened residuals under matched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rates (Tables~\ref{tab:matched_alert}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). (iii)~\emph{External baselines} (TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, PCA Forecaster, Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) use their published reference configurations ($206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ parameters vs.\ $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable for \emph{Lite}), identical training-only scaling, $W=320$ windowing, AdamW epoch budgets ($15\text{--}20$ dev epochs across $5$ seeds; $12$ early-stopped archive epochs across $3$ seeds), $64$-step gaps, and SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$), with zero per-dataset hyperparameter tuning for any method. All runs used PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090 (code: \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and scores $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$ are evaluated step by step without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} (\emph{causal} = live at $\tau$; \emph{buffered} = retrospective review), averaging seeds per stream before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) aggregation:
(1)~\emph{False alarms ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$):} healthy fraction flagged on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and on test steps $y(\tau)=0$ (reported via mean, median, $\text{p90}$, and $\text{p95}$).
(2)~\emph{Step accuracy (Point-F1, precision-recall (PR) AUC):} without point adjustment, Point-F1 is naturally $0.05\text{--}0.25$ because residuals peak near transitions and rare fault prevalence $\pi$ bounds trivial alarming at $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}=2\pi/(1+\pi)$; PR-AUC measures threshold-free ranking.
(3)~\emph{Episode coverage and speed (ER, Delay $\Delta t$):} ER is the fraction of contiguous $y=1$ runs hit at least once (interpreted alongside Bernoulli chance $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$), and $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ measures steps from onset $t^*$ over detected episodes (separate from the $S-1$ causal lag).
(4)~\emph{Operating controls and diagnostics:} \emph{Auto.}\ uses frozen healthy thresholds, $q^*=0.05$ uses $q_{0.95}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$ matches test FPR post hoc (diagnostic only), excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ measures $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ tail heaviness, and two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) distance $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) measures nominal drift.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
We keep experimental regimes separate: Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert} score one fixed $19$-stream $M=4$ cache; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) retrains each single-factor control ($3$ seeds, disjoint four-block protocol); Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) is a five-seed development run; and Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates the untouched $118$-stream archive:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cached dial ladder vs.\ why \emph{Lite} uses $128$ rather than $160$ dials:} On the fixed $M=4$ sequential cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding from $1$ to $224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$), and pruning fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to $160$ position-plus-drift dials reaches $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (unweighted $N_{224}$ shifts archive PR-AUC by $\le 0.0011$ with identical alarms). Retrained end-to-end on the parallel \emph{Lite} backbone (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), adding the fourth prefix ($S_3=48$, $160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) gives no Point-F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$) while raising causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.82\%$ to $1.06\%$) and slowing inference by $25.5\%$. On the $118$-stream archive rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), $128$-dial \emph{Lite} likewise matches or exceeds retrained $160$- and $224$-dial panels ($0.078$ causal F1 vs.\ $0.073$ and $0.074$).
    \item \emph{Single-factor encoder, predictor, and panel isolation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Four TCN blocks match five on F1 and ER ($p\ge 0.23$) while saving $23.3\text{k}$ parameters, and $64$D pooling heads match $128$D on F1 while raising causal ER ($55.9\%$ to $63.1\%$). Sequential rollout at $M=4$ lowers buffered F1 ($0.239$ to $0.195$) and doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%$ to $4.0\%$). Expanding $160$ to $224$ dials lifts ER ($63.6\%$ to $69.0\%$) only by inflating $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, and no panel removes the $S-1$ causal lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching omission:} Single-seed $118$-stream predictor-row edits (Steps Q1--Q4) leave causal F1 flat ($0.0901$ to $0.0904$) while shifting unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS, so \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
    \item \emph{Latent dimensionality, damping, and quorum sensitivity:} Across $D \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ ($32$ to $256$ dials, $3$ seeds, disjoint four-block protocol), \emph{Lite} exceeds Multi-Horizon 1D at every dimension ($56.1\%\text{--}61.0\%$ vs.\ $42.5\%\text{--}48.9\%$ causal ER; $0.062\text{--}0.083$ vs.\ $0.041\text{--}0.054$ causal F1). While $D=8$ ($\operatorname{RankMe}=6.4$) underfits buffered F1 ($0.131$ vs.\ $0.216$ at $D=32$, $\operatorname{RankMe}=25.0$), $D\in\{16,32\}$ forms a stable causal plateau peaking at $D=32$ in PR-AUC ($0.269$), and $D=64$ ($192\times 192$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) dips to $0.068$ causal F1. At $D=32$, damping is stable across $[10^{-3},10^{-2}]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.093$ F1, $\le 0.75\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; dropping at $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-1}$), dial tripwires $q_{\text{dial}}\in[0.975,0.990]$ keep causal F1 within $0.080\text{--}0.090$ via automatic $K_{\text{Lite}}$ scaling ($21.2\to 13.0$), and safety factors $\kappa\in[1.00,1.20]$ trade ER ($63.3\%\to 55.1\%$) smoothly against $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.21\%\to 0.55\%$), with $\kappa=1.12$ holding $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}<1\%$ before delay jumps at $\kappa=1.20$ ($98.9\to 149.5$ steps).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate rules fail through three distinct statistical mechanisms on the cached 19-stream residuals (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Cross-coordinate false-alarm compounding:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or even at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds marginal errors across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Discrete finite-sample calibration floor:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) across 128 coordinates or BH false discovery rate (FDR, $q=0.005$) across 224 dials fails even under stationarity because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot fall below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, paralyzing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{Out-of-sample distributional instability vs.\ stable aggregators:} Calibrating the aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) bypasses the discrete floor, exposing out-of-sample instability instead: discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ from boundary ties, and recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$ under mild cross-block covariance and mean drift. Among distributionally stable aggregators on this cache, the 160-dial panel reaches Point-F1 $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, above 128-coordinate top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.068$, $0.056$, $0.072$), while Fisher pooling reaches $0.109$ (Stouffer $0.095$) at $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Independently trained archive comparisons appear in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Synthetic vs.\ Natural Failures}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Separating fault mechanisms clarifies what the latent breaker panel can and cannot catch. The latent encoder reliably flags \emph{cross-channel consistency violations}, \emph{abrupt multi-sensor transients}, and \emph{in-range phase shifts} because they perturb $>K_{\text{Lite}}$ whitened coordinates simultaneously. Conversely, isolated single-sensor hardware faults (stuck-at freezing, variance collapse, rail saturation, quantization step-changes, intermittent packet dropouts, or single-channel bias drift) barely move the 32D summary: $K-1$ healthy channels dilute the input projection by $1/K$, and terminal LayerNorm rescales the remaining perturbation across all $32$ coordinates (on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials vs.\ $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip).

To test an auxiliary channel-level screen across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$), we threshold three 64-step per-channel statistics at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ (floor $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD), $24$-of-$40$ persistence): log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$. Across $57$ injected $200$-step single-channel events per class, the variance-collapse union lifts recall on collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%\text{--}82.5\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}94.7\%$, and the full branch lifts recall on shocks, linear degradation, and bias from $59.6\%\text{--}75.4\%$ to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$. On \emph{unmodified} test streams, however, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ climbs from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full branch). Because synthetic perturbations omit intermittent telemetry dropouts, quantization coarsening, and coupled hardware degradation, these injections demonstrate synthetic sensitivity only; we claim no real-world sensor-fault coverage, and keep the channel branch disabled by default.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End and Observation vs.\ Latent Space}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Retraining with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams ($3$ seeds) reveals a clear channel-count threshold: shell splitting lifts high-channel spacecraft F1 ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal $0.212 \to 0.321$) but lowers F1 on $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), motivating the $K \ge 25$ gate in \emph{Lite}. Across the 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), raw-observation reconstructors exhibit heavy tails, strong drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$); adding an observation-space LayerNorm bottleneck cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) while learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). Because \emph{Lite} and Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) share nearly identical score kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$), the panel's live gain stems from coordinate isolation rather than lighter tails.

\subsection{Computational Complexity and Real-Time Deployment Considerations}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
Operational spacecraft and industrial monitors require bounded memory and deterministic full-pipeline latency, including channel standardization, $W=320$ ring-buffer updates, encoder-predictor inference, $96\times 96$ whitening, stabilization blending, and 128-dial quorum evaluation. Across $K \in \{5, 9, 25, 38, 55, 72\}$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} holds $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable parameters ($1.10\text{--}1.18\,\text{MB}$ total FP32 resident weights including the EMA target encoder) plus $44.2\text{--}128.4\,\text{KB}$ of persistent per-stream state (ring buffer, $\mathbf{\Omega}$, and dial tripwires). Full-pipeline GPU inference (batch $64$, RTX 5090) takes $0.162\text{--}0.253\,\text{ms}$/window ($3{,}959\text{--}6{,}174$ windows/s, $33\text{--}91\,\text{MB}$ peak GPU memory), adding only $0.018\text{--}0.051\,\text{ms}$ over the neural forward pass ($0.144\text{--}0.201\,\text{ms}$). On a single CPU thread (batch $1$, embedded-processor proxy), full-pipeline streaming takes $7.11\text{--}7.45\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \le 9$ ($134\text{--}141$ windows/s, $>2\times$ Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate) and $9.76\text{--}10.79\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \in [25, 72]$ ($93\text{--}102$ windows/s; $7.28\text{--}10.24\,\text{ms}$ on $8$ CPU threads). Under unannounced sensor dropouts on the 19 development streams (imputing dropped channels at their training mean without retraining), dropping one or two active sensors reduces causal ER gradually from $58.9\%$ to $55.9\%$ and $54.7\%$ (Point-F1 $0.093 \to 0.085 \to 0.066$) while transiently raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.5\%$ to $7.9\%$ and $3.4\%$).

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} summarizes exploratory design-stage performance on the 33-stream suite (shared $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), while a separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external-baseline context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$). On the 19 non-GHL streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$. Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) explains why 1D scores degrade in live streaming: buffered review averages overlapping windows, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). A rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ hits $28.1\%$ of episodes at $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults that evolve much slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ Point-F1 across all $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Across linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$ ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points). Extending this experiment across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds on clean multivariate segments: abrupt step, $L\in\{64,256,1024\}$ ramps, exponential sensor degradation $1-e^{-t/512}$, and $0.001\sigma$/step accumulating bias) directly tests why slow faults evade short-horizon prediction: as onset slows from abrupt steps to accumulating bias, mean \emph{Lite} detection delay balloons nearly $20\times$ ($29.3 \to 70.7 \to 138.6 \to 361.4 \to 586.7$ steps; exponential degradation $433.9$ steps), context-to-target displacement $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$), and whitened residual RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$) as both windows ride the ramp together.

To test whether a complementary long-term reference closes this blind spot without altering \emph{Lite} weights, we evaluate a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ context features and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. On the $14$ real GHL industrial ramp streams ($3$ seeds), this frozen-reference monitor lifts causal ER from $0.8\%$ (\emph{Lite} alone, $0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$ ($58.5\%$ in a two-timescale union hybrid, $129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; Point-F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). However, because GHL's nominal baselines keep drifting after fault episodes, uncompensated test FPR rises to $74.4\%$, revealing a fundamental trade-off: sliding-window prediction rejects slow nominal drift at the cost of missing slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift catches slow ramps at the cost of flagging benign baseline wander.

Rather than emitting an opaque scalar, the 128-dial panel decomposes active trips $N_{128}(\tau)$ additively across short ($S_1{=}16$), medium ($S_2{=}32$), and long ($S_3{=}64$) horizons plus persistent drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit} evaluate the disjoint five-block archive across four explicit regimes: \emph{autonomous} (healthy-only thresholds), \emph{matched calibration rate}, \emph{post-hoc} test-FPR diagnostic control, and \emph{buffered} review:

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
While $88.1\%$ of untouched streams satisfy $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ (median $0.93\%$; $92.3\%\text{--}100\%$ of streams across SMD, MSL, OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, PSM, and CreditCard at $0.1\%\text{--}1.3\%$ dataset-mean FPR in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), a sharp upper tail pushes the $95$th percentile to $64.7\%$ ($14$ streams $>5\%$, concentrated in Spark workload switches on Exathlon, gait-trial shifts on Daphnet, and three command-mode shifts on SMAP). We distinguish two mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration failure vs.\ concept drift (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Randomly interleaving autocorrelation-preserving blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ keeps finite-sample size $N_{\text{cal}}$ and serial dependence intact while removing chronological drift. Across all 118 untouched streams ($3$-seed audit), stationary block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ collapses from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ ($\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams in Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a), including $1.00 \to 0.00$ on \texttt{SMAP G-6} and $0.75 \to 0.03$ on \texttt{Exathlon 10\_2}), attributing $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms to post-calibration \emph{concept drift} rather than calibration-tail estimation error.
    \item \emph{Internal drift drivers:} Streams with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ share nearly the same median $N_{\text{eff}}$ as stable streams ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$; Spearman $\rho=-0.01$ with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) and comparable post-LayerNorm latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$), but suffer $3.1\times$ larger stabilized residual-covariance shift ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ relative Frobenius norm), inflating marginal dial trip rates ($13.4\times$ vs.\ $1.5\times$ calibration rate) and quorum count variance ($4.8\times$ vs.\ $1.4\times$), tracked closely by quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Controlled healthy regime-shift stress test:} Injecting fault-free operating changes halfway through clean nominal segments on the 19 development streams ($3$ seeds) maps what breaks and what adapts. Moderate amplitude scaling ($1.5\times, 2.0\times$ on $25\%$ of channels) barely perturbs \emph{Lite} ($2.0\%$ and $3.3\%$ post-shift FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) because terminal LayerNorm absorbs scale changes, while $45^\circ$ cross-channel correlation rotation raises FPR to $6.8\%$ ($D_{\text{KS}}=0.43$). By contrast, persistent nominal mean steps ($+1\sigma, +2\sigma$), alternating duty cycles, and $1.5\sigma$ baseline walks do \emph{not} self-settle after $W=320$ steps because input standardization and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ remain anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$: steady-state FPR stays elevated for both \emph{Lite} ($24.3\%, 32.0\%, 14.6\%, 21.1\%$) and Multi-Horizon 1D ($14.0\%, 33.8\%, 10.7\%, 22.6\%$) as $D_{\text{KS}}$ rises to $0.43\text{--}0.69$. Triggering live recalibration on the first $400$ post-shift windows whenever $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ restores mean-step steady-state FPR from $24.3\%\text{--}32.0\%$ back to $4.8\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability of Coordinate and Quorum Calibration}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ median $7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams below $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)\ge 1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, giving $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Consequently, no distribution-free finite-sample tail guarantee can be claimed from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone. We retain $q_{0.995}$ ($\times 1.12$, floor $3.5$) for two empirical reasons. First, testing Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$ resamples of block length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) across all 118 untouched streams shows that `MBB-Median` yields virtually identical thresholds and false-alarm tails ($0.092$ F1, $65.2\%$ ER, $0.92\%$ median and $62.7\%$ p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ and $60.3\%$ deployed in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), `MBB-UCB90` (and binomial $N_{\text{eff}}$ upper bounds) trims p95 only to $47.7\%$ ($42\%$) while sacrificing $8\text{--}17$ ER points, and thinning $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ by $\tau_{\text{int}}$ worsens mean FPR to $10.4\%$ by starving sample support. Second, the five-step deployment protocol compensates directly for calibration uncertainty without test labels: (1)~fit weights, $\mathbf{\Omega}$, and (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; (2)~require $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ (routing shorter histories to 1D SPOT lifts pooled causal F1 to $0.095$, and two-fold cross-fitting on $T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$ cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$); (3)~audit frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; (4)~reject autonomous deployment when $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ ($50$ passing streams achieve $0.7\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $0.076$ vs.\ $0.039$ causal F1 over 1D, $p=3.5\times 10^{-4}$); and (5)~monitor live quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ to recalibrate when post-hold regime shifts arise.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Beyond proposing a specific telemetry monitor, this work establishes a general framework for decomposing multivariate predictive errors into independently calibrated, correlation-aware alarm mechanisms. Replacing scalar residual norms with per-coordinate tripwires and an empirical healthy quorum (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} as the operational 128-dial detector; 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as a diagnostic reference) yields four principal conclusions across 33 design-stage and 118 untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Coordinate isolation and live response:} Per-dial tripwires prevent a few drifting coordinates from inflating a global threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Under the five-block archive protocol, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ across all nine untouched datasets ($p=0.0039$, positive across all $12$ LODO folds) and cuts detection delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps on a compact $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$-parameter backbone ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s on one CPU thread), while doubling causal Point-F1 when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$).
    \item \emph{Label-free operating point vs.\ score ranking:} At matched test FPR the panel does not outrank scalar scoring; its advantage comes from placing the label-free quorum operating point at higher live episode coverage and faster response without test-label tuning.
    \item \emph{Calibration limits, concept drift, and deployment gating:} Although $88.1\%$ of untouched streams maintain $\le 5\%$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (median $0.93\%$), post-calibration nominal regime shifts ($D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.5$) rather than finite-sample quantile variance drive the $64.7\%$ $95$th-percentile tail. Autonomous deployment therefore requires $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, passing a held-out $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ acceptance gate, and live $D_{\text{KS}}$-triggered recalibration.
    \item \emph{Open boundaries:} Isolated single-sensor hardware failures require dedicated channel diagnostics without excessive false alarms, and slow progressive ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL) evade sliding-window prediction unless paired with a complementary frozen-reference long-horizon drift monitor.
\end{enumerate}
