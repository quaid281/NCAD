\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We investigate how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space under distribution shift, alongside an Operator-Entropy variant (OpEntropy JEPA) combining a linear Koopman transition operator, spectral entropy and shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier regularizers, coordinate saliency gating, and a multiscale dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, our primary finding is relative false-alarm suppression rather than universal detection superiority or exact attainment of the 0.1\% nominal design risk. Latent predictive scoring reduces held-out false-positive rates three- to four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (7.4\% to 10.1\% versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.1\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%), while point F1 and precision-recall ranking remain comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled ablations isolate two distinct mechanisms: LayerNorm bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. On a fixed Koopman backbone, equivalence testing confirms that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank without altering point F1 within a 0.01 to 0.03 margin, while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to unconstrained TS-JEPA. Static thresholding still overshoots its 0.1\% design target by 15- to 64-fold out of sample, driven primarily by inter-block distribution shift and serial dependence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments such as NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Operational monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains ambient sensor jitter and channel-scale disparities, observation-space residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and strong serial clustering. When paired with automated extreme-value thresholding such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), these heavy-tailed residuals suffer severe calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} To suppress false-alarm flooding without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}, we study how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space \cite{lecun2022path} under distribution shift, and formulate an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) transition architecture regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a shifted Bregman energy barrier. Rather than claiming that physics-inspired regularizers drive anomaly detection accuracy, we evaluate them strictly as algebraic conditioning priors on abstract latent coordinates. Our evaluation separates three operational questions: (i)~relative false-alarm suppression under automated calibration versus exact attainment of the nominal $0.1\%$ risk target, (ii)~discriminative detection across operating points and fault classes, and (iii)~structural score-emission latency versus empirical fault-detection delay.

\subsection{Contributions}
This study makes four focused contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Primary Mechanism Isolation (Compression vs.\ Drift):} Across 12 controlled backbone arms on all 19 non-GHL streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)), adding a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck to an observation forecaster compresses calibration kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$) and halves causal false-positive rates ($24.7\% \to 11.7\%$), while learned latent projection in TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA halves calibration-to-holdout Kolmogorov--Smirnov drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.209\text{--}0.214$) and lowers causal false alarms to $0.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$.
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Suppression vs.\ Detection Limits:} Across 33 streams from seven datasets (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), latent scoring reduces relative held-out false-alarm rates three- to four-fold under buffered attribution ($7.4\%\text{--}10.1\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$) and $8\times\text{--}20\times$ under strictly causal streaming ($1.1\%\text{--}2.8\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). Separating deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$) from post-hoc matched realized test-FPR curves ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)) shows higher low-FPR causal event recall ($0.395\text{--}0.431$ vs.\ $0.217$ at $f^*{=}0.01$), whereas Point-F1, PR-AUC, and buffered recall match reconstructors and quiet flatlines remain weakly detected ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ recall).
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy Conditioning and Equivalence Bounds:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} establishes non-triviality conditions for the Koopman transition operator under finite-$\epsilon$ and singular energy barriers. While the OpEntropy transition architecture cuts buffered detection delay from $110.3$ to $79.5$ steps ($127.0 \to 58.2$ causal), fixed-backbone Two One-Sided Tests ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ control, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)) confirm Point-F1 equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ and down to $\pm 0.010$, isolating the spectral regularizer as a conditioning constraint rather than a detection driver.
    \item \textbf{Operational Calibration Gap and Refitted Permutation Audit:} Static SPOT misses its nominal $0.1\%$ target out of sample ($1.51\%\text{--}1.95\%$ stream-macro causal and $5.54\%\text{--}6.38\%$ buffered FPR across 33 streams for JEPAs, or $15\text{--}20\times$ and $55\text{--}64\times$ overshoot vs.\ $142\text{--}222\times$ for reconstructors, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). A three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)) estimates that inter-block shift accounts for $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess causal overshoot for JEPAs ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ for reconstructors) and serial dependence for $29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Dynamical Systems, Reconstruction, and Forecasting Detectors:} Multi-sensor telemetry models system dynamics as state trajectories $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize velocity fields in raw observation space, where integrating through high-frequency sensor noise induces numerical stiffness. Koopman theory and dynamic mode decomposition \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn linear operators on observable embeddings, identifying disruptions via spectral decoherence; our proposed Operator-Entropy JEPA architecture transfers this principle to a latent transition operator regularized by spectral entropy.

Reconstructive detectors model nominal patterns via observation reconstruction: LSTM forecasting with dynamic thresholding for spacecraft telemetry \cite{Hundman2018b}, association-discrepancy attention in Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, 2D FFT-periodicity variation in TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and adversarial sub-series attention in TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}. These share a vulnerability: point-level losses bias models toward stochastic noise, accumulating residual variance across irrelevant channels in high-dimensional arrays.

\noindent\textbf{Threshold Calibration, Contrastive, and Self-Supervised Detection:} Unsupervised monitoring requires automated rules to map residual scores to binary alarms without labels. Non-parametric thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} and Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fit Generalized Pareto tails under design risk $q$; however, EVT asymptotic guarantees assume i.i.d.\ samples, which temporal autocorrelation and operational drift violate.

To avoid reconstructing noise, contrastive methods train discriminative representations: NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} injects synthetic anomalies, yielding lower calibrated point detection than predictive models ($0.058$ vs.\ $0.160$ macro Point-F1 across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} maximizes dual-attention consistency ($0.070$ macro Point-F1). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target representations in latent space with non-contrastive collapse prevention \cite{zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening, bardes2021vicreg}. Time-series extensions \cite{yue2022ts2vec} evaluate ranking or post-hoc threshold sweeps without studying how latent residuals behave under automated tail calibration.

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Priors and Conceptual Positioning:} PINNs \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian/Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} embed physical conservation laws as rigid differential constraints; their stiff competing gradients \cite{wang2021understanding} and known-PDE requirements fit poorly to uncalibrated multi-sensor telemetry. We strictly distinguish \emph{physics-inspired} algebraic regularizers (where latent coordinates are abstract learned features, not physical state variables) from \emph{physics-informed} conservation constraints; \emph{causal data access} from \emph{score-emission latency}; and \emph{operator non-degeneracy} from \emph{encoder representation diversity} and \emph{anomaly separability}.

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the UCI Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP and MSL entities, with two short-nominal MSL streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet FoG streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 GHL gasoil heating-loop streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample threshold tuning, each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Stream inclusion in the primary multi-seed protocol required: (i)~$N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows after splitting, and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed runtime. Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (6 Daphnet, $K{=}9$; 14 GHL, $K{=}16$; 4 MSL, $K{=}55$; 6 SMAP, $K{=}25$; Genesis, $K{=}18$; Metro, $K{=}5$; SWAN, $K{=}38$). The remaining 12 streams (CalIt2, $K{=}2$; two Room-Occupancy, $K{=}5$; two short-nominal MSL; GECCO, $K{=}9$; six 72-channel CICIDS flows) appear in operational incident case studies and a secondary 11-dataset, 130-stream single-seed baseline cross-check (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)); the dyadic front-end ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)) includes SMD \texttt{machine-1-2} ($K{=}38$).

Two external-validity caveats bound interpretation: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented mislabeled segments and point anomalies \cite{wu2023flawed}, so per-dataset scores represent upper bounds on operational detectability; and (ii)~because streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware and environmental characteristics, streams within a dataset are not independent domains ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report unweighted Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed SDs, leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), restricting conclusions strictly to the evaluated entities and sampling regimes.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ partitioned at each timestep $t$ into a historical context window $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and a suspect target horizon $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) train an autoencoder to minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X}))\|_F^2$, which forces the model to reconstruct high-frequency stochastic noise across all $K$ channels.

We instead adopt a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path} that shifts prediction from raw observation space into a low-dimensional latent space ($D=32$). An online context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ to a context representation $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while a momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ to a target representation $z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. The target parameters $\phi$ are updated via an exponential moving average (EMA) of the online parameters $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with cosine momentum schedule $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$, preventing representation collapse without requiring negative contrastive pairs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Predicting target embeddings in latent space filters out channel-level sensor jitter and conditions the residual distribution for automated tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We formulate Operator-Entropy JEPA as our primary transition architecture and evaluate it alongside an unconstrained MLP predictor (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and two comparative physics-inspired variants (Reynolds-Stress and Potential-Flow):

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics and Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, which represents nonlinear dynamical evolution through linear operators acting on observable embeddings, we parameterize the latent transition via a learnable linear operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ such that $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent $\mathbf{K}$ from collapsing onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or shrinking toward the zero matrix (energy collapse), we regularize its singular value spectrum while decoupling modal uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote the singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, with squared modal energies $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ and total Frobenius energy $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i$. We normalize the modal energies into a smoothed probability distribution $p_i = (\lambda_i + \epsilon_{\log}) / \sum_{j=1}^D (\lambda_j + \epsilon_{\log})$ with $\epsilon_{\log} = 10^{-12}$, and define the Von Neumann spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D p_i \log p_i$. The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} measures the Kullback--Leibler divergence of $\mathbf{p}$ from the uniform modal equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Notice that $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = 0$ if and only if all $D$ modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), whereas rank-1 collapse yields $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) \approx \log D$. Minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ drives $\mathbf{K}$ toward an well-conditioned, near-isometric transition spectrum.

To prevent total operator energy from collapsing toward zero without introducing hard gradient clipping, we impose a smooth shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier on the normalized energy ratio $r(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / D$:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log\bigl(r(\mathbf{K}) + \epsilon\bigr) + \log(1 + \epsilon) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
with shift $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. This barrier vanishes at the isometric scale $r(\mathbf{K}) = 1$ (where $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near the identity) and penalizes energy decay smoothly. Combining both terms defines the spectral operator regularizer $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K})$, and the complete training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{Lyap}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}},
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$, and $\beta_{\text{Lyap}} = 0.02$. Here $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ is a discrete Lyapunov covariance-alignment penalty that matches the trace-normalized batch covariance of predicted states $\mathbf{K}\operatorname{sg}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ to that of $z_{\text{tgt}}$, using stop-gradient $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ on $z_{\text{ctx}}$ so that $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ shape the transition operator $\mathbf{K}$ without distorting encoder representations.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Triviality Under Finite-$\epsilon$ and Singular Barriers]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Consider $\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}}, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$ and empirical context--target cross-covariance $\mathbf{M} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top$:
(i)~Under the finite-$\epsilon$ smooth barrier ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$), the regularizer gradient vanishes at the origin, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$, because $r(\mathbf{K})$ is quadratic in $\mathbf{K}$; however, whenever context and target embeddings exhibit non-zero cross-covariance ($\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$), $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$, which excludes $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ as a stationary point or minimizer.
(ii)~In the singular-barrier limit ($\epsilon \to 0^+$), $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$ unconditionally.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\epsilon > 0$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{2}{D}\Phi'_{\text{energy}}(r)\,\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$, and $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ yields uniform smoothed probabilities $p_i = 1/D$, minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H} \ge 0$ so $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$. Differentiating the prediction term gives $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$ whenever $\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$. As $\epsilon \to 0^+$, $-\log r(\mathbf{K}) \to +\infty$ as $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Scope of Regularization):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} guarantees non-degeneracy of the transition matrix $\mathbf{K}$, not diversity of the encoder representations $z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}}$. Unlike representation regularizers such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg} or Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow} that penalize encoder covariance collapse directly, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ constrains only the transition spectrum; encoder collapse is prevented empirically by the momentum target encoder and stop-gradient decoupling (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).

\subsubsection{Comparative Physical Priors (Reynolds-Stress and Potential-Flow Heads)}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
To test whether alternative continuum-mechanics priors improve anomaly detection, we evaluate two additional latent predictor heads under identical encoder backbones:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Second-Moment Closure Head):} Inspired by Reynolds decomposition, this variant augments the mean latent prediction $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ with a Cholesky factor head $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ ($d_s = 16$) that predicts the projected outer-product second moment $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}$ via $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}$. Training minimizes the sum of the latent prediction MSE and $0.1\operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}})$, and inference adds $0.5\,g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$ to the primary residual score.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA (Curl-Free Flow Matching Head):} Motivated by conservative vector fields, this variant parameterizes a curl-free velocity field $\hat{v} = -\nabla_{z_t}\Phi_\psi$ via a residual MLP conditioned on flow time $t \in [0,1]$, context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a soft router over $K_r = 4$ orthonormal subspaces. Training uses conditional flow matching along linear paths from Gaussian noise to $z_{\text{tgt}}$, and inference evaluates the velocity residual at the trajectory midpoint. Because conservative gradient fields penalize rotational trajectories, this bias is hypothesized to degrade detection on periodic telemetry (Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, sliding windows are processed at stride $1$. Let $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denote the latent prediction residual, and let $g(\mathbf{e})$ denote the coordinate saliency gate defined in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). Unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} scores windows directly via $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$. Because the final LayerNorm layer projects latent vectors onto the sphere of radius $\sqrt{D}$ and strips radial amplitude, Operator-Entropy JEPA records the pre-LayerNorm radial energy scaled by window RMS for both encoders ($e_{\text{ctx}}, e_{\text{tgt}}$), forms the predicted radial energy $e_{\text{pred}} = e_{\text{ctx}}\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}\|_2 / (\|z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2 + 10^{-6})$, and combines directional and radial discrepancies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{OpEnt}}(t) = g\bigl(\mathbf{e}(t)\bigr) + 0.2\,\frac{|e_{\text{tgt}}(t) - e_{\text{pred}}(t)|}{e_{\text{tgt}}(t) + e_{\text{pred}}(t) + 10^{-6}}.
    \label{eq:opent_score}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
To avoid confounding data access, window buffering, and alarm timing, we formalize online evaluation under three criteria (illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, the detector observes only historical data $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$; neither encoder accesses observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ encodes the $S$-step target block $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$, window score $s_t$ is emitted at the trailing edge $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3). This creates a structural score-emission latency of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO, and $63\,\text{h}$ on hourly Metro). This structural lag is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$, which measures the elapsed steps from ground-truth anomaly onset $t^*$ to the first timestamp $t_{\text{alarm}} \ge t^*$ whose attributed score exceeds threshold $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$. Shortening the horizon to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates score-emission latency when rapid tripwire response is required (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \textbf{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} Under \emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, window score $s_t$ is assigned only to the decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$ for zero-lookahead streaming. Under \emph{retrospective buffered smear attribution}, $s_t$ is averaged across all steps in $[t+1, t+S]$, which introduces fixed-lag smoothing and can smear post-onset scores up to $S$ steps before $t^*$. Both regimes are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults affecting a single subsystem can be diluted by nominal variance across the remaining coordinates. To amplify sparse latent deviations, we apply a softmax coordinate saliency gate $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e})$ to the residual vector $\mathbf{e} = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) = (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \quad
    w_d = \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau_g)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau_g)},
    \label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with fixed blend $\alpha = 0.5$ and temperature $\tau_g = 0.5$ across all latent models. Input channels are encoded with uniform weights in the deployed pipeline; an auxiliary context-variance input-channel gate is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}.

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Before the dilated TCN backbone, each input channel is split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic frequency shells on a $256$-point rFFT grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ to within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$). Each shell is processed by a causal 1D convolution with kernel sizes $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$ from coarse to fine scales and recombined via learned softmax routing. Because smooth Fourier shells overlap at band edges, this module acts as a multiscale frequency front-end rather than a strictly orthogonal wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert continuous scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ using nominal calibration data alone, we apply Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}. Score excesses above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ are fit to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with shape $\xi$ and scale $\sigma$ via Grimshaw maximum likelihood, yielding the threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ is the nominal design risk, $N_{\text{cal}}$ is the number of calibration windows, and $N_u$ is the exceedance count above $u$. All primary benchmarks use standard unadjusted SPOT ($\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$). In Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}, we also audit two heuristic kurtosis adjustments: total-threshold scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau_{\text{SPOT}}[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ and excess-only scaling $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau_{\text{SPOT}} - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline using a past context window of $C=256$ steps and a suspect target horizon of $S=64$ steps (total window length $W = C + S = 320$), evaluated at stride $1$. Context and target representations ($D=32$) are extracted by the multiscale frequency front-end followed by a 6-block causal dilated TCN (48 filters, kernel size $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$). Multi-statistic temporal pooling (terminal, mean, max, and channel statistics) projects features through a linear bottleneck before LayerNorm and dropout ($p=0.20$). Models are trained for $20$ epochs on nominal sequences using AdamW (initial learning rate $10^{-3}$ decayed to $10^{-5}$ via cosine annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size $32$).

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$. Standardization parameters and neural weights are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$.

Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2). Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations serve as context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}})$. Splitting $v$ at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H)$ (used to fit the SPOT threshold at $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, excluding $S=64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S)$. Because suspect target windows have length $S=64$, the last calibration target and first evaluation target are $100\%$ disjoint; the first $C=256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. A stricter full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320)$ so even passive context is disjoint across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) leaves stream-macro causal FPR virtually unchanged ($2.07\% \to 2.18\%$ for OpEntropy and $3.02\% \to 3.24\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $12.15\% \to 13.97\%$ for TimesNet and $9.05\% \to 9.73\%$ for TranAD). All primary tables use fixed standard SPOT thresholds during testing.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication and Equivalence Testing}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are evaluated across five random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ observations each) and ablation controls use three seeds. Seeds are averaged within each stream before computing stream-resampled paired bootstrap confidence intervals ($2{,}000$ iterations) and Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$).

To test whether physics-inspired regularizers alter detection relative to unregularized controls, we pair two-sided tests with Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) of equivalence: $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$), with overall $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}})$ and $90\%$ confidence intervals. We prespecify $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ Point-F1 for fixed-backbone regularizer controls ($\approx 13\%$ of baseline F1, $<0.09\times$ between-stream SD) and $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ for cross-architecture comparisons ($\approx 25\%$ of baseline F1, corresponding at prevalence $\pi \approx 0.15$ to an event-recall shift of $\le 0.04$ or $15\text{--}20$ false alarms per $1{,}000$ nominal steps), and additionally evaluate narrow-margin TOST sensitivity down to $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.010, \pm 0.015, \pm 0.020\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)). Formal equivalence requires both $p_{\text{TOST}} < 0.05$ and the $90\%$ CI strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures, Fairness Checks, and Cohort Structure}
We separate primary multi-seed conclusions (Cohorts 1--2 across 12 neural, linear, and non-parametric detectors) from secondary single-seed cross-checks. Three fairness audits verify that reconstructive baseline results are not artifacts of window length or training budget:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Training-budget sensitivity} ($7$ streams $\times 3$ seeds): Doubling training from $10$ to $20$ epochs slightly improves JEPA Point-F1 ($0.038 \to 0.048$ for TS-JEPA; $0.029 \to 0.065$ for OpEntropy) while reconstructor Point-F1 plateaus ($0.154 \to 0.151$ for TimesNet; $0.161 \to 0.158$ for TranAD) and held-out FPR remains unchanged ($8.7\%\text{--}9.2\%$ vs.\ $33.3\%$).
    \item \textbf{Native published window lengths} ($5$ streams): Evaluating TranAD ($W=10$) and TimesNet ($W=96$) at their published window sizes raises Point-F1 ($0.164$ and $0.218$), but observation residuals remain severely leptokurtic ($\gamma_2 = 448.3$ and $98.1$ vs.\ $3.1$ for TS-JEPA) with high nominal FPR ($28.4\%$ and $44.1\%$).
    \item \textbf{Secondary 130-stream single-seed cross-check:} Reconstructors replicate their published point-adjusted F1 on SMD and MSL, and Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) provides a secondary 130-stream, 11-dataset single-seed comparison against NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cohort Summary and Reproducibility Specifications}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Experiments are organized into five non-overlapping cohorts:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Cohort 1 (33-stream primary multi-seed benchmark):} 19 non-GHL streams (5 seeds) and 14 GHL streams (3 seeds), reported in Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)--(c) and \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 2 (19-stream mechanism and operating-curve suite):} 12 controlled backbone arms across 5 seeds, reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(e), Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b), and Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}.
    \item \textbf{Cohort 3 (6-stream operator sweep and quiet-fault suite):} Factorial regularizer sweep (3 seeds) and six injected single-channel fault types, reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c),(d).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 4 (5-stream horizon and frequency-shell panels):} Target-horizon latency sweep ($S \in \{1,4,16,32,64\}$, 3 seeds) and dyadic front-end ablation, reported in Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 5 (130-stream single-seed baseline screen):} Secondary cross-check across 11 datasets against NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).
\end{itemize}
At $K=25$ channels, model parameter counts are comparable across families: $285{,}701$ (OpEntropy JEPA), $289{,}061$ (TS-JEPA), $296{,}037$ (Reynolds-Stress), $338{,}209$ (Potential-Flow), $648{,}553$ (TimesNet), and $205{,}746$ (TranAD). Non-finite telemetry entries are zero-imputed with channel standard deviations floored at $10^{-2}$; SPOT fits GPD parameters $(\xi, \sigma)$ above $u=p_{98}$ via Grimshaw MLE with L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10], \sigma \ge 10^{-6}$). All 195 primary multi-seed runs converged on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (NVIDIA RTX 5090, $0.60\text{--}0.84\,\text{ms}$ per window, $184\,\text{MB}$ VRAM); stream manifests, fitted thresholds, and source code are available at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate models across three complementary tiers: (i)~\emph{primary calibration and accuracy endpoints}, comprising held-out nominal FPR, unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC; (ii)~\emph{operating characteristics}, evaluating event recall across deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)) and across post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in [0.01, 0.50]$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)); and (iii)~\emph{secondary operational endpoints}, reporting unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating), event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at anomaly prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether physics-inspired regularizers drive anomaly detection accuracy, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) disables each physical penalty on its fixed architecture across all 19 non-GHL streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs.\ $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ (fixed Koopman backbone):} Removing the spectral regularizer leaves buffered Point-F1 ($0.232$ vs.\ $0.235$, paired difference $-0.004$, two-sided $p=0.89$) and PR-AUC ($0.365$ vs.\ $0.365$) virtually identical. Two One-Sided Tests confirm formal equivalence within the prespecified $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ margin ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0007$) and across narrow-margin sensitivity checks down to $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0015$), $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$, with $90\%$ CI $[-0.0112, +0.0029]$ strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$), and $\Delta = \pm 0.010$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0088$), as well as within $\pm 0.010$ PR-AUC and $\pm 0.025$ event recall.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs.\ $\alpha_{\text{stress}}{=}0$ (fixed backbone):} Disabling the second-moment loss yields a $+0.013$ Point-F1 shift (two-sided $p=0.59$), confirming TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$) and down to $\Delta = \pm 0.035$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0031$).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs.\ no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling multi-regime routing shifts Point-F1 by $-0.054$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.17$), failing equivalence at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$.
\end{enumerate}
These fixed-backbone regularizer controls must be distinguished from cross-architecture comparisons against unconstrained TS-JEPA (which uses an MLP predictor without radial energy scoring or Lyapunov covariance alignment). On the 19-stream subset, the $+0.028$ Point-F1 difference between OpEntropy JEPA and TS-JEPA ($90\%$ CI $[-0.018, +0.074]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.11$) is statistically inconclusive at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$, whereas across all 33 primary streams OpEntropy JEPA ($+0.016$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.009, +0.045]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$) and Reynolds-Stress ($-0.008$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0017$) satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ (with OpEntropy vs.\ TS-JEPA also satisfying $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.030)$ at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0077$ and $\pm 0.015$ causal FPR at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0001$).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Factorial Isolation of the Entropy Term and Energy Barrier}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) crosses the spectral-entropy term and Bregman energy barrier across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in [0, 2.0]$ on six streams (three seeds). Three findings emerge. First, because the Koopman matrix $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near the identity, the unregularized operator is already near full rank ($\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}) = 31.53$ of $32$, condition number $\kappa = 1.437$); increasing $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$ tightens conditioning monotonically ($\kappa = 1.255$ at the deployed $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0.50$, and $\kappa = 1.154$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=2.00$) while lowering training loss variation ($\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}: 0.565 \to 0.545$). Second, because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ applies a stop-gradient to $z_{\text{ctx}}$, encoder effective ranks remain invariant across all regularizer strengths ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}}) \in [5.16, 5.54]$, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) \in [11.54, 11.58]$). Third, detection and false-alarm rates remain stable across arms, with the deployed setting ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0.50, \beta_{\text{norm}}=1$) attaining the highest causal event recall ($0.444$ vs.\ $0.369$ unregularized). On an eight-stream decoupling panel, pairing detached Lyapunov alignment ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0.02$) with the smooth Bregman barrier cuts causal detection delay from $127.0$ steps (TS-JEPA) and $80.2$ steps ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0$) to $58.2$ steps while raising causal Point-F1 from $0.044$ to $0.094$.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating and Quiet-Fault Monitors}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Comparing the auxiliary context-variance input-channel gate against uniform channel weighting on four streams confirms that uniform weighting achieves higher macro Point-F1 ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$), so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. In Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d), injecting six single-channel fault types into six multivariate streams shows that quiet faults (flatlines, stuck sensors, dropouts) yield low point recall ($0.02\text{--}0.07$) across all latent gating variants because LayerNorm embeddings absorb constant single-channel offsets as nominal background. Auxiliary observation-space monitors, namely a rolling variance floor (\texttt{lowvar}) and maximum channel $z$-score (\texttt{dev\_maxz}), recover quiet-fault recall ($0.18\text{--}0.32$) at the cost of inflating nominal FPR eight-fold ($14.1\%\text{--}14.9\%$ vs.\ $1.9\%$).

\subsection{Ablation 4: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
In Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), replacing the standard convolutional stem with the 4-shell Fourier front-end increases macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers calibration residual kurtosis ($1.409 \to 1.069$), while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to conservative threshold tightening on SMAP \texttt{P-3}. The module thus acts as a ranking-conditioning stem rather than a uniform F1 gain.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 12 controlled backbone arms across all 19 non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e) and Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a) separates geometric score compression from representation drift reduction:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Unnormalized observation forecasting (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred}):} Raw observation residuals exhibit heavy tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 6.10$), severe calibration-to-holdout KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$), and high causal false alarms ($24.7\%$).
    \item \textbf{LayerNorm observation bottleneck (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}):} Inserting a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck before decoding mechanically bounds excursions, compressing kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halving causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but leaves calibration-to-holdout drift elevated ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$).
    \item \textbf{Learned latent projection (\texttt{TS-JEPA}, \texttt{OpEntropy}):} Forecasting in learned latent coordinates halves calibration-to-holdout KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ and $0.209$, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$) and reduces causal false alarms an additional five- to eight-fold ($1.7\%\text{--}2.8\%$).
    \item \textbf{Context autoencoding (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}) and linear controls:} Autoencoding context embeddings without temporal foresight achieves comparable drift reduction ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, causal FPR $1.7\%$, event recall $0.542$), while linear projection (\texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN}) reaches $2.1\%$ causal FPR at lower F1 ($0.017$) and \texttt{IsoForest} collapses in recall ($0.149$). LayerNorm geometry thus bounds tail kurtosis, while learned latent projection is required to suppress temporal distribution drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} summarizes performance across all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT calibration protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$), reporting retrospective buffered attribution in Panel~(a), strictly causal streaming in Panel~(b), and per-dataset breakdowns in Panel~(c). The central empirical finding is substantial relative false-alarm suppression under automated thresholding, whereas overall Point-F1 and PR-AUC differences against reconstructive baselines are statistically inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Relative False-Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent predictive scoring via TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA lowers held-out nominal FPR three- to four-fold under buffered attribution ($9.3\%$ and $10.1\%$ versus $30.0\%$ for TimesNet and $30.7\%$ for TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming ($2.4\%$ and $2.8\%$ versus $24.3\%$ and $22.2\%$). Across all 33 streams, the paired buffered FPR reduction is statistically significant for both OpEntropy JEPA ($-14.4\%$ vs.\ TimesNet, $95\%$ CI $[-24.1\%, -6.5\%]$, $p=0.006$; $-14.2\%$ vs.\ TranAD, $p=0.006$) and TS-JEPA ($-15.3\%$, $p=0.002$; $-15.1\%$, $p=0.003$). Dataset-clustered permutation tests across the $N=7$ domain families remain significant ($p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$), and leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) folds hold macro buffered FPR to $3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ for JEPAs versus $8.4\%\text{--}18.3\%$ for reconstructors. On MSL, TimesNet achieves a lower buffered FPR than OpEntropy ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $17.7\%$), while on Genesis, Metro, and SWAN reconstructors emit zero alarms and achieve zero F1 due to overly high calibration thresholds.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1, Event Recall, and Ranking Parity:} Improved false-alarm control does not translate into universal detection superiority. Across the 19 non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a), buffered Point-F1 and event recall for TS-JEPA ($0.206$, recall $0.477$) and OpEntropy JEPA ($0.233$, recall $0.550$) are comparable to TimesNet ($0.197$, recall $0.583$) and TranAD ($0.130$, recall $0.490$). Across all 33 streams, paired differences against TimesNet ($+0.012$ Point-F1, $p=0.54$; $-0.000$ PR-AUC, $p=0.39$) and TranAD ($+0.051$ Point-F1, $p=0.88$; $+0.023$ PR-AUC, $p=0.16$) are statistically indistinguishable from zero. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c), between-stream standard deviations within each dataset are comparable to the means; only SMAP exceeds the trivial constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.396$), although threshold-free PR-AUC exceeds base prevalence $\pi$ on both SMAP ($0.504\text{--}0.525$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and MSL ($0.422\text{--}0.434$ vs.\ $\pi=0.255$).

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained regime:} Low-rate telemetry ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ spacecraft or hourly Metro traffic) where a $63$-step score-emission lag at $S=64$ ($10.5\,\text{min}$ to $63\,\text{h}$) is prohibitive for immediate tripwires and requires short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Flatlines, stuck sensors, and dropouts ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ point recall, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d)), which LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background and which require auxiliary variance monitors at higher alarm cost ($14\%\text{--}15\%$ FPR).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Conditioning vs.\ Detection}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
What does the Operator-Entropy formulation contribute beyond unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? Isolating the spectral regularizer on the fixed Koopman backbone ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$ control, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)) confirms strict TOST equivalence down to $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ and $\pm 0.010$ Point-F1 (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). However, the OpEntropy transition architecture (combining the linear Koopman operator $\mathbf{K}$, detached Lyapunov covariance alignment, and pre-LayerNorm relative radial energy scoring) shortens mean buffered detection delay from $110.3$ steps in TS-JEPA to $79.5$ steps (and from $124.0$ to $112.5$ steps causally in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b), or $127.0 \to 58.2$ steps on the eight-stream decoupling panel). Cross-architecture Point-F1 differences against TS-JEPA satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ across all 33 streams for both OpEntropy ($+0.016$, $p_{\text{TOST}}=0.0019$) and Reynolds-Stress ($-0.008$, $p_{\text{TOST}}=0.0017$), whereas Potential-Flow degrades Point-F1 ($-0.081$, $p=0.033$ unadjusted) because its curl-free gradient parameterization penalizes periodic nominal orbits. The spectral-entropy and Reynolds priors therefore act as well-posed conditioning constraints rather than detection drivers.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Strictly causal trailing attribution represents real-time streaming deployment, whereas buffered attribution represents retrospective fixed-lag analysis. Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} highlights a clear operational trade-off. Eliminating the $64$-step lookahead buffer lowers nominal false alarms from $9.3\%\text{--}10.1\%$ to $2.4\%\text{--}2.8\%$ for TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA (and $1.1\%$ for Reynolds-Stress), compared to $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for TranAD and TimesNet. Without retrospective smoothing, causal Point-F1 ($0.153\text{--}0.161$ vs.\ $0.209$ for TimesNet) and causal event recall ($0.487\text{--}0.517$ vs.\ $0.588$) are more conservative, and mean onset-to-alarm delay increases ($\Delta t = 112.5\text{--}124.0$ steps vs.\ $62.2$ steps for TimesNet). In online streaming without lookahead buffers, latent predictive scoring thus functions as a conservative high-precision filter that trades raw onset speed for an $8\text{--}20\times$ reduction in false alarms. On five non-stationary incident streams, pairing latent scores with dynamic rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms by up to $21\times$ under regime drift.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
At target horizon $S=64$, window scores emit $S-1=63$ steps after window opening, which is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$ measured after anomaly onset (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3). To evaluate whether shorter horizons eliminate emission latency without sacrificing false-alarm control, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero emission lag), OpEntropy JEPA holds causal FPR to $0.5\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving $0.729$ event recall and a detection delay of $\Delta t = 76.3$ steps. As horizon $S$ increases from $1$ to $64$, causal FPR for TimesNet rises nearly nine-fold ($2.5\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas OpEntropy and TS-JEPA remain bounded within $0.3\%\text{--}2.0\%$. Time-critical monitoring applications can therefore operate at $S \in \{1, 4\}$ to eliminate score-emission latency while preserving relative false-alarm suppression.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstructive baselines calibrate to a much higher realized false-alarm rate ($22\%\text{--}31\%$) than latent predictors ($1\%\text{--}10\%$), raw recall at a single SPOT threshold confounds discriminative sensitivity with threshold placement. Indeed, when anomaly segments span hundreds to over a thousand timesteps (mean length $402$ steps in Daphnet and $1{,}102$ steps in SMAP), a detector alarming randomly at $30\%$ FPR intersects $>95\%$ of segments by chance alone ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^L$). We therefore evaluate two complementary operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)):} Thresholds are set at fixed empirical quantiles of the nominal calibration block without test labels. At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$, TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.752$) and higher precision ($0.251$ and $0.259$) at fewer false alarms ($177\text{--}203$ per $1{,}000$ nominal steps) than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall, $262$ alarms) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall, $270$ alarms).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR curves ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)):} Oracle thresholds are chosen to equalize realized test nominal FPR across models. At a stringent $1\%$ realized causal false-alarm budget ($f^* = 0.01$), latent models nearly double event recall ($0.371\text{--}0.431$) compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$). At relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$ causal and across buffered mappings), recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$ over $f^* \in [0.10, 0.50]$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-false-alarm operating regime ($f^* \le 0.01$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Does static extreme-value thresholding achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample? It does not. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro (cell-weighted) causal FPR of $1.51\%$ ($1.71\%$) for TS-JEPA and $1.95\%$ ($2.12\%$) for OpEntropy, which is roughly $15\text{--}20\times$ the $0.1\%$ design target, and buffered stream-macro (cell-weighted) FPR of $5.54\%$ ($6.40\%$) and $6.38\%$ ($7.25\%$), or $55\text{--}64\times$ ($64\text{--}73\times$) the design target. Although this is an order-of-magnitude improvement over TimesNet ($16.11\%$ causal, $19.85\%$ buffered) and TranAD ($14.23\%$ causal, $19.63\%$ buffered; $142\text{--}222\times$ overshoot), a $1.5\%\text{--}6.4\%$ nuisance-alarm rate still falls short of strict $0.1\%$ operational targets. Closing this remaining $15\text{--}64\times$ calibration gap requires pairing latent residuals with (i)~block-declustered GPD fitting (replacing raw exceedances $N_u$ with cluster maxima scaled by the Ferro--Segers extremal index $\hat{\theta}$), (ii)~online conformal or rolling quantile updates gated by prediction-error variance under discrete regime shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), or (iii)~state-conditioned threshold tables indexed by telemetry operating mode. Three diagnostics in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} explain the static overshoot:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability and quantile/length sensitivity:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ numerical solver fallback and low bootstrap threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in only $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR unchanged ($2.41\%\text{--}2.42\%$ for TS-JEPA), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) raises causal FPR to $3.72\%$ (TS-JEPA) and $4.13\%$ (OpEntropy).
    \item \textbf{Platykurtic collapse under total-threshold kurtosis scaling:} While total-threshold kurtosis scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ reduces buffered FPR on leptokurtic cells ($\gamma_2 \ge 0$, $4.49\% \to 1.11\%$ for TS-JEPA), $64$-step buffered smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of TS-JEPA cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$) with tight relative dispersion ($\sigma_{\text{cal}}/\mu_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.02\text{--}0.05$). Scaling total $\tau$ toward zero pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below the $u=p_{98}$ floor in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating overall buffered FPR from $6.40\%$ to $16.30\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Scaling strictly the tail excess above $u$, $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$, prevents threshold collapse below $u$ and holds 19-stream buffered FPR to $9.19\%$ vs.\ $9.29\%$ unadjusted ($2.40\%$ vs.\ $2.42\%$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)):} Simply permuting held-out evaluation scores against a fixed threshold cannot change the exceedance rate. Instead, our three-stage protocol pools the nominal calibration and evaluation vectors $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{cal}} + N_{\text{hold}}}$ and \emph{refits} the SPOT threshold ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) from scratch on each permuted calibration split: Stage~1 (chronological) fits $\tau_{\text{chrono}}$ on $v_{\text{cal}}$ and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}}$ on $v_{\text{hold}}$; Stage~2 ($L=64$ block permutation) shuffles contiguous $64$-step blocks across $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}}$, splits into permuted calibration ($N_{\text{cal}}$) and evaluation ($N_{\text{hold}}$) halves, \emph{refits} $\tau_{\text{block}}$, and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$ (equalizing macro-block distributions while preserving intra-block autocorrelation); and Stage~3 (pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation) uniformly shuffles all points in $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}}$, splits into calibration and evaluation halves, \emph{refits} $\tau_{\text{point}}$, and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$. Across the $18$ streams with $N_{\text{cal}}, N_{\text{hold}} \ge 100$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)), inter-block distribution shift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$) accounts for an estimated $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess causal overshoot for JEPAs ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ for reconstructors), and serial dependence ($\text{FPR}_{\text{block}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$) for $29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$. Because latent predictors achieve $\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} = 0$ on 11 of 18 streams (concentrating stream-level variance onto the seven drifting streams so the TS-JEPA and OpEntropy inter-block $95\%$ CIs in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c) span zero), these percentages represent estimated shares of aggregate excess risk rather than invariant per-stream causal proportions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a chronological held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Mechanism Isolation and Regularizer Equivalence:} Comparing 12 controlled backbones decouples score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks bound residual kurtosis and halve false alarms, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.209\text{--}0.214$) and reduce causal false-positive rates to $0.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$. On a fixed Koopman backbone, the spectral-entropy regularizer satisfies TOST equivalence down to $\Delta = \pm 0.010$ Point-F1, functioning as an operator conditioning constraint while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to unconstrained TS-JEPA ($110.3 \to 79.5$ steps buffered; $127.0 \to 58.2$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Control vs.\ Detection Parity:} Latent predictive scoring lowers held-out false alarms three- to four-fold under buffered attribution and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming, and nearly doubles causal event recall at stringent realized false-alarm budgets ($f^* = 0.01$), while overall Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain comparable to observation-space reconstructors.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Drift and Operational Boundaries:} Static SPOT still overshoots its nominal $0.1\%$ design risk by $15\text{--}64\times$ out of sample, driven primarily by inter-block distribution shift ($56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess risk) and serial dependence ($29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$). Future work should pair latent predictive residuals with declustered GPD fitting, online conformal quantile updates, and hybrid variance monitors to close the remaining calibration gap and cover quiet single-channel flatlines.
\end{enumerate}
