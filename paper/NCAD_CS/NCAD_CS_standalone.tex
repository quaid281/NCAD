\begin{abstract}
Automated anomaly thresholds can generate excessive alarms when telemetry scores change between calibration and deployment. We compare latent predictive, observation-space, and simple bounded scores under chronological held-out calibration on 33 streams from seven datasets; a 19-stream non-GHL cohort supports the five-seed controls. On that cohort, the latent predictor has lower nominal false-positive rates than two observation-space reconstruction baselines. Still, a bounded, dimensionality-matched principal-component control and a bounded observation-space TCN control also produce light residual tails. The TCN control, nevertheless, has higher calibration false-positive rates, indicating that tail shape and threshold transfer are distinct. Causal event-recall curves suggest a different ordering when plotted against realized false-positive rate, but paired interpolation on a common observed range covers few streams and does not establish an aggregate advantage; buffered attribution is analyzed separately. Point-F1 and precision-recall differences remain imprecise, not equivalent. Static peaks-over-threshold calibration substantially overshoots its target risk under this short-block protocol. An exchangeable permutation control demonstrates that inter-block distribution drift accounts for 84\% to 96\% of this overshoot, while sample scarcity and autocorrelation are secondary. Three physics-inspired variants show no reliable aggregate detection gain. These findings emphasize the use of matched-score controls, the reporting of realized alarm rates, and the limits of what nominal-budget curves can establish.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments --- NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, wearable biometric arrays --- dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs.

\subsection{Challenges in Telemetry Threshold Calibration}
Multi-sensor telemetry monitoring requires automated decision rules that reliably detect physical faults while controlling false alarms. Setting alarm thresholds on continuous telemetry error streams encounters two fundamental difficulties: non-stationary distribution drift between nominal calibration periods and live deployment, and differences in scoring geometry across model families. Reconstructive models score unconstrained observation-space errors, whereas latent predictive architectures and bounded controls produce bounded residuals with lighter tails.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detection Benchmarks}
Comparing detectors at a single nominal threshold probability (such as design risk $q = 10^{-3}$) conflates scoring scale with discriminative detection quality. Retrospective evaluation protocols that paint alarms over multi-step temporal buffers mechanically inflate per-timestep false-alarm rates, confounding operational evaluation. Standard benchmarks often fail to isolate whether low alarm rates stem from predictive dynamics, bounded score geometry, or representation conservatism.

\subsection{Investigating Latent Prediction and Score Conditioning}
We evaluate score conditioning, discriminative detection, and threshold transfer under strict chronological partitions. A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) forecasts target representations from context representations; observation-space models, a matched-backbone control, a LayerNorm-bounded observation-space TCN, and a dimensionality-matched PCA forecast test competing structural explanations. We also evaluate spectral entropy, latent second moment, and potential-flow variants as physics-inspired controls.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Disentangling Alarm Rates and Score Quality:} On 19 non-GHL streams, nominal held-out FPR is $7.6\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $30\%$ for TimesNet, but bounded PCA and TCN controls also yield light residual tails, and the TCN does not preserve low calibration FPR. Larger JEPA event-recall areas over nominal budgets under causal attribution do not establish dominance at matched realized FPR: few streams share realized-FPR support, and paired contrasts are inconclusive. Buffered and causal results are reported separately.
    \item \textbf{Null Results for Physics-Inspired Variants:} Across the 33-stream SPOT cohort, the tested regularizers provide no reliable aggregate detection gain, passing formal two-one-sided equivalence tests within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 ($p \le 0.0054$).
    \item \textbf{Resolving Calibration Failure via Exchangeable Controls:} Static POT thresholds systematically overshoot target risk under chronological deployment ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at $q{=}10^{-3}$). An exchangeable permutation control proves that inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of nominal threshold overshoot, while sample scarcity and autocorrelation are secondary.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark:} The SPOT benchmark spans 33 streams across seven datasets (19 non-GHL at five seeds; 14 GHL at one to three seeds). All experimental logs, stream manifests, and replication scripts are archived in the project repository.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Dynamical Systems, Reconstruction, and Forecasting Detectors}
Multi-sensor telemetry models system dynamics as state trajectories $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize velocity fields in raw observation space, where integrating through high-frequency sensor noise induces numerical stiffness. Koopman theory and dynamic mode decomposition \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn linear operators on observable embeddings, identifying disruptions via spectral decoherence; our Operator-Entropy variant transfers this intuition to a latent transition operator regularized by spectral entropy.

Reconstructive detectors model nominal patterns via observation reconstruction: LSTM forecasting with dynamic thresholding for spacecraft telemetry \cite{Hundman2018b}, association-discrepancy attention in Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, 2D FFT-periodicity variation in TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and adversarial sub-series attention in TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}. These share a vulnerability: point-level losses bias models toward stochastic noise, accumulating residual variance across irrelevant channels in high-dimensional arrays.

\subsection{Threshold Calibration, Contrastive, and Self-Supervised Detection}
Unsupervised monitoring requires automated rules to map residual scores to binary alarms without labels. Non-parametric thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} and Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fit Generalized Pareto tails under design risk $q$; however, EVT asymptotic guarantees assume i.i.d.\ samples, which temporal autocorrelation and operational drift violate.

To avoid reconstructing noise, contrastive methods train discriminative representations: NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} injects synthetic anomalies, yielding lower calibrated detection than predictive models ($0.058$ vs.\ $0.160$ macro Point-F1, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:sota_baselines}). DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} maximizes dual-attention consistency. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target representations in latent space with non-contrastive collapse prevention \cite{zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening, bardes2021vicreg}. Time-series extensions \cite{yue2022ts2vec} evaluate ranking or post-hoc threshold sweeps without studying how latent residuals behave under automated tail calibration.

\subsection{Physics-Inspired Priors and Conceptual Positioning}
PINNs \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian/Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} embed dynamics as rigid constraints; their stiff competing gradients \cite{wang2021understanding} and PDE requirements fit poorly to uncalibrated telemetry. We distinguish \emph{physics-inspired} structures (exploratory regularizers; latent coordinates are not physical state variables) from \emph{physics-informed} constraints; \emph{causal preprocessing} from \emph{causal decision-time access}; and operator non-degeneracy from representation collapse. This work evaluates three regularizers against unconstrained latent controls under held-out nominal calibration to quantify the gap between design risk and realized false alarms.

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the UCI Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP Earth-observation \texttt{A-1}/\texttt{A-2}/\texttt{A-7}/\texttt{D-3}/\texttt{P-3}/\texttt{E-1} power, thermal, and attitude subsystems; MSL rover \texttt{C-1}/\texttt{C-2}/\texttt{D-14}/\texttt{D-15}/\texttt{D-16}/\texttt{M-1} actuator and environmental channels, with two short-nominal streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet FoG streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 GHL gasoil heating-loop streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample calibration, each stream's nominal sequence is partitioned chronologically into training (65\%), calibration (17.5\%), and held-out evaluation (17.5\%) blocks separated by an isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). The held-out protocol spans 33 streams across seven datasets (6 Daphnet, 14 GHL, 4 MSL, 6 SMAP, Genesis, Metro, SWAN); the remaining 12 streams are reserved as operational case studies --- CalIt2, two Room-Occupancy, two short-nominal MSL, GECCO, and six CICIDS --- because their nominal blocks are too short for disjoint partitions ($N_{\text{cal}} < 500$) or, for the 80-channel CICIDS network flows, high-dimensional stride-1 evaluation across multi-seed runs was computationally prohibitive compared to lower-dimensional physical telemetry. The dyadic front-end ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b) uses a separate five-stream panel that additionally draws one SMD channel outside this manifest.

Two caveats bound interpretation. SMAP and MSL contain documented mislabeled segments and near-trivial anomalies \cite{wu2023flawed}; their per-dataset numbers are upper bounds on detectability. Entity coverage is also incomplete --- 6 of 55 SMAP and 4 of 27 MSL channels, selected by nominal-block length and stride-1 runtime constraints, not labels --- so claims apply to the evaluated entities, not the full archives.

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dataset_specs} and the benchmark stream manifest enumerate the suite: $2{,}079{,}507$ nominal training and $2{,}986{,}574$ test timesteps ($>$5.06M observations across 33 primary and 12 case-study streams), recording channel dimensions, contamination ratios, anomaly typologies, split lengths, and evaluated seeds. All counts in this paper reconcile against this manifest.

\section{Methodology: Latent Representation Forecasting and Structured Inductive Priors}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a multivariate stream partitioned into context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize pointwise error $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}\| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2$, which can weight channels with different noise scales unequally; whether this consumes model capacity is not tested here. We instead adopt the JEPA framework \cite{lecun2022path}: a context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an EMA momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}}$, with $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$, $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$. The objective forecasts target representations in latent space and tests whether structured priors condition prediction residuals for tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
To examine whether structured dynamical inductive biases stabilize representation forecasting, we evaluate an unconstrained latent predictor alongside three candidate formulations inspired by physical dynamical principles:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, where nonlinear trajectories are mapped via linear operators on observable functions, we parameterize the predictive latent transition via a learned operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent collapse onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or to zero (energy collapse), the regularizer decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ eigenvalues of $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^\top$, and $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ total energy. For $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) > 0$, the scale-free modal distribution is $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2$. The Von Neumann spectral entropy is $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i: p_i > 0} p_i \log p_i$ (with $0 \log 0 = 0$). The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} measures Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while dimensional collapse to rank 1 yields $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \log D$. Minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ drives $\mathbf{K}$ toward an isometric modal equipartition rather than low-rank dominant modal truncation as in classical Koopman decompositions, acting directly on learned embeddings without infinite-dimensional lifting.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) without explosive gain, we impose a non-negative Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) = \max(\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / (\gamma D), \epsilon)$ with numerical floor $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. The floored barrier caps penalty at $\approx 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$ rather than diverging. With $\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$, $r_{\text{safe}} \approx 1$ and training begins with vanishing barrier penalty. The composite regularizer and objective are:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$ and $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$. All shared representation components are held fixed across models and controls. $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts on the operator matrix; it does not analytically prevent encoder representation collapse, which is mitigated empirically by EMA target dynamics and stop-gradient operations \cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Triviality and Barrier Behavior]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$, and non-degenerate context--target coupling $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$. Then: (i) under the floored barrier ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$), the energy penalty is bounded ($\le 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$) yet $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is strictly not a local or global minimizer; and (ii) under unfloored idealization ($\epsilon \to 0^+$), $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.)

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Operator Non-Degeneracy vs. Representation Collapse):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} applies to the transition operator $\mathbf{K}$ and does not analytically prevent encoder collapse: if $E_\theta$ maps all inputs to a constant, $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ attains zero loss. Preventing representation collapse rests empirically on stop-gradient momentum dynamics.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary Quadratic Second-Moment Head}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Inspired by Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence, which separates mean flow from velocity fluctuations, we evaluate an auxiliary quadratic second-moment head in latent space. The observed matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$ is an instantaneous rank-1 projected outer product plus ridge penalty, not a physical turbulent fluctuation covariance.

A two-layer MLP predicts mean target latent $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and linear projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$ ($d_s = 16$) yields $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}$. The closure head $h_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})$ outputs lower-triangular $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ with positive diagonal $L_{ii} = \operatorname{softplus}(a_{ii}) + \epsilon$, reconstructing predicted covariance:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}.
\end{equation}
The training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}).
\end{equation}
At test time, the anomaly score adds the second-moment error: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. Downstream performance is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a).

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory and Flow Matching)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, a smooth field splits into a curl-free potential gradient and a solenoidal component: $v = -\nabla \Phi + \nabla \times A$. Dissipative gradient flows provide theoretical motivation:

\begin{proposition}[Idealized Continuous Gradient Flow Motivation (Non-Binding)]
\label{prop:no_limit_cycles}
Let $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$ be an autonomous gradient flow on $\mathbb{R}^D$ with $\Phi \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R})$. There exist no non-constant periodic orbits.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.)

\paragraph{Velocity Field, Multi-Subspace Routing, and Flow Matching}
The continuous field is $\hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi$, where $\Phi_\psi$ is a three-layer residual MLP conditioned on state $z_t$, diffusion time $t \in [0,1]$, context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and regime projection $p_{\text{regime}}$, with tangent projection $v_\perp = v - \langle u, v\rangle u$ ($u = z_t/\|z_t\|_2$). Operational regimes use $K_r = 4$ orthonormal subspace projections $\mathbf{U}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d}$ ($d{=}8, D{=}32$), selected by maximal captured context energy $p_{\text{regime}} = \mathbf{U}_{k^*} \mathbf{U}_{k^*}^\top z_{\text{ctx}}$ with separation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{subspace}} = \frac{2}{K_r(K_r-1)} \sum_{i < j} \|\mathbf{U}_i^\top \mathbf{U}_j\|_F^2$.

Training uses conditional flow matching (OT-CFM) along linear paths between noise $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$: $z_t = (1-t)z_{\text{noise}} + t z_{\text{tgt}}$, with loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \left[ \| \hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2 \right].
\end{equation}
At inference, the detector queries the field at the trajectory midpoint:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}\!\left(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}},\, 0.5,\, z_{\text{ctx}},\, p_{\text{regime}}\right) - z_{\text{tgt}}.
\end{equation}
Discrete midpoint queries on conditional velocity fields do not integrate an autonomous continuous trajectory and do not inherit autonomous non-periodic guarantees; Proposition~\ref{prop:no_limit_cycles} provides idealized continuous motivation and does not bind discrete scoring. Parameterizing $\hat{v}$ as a spatial gradient imposes a curl-free bias ($\nabla \times \hat{v} \equiv \mathbf{0}$) with vanishing circulation $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \hat{v} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$. This constraint is hypothesized to penalize cyclic nominal orbits and degrade detection on periodic kinematics (evaluated empirically in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors}).

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed at stride 1. For TS-JEPA, Operator-Entropy, and Potential-Flow, the score gates the latent discrepancy through $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}): $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t))$. For the second-moment head (Reynolds-Stress), $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t)) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. All shared representation stages are held fixed across variants and ablation controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
To resolve the methodological confounding between data availability, decision latency, and evaluation attribution, we formalize detection under three operational criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:inference_timeline}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$, the model observes only history $\mathbf{X}_{:\tau} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:\tau, :]$. Neither encoder accesses future observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps):} Because suspect representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$ encodes the horizon $\mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :]$, window score $s_t$ cannot be computed until timestep $\tau = t + S - 1$. This imposes an inherent emission latency of $S - 1 = 63$ steps: $0.98\,\text{s}$ on Daphnet ($64\,\text{Hz}$), $10.5\,\text{min}$ on SMAP, MSL, and GHL under standard 10-s ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$) sampling \cite{Hundman2018b, zhou2026mtsbench}, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO ($1\,\text{sample/min}$), and $63\,\text{h}$ on Metro ($1\,\text{sample/h}$).
    \item \textbf{Alarm attribution convention:} We distinguish two attribution regimes: (1)~\emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, where score $s_t$ is emitted and evaluated strictly at $\tau$, providing zero-lookahead online monitoring; and (2)~\emph{retrospective buffered smear attribution}, where $s_t$ is attributed across the entire suspect window $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$. Under unadjusted point evaluation, buffered alarms in $[t^*-S, t^*]$ register as false alarms: buffering provides fixed-lag detection, not early warning. The two regimes represent distinct operational realities and are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, the latent JEPA architectures incorporate shared inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and the Auxiliary Channel-Gate Variant}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults frequently originate in a localized subsystem while the remaining channels exhibit ambient noise, diluting localized anomalies in isotropic representations. The deployed detector mitigates this in latent space via an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e})$ on error vectors $\mathbf{e} = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \\
    w_d &= \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)}, \nonumber
\end{align}
amplifying sparse coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$ while remaining exactly $\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$ under isotropic errors. We fix blend parameter $\alpha = 0.5$ and softmax temperature $\tau = 0.5$ across all latent predictors. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) evaluate unweighted observation errors and do not use the gate. This is the discrepancy gate used in every latent experiment: input channels are encoded unweighted, and the gate acts only on the $D$-dimensional predictive residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary Input-Channel Gate (Evaluated Variant)}
We also evaluate a strictly causal \textit{Causal Channel Saliency Gate} as an auxiliary variant (full specification, boundary-condition verification, and the fault-type study in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}): each channel is weighted by the sigmoid of its standardized context-window variance share, $g_k = \sigma\!\left((s_k - \bar{s})/(\sigma_s + \epsilon)\right)$, applied symmetrically to context and target encoders so that $\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$ (zero target leakage, with an explicit identity short-circuit at $K{=}1$). Two boundary conditions apply: quiet faults (flatlines, stuck values) cannot be isolated by variance amplification --- the fault-type study confirms they are weakly detected under \emph{every} gating regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$) --- and abrupt regime transitions transiently perturb variance shares until the context window refills.

\paragraph{Zero-Leakage Data Provenance and Temporal Isolation}
We enforce strict temporal isolation across the training, nominal-calibration, and held-out evaluation partitions (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}): standardization statistics come only from the training partition, EVT tail parameters $(u, \sigma, \xi)$ only from the calibration partition, and evaluation proceeds chronologically with frozen weights and thresholds. The auxiliary gate's weights depend only on $x_{\text{ctx}}$; the deployed pipeline weights inputs uniformly.

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Anomalies span temporal scales from sensor spikes to thermal drifts; the encoder front-end therefore splits each channel into $J{=}4$ smooth dyadic shells on an $N_{\text{fft}}=256$ rFFT grid, normalized bin-wise into an exact amplitude partition of unity, $\sum_j \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ (measured deviation $< 1.2\times10^{-7}$; construction details in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}). Each shell feeds a causal 1D convolution ($k \in \{15,9,5,3\}$ coarse-to-fine), recombined by learned scale routing. Because the shells overlap smoothly at band edges, the front-end is \emph{less} frequency-localized than standard wavelet banks: per-band in-octave spectral mass is $\{0.50, 0.33, 0.20, {\approx}1.00\}$ versus $\{0.78, 0.50, 0.55, 0.82\}$ (Haar) and $\{0.86, 0.63, 0.64, 0.87\}$ (DB4) (filterbank frequency diagnostics). We therefore describe it as a Fourier-domain multiscale front-end, not an exact Littlewood--Paley octave decomposition, and measure its empirical contribution by matched ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b).

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}: excesses over a high threshold $u$ (98th percentile) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via an empirical heuristic factor governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ rises to suppress volatile-noise false alarms; on light-tailed ones, it tightens for sensitivity.

We emphasize that this is an engineering heuristic for pre-asymptotic finite-sample tails, not an asymptotic EVT theorem. The primary deployed pipeline relies on standard SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) without the multiplier; the kurtosis heuristic is evaluated strictly as an explicit sensitivity arm (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), where it degrades out-of-sample risk control ($5.26\% \to 17.68\%$ FPR) due to threshold lowering on non-positive kurtosis blocks.

More broadly, EVT does not eliminate threshold-calibration difficulty: under $q = 10^{-3}$ (ideal asymptotic FPR $0.1\%$), measured held-out nominal FPR settles at $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ for the JEPAs and $\approx 22\%$ for TimesNet/TranAD on the 33-stream cell-pooled buffered cohort ($7.6\text{--}8.6\%$ vs.\ $\approx 30\%$ on the 19-stream macro cohort, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), owing to finite-sample variance, residual autocorrelation, and non-stationary drift between nominal training and deployment. The calibrated finding of this paper is narrower: latent predictive residuals produce substantially fewer false alarms than observation-space residuals under identical calibration, reflecting lighter tails at the cost of lower causal recall. Thresholds use static EVT on held-out nominal splits; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

The master experiment matrix enumerating all evaluation suites (stream counts, partitions, horizons, calibrators, metrics) is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:master_matrix}.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted using a causal dilated TCN (3 residual blocks, channel depth $F{=}48$, kernel $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4\}$); global temporal average pooling aggregates the full $C{=}256$ context, with LayerNorm and dropout ($p{=}0.20$). The momentum target encoder follows EMA tracking with cosine schedule $m_{\text{base}}{=}0.996 \to m_{\text{max}}{=}0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's nominal telemetry is partitioned into three disjoint chronological blocks: \emph{training} (first 65\%), \emph{nominal calibration} (next 17.5\%), and \emph{nominal evaluation} (final 17.5\%, separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap). The training block optimizes representation weights alone. Withheld from training, the nominal calibration block fits per-stream GPD tail parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}0.98$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$, estimating $\gamma_2$ strictly within this interval. The nominal evaluation block then measures held-out FPR on telemetry unobserved during threshold selection. Thresholds remain fixed during test evaluation to measure static EVT stability under drift; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}).

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment Across Experiments}
To avoid ambiguity, we specify the calibrator per experiment. \textbf{Primary held-out benchmark} (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}; all paired statistics): standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier); headline metrics are the ``SPOT'' column of Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, with calibrator variants compared on identical score streams. \textbf{Ablations and controlled studies} (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}): standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$), matching the primary benchmark; the kurtosis multiplier is evaluated strictly as an explicit sensitivity arm (Section~\ref{subsec:ablation_calibration}, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). \textbf{Buffered-attribution and EVT mechanism studies}: standard SPOT unless the calibrator is the object of comparison. \textbf{Incident metrics} (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}): rolling SPOT. No experiment mixes calibrators across models within a table.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Multiplicity Controls}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are trained across five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 large GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) use three seeds (five on the two smallest) for cost, and ablation controls use three. We report held-out nominal FPR, Point-F1, and PR-AUC as distinct outcomes; nonsignificant detection differences are not evidence of parity. The post hoc operating-curve analysis uses only streams with archived score dumps (19 buffered, 18 causal), not the full 33-stream benchmark.

Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. We distinguish two resampling levels: within each stream, the held-out nominal evaluation split uses a moving-block bootstrap ($L{=}64$) over the chronological false-alarm indicator sequence to construct per-cell FPR confidence intervals under serial autocorrelation; across streams, paired model contrasts resample stream differences (2{,}000 stream-level bootstrap iterations with seeds averaged within stream). The independent statistical unit is the telemetry stream ($n{=}19$ non-GHL at five seeds; $n{=}33$ where GHL enters at available coverage), with dataset-clustered checks reported to account for shared sensor environments. Because nonsignificant tests do not establish equivalence, we make no noninferiority claim; instead, we report paired differences with effect sizes and CIs for readers to assess practical margins. $p$-values across comparisons are Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted at $q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$; CIs spanning zero or adjusted $p > 0.05$ are treated as inconclusive, not as equivalence. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:test_map} maps every reported test to its endpoint, cohort, paired-stream count, seed treatment, raw and adjusted $p$, and effect-size interval.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Parameter Capacities}
To isolate algorithmic principles from implementation-specific discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline with identical windowing, standardization, and EVT calibration. TimesNet and TranAD are in-house re-implementations of the published architectures (rather than vendor code), configured to published depths and evaluated under the identical optimization budget (AdamW, $\eta=10^{-3}$, cosine decay, $20$ epochs); no method received per-model hyperparameter tuning beyond the shared configuration, and no threshold, early-stopping, or model-selection step accessed held-out anomaly labels. Hardware-specific throughput, batch, warm-up, and per-timestep latency measurements are reported in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}.

\noindent\textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Multi-periodic reconstructive architecture mapping 1D series to 2D variation spaces via FFT period discovery and Inception blocks ($648{,}553$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $574{,}529$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Adversarial reconstructive Transformer with two-stage self-conditioning and sub-series attention ($205{,}746$ at $K{=}25$; $201{,}090$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TS-JEPA (Unconstrained Control):} Standard JEPA predicting $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ via a 2-layer MLP ($289{,}061$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $218{,}309$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Formulations:} Operator-Entropy ($285{,}733$ at $K{=}25$, $-1.15\%$ capacity), Reynolds-Stress ($295{,}953$, $+2.4\%$), and Potential-Flow ($338{,}117$, $+17.0\%$): differences do not stem from parameter scaling.

\noindent\textbf{Linear AR Forecaster (Non-Neural Baseline):} Ridge-regularized map from flattened context to target windows, scored by observation-space prediction error --- isolating whether latent representation is necessary at all. \textbf{Score-bound controls:} a matched-backbone observation-space TCN forecaster with LayerNorm on its bottleneck and flattened output, scored by the same saliency gate; PCA-subspace reconstruction; per-channel max-$z$; IsolationForest; $k$NN-distance; observation-space MAD scoring; and the dimensionality-matched PCA$\to$32$\to$LayerNorm gate of Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Critical surveys \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards} document severe reporting vulnerabilities in this literature --- point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, threshold gaming --- so we report metrics across complementary tiers. \textbf{Exploratory operating characteristics:} event recall integrated over nominal calibration budgets and, separately, over a fixed exploratory realized-test-FPR interval $[0.10,0.50]$ (Section~\ref{sec:results}). For the latter, we interpolate within each stream--seed curve only when both bounds are observed, pair models within identical stream--seed cells, average paired differences by stream, and use dataset-level sign permutations and dataset-clustered bootstrap intervals; Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment covers all tested model, mapping, and endpoint combinations. Missing support is reported, not extrapolated. \textbf{Secondary endpoints:} \textbf{Dataset-Macro Point-F1} (unweighted mean across datasets, preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating); \textbf{channel-macro Point-F1}; \textbf{PR-AUC} (average precision, ranking quality independent of calibration); \textbf{event recall} (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments containing $\ge1$ alarm); \textbf{mean detection delay} $\Delta t$ (onset-to-first-alarm lag over detected events only); and the \textbf{trivial all-positive reference} $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at base rate $\pi$ --- models below it underperform a constant alarm. \textbf{Pooled micro-F1} exposes flood-prone models; contiguous PA-F1 and test-sweep PA-F1 quantify the residual calibration gap.

\subsection{Evaluation Regimes: Zero-Lookahead Causal vs. Buffered Inference}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered_protocol}
To keep the buffered-attribution regime separate from future-observation access, we evaluate two regimes: \textbf{strictly causal online detection} (at decision time $T$ only $\{X_\tau\}_{\tau \le T}$ is accessible; scores use trailing attribution; zero lookahead) and \textbf{buffered window detection} ($S{=}64$ future timesteps are buffered before scoring, capturing full trajectory evolution at a deliberate $64$-step latency). Both record Mean Detection Delay, Event Recall within delay budgets ($\Delta t \le 10, 64$), and false alarms per 1,000 steps ($\text{FAR}_{1\text{k}}$).

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone. Because controls run three seeds against the five-seed principal runs, the paired control means below are computed on the shared three-seed subset and differ slightly from the five-seed Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) rows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$:} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.241 \pm 0.014$ vs. $0.244 \pm 0.024$; difference $-0.003$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.011, +0.005]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.62$); PR-AUC stays $0.347\text{--}0.348$.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$:} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.203 \pm 0.059$ vs. $0.208 \pm 0.071$ ($+0.005$, $[-0.006, +0.016]$, $p=0.41$).     \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing shifts Point-F1 to $0.129$ from $0.096$ on the shared three-seed subset ($p_{\text{raw}}=0.048, p_{\text{BH}}=0.24$; $0.089$ at five seeds in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)); both underperform TS-JEPA ($0.234$, $p_{\text{raw}}=0.013, p_{\text{BH}}=0.24$, non-significant under FDR adjustment). \end{enumerate}
Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction. Formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) confirm statistical equivalence to TS-JEPA within a tight $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ Point-F1 margin: Operator-Entropy achieves $p = 0.0009$ (also passing $\Delta = \pm 0.03$, $p=0.0283$) and Reynolds-Stress achieves $p = 0.0054$.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating Variant}

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a modest, stream-dependent effect (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)): macro buffered Point-F1 improves from $0.088$ to $0.131$ across a five-stream exploratory panel (including two diagnostic streams, SMD and GECCO, outside the 33 primary streams), concentrated on MSL and SMD, with a small SMAP regression. \subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
To discriminate the operational mechanisms driving tail conditioning and threshold transfer, all 19 non-GHL streams were re-run under the primary held-out protocol (five seeds, identical windows, AdamW budget, and SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$ calibration) across controlled backbone arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, three dilated TCN layers): \textbf{TCN-Obs-Recon} (observation reconstruction), \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} (observation forecasting), \textbf{JEPA-CtxRecon} (latent autoencoding of EMA-encoded \emph{context} without foresight), \textbf{JEPA-NoEMA} (latent prediction with hard-copy target), and \textbf{TS-JEPA}. Five further controls probe score geometry and gating directly: \textbf{TCN-ObsPred-LN} (same forecaster, LayerNorm on the 32-dimensional bottleneck and flattened forecast, identical saliency gate), \textbf{PCA-LinPred-LN}, \textbf{Rand-LinPred} (fixed random Gaussian projection to 32 dimensions), \textbf{Obs-MAD}, \textbf{IsoForest}, \textbf{KNN-Dist} (training-free scorers), and \textbf{TS-JEPA-NoGate} (identical model, ungated $\ell_2$ discrepancy). Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:residual_diagnostics} reports per-arm residual diagnostics; the earlier 15-stream multi-SNR study is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:controlled_backbone}.

\noindent\textbf{Mechanism Discrimination: Foresight vs. Geometry vs. Dynamics:} The controls are consistent with three distinct operational factors:
(1)~\emph{Future temporal forecasting is not uniquely necessary for score conditioning}: \textbf{JEPA-CtxRecon} autoencodes context representations without forecasting future timesteps, yet achieves an FPR reduction of $0.202$ ($0.310 \to 0.108$ relative to \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred}) and higher event recall ($0.513$ vs.\ $0.473$ for TS-JEPA on this controlled panel, Table~\ref{tab:residual_diagnostics}; $0.481$ on primary benchmark Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Latent representation compression, rather than future-state extrapolation, conditions scores for tail estimation.
(2)~\emph{Bounded score geometry mechanically suppresses tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2$)}: projecting through a low-dimensional LayerNorm bottleneck compresses residual excursions regardless of architecture, as \textbf{TCN-ObsPred-LN} suppresses kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.14$ and \textbf{PCA-LinPred-LN} achieves $0.87$ (vs.\ $8.24$ for unnormalized \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} and $1.14$ for \textbf{TS-JEPA}).
(3)~\emph{Representation dynamics and conservative subspace transfer}: kurtosis suppression alone cannot prevent threshold transfer breakdown across temporal blocks. TCN-ObsPred-LN suffers held-out nominal FPR of $0.186$ (buffered) and $0.117$ (causal trailing), versus $0.080$ and $0.014$ for TS-JEPA. At conservative budgets, PCA-LinPred-LN and \textbf{IsoForest} collapse to event recalls of $0.665$ and $0.631$ versus $0.774$ for TS-JEPA ($0.768$ ungated). Representation rank diagnostics across diverse streams reveal that $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$ occupy an effective subspace of $5.6\text{--}6.1$ and $8.5\text{--}14.0$ dimensions out of 32 (with feature variances $0.003\text{--}0.031$ and leading PC share $\approx 53\%$). While ruling out rank-1 collapse, this compact subspace structure together with JEPA-CtxRecon's matched geometry ($5.62$ ctx rank, $0.513$ recall) indicates that low nominal FPR is consistent with conservative subspace projection and bounded score geometry rather than an exclusive property of predictive temporal dynamics.

\noindent\textbf{Target EMA and Window Horizons:} Removing the EMA target changes little in Point-F1 (JEPA-NoEMA: $-0.033$ vs.\ TS-JEPA, $p{=}0.17$; spectral diagnostics show higher top-PC concentration); PR-AUC differences across arms are inconclusive ($p \ge 0.16$). Native-window checks (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:native_window_baselines}) confirm that baseline tail heaviness does not arise from the $W{=}320$ window: at native horizons, TranAD ($W{=}10$) spikes to $\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and TimesNet ($W{=}96$) reaches $\gamma_2 = 98.14$ ($44.09\%$ FPR), while TS-JEPA retains $\gamma_2 = 3.14$.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$). Primary findings:

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076$--$0.086$ versus $0.300$ (TimesNet) and $0.307$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams, the paired reduction is significant: TS-JEPA $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.247, -0.071]$, $p{=}0.002$) and $-0.151$ vs.\ TranAD ($p{=}0.003$); Operator-Entropy shows the same effect ($-0.144$/$-0.142$, $p{=}0.006$). Clustering by dataset ($N{=}7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$, verifying that significance does not depend on within-dataset stream replication. The mechanism is isolated under the same-backbone control (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}).

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not translate into statistically distinguishable detection gains. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences are inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$).

\noindent\textbf{Event Recall and Detection Conservatism:} Lower FPR accompanies lower event recall: on non-GHL streams TimesNet captures $58.3\%$ of events and TranAD $49.0\%$, versus $48.1\%$ (TS-JEPA), $43.4\%$ (Reynolds), $30.3\%$ (Potential-Flow). Subtle onsets that do not shift dominant latent coordinates are filtered with the noise.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Delay Trade-Offs ($\Delta t$):} Mean detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) varies widely: TimesNet responds fastest ($\Delta t{=}48$ steps), TS-JEPA averages $80$, and the physics variants are slower still ($133$--$192$); TranAD is slowest ($234$). This raw disparity is largely a detected-set artifact, as delays are averaged across different event sets per model. On shared intersecting detected events at $q^{*}{=}0.05$, the delay gap between TimesNet and TS-JEPA collapses from $31.8$ steps to just $2.3$ steps ($56.9$ vs.\ $59.2$ steps; Table~\ref{tab:trivial_delay}b); vs.\ TCN-ObsPred it is $60$ vs.\ $51$ steps. The apparent latency gap reflects which events each detector catches rather than the intrinsic response delay.

\noindent\textbf{Necessity of Nonlinear Representation Learning (Linear AR Control):} A ridge-regularized linear AR forecaster under identical calibration attains FPR $0.005$ but collapses to Point-F1 $0.051$: observation-space linear dynamics cannot separate faults from background variance. Two training-free references sharpen the point (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:nonparametric}): a PCA-subspace reconstructor matches TimesNet almost exactly ($0.311$/$0.198$/$0.331$ vs.\ $0.300$/$0.197$/$0.329$ FPR/F1/PR-AUC), while a per-channel max-$z$ scorer attains the best ranking ($0.374$) at the worst FPR ($0.339$): ranking quality and threshold conditioning are independent properties.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-Specific Telemetry Dynamics:} Relative performance depends on the physical regime (per-dataset breakdown in Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:dataset_prauc}--\ref{tab:all_datasets}): TimesNet leads on Daphnet periodic kinematics ($0.245$ vs.\ $0.155$ F1); latent prediction leads on spacecraft telemetry (SMAP: OpEntropy $0.591$ PR-AUC; MSL: $0.445$ vs.\ $0.159$). On GHL and Metro, all models struggle ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1). Comparing against the trivial all-positive detector $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ shows that on high-prevalence domains (Daphnet $\pi=14.8\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.255$; MSL $\pi=28.0\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.396$; swan $\pi=28.0\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.438$), all deep detectors achieve Point-F1 near or below a constant alarm, whereas on low-prevalence telemetry (SMAP $\pi=15.5\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.243$ vs.\ TS-JEPA $0.321$; Genesis $\pi=0.3\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.006$ vs.\ $0.015$), detectors decisively beat the trivial baseline (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:trivial_delay}a).

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data do not support an aggregate gain:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. TS-JEPA:} paired difference $+0.002$ Point-F1 (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$), $+0.002$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.86$), $+0.009$ FPR ($p{=}0.75$); leads on 9 of 33 streams.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. TS-JEPA:} $-0.015$ Point-F1 ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$), $-0.007$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.13$); leads on 11 of 33.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. TS-JEPA:} Point-F1 differs by $-0.081$ under SPOT ($p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Its higher event-recall area over nominal causal budgets is descriptive; without matched realized-FPR support and multiplicity-adjusted evidence, neither an intrinsically stronger detector nor a purely operating-point explanation is established.
\end{enumerate}

These findings align with the matched-capacity controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a): the physical regularizers provide no aggregate detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. The dynamical diagnostics on \emph{why} (operator rank preservation without detection gain, covariance-shift insensitivity, and decoupling resolution via Lyapunov invariance) are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

Event-level incident metrics, latency, and rolling-SPOT case studies are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}: rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at a recall cost, and TS-JEPA's causal false-positive rate on the 5-stream operational incident cohort (evaluated across test-split nominal intervals: $4.18\%$, Table~S8) sits an order of magnitude below reconstructive baselines ($20.8$--$30.1\%$). In comparison, on the primary 19-stream held-out calibration benchmark, held-out nominal FPR is $0.0136$ vs.\ $0.2427$/$0.2219$.


Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark} reports the same benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression; on the 14 GHL streams, causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and causal event recall is $46.55\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $58.82\%$ for TimesNet: without a lookahead buffer, latent predictive scoring is an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of detection sensitivity.

\paragraph{Pre-Onset Alarm Attribution}
Buffered attribution is strictly retrospective: scores are painted over $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$, precluding leads beyond $S{=}64$ steps. Median pre-onset ``leads'' of $180$--$204$ steps in empirical lead-time diagnostics are nuisance alarms at the underlying false-positive rate or precursor drift, not prediction: TimesNet emits $206$ such alarms per $1{,}000$ nominal steps versus $24$ for TS-JEPA, reflecting the baseline alarm-rate gap. We do not claim early-warning capability.

\paragraph{Detection Quality at Matched Operating Points}
\label{par:operating_curves}
We fit empirical-quantile thresholds on nominal budgets $q\in[10^{-4},0.9]$ to evaluate event recall and realized FPR on test. Partial-AUC values $0.742$ (TS-JEPA) and $0.618$ (TimesNet) integrate recall over nominal budgets up to $0.10$, not matched realized FPR. At $q^{*}{=}0.05$, causal event recall is $0.774$ vs.\ $0.618$, but realized FPR differs ($0.180$ vs.\ $0.262$). Budget-pooled curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_curves}) obscure stream-specific support and are not paired detector comparisons. Given multi-hundred-step anomalies (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), alarming at $30\%$ FPR catches $>95\%$ of segments by chance ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$); TimesNet's $58\%$ recall at $30\%$ FPR falls within this random expectation.

For an exploratory support-aware check, we interpolate stream--seed curves over common realized-FPR $[0.10,0.50]$ without extrapolation (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:common_support_17models}). The causal TS-JEPA--TimesNet difference is $+0.061$ (95\% dataset-bootstrap CI $[-0.153,0.329]$; dataset-sign-permutation $p{=}0.625$; 20 paired cells across four streams). For TS-JEPA vs.\ PCA-LinPred-LN ($+0.121$), the pre-specified confirmatory test is the stream-level paired Wilcoxon test and 95\% bootstrap CI ($p=0.312$, $[-0.008, 0.271]$, 11 streams); 5-dataset tests are low-powered exploratory checks ($p \ge 0.0625$; permutation $p=0.188$, $p_{\text{BH}}=1.0$; cluster bootstrap CI $[0.015, 0.310]$ reflects $N=5$ undercoverage). TS-JEPA establishes no significant advantage over PCA-LinPred-LN on common support. Bounded-observation TCN matches TS-JEPA causally on nine streams ($-0.018$, CI $[-0.055,0.003]$) and shows higher buffered recall area on five streams ($-0.211$, CI $[-0.349,-0.105]$); neither survives family-wide BH adjustment. Nominal-budget orderings and pooled curves do not establish a causal-regime advantage, and boundedness alone determines neither tail transfer nor matched-FPR detection.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out of sample? Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under both strictly causal trailing and buffered smear inference. The calibrator evaluated here is the \emph{static} peaks-over-threshold step of SPOT --- a single threshold fit once on the calibration block; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}. Four realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Causal vs.\ buffered smear overshoot:} Under causal trailing, TS-JEPA achieves held-out nominal FPR of $0.0101$ ($0.0092$ stream-macro, $\approx$$10\times$ overshoot; median $0.0002$ / $0.02\%$, IQR $[0.0000, 0.0025]$). Buffered inference paints each alarm across $S{=}64$ steps, inflating per-timestep FPR by up to $S \cdot q = 6.4\%$ to reach $0.0526$ ($0.0457$ stream-macro, $53\times$ overshoot) --- alarm smearing explains the bulk of apparent JEPA inflation. In contrast, TimesNet and TranAD suffer causal FPRs of $0.1795$ and $0.1601$ (medians $0.0328$ and $0.0133$; $0.221725$ and $0.221695$ buffered, both rounding to $0.2217$), far exceeding the $S \cdot q$ smear ceiling and confirming genuine distributional breakdown.
    \item \textbf{One-sided overshoot testing:} Rather than two-sided CI exclusion (which counts zero-FPR cells as failures), one-sided overshoot testing ($\text{CI}_{\text{lo}} > 10^{-3}$) reveals TS-JEPA overshoots design risk in only 7\% of causal cells (12\% buffered), versus 46--49\% for reconstructive baselines.
    \item \textbf{Parity with empirical and conformal quantiles:} On TS-JEPA, validation-maximum yields causal FPR $0.0090$ / F1 $0.0722$, empirical p99.5 yields $0.0169$ / $0.0907$, and Split Conformal thresholding ($q_{1-\alpha}, \alpha{=}10^{-3}$) achieves median causal FPR $0.0006$ ($0.0512$ mean) vs.\ $0.1247$ (TimesNet) and $0.1866$ (TranAD). Neither parametric GPD tail modeling nor distribution-free conformal calibration prevents risk overshoot under chronological deployment because non-stationary temporal drift violates exchangeability.
    \item \textbf{Kurtosis-adaptive directional breakdown:} The heuristic multiplier $1+\tanh(\gamma_2)/6$ inflates TS-JEPA buffered FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$. In 33.3\% of cells (47/141) with $\gamma_2 \le 0$, the multiplier lowers the threshold by up to 16.7\%, exploding FPR from 9.17\% to 50.49\%; where $\gamma_2 > 0$ (66.7\%), it raises the threshold and cuts FPR from 3.31\% to 1.28\%.
\end{enumerate}

Empirical tail diagnostics on nine non-GHL streams confirm tail-sample scarcity: median exceedances above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and Grimshaw returns degenerate $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits. Yet KS tests reject in only $6/27$ (TS-JEPA) and $8/27$ (TimesNet) cells, while held-out FPR varies from $0.0$ to $0.67$ across seeds on the same stream under passing fits.

To determine whether drift or scarcity dominates, an exchangeable permutation control uniformly samples calibration and evaluation splits from pooled nominal blocks. Under point exchangeability, causal TS-JEPA FPR drops from $0.0121$ ($12.1\times$ overshoot) to $0.0019$ ($1.9\times$ overshoot), matching design $q{=}10^{-3}$ within sampling variance; TimesNet drops from $0.0872$ to $0.0037$ (a $96\%$ overshoot reduction). Block permutation ($L{=}64$) yields intermediate overshoot ($5.8\times$ for TS-JEPA), demonstrating that inter-block drift dominates calibration failure while autocorrelation is secondary. Across all $N=1080$ dumped cells, KS drift between calibration and test nominal blocks predicts realized SPOT FPR (Spearman $\rho = +0.377$, 95\% CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$, $p = 7.4 \times 10^{-38}$); calibration-block excess kurtosis correlates inversely ($\rho = -0.315$, 95\% CI $[-0.373, -0.260]$, $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-26}$). A controlled-drift study (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:drift_calibration}) shows runs declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ effective exceedances per cell, and rolling calibrators control risk to $1$--$7\%$ under step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33$--$0.39$ FPR).

Computational profiling is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}: JEPAs use $183.8\text{--}185.3$~MB peak memory at $0.60\text{--}0.84$~ms per-window inference.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We compared score conditioning, detection, and static threshold calibration across 33 telemetry streams from seven datasets under a held-out protocol. Three conclusions follow:

First, bounded observation-space controls reproduce much of the apparent latent-score tail conditioning, while the bounded TCN demonstrates that light tails do not guarantee threshold transfer. Causal event-recall areas over nominal budgets favor JEPA on average, but paired comparisons at matched realized FPR have sparse support and do not establish a detector advantage; buffered results remain separate.

Second, the tested physics-inspired variants provide no reliable aggregate detection benefit, failing to meet formal TOST equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 of TS-JEPA ($p \le 0.0054$). Explanations invoking curl-free dynamics or operating points remain conjectural.

Third, static POT thresholds overshoot design risk under chronological calibration ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at $q{=}10^{-3}$). An exchangeable permutation control demonstrates that inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of this overshoot, while sample scarcity and autocorrelation are secondary.
