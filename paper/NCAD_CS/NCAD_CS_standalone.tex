\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag faults quickly while staying quiet during healthy operation, yet waveform reconstructors fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries, though single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one shared threshold. We replace scalar scoring with coordinate circuit breakers. Each coordinate's horizon errors and mean drift receive healthy tripwires, and an alarm fires when a healthy-calibrated quorum trips. Each stream fits independently on its own healthy prefix. Rather than transferring pretrained weights across assets, this design transfers a frozen architecture and calibration rule across sensor suites. On 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets, our 128-breaker Laminar-JEPA-Lite detector lifts causal event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA and cuts end-to-end alarm latency from 133.5 to 69.0 steps. Latency includes a 63-step window fill lag, while the Point-F1 shift (0.076 to 0.086) stays uncertain across datasets. Under the same protocol, TimesNet and TranAD incur 24.6 to 25.6 percent mean false-positive rates versus 6.4 percent mean (0.93 percent median) for our panel. Autonomous deployment uses two-stage acceptance (52 of 118 accepted; 66 fallback) with quarantined mode-confirmed recalibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet stochastic jitter, benign thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ heterogeneous channels allows one noisy sensor to produce heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibration via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) on later healthy data across our 19 development streams. Because faults span many steps, random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes, matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next window's summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and one threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} drift during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1 and $138\text{--}154$-step delay on development streams). Published benchmarks are further inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial amplitude spikes, high anomaly density, and run-to-failure label bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint nominal blocks, benchmark against random-rate bounds, and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace the single scalar alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, like a building breaker box where one circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across dyadic target prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, each of the 32 coordinates receives \emph{dials} tracking horizon error size and persistent drift under individual healthy thresholds, alarming when a quorum trips together. Drifting dials widen only their own thresholds instead of blinding the rest of the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Unlike independent detectors, the dials are structured views of one error matrix with thresholds fitted to joint calibration trip counts. Rather than improving score ranking everywhere, the panel improves the label-free operating-point trade-off for abrupt to intermediate-onset faults within one $256$-step context window (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $(3+1)\times 32=128$ position-plus-drift dials under one quorum ($120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable parameters).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic reference): shares the exact backbone across $M=4$ horizons ($224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate binary onset relays excluded from the $224$-dial count); fluctuation and relay dials add false alarms without aiding live causal F1.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Per-stream prefix fitting tests zero-tune transfer of our frozen architecture and calibration rule across sensor suites rather than cross-asset weight transfer. Thirty-three development streams guide design, while 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets (from a 141-stream, 12-dataset archive) under a disjoint five-block protocol evaluate calibration and generalization (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Theory and live operating point:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} proves quorum invariance under coordinate-scale drift. On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$) and cuts end-to-end alarm latency from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps (including the $63$-step fill lag) at higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; $0.93\%$ median). Overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) stays uncertain under dataset clustering, whereas $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ streams ($n=59$) double Point-F1 ($0.040\to 0.085$, sign-flip $p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{Operating-point attribution and gated acceptance:} Matched test-FPR checks confirm no ranking gain over 1D scoring; gains stem strictly from quorum placement. Although $88.1\%$ of untouched streams hold $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$, two-stage acceptance ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ and $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$) retains $44.1\%$ ($52/118$) and routes $66$ streams to fallback. Block-permutation audits trace $92.3\%$ of tail false alarms to concept drift ($3.1\times$ covariance shift), motivating quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}$ recalibration requiring confirmed mode switches.
    \item \emph{External baselines and alarm attribution:} Across all $118$ untouched streams under SPOT, TimesNet and TranAD incur $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ versus $6.4\%$ for \emph{Lite} (dropping to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ vs.\ $51.2\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR). Single-core CPU execution sustains $93\text{--}141$ windows/s ($\le 128\,\text{KB}$ state), and latent alarm decomposition pairs with post-alarm leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) attribution ($85.7\%$ Top-1 and $92.9\%$ Top-3 on $14$ multi-channel dev streams).
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds horizon energy (null reweighting), $105/118$ streams lack independent tail bounds, synthetic channel guards cost $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$ FPR without proving field coverage, and frozen-reference GHL slow-ramp recovery ($0.8\%\to 58.5\%$ ER) incurs $74.4\%$ test FPR from baseline wander.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly, while TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention. Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention, and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches such as temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}. We instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate alternatives such as univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy history followed by a labeled test segment. Two cohorts play distinct roles. Forty-five streams across 11 datasets guided design, while 141 archive streams across 12 datasets (118 never inspected during design) provide the primary evaluation.

\noindent\emph{Development suite:} Drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench} ($K\in[2,72]$), the 45 streams span three domains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar:} six SMAP ($K{=}25$) and six MSL ($K{=}55$) streams, split by NASA into out-of-range \emph{point} and in-range \emph{contextual} faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, plus SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$).
    \item \emph{Wearables:} six $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes; $S{=}64$ spans $1\,\text{s}$).
    \item \emph{Industrial and infrastructure:} 14 slow-ramp Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), Genesis ($K{=}18$), GECCO ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three streams ($19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL across $7$ datasets) meet $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$, $K\le 55$, and $\le 1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c). The remaining $12$ ($5$ short histories of $764\text{--}1{,}492$ steps, $6$ high-channel CICIDS streams, and GECCO) are held aside or evaluated in the archive.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive holds every qualifying stream across 12 datasets with $\ge 100$ calibration windows and one labeled anomaly: SMAP ($47$), Daphnet ($22$), MSL ($18$), SMD ($18$), Exathlon ($17$), OPPORTUNITY ($13$), plus single-stream GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN ($1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ nominal and $2{,}158\text{--}114{,}001$ test steps). Removing the $23$ tuning streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets: $37$ streams spanning $4$ strictly zero-tuning unseen datasets (SMD, Exathlon, PSM, CreditCard, plus $1$ archive-heldout GECCO stream) and $80$ unseen streams from $4$ partially tuned datasets (SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, OPPORTUNITY; Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and transfer:} Each stream fits independently on its own nominal prefix, so new assets require a healthy warm-up history rather than pretrained-weight transfer. What transfers zero-tune across heterogeneous sensor counts ($K\in[2,55]$) is the frozen architecture, damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum rule. Fixed-epoch dev runs allocate $65\%$ to fitting, $17.5\%$ to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, a $64$-step gap, and $17.5\%$ to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$, whereas primary archive runs subdivide fitting into disjoint selection and adaptation blocks (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and same-facility streams share hardware traits. We therefore combine dataset-clustered inference ($7$ dev, $9$ untouched datasets), separate zero-tuning unseen vs.\ partially tuned cohorts, nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) hyperparameter selection, and 9-fold untouched-archive exclusion sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.66\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-7pt}
\caption{Four-stage pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational, $M{=}3$, $128$ dials) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic, $M{=}4$, $224$ dials plus $32$ separate onset relays): \textbf{(1)}~sliding window ($C{=}256, S{\le}64$) into online/EMA encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) with $K{\ge}25$ rFFT shells, multi-depth taps, centering, and LayerNorm; \textbf{(2)}~parallel MLP multi-horizon prediction and $MD{\times}MD$ damped whitening; \textbf{(3)}~position-plus-drift dial expansion; and \textbf{(4)}~per-dial tripwires and correlation-aware quorum alarm.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-7pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time, splitting at $t$ into a $C = 256$-step \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and an $S = 64$-step \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ ending at causal decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features split into four smooth dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose weights sum to one at every frequency, partitioning the Nyquist interval without over-fragmenting $256$-step windows. These four bands pass through depthwise causal convolutions with kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$ and recombine via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) with dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ \cite{bai2018empirical}. Lower-channel streams ($K < 25$) feed directly into the TCN.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) map each window to latent dimension $D=32$ (ablated across $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Each encoder pools three temporal descriptors (last step, max, and mean in $\mathbb{R}^{144}$) and three statistical descriptors (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, and mean absolute first difference in $\mathbb{R}^{144}$) through $D$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads. The target encoder $E_\phi$ tracks an exponential moving average (EMA) of $E_\theta$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D controls), preventing representation collapse across $10\text{--}20$ epochs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Terminal affine-free LayerNorm projects embeddings onto the $\sqrt{D}$-sphere to curb heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$). However, rescaling by total feature variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines by $O(1/\sqrt{KD})$ across coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two native mechanisms preserve fast transients and training offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Both encoders read the front-end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, passing concatenated mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{288}$) through a bias-free $288\to 32\to 32$ GELU-LayerNorm head blended at $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} A running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, synchronized into $E_\phi$) is subtracted before terminal LayerNorm, removing static offsets without guaranteeing invariance to post-calibration regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA} predicts one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the 64-step target window via a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant term ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Because one $64$-step vector averages out brief transients, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ dyadic target prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\} = \{S/4, S/2, S\}$ steps, spanning impulse, intermediate, and sustained timescales), while the diagnostic reference \emph{Laminar-JEPA} adds $S_3=48$ ($M=4$). From $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, one parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP predicts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$ behind a single terminal LayerNorm, weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ with a batch anti-collapse penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. The penalty acts on each batch of predicted summaries and does two jobs: it keeps every coordinate varying, and it discourages coordinates from copying one another. Unlike log-determinant penalties, which need batches larger than $D$, it works at our batch size $B=16<D=32$. The diagonal entries of $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$, push each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma = 0.75$; the off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize the sample covariance (divisor $B-1$). Hence
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 .
\end{equation}
The penalty raises the effective rank of the target summaries, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}, from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first passed through a \emph{damped whitening} step fitted on healthy training windows, which decorrelates them and brings well-estimated directions close to unit variance. We flatten $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($128$ in full, $96$ in Lite) and compute its \texttt{float32} sample mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and unbiased covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = (N_{\text{fit}}-1)^{-1}\sum_t (\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})(\cdot)^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Eigendecomposing $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ and clamping $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$ yields the precision operator $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})$ by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ with training eigenvalues $\lambda_k^2/(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2$: well-estimated directions approach unit variance, while near-zero modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$) are attenuated toward $0$ rather than amplified.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a disjoint healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$ rather than $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}) and blend them toward a per-coordinate RMS scaling $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=e_{m,c}(t)/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} Gradient-free predictor-row editing (Steps Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$ gave no archive gain (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}) and is omitted from the unified backbone.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} states two exact algebraic properties of this split: mean and fluctuation partition squared horizon energy orthogonally, and the fluctuation spectrum spans at most $M-1$ modes.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ (so the RMS horizon error equals $\sqrt{|\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)}$, not $|\bar{e}_c(t)| + d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$) and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
In plain terms, multivariate residual scoring faces three design options across $J$ coordinates or dials $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$. Global quadratic aggregation $S_{\text{glob}}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$ sums all coordinates into one threshold, whereas a marginal union $\max_j \mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$ compounds per-coordinate false alarms across $J$ tests. A correlation-aware coordinate quorum instead gives every dial its own quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and sets a joint trip-count threshold $K_q$ on healthy calibration data. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes why global aggregation fails when even $k\ll J$ ``hijacker'' coordinates exhibit larger post-training variance on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, whereas per-coordinate tripwires isolate that inflation completely.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Quorum Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^J$ be a nominal residual vector on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, and let $\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}$ rescale a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k=|\mathcal{H}|<J$ drifting coordinates by $\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta\ge 0$) while leaving $j\notin\mathcal{H}$ unchanged:
(i)~under global scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-calibration quantile satisfies $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_j^2\bigr)$, growing unboundedly in $\eta^2$ so any live fault $\mathbf{u}(t^*)$ supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected when $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous tripwires $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}|)$, every binary trip $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)})$ is invariant to $\eta$ for all $\eta\ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate dependence, leaving $N_{\text{cal}}=\sum_{j=1}^J b_j(t)$ and $K_q=\kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$ unchanged and guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $\sum_{j=1}^J (u_j^{(\eta)})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_j^2$ pointwise, monotonicity and positive homogeneity of quantiles give~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ yields $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u}(t^*))=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2\le\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity gives $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}\,\tau_j^{(0)}$ for $j\in\mathcal{H}$ (and $\tau_j^{(0)}$ otherwise); dividing $|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)}$ by $\sqrt{1+\eta^2}>0$ yields $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=b_j^{(0)}(t)$ identically at every $t$, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $K_q$, while $m$ active coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ yield $N(t^*)\ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration and validity assumptions:} For $J=32$ standard normal coordinates at $\alpha=0.005$ and $\beta=0.015$, inflating $k=2$ hijackers by $\eta=5$ raises $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$, masking a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault ($144.4 < 252.4$) while $K_q=3.5$ stays invariant and trips $10 > 3.5$. This isolation assumes \emph{multi-dial fault coverage ($m > K_q$)}, so diluted single-sensor flatlines miss quorum, and \emph{stationarity on $\ge J-\lfloor K_q\rfloor$ dials} unless gated via $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and live $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) implements this quorum on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Because dials share the $M \times 32$ error matrix, trips correlate strongly (median effective rank $65/224$; count variance $7.2\times$ binomial independence), so quorums are fitted directly to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 separate onset relays:} When a sudden fault enters the target window's right edge while $m \in \{1,2\}$ still see healthy data, coordinate $c$ drives an auxiliary binary relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ (excluded from the $224$-dial vector $\mathbf{d}(t)$) under $q_{0.993} + 10^{-4}$, firing when $m=4$ position trips jointly with endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, window $[t,t+S)$ emits at right edge $\tau=t+S-1$, when all $S=64$ target steps exist. Because windows starting at fault onset $t^*$ finish at $t^*+S-1$, full-window alignment imposes an $S-1=63$-step reporting lag, though sharp right-edge faults can trigger earlier ($\Delta t < 63$). All causal delays $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ reported here measure end-to-end online alarm latency from $t^*$ to live emission $\tau_{\text{alm}}$ and thus include this reporting lag. Under \emph{buffered attribution} (retrospective review only), position trips map to $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. Summing the $224$ dials gives $N_{224}(\tau)$ (with $128$-dial position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau)$) while summing the $32$ excluded relays gives $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$; ranking checks score $N_{224}(\tau)$. An alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with calibration quorums $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$ (linear quantiles, strict inequalities).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both tiers share one $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$-parameter backbone ($120.0\text{k}$ at $K < 25$; $127.7\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$) using four adaptive TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $D$-wide pooling and hook-free tap heads, depthwise rFFT shells gated at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbb{R}^{32} \to 64 \to \text{GELU} \to \text{LN} \to 32$ behind $\operatorname{LN}_{\text{no-aff}}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and monitors $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ plus $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ under $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$, omitting fluctuation/endpoint dials and relays.
    \item \emph{Diagnostic $7D=224$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) with all $224$ continuous dials plus the $32$ excluded auxiliary relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA (224+32)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\State Partition $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$ or $S_{1:4}{=}\{16,32,48,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and freeze $K_{\text{Lite}}{=}\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$ (or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum})).
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t){=}\mathbb{I}(d_j(t){>}\tau_j)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (\text{causal}) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (\text{buffered}); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ when active counts exceed quorum.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (RMS of full-tensor and peak-horizon scores, $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$) blend $\ell_2$ norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\subsection{Setup and Calibration Protocol}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures use context $C=256$, target horizon $S=64$ ($W=320$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), latent dimension $D=32$, nominal training stride $16$, and evaluation stride $1$. Development cohorts train at batch $16$ for $15\text{--}20$ fixed epochs, while archive cohorts train at batch $16\text{--}64$ for up to $12$ early-stopped epochs with gradient clipping ($1.0$) and cosine annealing.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ splits chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}) into a fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and validation suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$, with weights and thresholds frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint fitting, selection, and adaptation):} Reusing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for early stopping or stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) biases thresholds, so the primary 118/141-stream archive uses a disjoint \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$): $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits scaling, weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the checkpoint; and $S$-isolated $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$) freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before touching $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Exploratory fixed-epoch suites use $65\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$, while a 4-block check ($50\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$) on the 19 dev streams confirms matching $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.96\%$ for \emph{Lite}; $1.05\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) at lower F1 ($0.133\to 0.071$).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched calibration and dependence):} Splitting $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ windows at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$ across an $S=64$-step gap ($G=320$ shifts FPR by $<0.25$ points). Under independence, $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ gives $2.5$ expected tail windows above $q_{0.995}$; under serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$ via Sokal's $c=5$, median $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}} = 65$), only $0.325$ independent exceedances remain, making $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ an empirical operating cutoff rather than a statistical tail guarantee (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}).

\paragraph{Statistical vs.\ Practical Significance}
Seeds average per stream with seed standard deviation (SD) $\le 0.008$ on causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER, making the stream the independent unit. We report both \emph{dataset-clustered inference}, using $95\%$ cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) and exact $2^D$ sign-flip tests, and stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests ($2{,}000\text{--}4{,}000$ resamples), applying Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} across the four primary contrasts (\emph{Lite} vs.\ 1D and TS-JEPA on F1 and ER). We also separate zero-tuning unseen datasets from partially tuned cohorts and pair nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) tuning selection with 9-fold untouched-archive exclusion sensitivity (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Cohorts, Baseline Fairness, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Causal and buffered runs share identical splits:
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts:} Used during design (33-stream suite shares $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for thresholds and blending), these cover the 19-stream dial cache ($6$ sets; Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:matched_alert}) and 33-stream external suite ($19$ non-GHL plus $14$ GHL, $7$ sets; Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Hyperparameters were frozen once on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched archive:} Removing those $23$ streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($37$ strictly zero-tuning unseen streams plus $1$ GECCO, and $80$ unseen streams from $4$ tuned sets; Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit}).

\noindent\emph{Baseline fairness across three tiers:} (i)~\emph{Matched-backbone controls} (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon 1D, and w/o Quench) share identical encoders, epochs, and seeds ($\{42,123,456\}$); we also test Wu--Keogh cycle-swap, phase-desync, and Gaussian-noise faults against $\max_c|x_c(t)|$ \cite{wu2023flawed}. (ii)~\emph{System aggregators} score identical frozen residuals under matched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alert rates. (iii)~\emph{External baselines} span TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}; Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), TranAD, and TimesNet also run across all $118$ untouched streams ($3$ seeds, five-block SPOT). All external models use published configs ($206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ params vs.\ $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ for \emph{Lite}), $W=320$, $64$-step gaps, and SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) on PyTorch 2.4 / RTX 5090 (\url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Stepwise predictions without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} average seeds per stream before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) summaries: (1)~$\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ report healthy alarm rates (mean, median, p90, p95); (2)~Point-F1 (naturally $0.05\text{--}0.25$ at prevalence $\pi$, bounded by $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}=2\pi/(1+\pi)$) and precision-recall (PR) AUC measure step accuracy and ranking; (3)~ER tracks hit fraction against $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$, while causal $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ measures end-to-end online alarm latency from onset $t^*$ to emission $\tau_{\text{alm}}$ (including the $S-1=63$-step fill lag); and (4)~\emph{Auto.}\ vs.\ $q^*=0.05$ vs.\ post-hoc $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$ pair with kurtosis $\gamma_2$ and Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
To isolate which components drive live detection without conflating cached residuals and retrained backbones, we evaluate four controlled ablation tiers across Table~\ref{tab:ablations}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cached dial ladder vs.\ $128$ vs.\ $160$ dials:} On the fixed $19$-stream sequential cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding $1\to 224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$), and pruning fluctuation/relay dials (160 pos.+drift) reaches $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Unweighted $N_{224}$ leaves binary alarms unchanged and shifts archive PR-AUC by $\le 0.0011$. When retrained end-to-end on \emph{Lite} (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), adding a fourth prefix ($160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) gives no Point-F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$) while raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.82\%\to 1.06\%$) and slowing inference by $25.5\%$; on the $118$-stream archive rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} note), $128$-dial \emph{Lite} likewise matches retrained $160$- and $224$-dial panels ($0.078$ vs.\ $0.073\text{--}0.074$ F1).
    \item \emph{Single-factor isolation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Under $3$-seed four-block retraining, four TCN blocks match five on F1 and ER ($p\ge 0.23$, saving $23.3\text{k}$ params), and $64$D pooling heads lift causal ER ($55.9\%\to 63.1\%$). Sequential rollout at $M=4$ drops buffered F1 ($0.239\to 0.195$) and doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%\to 4.0\%$), while expanding $160\to 224$ dials lifts ER only by inflating $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$.
    \item \emph{Quenching omission:} On a single-seed $118$-stream screen, predictor-row edits (Q1--Q4) leave causal F1 flat ($0.0901$ vs.\ $0.0904$) while shifting unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS, so \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
    \item \emph{Dimensionality, damping, and quorum sweeps:} Retraining $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ ($3$ seeds) beats Multi-Horizon 1D at every size ($56.1\%\text{--}61.0\%$ vs.\ $42.5\%\text{--}48.9\%$ ER). Narrow $D=8$ underfits buffered F1 ($0.131$ vs.\ $0.216$ at $D=32$); $D\in\{16,32\}$ plateaus with peak PR-AUC at $D=32$ ($0.269$), while $D=64$ dips to $0.068$ causal F1. Performance is flat across whitening damping $[10^{-3},10^{-2}]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.093$ F1, $\le 0.75\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) and $q_{\text{dial}}\in[0.975,0.990]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.090$ F1 as $K_{\text{Lite}}$ adapts $21.2\to 13.0$). Varying $\kappa\in[1.00,1.20]$ trades ER ($63.3\%\to 55.1\%$) against $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.21\%\to 0.55\%$); $\kappa=1.12$ holds $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}<1\%$ before delay jumps at $\kappa=1.20$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate rules fail through three distinct statistical mechanisms on the cached 19-stream residuals (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Cross-coordinate false-alarm compounding:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds marginal errors across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Discrete finite-sample calibration floor:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k at $0.5\%$ across 128 coordinates or BH FDR ($q=0.005$) across 224 dials fails even under stationarity because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot fall below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, collapsing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{Out-of-sample distributional instability vs.\ stable aggregators:} Calibrating an aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) bypasses the discrete floor but exposes out-of-sample tail sensitivity. Discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ from boundary ties, while recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$ under mild cross-block drift. Among distributionally stable aggregators on this cache, the 160-dial panel reaches $0.098$ Point-F1 at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, ahead of 128-coordinate top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.056\text{--}0.072$), while Fisher pooling reaches $0.109$ at $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Independently trained archive comparisons appear in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Synthetic vs.\ Natural Failures}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Separating fault mechanisms clarifies what the latent breaker panel can and cannot catch. The latent encoder reliably flags \emph{cross-channel consistency violations}, \emph{abrupt multi-sensor transients}, and \emph{in-range phase shifts} because they perturb $>K_{\text{Lite}}$ whitened coordinates simultaneously. Conversely, isolated single-sensor hardware faults barely move the 32D summary, whether from stuck-at freezing, variance collapse, rail saturation, quantization steps, packet dropouts, or single-channel bias drift. In these single-sensor cases, $K-1$ healthy channels dilute the input projection by $1/K$ and terminal LayerNorm rescales the remaining perturbation across all $32$ coordinates; on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip.

To test an auxiliary channel-level screen across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$), we threshold three 64-step per-channel statistics at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ with a $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD) floor and $24$-of-$40$ persistence. These statistics track log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, first-order autoregressive (AR) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$. Across $57$ injected $200$-step single-channel events per class, the variance-collapse union lifts recall on collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%\text{--}82.5\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}94.7\%$, and the full branch lifts recall on shocks, linear degradation, and bias from $59.6\%\text{--}75.4\%$ to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$. On \emph{unmodified} test streams, however, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ climbs from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full branch). Because synthetic perturbations omit intermittent telemetry dropouts, quantization coarsening, and coupled hardware degradation, these injections demonstrate synthetic sensitivity only; we claim no real-world sensor-fault coverage and keep the channel branch disabled by default.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End and Observation vs.\ Latent Space}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Retraining with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams ($3$ seeds) reveals a clear channel-count threshold. Shell splitting lifts high-channel spacecraft F1 ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal $0.212 \to 0.321$) but lowers F1 on $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), motivating the $K \ge 25$ gate in \emph{Lite}. Across the 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), raw-observation reconstructors exhibit heavy tails, strong drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$). Adding an observation-space LayerNorm bottleneck cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$), while learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). Because \emph{Lite} and Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) share nearly identical score kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$), the panel's live gain stems from coordinate isolation rather than lighter tails.

\subsection{Computational Complexity and Real-Time Deployment Considerations}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
Operational spacecraft and industrial monitors require bounded memory and deterministic full-pipeline latency across channel standardization, $W=320$ ring-buffer updates, encoder-predictor inference, $96\times 96$ whitening, stabilization blending, and 128-dial quorum evaluation. Across $K \in \{5, 9, 25, 38, 55, 72\}$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} holds $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable parameters ($1.10\text{--}1.18\,\text{MB}$ FP32 weights including the EMA target encoder) plus $44.2\text{--}128.4\,\text{KB}$ of persistent per-stream state. Full-pipeline GPU inference (batch $64$, RTX 5090, $33\text{--}91\,\text{MB}$ peak memory) takes $0.162\text{--}0.253\,\text{ms}$/window, adding only $0.018\text{--}0.051\,\text{ms}$ over the neural forward pass ($0.144\text{--}0.201\,\text{ms}$). On a single CPU thread (batch $1$, embedded-processor proxy), full-pipeline streaming takes $7.11\text{--}7.45\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \le 9$ ($134\text{--}141$ windows/s, $>2\times$ Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate) and $9.76\text{--}10.79\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \in [25, 72]$ ($93\text{--}102$ windows/s, or $7.28\text{--}10.24\,\text{ms}$ on $8$ threads). Under unannounced sensor dropouts on the 19 development streams (mean-imputing dropped channels without retraining), dropping one or two active sensors reduces causal ER gradually from $58.9\%$ to $55.9\%\text{--}54.7\%$ (Point-F1 $0.093$ to $0.066$) while raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $1.5\%$ to $3.4\%\text{--}7.9\%$.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults that evolve much slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ Point-F1 across all $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). When context and target windows ride a slow ramp together, their latent representations shift in tandem and predictive residuals cancel. Across linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$ ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points). Testing six onset profiles on clean multivariate segments ($3$ seeds: abrupt step, $L\in\{64,256,1024\}$ ramps, exponential degradation $1-e^{-t/512}$, and $0.001\sigma$/step accumulating bias) confirms this mechanism. From abrupt steps to accumulating bias, mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ to $586.7$ steps ($433.9$ steps on exponential degradation) as context-to-target displacement $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened residual RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

To test whether a long-term reference closes this blind spot without altering \emph{Lite} weights, we pair the panel with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ context features and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. On the $14$ real GHL ramp streams ($3$ seeds), this frozen-reference monitor lifts causal ER from $0.8\%$ (\emph{Lite} alone, $0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a two-timescale union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, Point-F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Because GHL's nominal baselines keep wandering after fault episodes, however, uncompensated test FPR rises to $74.4\%$. Sliding-window prediction thus rejects slow nominal drift at the cost of missing slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift catches slow ramps at the cost of flagging benign baseline wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Post-Alarm Sensor Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across horizons ($S_1{=}16, S_2{=}32, S_3{=}64$) and drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $S_2, S_3$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking maps alarms back to physical sensors. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Sub-quorum single-sensor flatlines remain limited by $1/K$ latent dilution.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Concept drift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration vs.\ concept drift (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and serial dependence while erasing chronological drift. Across $n=118$, block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ falls from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ ($\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams), blaming post-calibration drift for $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms.
    \item \emph{Internal drift drivers:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) match stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$). They diverge through a $3.1\times$ larger stabilized residual-covariance shift ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), which inflates quorum variance ($4.8\times$) and drives $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In fault-free dev stress tests ($3$ seeds), terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and $45^\circ$ rotations stay moderate ($6.8\%$), whereas mean steps, duty cycles, and baseline walks fail to self-settle ($14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ FPR; $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$). Blindly auto-recalibrating on $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, however, absorbs persistent faults: fitting $400$ post-shift windows during a persistent $+2.5\sigma$ fault cuts post-buffer recall to $9.1\%$. Our conditional policy therefore flags $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ under frozen weights, quarantines candidate windows, and permits threshold updates only after operator or control-state confirmation ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall preserved; $4.8\%$ FPR on confirmed shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability and Two-Stage Deployment Funnel}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite histories lack distribution-free tail guarantees. Serial dependence (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams with $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Resampling finite blocks via Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) tests dependence sensitivity rather than generating unobserved extremes. On an independent $3$-seed audit across $n=118$, `MBB-Median` matches that audit's unbootstrapped quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR; $62.7\%$ vs.\ $60.3\%$ p95; $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1), matching the primary run's $0.93\%$ median and $64.7\%$ p95 (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Conservative `MBB-UCB90` and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds cost $8\text{--}17$ ER points, while thinning raises mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

Autonomous operation relies on a two-stage acceptance funnel (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). After disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$ isolation, requiring empirical $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59/118$ ($50.0\%$) streams (cross-fitting below $2{,}500$ steps cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$). Auditing frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ accepts $52$ streams ($44.1\%$ of $118$; $0.9\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $0.076$ vs.\ $0.034$ F1 over 1D, $p=3.2{\times}10^{-5}$) and rejects $7$ ($35$ pass optional $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$). Routing all $66$ non-accepted streams to 1D SPOT produces a $118$-stream hybrid ($0.094$ F1, $60.9\%$ ER, $4.6\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) supervised by quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}$ monitoring.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
In practice, deploying \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} follows a gated workflow designed for unlabelled telemetry. Each incoming stream first passes through the two-stage acceptance funnel: streams with at least $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ calibration windows and a frozen held-out false positive rate at or below $5\%$ ($52/118$ archive streams) run the coordinate tripwire detector autonomously. The remaining $66$ short or rapidly drifting streams route to the 1D SPOT fallback, avoiding blind spots where tail estimates are statistically unsupported. When an alarm fires, batched LOCO attribution isolates the physical sensors driving the latent exceedance ($85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy), giving operators an actionable starting point without retraining. If persistent distribution shift triggers the $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ monitor, candidate windows enter a $400$-window quarantine buffer until an operator or control-state signal confirms a benign mode switch, preventing silent adaptation to genuine faults.

This workflow relies on per-stream healthy-prefix fitting rather than zero-shot pretrained foundation weights. Across aerospace, server, and process datasets, channel counts span $K=1$ to $55$ with incompatible physical units, sampling cadences, and nominal regimes. Fitting each stream's healthy prefix directly transfers the frozen architecture, algebraic normalization, and calibration rule across domains while running at $93\text{--}141$ windows/s on one CPU thread without cross-asset label leakage.

Taken together, our null results on physics-inspired regularizers and matched-FPR ranking parity with scalar JEPAs clarify where the performance gain originates. The advantage lies in operating-point calibration rather than backbone complexity: isolating coordinates keeps one drifting dimension from raising a shared scalar threshold. Practitioners should still avoid deploying coordinate tripwires alone in three regimes where complementary guards are required: $1/K$-diluted single-channel flatlines in high-dimensional streams, unconditioned slow-ramp baseline wander such as GHL process drift, and short prefixes below $500$ calibration windows.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Decomposing multi-horizon latent predictive errors into independently calibrated coordinate tripwires (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} at $128$ dials; \emph{Laminar-JEPA} at $224$ dials plus $32$ separate onset relays) yields three main findings across $33$ design-stage and $118$ untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Coordinate isolation and live operating point:} Per-dial tripwires prevent drifting coordinates from inflating a shared scalar threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), catching contextual and phase-desync faults missed by 1D SPOT \cite{wu2023flawed}. Across $118$ untouched streams ($9/9$ datasets, $37$ zero-tuning unseen streams, and $9$-fold nested LODO), \emph{Lite} lifts causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$) and cuts end-to-end latency from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps. Overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) remains cluster-uncertain, while streams meeting $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ double Point-F1 ($0.040\to 0.085$, sign-flip $p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{Operating point vs.\ external baselines:} Matched test-FPR checks show no ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs. Under untouched-archive SPOT calibration, however, TimesNet and TranAD suffer $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and fall to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR (vs.\ $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}), confirming a distinct label-free operating point paired with post-alarm LOCO sensor attribution.
    \item \emph{Drift, gated acceptance, and quarantined adaptation:} Block permutation links $92.3\%$ of tail false alarms to covariance drift. Autonomous deployment pairs two-stage held-out acceptance ($52/118$ accepted, $66$ fallback) with quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ recalibration requiring confirmed mode switches ($31.9\%$ vs.\ $9.1\%$ fault recall; $4.8\%$ confirmed FPR).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Key directions include low-rank streaming updates to $\mathbf{\Omega}$ and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ without $400$-window buffers, control-conditioned slow-ramp monitoring to cut GHL wander ($74.4\%$ test FPR), and low-FPR univariate guards for $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines.
