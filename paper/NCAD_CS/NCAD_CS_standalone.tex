\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We investigate how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a 32-dimensional latent space under distribution shift, examining unconstrained predictors, Operator-Entropy JEPA (OpEntropy JEPA) with spectral conditioning, and Reynolds-Stress JEPA with a Cayley-accretive Crouzeix linear core and Stiefel covariance closure. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets (19 non-Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams at five seeds and 14 GHL streams at three seeds) under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) calibration, we evaluate stream-macro realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and out-of-sample calibration error from the 0.1\% design risk as primary outcomes. Our central contribution is relative false-alarm suppression and drift reduction rather than attainment of the nominal 0.1\% target or universal detection superiority. Latent scoring lowers unweighted stream-macro held-out FPRs three-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (9.3\% to 10.9\% for base latent predictors versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD across the 19 non-GHL streams; 5.5\% to 6.4\% versus 19.6\% to 19.9\% stream-macro across all 33 streams) and eight- to ten-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.9\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\% across 19 non-GHL streams; 1.5\% to 2.0\% versus 14.2\% to 16.1\% stream-macro across all 33 streams), while point F1 and precision-recall ranking remain comparable to baselines. Matched-encoder factorial ablations show that Layer Normalization (LayerNorm) bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. Equivalence testing confirms that Koopman spectral regularization conditions operator spectra without altering point F1 within a 0.015 to 0.030 margin, while short prediction horizons (one to four steps) eliminate structural emission latency and hybrid sensor variance monitors recover quiet single-channel faults at a quantified false-alarm cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains sensor jitter and scale disparities, observation residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), heavy-tailed residuals suffer calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} Predicting the \emph{summary} of a future window, rather than every raw sample, ignores unforecastable sensor jitter. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which forecast a 32-dimensional latent summary of the next $S$ steps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for low-latency tripwires) from the preceding 256, without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}. Beyond unconstrained predictors (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formulate physically grounded variants: an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a Bregman energy barrier, and a \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} enforcing Cayley-accretive Crouzeix contractivity and Stiefel positive semidefinite second-moment closure. We evaluate these structures as geometric conditioning priors via equivalence tests rather than claims of detection superiority. Our contribution is relative false-alarm suppression and reduced calibration-to-holdout drift rather than solved absolute calibration at $q=10^{-3}$. We evaluate: (i)~how much latent residuals reduce realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ under static, declustered, and rolling calibrators; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent projection, and horizon isolate the mechanism of improvement; (iii)~how detection varies across abrupt, gradual, and quiet single-channel faults (and the false-alarm cost of hybrid sensor monitors); and (iv)~the trade-off between score-emission latency ($S-1$) and detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Factorial isolation of tail compression vs.\ drift reduction:} Across 14 matched-encoder and non-parametric arms ($269\text{k}\text{--}307\text{k}$ online parameters), a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) bottleneck bounds heavy residual tails ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), whereas a learned latent projection halves calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.193\text{--}0.214$) relative to normalized observation forecasters (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \textbf{Fewer false alarms, comparable detection, and hybrid quiet-fault recovery:} On 33 streams from seven datasets, latent scoring cuts held-out FPRs and calibration error three-fold under retrospective (buffered) attribution and eight- to ten-fold under strictly causal streaming, and nearly doubles event recall at a $1\%$ realized FPR budget. Point-wise F1 (Point-F1) and precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) remain comparable to baselines, and pairing latent prediction with a sensor variance monitor raises quiet single-channel event recall from $0.19\text{--}0.28$ to $0.42\text{--}0.50$ at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)).
    \item \textbf{Verified operator conditioning and short-horizon latency control:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves Koopman non-degeneracy across the full objective (including the discrete Lyapunov covariance defect, repulsive origin curvature at $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{0}$, and finite-$\epsilon$ energy bounds), while the Cayley-accretive Crouzeix linear core and sign-stabilized QR Stiefel retractions enforce contractive linear transport and interior covariance closure. Equivalence tests confirm that Koopman spectral regularization leaves Point-F1 unchanged within $\Delta = \pm 0.015\text{--}0.030$ while shortening detection delay, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and~\ref{tab:ablations}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Decomposed calibration error across static, declustered, and adaptive rules:} A refitted permutation decomposition estimates point-estimate shares of $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ for inter-block drift in pooled excess JEPA risk above $q=10^{-3}$ ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ with resolved positive confidence intervals (CIs) for reconstructors, whereas per-stream JEPA CIs include zero across 11 zero-FPR streams), and runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles narrow the gap on step and variance shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timesteps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that destabilize extreme-value threshold fitting under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\textbf{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximizes contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent prediction across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ produced by a separate target encoder, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}. How latent predictive residuals behave under unsupervised extreme-value threshold calibration remains unexplored.

\noindent\textbf{Koopman Operator Theory and Physics-Inspired Priors:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral} lifts nonlinear dynamics into an observable space governed by a linear transition matrix $\mathbf{K}$ \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa}. We regularize the singular value spectrum of $\mathbf{K}$ via Von Neumann spectral entropy and a Bregman energy barrier, distinguishing \emph{physics-inspired} algebraic priors on abstract latent coordinates from \emph{physics-informed} conservation laws and testing whether operator conditioning alters downstream detection via equivalence tests.

\noindent\textbf{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration and Evaluation Rigor:} Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to score exceedances above quantile $u$ to extrapolate a threshold at design risk $q$. However, continuous telemetry violates the classical independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption through short-range serial correlation (quantified by the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}) and inter-block regime drift. Because ``point-adjusted'' F1 severely inflates scores \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}, we evaluate all models using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm operating curves.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out protocol and 12 are reserved with explicit inclusion accounting below. Streams span four physical domains and distinguish naturally occurring operational or physiological anomalies from testbed or simulated faults: \emph{spacecraft and solar telemetry} (six SMAP, $K{=}25$, $7$ anomaly events; four primary and two short-nominal MSL entities, $K{=}55$, $6$ primary events; and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}, $K{=}38$, $6{,}615$ flare events, all natural operational faults); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$, $K{=}9$, $72$ natural gait-freezing episodes, where $S{=}64$ is a $1\,\text{s}$ horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams, $K{=}16$, $26$ testbed valve/heater faults; the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, $K{=}18$, $2$ actuator faults; and GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution, $K{=}9$, $37$ natural hydrological anomalies); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Metro I-94 hourly highway traffic, $K{=}5$, $25$ natural traffic events; Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, $K{=}5$; CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, $K{=}2$; and six CICIDS network streams, $K{=}72$, simulated attack flows). Separately, Cohort~3 (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}) injects six controlled synthetic single-channel faults into six real streams.

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$ across the 33 primary streams) and labeled anomaly test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$); channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatlines, matching standardized archive subsets in prior benchmarks \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly}.

Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Inclusion in the primary multi-seed protocol required two prespecified criteria: (i)~$N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows ($M_{\text{val}} = \lceil 0.35\,T_{\text{nom}}\rceil - 319 \ge 1{,}000$, i.e., $T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$), and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed runtime ($K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$). Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). All 12 reserved streams are accounted for: five fail $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ (CalIt2 \texttt{traffic}, $T_{\text{nom}}{=}940, N_{\text{cal}}{=}4$; MSL \texttt{C-2}, $T_{\text{nom}}{=}764, N_{\text{cal}}{=}0$; MSL \texttt{D-16}, $T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}451, N_{\text{cal}}{=}94$; Room-Occupancy \texttt{default}, $T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}073, N_{\text{cal}}{=}28$; and \texttt{1}, $T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}492, N_{\text{cal}}{=}101$); six network streams exceed the stride-1 multi-seed runtime envelope (CICIDS \texttt{0,2,3,5,6,7}, $K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \in [1.36\times 10^5, 4.15\times 10^5]$); and GECCO \texttt{water\_quality} ($K{=}9$, $1$ held-out event) is used in Cohorts 3--4. All 11 datasets and 130 archive streams appear in the secondary single-seed cross-check (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware platforms ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations (SDs), zero-tuning leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)).

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} (Stage~1), a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the series at index $t$ into: (i)~a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$), establishing the nominal regime; and (ii)~a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$), inspected for trajectory departures.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit leptokurtic sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro} and~\ref{sec:related}), we shift prediction into a 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$, Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. An online context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ into $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while a target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. To prevent constant-vector collapse, target weights $\phi$ are updated as an EMA of online weights $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with cosine momentum $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures: Operator-Entropy and Reynolds-Stress JEPA}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We evaluate three nested latent predictors sharing the same multiscale encoder backbone and coordinate saliency gate: an unconstrained multi-layer perceptron (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), a spectral-regularized Koopman transition (\texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA}), and a structurally constrained second-moment closure model (\texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}).

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Conditioning)}
\noindent\textbf{Formulation.} Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral} represents nonlinear dynamics as a linear map on learned observables, $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$ with $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$. Trained on prediction error alone, $\mathbf{K}$ can suffer rank or norm collapse. We regularize both the shape and scale of its singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ via $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K})$. Normalizing $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ into $p_i = (\lambda_i + \epsilon_{\log}) / \sum_{j=1}^D (\lambda_j + \epsilon_{\log})$ ($\epsilon_{\log} = 10^{-12}$) defines the Von Neumann spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D p_i \log p_i$ and the \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} relative to uniform $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Because $\Delta\mathcal{H}(c\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K})$ is scale-invariant for $c > 0$, a convex Bregman barrier on the energy ratio $r(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / D$ prevents norm decay ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) &= r(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log\bigl(r(\mathbf{K}) + \epsilon\bigr) \\
    &\quad + \log(1 + \epsilon) \ge 0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bregman_barrier}
\end{equation}
Combining both terms yields the training objective:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} &= \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bigl\| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} \bigr\|_2^2 \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{Lyap}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:op_entropy_loss}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the batch size, $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$, and $\beta_{\text{Lyap}} = 0.02$ weights the discrete Lyapunov defect $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}} = D^{-1} \|\mathbf{K}\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\text{ctx}}\mathbf{K}^\top - \mathbf{C}_{\text{tgt}}\|_F^2$ on trace-normalized batch covariances $\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\text{ctx}} = \operatorname{sg}(\operatorname{Cov}(z_{\text{ctx}})) / (\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{Cov}(z_{\text{ctx}}))/D + 10^{-6})$ and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{tgt}} = \operatorname{Cov}(z_{\text{tgt}}) / (\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{Cov}(z_{\text{tgt}}))/D + 10^{-6})$ using stop-gradient $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$. We verify both finite-$\epsilon$ repulsion and non-degeneracy across the complete objective.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Degeneracy, Lyapunov Invariance, and Finite-$\epsilon$ Energy Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathbf{M} = (BD)^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top$ denote the cross-covariance under (\ref{eq:op_entropy_loss}) with $\lambda_{\text{ent}}, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$ and $\epsilon \in (0, 1)$:
(i)~\emph{Vanishing Lyapunov origin gradient and non-stationarity for $\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$:} At $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$, and because $\mathbf{K}\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\text{ctx}}\mathbf{K}^\top$ is quadratic in $\mathbf{K}$, the derivative $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$ vanishes identically. Thus $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}}(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$, so $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is not stationary under the full objective.
(ii)~\emph{Strict local maximum when $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$:} Even if $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$, for any $\mathbf{K}$ with $r(\mathbf{K}) < 1-\epsilon$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\frac{2(1 - r(\mathbf{K}) - \epsilon)}{D(r(\mathbf{K}) + \epsilon)}\mathbf{K}$ points strictly radially outward, and the origin Hessian $\nabla^2_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{0}) = -\frac{2(1-\epsilon)}{D\epsilon}\mathbf{I}_{D^2} \prec \mathbf{0}$ is strictly negative definite, making $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ an unstable repeller.
(iii)~\emph{Finite-$\epsilon$ energy lower bound and asymptotic barrier:} Over $r \ge 0$, $\Phi_{\text{energy}}$ attains its global minimum at $r^* = 1-\epsilon$ with value $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(r^*) = \ln(1+\epsilon) - \epsilon = -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2} + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^3) \approx -5\times 10^{-13}$ for $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ (non-negative under standard numerical clamping $\max(\Phi_{\text{energy}}, 0)$). At $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$, the finite barrier is $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{0}) = \ln(1+\epsilon) - 1 - \ln\epsilon \approx 12.82$~nats with steep restoring slope $\partial\Phi_{\text{energy}}/\partial r|_{r=0^+} = 1 - 1/\epsilon \approx -10^6$, recovering an infinite barrier in the asymptotic limit $\lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{0}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$, spectral probabilities $p_i = 1/D$ yield $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$. For $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$, let $\mathbf{\Delta}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathbf{K}\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\text{ctx}}\mathbf{K}^\top - \mathbf{C}_{\text{tgt}}$. Expanding the directional derivative in direction $\mathbf{V}$ gives $\langle \nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}(\mathbf{K}), \mathbf{V}\rangle = \frac{4}{D}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{\Delta}(\mathbf{K})\mathbf{K}\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\text{ctx}}\mathbf{V}^\top)$, which vanishes at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$, confirming $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$. By the chain rule on (\ref{eq:bregman_barrier}), $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\frac{2(1-r(\mathbf{K})-\epsilon)}{D(r(\mathbf{K})+\epsilon)}\mathbf{K}$, vanishing at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ (leaving $-2\mathbf{M}$ when $\mathbf{M}\neq\mathbf{0}$) with negative-definite Jacobian $-2(1-\epsilon)(D\epsilon)^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{D^2} \prec \mathbf{0}$. Differentiating $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(r)$ gives $d\Phi_{\text{energy}}/dr = 1 - (r+\epsilon)^{-1}$, vanishing at $r^* = 1-\epsilon$ where $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(1-\epsilon) = \ln(1+\epsilon) - \epsilon \approx -5\times 10^{-13}$. Evaluating at $r=0, \epsilon=10^{-6}$ yields $12.82$~nats, with $\lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} [-\ln\epsilon] = +\infty$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Encoder Non-Collapse):} While Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} prevents collapse of $\mathbf{K}$, encoder collapse ($z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}} \to \mathbf{c}$) is prevented structurally by terminal LayerNorm ($\|z\|_2 = \sqrt{D}$), EMA momentum with stop-gradient decoupling, and data-projected covariance entropy on $\mathbf{K}z_{\text{ctx}}$ ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) \approx 11.6$, Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a)).

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Second-Moment Subgrid Closure)}
\noindent\textbf{Physical Motivation and Structural Parsimony.} Inspired by Reynolds averaging in turbulence, which splits a flow into its resolved mean $\bar{z}$ and subgrid covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$, Reynolds-Stress JEPA predicts both moments while enforcing stability and positive semidefinite (PSD) geometry directly inside the network layers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textit{Cayley-accretive Crouzeix linear core and stability bounds:} The predictor separates linear transport from nonlinear shear: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}} z_{\text{ctx}} + \tanh(\gamma_{\text{shear}})\psi_{\text{MLP}}(z_{\text{ctx}})$. The linear core parameterizes a strictly accretive generator $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{S}_{\text{skew}} + \mathbf{D}_{\text{visc}}$, with skew-symmetric $\mathbf{S}_{\text{skew}} = 0.5(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}^\top)$ and PSD $\mathbf{D}_{\text{visc}} = D^{-1}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^\top + \nu_0\mathbf{I}_D \succeq \nu_0\mathbf{I}_D$ ($\nu_0 = 10^{-3}$), and applies the Cayley transform $\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}} = (\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{G})^{-1}(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{G})$. Because $\operatorname{Re}\langle u, \mathbf{G}u\rangle = \langle u, \mathbf{D}_{\text{visc}}u\rangle \ge \nu_0\|u\|_2^2$, substituting $u = (\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{G})^{-1}v$ into $\|(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{G})u\|_2^2 - \|(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G})u\|_2^2 = 4\operatorname{Re}\langle u, \mathbf{G}u\rangle \ge 4\nu_0\|u\|_2^2$ proves that the linear core is a strict Euclidean operator-norm contraction: $\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}}\|_2 \le (1-\nu_0)/(1+\nu_0) < 1$ ($0.9980$). This places its numerical range $W(\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}}) \subset \mathbb{D}$ strictly inside the open unit disk. By Crouzeix's theorem \cite{crouzeix2004numerical}, $\|p(\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}})\|_2 \le (1+\sqrt{2})\sup_{z \in \mathbb{D}}|p(z)|$ for every polynomial $p$. Across trained models, numerical verification confirms $\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}}\|_2 \in [0.998006, 0.998009]$, spectral radius $\rho(\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}}) \in [0.996449, 0.997700]$, and numerical radius $r_{\text{num}}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}}) \in [0.997639, 0.997841]$, while nonlinear shear corrections remain perturbative ($|\tanh(\gamma_{\text{shear}})| \in [0.014, 0.033]$).
    \item \textit{Sign-stabilized QR Stiefel retraction and PSD cone closure:} Alongside $\bar{z}_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, the encoder centers the $64$-channel TCN sequence $h(\tau) - \bar{h}$ and projects it via $\mathbf{Q}_{\text{St}} \in \mathrm{St}(32, 64)$ into fluctuations $z'_{\text{tgt}}(\tau) \in \mathbb{R}^D$, which a subgrid frame $\mathbf{V} = [\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_{d_s}]^\top \in \mathrm{St}(d_s, 32)$ ($d_s = 16$) compresses into $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} \in \mathcal{S}_+^{d_s}$. Both $\mathbf{Q}_{\text{St}}$ and $\mathbf{V}$ are initialized to the truncated identity and retracted onto the column-orthonormal Stiefel manifold via sign-stabilized reduced QR factorization ($\mathbf{W}^\top = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{R}$, multiplying column $i$ of $\mathbf{Q}$ by $\operatorname{sign}(R_{ii})$ with $0 \mapsto +1$ to eliminate QR sign-flip gradient discontinuities). On $\bar{z} = 0.5(z_{\text{ctx}} + \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}})$, the closure head predicts $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \sum_{k=1}^{d_s} c_k(\bar{z})\mathbf{v}_k\mathbf{v}_k^\top + \gamma\mathbf{I}_{d_s} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{d_s}$ with positive weights $c_k = \operatorname{softplus}(w_k(\bar{z})) + 10^{-4}$ and isotropic floor $\gamma = 10^{-3}$ ($\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}) \ge 10^{-3}$ strictly enforced by construction) under the two-term objective:
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} &= \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B \bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\bigr\|_2^2 \\
    &\quad + \frac{\alpha_{\text{stress}}}{B d_s^2}\sum_{b=1}^B \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}^{(b)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}^{(b)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{\text{stress}} = 0.1$ and no auxiliary loss penalties.

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, windows are scored at stride $1$. Let $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denote the latent residual and $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv g_D(\mathbf{e})$ the dimension-agnostic coordinate saliency gate defined in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). Unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} uses $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$. Because terminal LayerNorm strips radial amplitude, \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} tracks pre-LayerNorm energy ($e_{\text{ctx}}, e_{\text{tgt}}$) with $e_{\text{pred}} = e_{\text{ctx}}\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}\|_2 / (\|z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2 + 10^{-6})$, while \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} adds 4-octave dyadic shell-energy leakage $\Delta E_{\text{LP}}(t)$ and saliency-gated stress discrepancy ($k_s = \min(K, d_s)$):
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{OpEnt}}(t) &= g\bigl(\mathbf{e}(t)\bigr) + 0.2\,\frac{|e_{\text{tgt}}(t) - e_{\text{pred}}(t)|}{e_{\text{tgt}}(t) + e_{\text{pred}}(t) + 10^{-6}}, \label{eq:opent_score} \\
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{Rey}}(t) &= g\bigl(\mathbf{e}(t)\bigr) + 0.2\,\Delta E_{\text{LP}}(t) \nonumber \\
    &\quad + \frac{0.2}{\sqrt{k_s}}\,g_{d_s^2}\bigl(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}})\bigr), \label{eq:reynolds_score}
\end{align}
where $g_{d_s^2}$ operates on the vectorized $d_s \times d_s$ covariance departure ($d_s^2 = 256$) normalized by $\sqrt{k_s}$ to preserve scale compatibility with $g_D$.

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
We formalize online evaluation under three criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, the detector observes only $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$; neither encoder accesses observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ summarizes suspect observations $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$ spanning sample indices $[t, t+S-1]$, score $s_t$ is emitted at the trailing edge $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3), creating a structural emission lag of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO, and $63\,\text{h}$ on hourly Metro). This lag is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$ from onset $t^*$ to the first alarm $t_{\text{alarm}} \ge t^*$. Shortening the horizon to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates emission latency for rapid tripwires (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \textbf{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} \emph{Causal trailing attribution} assigns $s_t$ only to $\tau = t + S - 1$ for zero-lookahead streaming. \emph{Retrospective buffered smear attribution} averages $s_t$ across all suspect timestamps $[t, t+S-1]$, introducing fixed-lag smoothing. Both regimes are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and Scale Equivariance}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
To amplify sparse departures in high-dimensional representations while mitigating channel drowning, we define the coordinate saliency gate $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for any vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    g_d(\mathbf{v}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{d \sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}, \\
    w_i &= \frac{\exp\bigl(|v_i| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^d \exp\bigl(|v_j| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}, \quad \sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau_g = 0.5$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. When $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$, $g_d(\mathbf{0}) = 0$. For non-zero $\mathbf{v}$, rescaling $\mathbf{v} \mapsto c\mathbf{v}$ with $c > 0$ yields $\sigma_{c\mathbf{v}} = c\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon = c\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} + (1-c)\epsilon$. In the operational regime where $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 \gg \epsilon$, the softmax weights $w_i$ are scale-invariant up to an $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/\|\mathbf{v}\|_2)$ perturbation, satisfying approximate degree-1 homogeneity $g_d(c\mathbf{v}) \approx c\,g_d(\mathbf{v})$ and recovering exact degree-1 homogeneity in the limit $\epsilon \to 0^+$. Input channels use uniform weights in the deployed pipeline (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
For multivariate streams ($K > 1$), a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Each projected channel is split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point rFFT grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax scale router. Because smooth Fourier shells overlap at band edges ($\sum \Psi_j \equiv 1$ rather than $\sum \Psi_j^2 \equiv 1$), this acts as a multiscale front-end and frequency-domain leakage feature ($\Delta E_{\text{LP}}$) rather than an orthogonal Parseval wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ from nominal calibration data alone, Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits exceedances $Y = \mathcal{S}(t) - u > 0$ above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with shape $\xi$ and scale $\sigma$ via Grimshaw maximum-likelihood estimation \cite{grimshaw1993computing} with bounded L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10]$). Inverting the GPD tail at target risk $q = 10^{-3}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = \begin{cases}
    u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right], & |\xi| \ge 10^{-5}, \\
    u - \sigma \ln\left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right), & |\xi| < 10^{-5},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:spot_threshold}
\end{equation}
where the lower branch is the continuous exponential-tail limit as $\xi \to 0$, evaluated directly to avoid division by zero. Here $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $N_u$ count post-attribution score timestamps on the calibration block. Primary tables use standard SPOT; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} evaluates runs-declustered GPD fitting, rolling calibrators, and kurtosis-scaled thresholds ($\tau_{\text{eff}}$ and $\tau_{\text{exc}}$).

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$. Standardization parameters and neural weights are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$.

Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2). Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations serve as context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}})$. Splitting $v$ at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H)$ (used to fit the SPOT threshold at $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, excluding $S=64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S)$. Because suspect target windows have length $S=64$, the last calibration target and first evaluation target are $100\%$ disjoint; the first $C=256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. A stricter full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320)$ so even passive context is disjoint across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) leaves stream-macro causal FPR virtually unchanged ($2.07\% \to 2.18\%$ for OpEntropy and $3.02\% \to 3.24\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $12.15\% \to 13.97\%$ for TimesNet and $9.05\% \to 9.73\%$ for TranAD). All primary tables use fixed standard SPOT thresholds during testing.

\paragraph{Independent Statistical Units, Multi-Seed Replication, and Equivalence Bounds}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are evaluated across five random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ observations each) and ablation controls use three seeds ($\{7, 42, 2024\}$). To prevent pseudoreplication, every statistical test specifies its independent unit explicitly: (i)~seeds are averaged within each stream first to quantify within-stream optimization variance ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$), (ii)~stream-level paired tests treat the telemetry stream ($n=33$ primary or $n=19$ non-GHL) as the unit of analysis, and (iii)~domain-level tests aggregate streams within each hardware platform first and treat the dataset family ($N=7$) as the independent cluster. We employ three statistical tools:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and false-discovery-rate adjustment:} The Wilcoxon signed-rank test evaluates whether the median paired difference across streams ($n=33$ or $n=19$) is zero without Gaussian assumptions. We adjust $p$-values across multiple comparisons via the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$), report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples), and cross-check at the dataset-cluster level ($N=7$) via exact sign-flip permutation tests and zero-tuning LODO sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for statistical equivalence:} To test whether a physics-inspired regularizer produces practically negligible change relative to an unregularized control on the same backbone ($n=19$ streams), TOST tests $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$), establishing equivalence when $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}) < 0.05$ (i.e., the $90\%$ paired CI lies strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$). We prespecify $\Delta = \pm 0.030$ Point-F1 ($\approx 13\%$ of baseline F1, $<0.09\times$ between-stream SD) and report sensitivity across $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.010, \pm 0.015, \pm 0.020\}$, confirming equivalence down to $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Distributional tail and drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} On nominal windows we measure: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, distinguishing heavy-tailed \emph{leptokurtic} ($\gamma_2 > 0$) from light-tailed \emph{platykurtic} ($\gamma_2 < 0$) residuals; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, quantifying distributional shift between calibration ($F_{\text{cal}}$) and held-out nominal ($F_{\text{hold}}$) blocks.
\end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Cohort 2 (19-stream matched-backbone factorial and operating-curve suite):} 14 controlled arms across 5 seeds, reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 3 (6-stream operator sweep, quiet-fault, and hybrid-monitor suite):} Factorial regularizer sweep (3 seeds) and six injected fault types with hybrid sensor monitors, reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a),(b).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 4 (5-stream horizon-latency and frequency-shell panels):} Target-horizon sweep ($S \in \{1,4,16,32,64\}$, 3 seeds, matched emission lag $S{-}1$) and dyadic front-end ablation, reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 5 (130-stream single-seed baseline screen):} Secondary breadth cross-check across 11 datasets against NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)), treated strictly as auxiliary evidence rather than equivalent to the multi-seed benchmark.
\end{itemize}
At $K=25$ channels, online trainable parameter counts are closely matched across latent and observation-space backbones: $272{,}266$ online parameters for TS-JEPA ($540{,}148$ total including the stop-gradient EMA target encoder), $268{,}906$ online ($536{,}788$ total) for OpEntropy JEPA, $281{,}732$ online ($549{,}614$ total) for Reynolds-Stress JEPA, $304{,}138$ for matched-encoder \texttt{TCN-ObsPred}, $307{,}402$ for \texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}, $648{,}553$ for TimesNet, and $205{,}746$ for TranAD. Non-finite telemetry entries are zero-imputed with channel standard deviations floored at $10^{-2}$. Causal and retrospective buffered scores receive separately fitted thresholds $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ fitted strictly within their respective calibration windows $v_{\text{cal}}$, where $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $N_u$ count post-attribution score timestamps rather than raw telemetry samples. GPD parameters $(\xi, \sigma)$ above $u=p_{98}$ are estimated via Grimshaw maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE) with a bounded quasi-Newton (L-BFGS-B) fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10], \sigma \ge 10^{-6}$), switching continuously to the exponential tail limit for $|\xi| < 10^{-5}$. All 195 primary multi-seed runs converged under the $20$-epoch schedule on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (NVIDIA RTX 5090, $0.60\text{--}0.84\,\text{ms}$ per window, $184\,\text{MB}$ of GPU memory); stream manifests, fitted thresholds, and source code are available at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate models across three tiers: (i)~\emph{primary calibration and accuracy outcomes}, comprising realized held-out nominal FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ relative to design risk $q = 10^{-3}$, unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC; (ii)~\emph{operating characteristics}, evaluating event recall across deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) and across post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)); and (iii)~\emph{secondary operational endpoints}, reporting unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Operator Regularizer Control on Fixed Architecture}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the spectral regularizer drives anomaly detection accuracy on its fixed Koopman backbone, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) compares Operator-Entropy JEPA against an unregularized control ($\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0$) across all 19 non-GHL streams. Removing the spectral regularizer leaves buffered Point-F1 ($0.233$ vs.\ $0.235$, paired difference $-0.004$, two-sided $p=0.89$) and PR-AUC ($0.351$ vs.\ $0.365$) virtually identical. Two One-Sided Tests confirm formal equivalence within the prespecified $\Delta = \pm 0.030$ margin ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0007$) and across sensitivity checks down to $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0015$) and $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$), where the $90\%$ CI $[-0.0112, +0.0029]$ lies strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$. At the tighter $\Delta = \pm 0.010$ margin, the parametric lower bound extends marginally outside the bound ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.086$), while equivalence holds within $\pm 0.010$ PR-AUC and $\pm 0.025$ event recall.

This fixed-backbone regularizer control aligns with findings on the Reynolds-Stress JEPA architecture: isolating the base predictive residual from the quadratic stress closure discrepancy ($\alpha_{\text{stress}} = 0$) leaves buffered PR-AUC ($0.281$ vs.\ $0.285$) and Point-F1 ($0.167$ vs.\ $0.148$ across all seeds; $0.167$ vs.\ $0.158$ on matched seeds, paired difference $-0.009$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.052, +0.032]$, TOST $p = 0.211$, two-sided Wilcoxon $p = 0.758$) comparable to the full Reynolds model while achieving $0.019$ causal held-out FPR and leading matched-FPR event recall ($0.457$ at $f^*{=}0.01$ and $0.633$ at $f^*{=}0.05$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c)). Across both physics-inspired architectures, regularizer penalties condition latent representations without driving aggregate detection gains, confirming that false-alarm suppression is governed by the joint-embedding prediction framework rather than penalty additions.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Factorial Isolation of the Entropy Term and Energy Barrier}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) crosses the spectral-entropy term and Bregman energy barrier across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in [0, 2.0]$ on six streams (three seeds), tracking effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe} = \exp(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i) \in [1, 32]$ and condition number $\kappa = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min} \ge 1$. Because $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near the identity, the unregularized operator is already near full rank ($\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}) = 31.53$, $\kappa = 1.437$); increasing $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$ tightens conditioning monotonically ($\kappa = 1.255$ at the deployed $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0.50$, and $\kappa = 1.154$, $\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}) = 31.95$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=2.00$) while lowering loss variation ($\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}: 0.565 \to 0.545$). Because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ applies a stop-gradient to $z_{\text{ctx}}$, encoder effective ranks remain invariant across all strengths ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}}) \in [5.16, 5.54]$, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) \in [11.54, 11.58]$), as do false-alarm rates ($0.016\text{--}0.017$ causal, $0.070\text{--}0.074$ buffered; buffered ER $0.250\text{--}0.266$, F1 $0.032\text{--}0.047$; causal F1 $0.004\text{--}0.011$), while the deployed setting ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0.50, \beta_{\text{norm}}=1$) attains the highest causal event recall ($0.444$ vs.\ $0.369$ unregularized). On an eight-stream decoupling panel, pairing detached covariance alignment ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0.02$) with the smooth Bregman barrier cuts causal detection delay from $127.0$ steps (TS-JEPA) and $80.2$ steps ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0$) to $58.2$ steps while raising causal Point-F1 from $0.044$ to $0.094$.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Fault-Class Breakdown, Quiet-Fault Mechanism, and Hybrid Monitor}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Comparing the auxiliary context-variance input-channel gate against uniform channel weighting on four streams confirms that uniform weighting achieves higher macro Point-F1 ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$), so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. Across six injected single-channel fault types on six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), uniform latent encoding (\texttt{Unif.}) achieves $0.278$ event recall on abrupt $+5\sigma$ spikes ($0.306$ with variance gating), versus $0.194$ event recall and $0.020\text{--}0.030$ point recall on persistent quiet faults (flatlines, stuck sensors, dropouts) and low-amplitude steps ($0.043\text{--}0.068$ under variance or median absolute deviation (MAD) gating). When a single channel $k$ freezes or shifts by $\delta$ out of $K$ channels, its contribution to the pre-LayerNorm pooled vector scales as $O(1/K)$ and is rescaled onto the $\sqrt{D}$ sphere by LayerNorm along with nominal cross-channel variance. Standalone sensor monitors, namely a rolling variance-collapse detector (\texttt{lowvar}) and maximum channel $z$-score (\texttt{dev\_maxz}, $0.087\text{--}0.108$ point and $0.250\text{--}0.306$ event recall at $14.1\%$ FPR), trigger on different streams than TS-JEPA. Consequently, a hybrid union detector (\textbf{Hybrid}, triggering when either TS-JEPA or \texttt{lowvar} alarms) raises point recall to $0.202\text{--}0.318$ and event recall to $0.417\text{--}0.500$ across all six fault classes at $+15.1\%$ additional nominal FPR ($1.9\% \to 17.0\%$; or $0.333\text{--}0.472$ event recall at $16.1\%$ FPR with \texttt{dev\_maxz}).

\subsection{Ablation 4: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
In Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), replacing the standard convolutional stem with the 4-shell Fourier front-end increases macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers calibration residual kurtosis ($1.409 \to 1.069$), while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to conservative threshold tightening on SMAP \texttt{P-3}. The module thus acts as a ranking-conditioning stem rather than a uniform F1 gain.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 14 factorial arms across all 19 non-GHL streams under identical splits, training budgets, and comparable parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates six architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Prediction vs.\ reconstruction in observation space (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred} vs.\ \texttt{TCN-ObsRecon}):} Using the exact same \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} backbone, forecasting versus reconstructing raw observations leaves kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 6.10$ vs.\ $6.24$), calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$ vs.\ $0.445$), and causal FPR ($24.7\%$ vs.\ $24.5\%$) virtually unchanged.
    \item \textbf{LayerNorm bottleneck + saliency gate in observation space (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}):} Adding the $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck and coordinate saliency gate to the observation forecaster ($307\text{k}$ parameters) compresses tail kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but leaves calibration-to-holdout drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and lowers causal Point-F1 to $0.084$.
    \item \textbf{Learned latent target projection (\texttt{TS-JEPA}, \texttt{OpEntropy}, \texttt{Reynolds}):} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ latent space ($269\text{k}\text{--}282\text{k}$ online parameters) halves calibration-to-holdout drift relative to \texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN} ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.193\text{--}0.274$, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), cuts causal FPR to $1.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$ for base predictors, and raises causal Point-F1 from $0.084$ to $0.153\text{--}0.161$.
    \item \textbf{Learned TCN encoder vs.\ linear subspace (\texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN}) and isolation forest:} \texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN} shares $D=32$, LayerNorm, and the saliency gate, achieving $2.1\%$ causal FPR via tail bounding, but its causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ ($0.104$ buffered; \texttt{IsoForest} event recall $0.149$), proving that the learned nonlinear encoder is required for anomaly discriminability.
    \item \textbf{Temporal foresight (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}), EMA target (\texttt{JEPA-NoEMA}), and saliency gate (\texttt{TS-JEPA-NoGate}):} Autoencoding context embeddings (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}) reduces drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, causal FPR $1.7\%$) at lower causal F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.161$); hard-copy targets (\texttt{JEPA-NoEMA}) reach $0.9\%$ causal FPR and $0.146$ causal F1; and replacing the saliency gate with plain $\ell_2$ norm (\texttt{TS-JEPA-NoGate}) yields $1.8\%$ causal FPR and $0.156$ causal F1, confirming that false-alarm reduction does not depend on the saliency gate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, whereas aggregate Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain comparable to reconstructive baselines.

\noindent\textbf{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring lowers held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three- to four-fold under buffered attribution ($9.3\%$ and $10.1\%$ for TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA versus $30.0\%$ and $30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to ten-fold under causal streaming ($2.4\%$ and $2.8\%$, calibration error $0.023$ and $0.027$, versus $24.3\%$ and $22.2\%$, calibration error $0.242$ and $0.221$). The reduction holds across all 33 streams in paired tests (buffered FPR $-14.4\%$ to $-15.3\%$ versus TimesNet, $p\le 0.006$; $95\%$ CI for OpEntropy $[-24.1\%, -6.5\%]$) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation (where neither hyperparameter selection nor threshold calibration accesses the held-out dataset family), leaving one dataset out at a time keeps causal FPR at $1.0\%\text{--}2.3\%$ and buffered FPR at $3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ for JEPAs versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ causal and $8.4\%\text{--}22.1\%$ buffered for reconstructors. In Panel~(c), under strictly causal attribution, latent predictors achieve lower held-out FPR than TimesNet and TranAD on every multi-stream family including MSL ($2.4\%\text{--}6.9\%$ vs.\ $13.2\%\text{--}16.1\%$), whereas under buffered attribution TimesNet has a lower FPR than OpEntropy on MSL ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $17.7\%$), and on single-stream Genesis, Metro, and SWAN reconstructors emit zero alarms because extreme calibration tails set overly conservative thresholds.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Quality Is Comparable, Not Superior:} Lower false-alarm rates do not make latent scoring a universally stronger detector. Buffered Point-F1 and event recall for TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA fall in the same range as TimesNet and TranAD (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), and paired differences across all 33 streams are indistinguishable from zero (Point-F1 $+0.012$ and $+0.051$, $p=0.54$ and $0.88$; PR-AUC $-0.000$ and $+0.023$, $p=0.39$ and $0.16$ versus TimesNet and TranAD). Per-dataset standard deviations are as large as the means (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). Only SMAP beats the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.396$), although PR-AUC exceeds base prevalence $\pi$ on both SMAP ($0.504\text{--}0.525$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and MSL ($0.422\text{--}0.434$ vs.\ $\pi=0.255$).

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (\texttt{TS-JEPA} $\lor$ \texttt{lowvar}) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluating Physics-Inspired Structures: Component-Level Justification}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Rather than treating operator rank or contractivity as proxies for Point-F1, Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) isolate three component-level effects beyond unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}: (i)~\emph{Koopman spectral entropy and Bregman barrier (\texttt{OpEntropy JEPA})} tighten operator conditioning ($\kappa(\mathbf{K}): 1.437 \to 1.255$, $\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}): 31.53 \to 31.85$) and shorten mean buffered detection delay from $110.3$ to $79.5$ steps ($124.0 \to 112.5$ causally across 19 streams, and $127.0 \to 58.2$ on the decoupling panel), while leaving Point-F1 equivalent down to $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.0112, +0.0029]$); (ii)~\emph{Cayley-accretive Crouzeix transport (\texttt{Reynolds} with $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$)} guarantees unit-disk stability ($W(\mathbf{W}_{\text{Crouzeix}}) \subset \mathbb{D}$), achieves the lowest drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.193$) and causal held-out FPR ($1.9\%$ across 19 streams), and attains the highest matched-FPR causal event recall ($0.457$ at $f^*{=}0.01$ and $0.633$ at $f^*{=}0.05$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)); and (iii)~\emph{second-moment Stiefel stress closure ($\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0.1$)} tracks subgrid covariance shifts on $\mathcal{S}_+^{16}$, but adding Frobenius covariance discrepancy to the score inflates causal FPR from $1.9\%$ to $8.6\%$ and lowers Point-F1 ($0.167 \to 0.148$ across all seeds; $0.167 \to 0.158$ on matched seeds, $90\%$ CI $[-0.052, +0.032]$), which we report as a negative aggregate detection result.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} isolates the cost of zero-lookahead streaming. Without retrospective smoothing, lower false-alarm rates ($2.4\%\text{--}2.8\%$) trade onset speed and sensitivity: causal Point-F1 and event recall are more conservative ($0.153\text{--}0.161$ and $0.487\text{--}0.517$, vs.\ $0.209$ and $0.588$ for TimesNet), and mean delay increases to $112.5\text{--}124.0$ steps ($62.2$ for TimesNet). On five non-stationary incident streams with discrete regime shifts or localized variance steps (including Room-Occupancy, CalIt2, and SMAP \texttt{P-3}), pairing latent scores with dynamic rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms by up to $21\times$. However, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), this suppression applies specifically to discrete shifts; under continuous monotonic mean ramps, trailing calibration windows continually lag the drifting mean, producing high false-alarm rates ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$) across all models.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), OpEntropy JEPA holds causal FPR to $0.5\%$ and $0.7\%$ ($0.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ for TS-JEPA, vs.\ $2.5\%$ and $3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving higher causal PR-AUC ($0.182$ and $0.189$ vs.\ $0.150$ and $0.162$ for TimesNet), comparable Point-F1 at $S=1$ ($0.028$ vs.\ $0.025$), and event recall of $0.729$ and $0.624$ ($\Delta t = 76.3$ and $179.3$ steps; TS-JEPA $\Delta t = 57.8$ and $85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases across $\{16, 32, 64\}$, TimesNet causal FPR escalates nearly nine-fold ($6.1\% \to 9.8\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas OpEntropy ($0.3\% \to 1.1\% \to 2.0\%$) and TS-JEPA ($0.3\% \to 0.3\% \to 0.4\%$) remain bounded. Thus $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, e.g., $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$), whereas $S=64$ is unacceptable for low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, e.g., hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) or rapid tripwires, which require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $1\%\text{--}10\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we compare models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.752$) and precision ($0.251$ and $0.259$) at fewer false alarms ($177\text{--}203$ per $1{,}000$ nominal steps) than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall, $262$ alarms) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall, $270$ alarms).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models, at $f^* = 0.01$ latent models nearly double causal event recall ($0.371\text{--}0.457$) compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-FPR regime ($f^* \le 0.01$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $1.51\%$ for TS-JEPA and $1.95\%$ for OpEntropy ($|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.014$ and $0.019$, corresponding to $15\times$ and $20\times$ the $q=10^{-3}$ design risk), and buffered FPR of $5.54\%$ and $6.38\%$ ($55\text{--}64\times$). These rates are an order of magnitude below TimesNet ($16.11\%$ causal, $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.160$, $161\times$) and TranAD ($14.23\%$ causal, $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.141$, $142\times$), showing that latent prediction reduces false alarms and drift without solving static calibration at $q=10^{-3}$. Comparing static SPOT against runs-declustered GPD fitting (\texttt{Decl.\ SPOT}, run-length $r=5$ via the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}) and adaptive rolling calibrators (\texttt{Roll.\ emp.}, \texttt{Roll.\ SPOT}) across 16 streams and nine drift regimes in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b) shows that: (i)~on stationary nominal streams under causal scoring, \texttt{Decl.\ SPOT} lowers TS-JEPA FPR from $0.72\%$ to $0.44\%$ ($1.36\% \to 1.07\%$ on the 4-stream tail panel, and $24.4\% \to 18.9\%$ for TimesNet), whereas under buffered smoothing declustering reduces $N_u$ exceedances to $N_c \approx 4\text{--}6$ clusters, increasing GPD variance ($0.159$ pooled); and (ii)~adaptive \texttt{Roll.\ emp.} reduces pooled FPR from $6.1\%\text{--}11.3\%$ to $1.1\%\text{--}2.4\%$ under discrete step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts, holding causal OpEntropy ($0.06\%\text{--}0.10\%$) and TS-JEPA ($0.40\%$) near the $0.1\%$ design risk, whereas continuous monotonic ramps defeat trailing rolling windows ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$). Three diagnostics explain the static overshoot:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability and quantile/length sensitivity:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR unchanged ($2.41\%\text{--}2.42\%$ for TS-JEPA), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) raises causal FPR to $3.72\%$ (TS-JEPA) and $4.13\%$ (OpEntropy).
    \item \textbf{Platykurtic collapse under total-threshold kurtosis scaling:} Total-threshold scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ reduces buffered FPR on leptokurtic cells ($\gamma_2 \ge 0$, $4.49\% \to 1.11\%$ for TS-JEPA), but $64$-step buffered smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of TS-JEPA cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$). Scaling total $\tau$ pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below $u=p_{98}$ in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating buffered FPR from $6.40\%$ to $16.30\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Excess-only scaling $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$ prevents collapse below $u$ ($9.19\%$ buffered, $2.40\%$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)):} Pooling nominal calibration and evaluation scores $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ and \emph{refitting} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) on each permuted split separates chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, equalizing macro-block distributions while preserving intra-block autocorrelation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$) rates. Across the $18$ streams with $N_{\text{cal}}, N_{\text{hold}} \ge 100$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)), inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess causal overshoot for JEPAs ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ for reconstructors) as descriptive point-estimate shares under the permutation protocol, while serial dependence ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$) accounts for $29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$. Whereas reconstructors exhibit statistically resolved positive inter-block excess ($+0.067$ $[+0.031, +0.108]$ for TimesNet and $+0.067$ $[+0.035, +0.106]$ for TranAD), JEPA bootstrap intervals span zero ($[-0.005, +0.019]$ and $[-0.002, +0.034]$) because $11$ of $18$ streams achieve $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a chronological held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Regularizer Equivalence:} Matched-encoder factorial ablations ($269\text{k}\text{--}307\text{k}$ online parameters) decouple score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks bound residual kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), while learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.193\text{--}0.214$) and cut causal false-positive rates to $1.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$. On a fixed Koopman backbone, the spectral regularizer satisfies TOST equivalence down to $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$), OpEntropy shortens detection delay ($110.3 \to 79.5$ buffered; $127.0 \to 58.2$ causally on the decoupling panel), and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) remove the 63-step lag.
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Control, Detection Parity, and Hybrid Quiet-Fault Recovery:} Latent scoring lowers held-out false alarms and calibration error three-fold under buffered attribution and eight- to ten-fold under causal streaming, doubling causal event recall at $1\%$ false-alarm budgets ($f^* = 0.01$) while aggregate Point-F1 and PR-AUC match reconstructive baselines. Pairing TS-JEPA with a rolling variance monitor raises quiet single-channel event recall from $0.19\text{--}0.28$ to $0.42\text{--}0.50$ at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Drift and Adaptive Thresholding:} Static SPOT overshoots its $0.1\%$ design risk by $15\text{--}64\times$ out of sample ($|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.014\text{--}0.063$), driven by inter-block drift ($56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ descriptive share) and serial dependence ($29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$). Runs-declustered SPOT ($0.44\%$ stationary causal FPR) and rolling quantiles ($0.06\%\text{--}0.40\%$ across step/variance shifts) narrow this gap, motivating future regime-conditioned conformal calibration.
\end{enumerate}
