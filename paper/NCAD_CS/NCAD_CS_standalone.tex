\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one number so drifting coordinates inflate the shared threshold. We replace that scalar score with coordinate-level circuit breakers. Across target horizons, each coordinate's error magnitude and horizon-mean drift receive healthy-fitted tripwires, and an alarm fires when a correlation-aware quorum trips on healthy-calibrated counts. Rather than improving post-hoc score ranking, this quorum shifts the label-free operating point toward higher live event coverage and faster response. On 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets under a disjoint five-block protocol, our operational detector, Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 breakers across three horizons), raises live event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent across all nine datasets over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. It also cuts mean detection delay from 133.5 to 69.0 steps at a higher mean held-out false-positive rate of 6.4 versus 4.4 percent (median 0.93 percent). Because nominal regime shifts drive upper-tail false alarms and slow ramps evade fixed-window prediction, autonomous deployment requires a held-out acceptance check and live drift monitoring.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Anomaly detection, Multivariate time series, Representation learning, Self-supervised learning, Statistical process control.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet stochastic jitter, benign thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ heterogeneous channels allows one noisy sensor to produce heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibration via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) on later healthy data across our 19 development streams. Because faults span many steps, random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes, matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next window's summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and one threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} drift during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1 and $138\text{--}154$-step delay on development streams). Published benchmarks are further inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial amplitude spikes, high anomaly density, and run-to-failure label bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint nominal blocks, benchmark against random-rate bounds, and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace the single scalar alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, like a building breaker box where a short in one circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across dyadic target prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, each of the 32 coordinates receives separate \emph{dials} tracking horizon error size and persistent drift, each with its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and the system alarms when a quorum of dials trips together. Drifting dials widen only their own thresholds instead of blinding the rest of the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Unlike a bank of independent detectors, the dials are structured views of one error matrix, and the quorum is fitted directly to joint trip counts on healthy data so cross-dial correlation is measured rather than assumed away. The panel does not act as a universally superior anomaly ranking score; instead, correlation-aware coordinate thresholding improves the label-free operating-point trade-off between live event coverage and detection delay without test-label threshold optimization. Targeting abrupt to intermediate-onset faults (within roughly one $256$-step context window), we establish a clear architectural hierarchy (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (primary operational architecture): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $(3+1)\times 32=128$ position-plus-drift dials under a single quorum ($120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable parameters).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic and ablation reference): shares the exact same encoder and parallel predictor across $M=4$ horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays) solely to isolate which error views aid live causal versus retrospective buffered attribution; adding fluctuation and relay dials inflates false alarms without improving live operational F1, making \emph{Lite} the preferred detector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Thirty-three development streams guide design-stage comparisons, while 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets (within a 141-stream, 12-dataset archive) under a disjoint five-block protocol test generalization and calibration reliability (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}), separating live \emph{causal} streaming from \emph{buffered} retrospective attribution:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Theory and live operating-point gain:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} proves that per-dial tripwires and quorums stay invariant under coordinate-scale drift that inflates global scalar thresholds. On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ across all $9$ datasets ($p=0.0039$) and cuts delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps at higher mean causal FPR ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$). When $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, it doubles causal Point-F1 from $0.040$ to $0.085$ ($p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Operating-point attribution and regime-shift failure modes:} Matched test-FPR controls confirm no score-ranking gain over 1D scoring; the benefit comes from where the label-free quorum sets the operating point. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams hold causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($92\%\text{--}100\%$ across six datasets). Stationary block-permutation and moving-block bootstrap audits prove that post-calibration concept drift ($3.1\times$ residual-covariance shift) rather than finite-sample quantile variance drives the $64.7\%$ $95$th-percentile tail ($92.3\%$ drift share), motivating our five-step acceptance and live $D_{\text{KS}}$ protocol.
    \item \emph{Baselines, interpretability, and deployment:} Latent prediction cuts development causal FPR from $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ (reconstructors) to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$. Full-pipeline benchmarks verify single-core CPU streaming ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s, $\le 128\,\text{KB}$ state) and multi-horizon coordinate attribution across abrupt, contextual, and slow-ramp events.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds horizon energy (null scalar reweighting), while $105/118$ streams lack independent tail support for standalone guarantees. Synthetic channel-branch gains cost $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$ FPR without proving natural sensor-fault coverage, and our frozen-reference drift hybrid lifts real GHL slow-ramp ER from $0.8\%$ to $58.5\%$ while exposing post-fault baseline wander ($74.4\%$ test FPR).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly, while TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention. Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention, and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches such as temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}. We instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate alternatives such as univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy history followed by a labeled test segment. Two cohorts play distinct roles. Forty-five streams across 11 datasets guided design, while 141 archive streams across 12 datasets (118 never inspected during design) provide the primary evaluation.

\noindent\emph{Development suite:} Drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench} ($K\in[2,72]$), the 45 streams span three domains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar:} six SMAP ($K{=}25$) and six MSL ($K{=}55$) streams, split by NASA into out-of-range \emph{point} and in-range \emph{contextual} faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, plus SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$).
    \item \emph{Wearables:} six $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes; $S{=}64$ spans $1\,\text{s}$).
    \item \emph{Industrial and infrastructure:} 14 slow-ramp Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), Genesis ($K{=}18$), GECCO ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three streams ($19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL across $7$ datasets) meet $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$, $K\le 55$, and $\le 1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c). The remaining $12$ ($5$ short histories of $764\text{--}1{,}492$ steps, $6$ high-channel CICIDS streams, and GECCO) are held aside or evaluated in the archive.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive contains every qualifying stream from 12 datasets with at least $100$ calibration windows and one labeled anomaly. Multi-stream cohorts comprise SMAP ($47$), Daphnet ($22$), MSL ($18$), SMD ($18$), Exathlon ($17$), and OPPORTUNITY ($13$), alongside single-stream GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN. Healthy histories span $1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ steps and test segments span $2{,}158\text{--}114{,}001$ steps; removing the $23$ tuning streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and zero-tune transfer:} Every stream is fitted independently on its own unlabeled nominal prefix, so deploying on a new physical asset requires a healthy warm-up history rather than cross-asset weight transfer. What transfers zero-tune across heterogeneous sensor counts ($K\in[2,55]$) and sampling rates is the architecture, whitening damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum rule. In fixed-epoch development runs, the first $65\%$ of nominal data fits weights and standardization ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$), the next $17.5\%$ calibrates thresholds ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), and after a $64$-step gap the final $17.5\%$ checks held-out false alarms ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$). Primary archive runs subdivide the fitting prefix into disjoint selection and adaptation blocks (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams from one facility share hardware characteristics, distinguishing cross-stream generalization within an archive from cross-domain generalization to unseen sensor suites. We therefore evaluate both dataset-clustered inference ($7$ dev, $9$ untouched, and $12$ full-archive datasets) and 12-fold leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) audits (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.66\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-7pt}
\caption{Four-stage pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational, $M{=}3$, $128$ dials) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic, $M{=}4$, $224$ dials): \textbf{(1)}~sliding window ($C{=}256, S{\le}64$) into online/EMA encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) with $K{\ge}25$ rFFT shells, multi-depth taps, centering, and LayerNorm; \textbf{(2)}~parallel MLP multi-horizon prediction and $MD{\times}MD$ damped whitening; \textbf{(3)}~position-plus-drift dial expansion; and \textbf{(4)}~per-dial tripwires and correlation-aware quorum alarm.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-7pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time, splitting at $t$ into a $C = 256$-step \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and an $S = 64$-step \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ ending at causal decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features split into four smooth dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose weights sum to one at every frequency, partitioning the Nyquist interval without over-fragmenting $256$-step windows. These four bands pass through depthwise causal convolutions with kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$ and recombine via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) with dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ \cite{bai2018empirical}. Lower-channel streams ($K < 25$) feed directly into the TCN.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) map each window to latent dimension $D=32$ (ablated across $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Each encoder pools three temporal descriptors (last step, max, and mean in $\mathbb{R}^{144}$) and three statistical descriptors (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, and mean absolute first difference in $\mathbb{R}^{144}$) through $D$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads. The target encoder $E_\phi$ tracks an exponential moving average (EMA) of $E_\theta$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D controls), preventing representation collapse across $10\text{--}20$ epochs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Terminal affine-free LayerNorm projects embeddings onto the $\sqrt{D}$-sphere to curb heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$). However, rescaling by total feature variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines by $O(1/\sqrt{KD})$ across coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two native mechanisms preserve fast transients and training offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Both encoders read the front-end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, passing concatenated mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{288}$) through a bias-free $288\to 32\to 32$ GELU-LayerNorm head blended at $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} A running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, synchronized into $E_\phi$) is subtracted before terminal LayerNorm, removing static offsets without guaranteeing invariance to post-calibration regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA} predicts one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the 64-step target window via a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant term ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Because one $64$-step vector averages out brief transients, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ dyadic target prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\} = \{S/4, S/2, S\}$ steps, spanning impulse, intermediate, and sustained timescales), while the diagnostic reference \emph{Laminar-JEPA} adds $S_3=48$ ($M=4$). From $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, one parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP predicts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$ behind a single terminal LayerNorm, weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ with a batch anti-collapse penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. The penalty acts on each batch of predicted summaries and does two jobs: it keeps every coordinate varying, and it discourages coordinates from copying one another. Unlike log-determinant penalties, which need batches larger than $D$, it works at our batch size $B=16<D=32$. The diagonal entries of $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$, push each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma = 0.75$; the off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize the sample covariance (divisor $B-1$). Hence
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 .
\end{equation}
The penalty raises the effective rank of the target summaries, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}, from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first passed through a \emph{damped whitening} step fitted on healthy training windows, which decorrelates them and brings well-estimated directions close to unit variance. We flatten $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($128$ in full, $96$ in Lite) and compute its \texttt{float32} sample mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and unbiased covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = (N_{\text{fit}}-1)^{-1}\sum_t (\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})(\cdot)^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Eigendecomposing $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ and clamping $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$ yields the precision operator $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})$ by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ with training eigenvalues $\lambda_k^2/(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2$: well-estimated directions approach unit variance, while near-zero modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$) are attenuated toward $0$ rather than amplified.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a disjoint healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$ rather than $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}) and blend them toward a per-coordinate RMS scaling $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=e_{m,c}(t)/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} Gradient-free predictor-row editing (Steps Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$ gave no archive gain (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}) and is omitted from the unified backbone.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} states two exact algebraic properties of this split: drift and fluctuation partition horizon energy orthogonally, and the fluctuation spectrum spans at most $M-1$ modes.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
In plain terms, multivariate residual scoring faces three design options across $J$ coordinates or dials $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$. Global quadratic aggregation $S_{\text{glob}}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$ sums all coordinates into one threshold, whereas a marginal union $\max_j \mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$ compounds per-coordinate false alarms across $J$ tests. A correlation-aware coordinate quorum instead gives every dial its own quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and sets a joint trip-count threshold $K_q$ on healthy calibration data. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes why global aggregation fails when even $k\ll J$ ``hijacker'' coordinates exhibit larger post-training variance on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, whereas per-coordinate tripwires isolate that inflation completely.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Quorum Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^J$ be a nominal residual vector on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, and let $\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}$ rescale a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k=|\mathcal{H}|<J$ drifting coordinates by $\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta\ge 0$) while leaving $j\notin\mathcal{H}$ unchanged:
(i)~under global scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-calibration quantile satisfies $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_j^2\bigr)$, growing unboundedly in $\eta^2$ so any live fault $\mathbf{u}(t^*)$ supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected when $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous tripwires $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}|)$, every binary trip $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)})$ is invariant to $\eta$ for all $\eta\ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate dependence, leaving $N_{\text{cal}}=\sum_{j=1}^J b_j(t)$ and $K_q=\kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$ unchanged and guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $\sum_{j=1}^J (u_j^{(\eta)})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_j^2$ pointwise, monotonicity and positive homogeneity of quantiles give~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ yields $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u}(t^*))=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2\le\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity gives $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}\,\tau_j^{(0)}$ for $j\in\mathcal{H}$ (and $\tau_j^{(0)}$ otherwise); dividing $|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)}$ by $\sqrt{1+\eta^2}>0$ yields $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=b_j^{(0)}(t)$ identically at every $t$, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $K_q$, while $m$ active coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ yield $N(t^*)\ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration and validity assumptions:} For $J=32$ standard normal coordinates at $\alpha=0.005$ and $\beta=0.015$, inflating just $k=2$ hijacker coordinates by $\eta=5$ raises $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$. That threshold inflation masks a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault on $\mathcal{H}^c$ ($10\times 3.8^2=144.4 < 252.4$), whereas $K_q$ stays invariant at $3.5$ and fires with $10 > 3.5$ trips. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} also makes two required assumptions explicit. First, faults must provide \emph{multi-dial coverage ($m > K_q$)}, so single-sensor flatlines diluted below $\tau_j$ do not reach quorum. Second, \emph{nominal stationarity must hold on at least $J-\lfloor K_q\rfloor$ dials}: a post-calibration shift that simultaneously inflates $>K_q$ dials violates calibration exchangeability unless gated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and live $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) implements this correlation-aware quorum across the healthy calibration block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Because all dials view the same $M \times 32$ error matrix, their trips are strongly correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on development streams; count variance $7.2\times$ binomial independence), so quorums are fitted directly to empirical $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ trip counts rather than binomial formulas:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded directly at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 onset relays:} When a sudden fault first enters the right edge of the target window, the short prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover healthy data, so few position dials trip. To catch this moment, each coordinate $c$ has stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($q_{0.993}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$) and fires a relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when its full-window position dial ($m=4$) trips together with its endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$ dial.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, the trips from window $[t,t+S)$ are reported at its right end $\tau=t+S-1$, the earliest moment all of its data exist; this fixes a reporting lag of $S-1$ steps, separate from the detection delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$ after fault onset $t^*$. Under \emph{buffered attribution}, used for retrospective review, position trips map to $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation trips map to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint trips map to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. Summing active trips at step $\tau$ yields the total count $N_{224}(\tau)$, the position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and the relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$, with ranking metrics using $N_{224}(\tau)$ directly. The binary alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with quorums set on calibration counts: $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$. All quantiles use linear interpolation, the safety factor is applied before the floor, quorums stay real-valued, and both trips and alarms use strict inequalities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both tiers share the exact $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$-parameter backbone ($120.0\text{k}$ at $K < 25$ and $127.7\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$, down from $295.8\text{k}$ in the legacy six-block sequential model) and differ only in horizon count and dial expansion:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Shared streamlined backbone:} Four adaptive TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $D$-wide pooling and native hook-free tap heads, depthwise rFFT shells gated at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbf{u}_m = z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{32} \to \text{Linear}(32,64) \to \text{GELU} \to \text{LayerNorm}(64) \to \text{Linear}(64,32)$, returning $\operatorname{LN}_{\text{no-aff}}(\mathbf{u}_m + \Delta_m)$).
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and keeps only the $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ under a single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$, removing fluctuation/endpoint dials and onset relays.
    \item \emph{Diagnostic $7D=224$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and expands errors into all $224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA (224 Dials)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\State Partition $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$ or $S_{1:4}{=}\{16,32,48,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and freeze $K_{\text{Lite}}{=}\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$ (or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum})).
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t){=}\mathbb{I}(d_j(t){>}\tau_j)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (\text{causal}) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (\text{buffered}); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ when active counts exceed quorum.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (RMS of full-tensor and peak-horizon scores, $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$) blend $\ell_2$ norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is split chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}) into a fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a validation suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$, with all weights and thresholds frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint fitting, selection, and adaptation):} Reusing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for early stopping or stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) can make thresholds optimistic. The primary 118/141-stream archive therefore uses a disjoint \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Specifically, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits standardization, network weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the checkpoint; and $S$-isolated $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$ windows) fits and freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched. Exploratory fixed-epoch suites omit $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($65\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$, unblended on the 19-stream cache and 130-stream screen, or sharing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ on the 33-stream suite). A disjoint four-block check ($50\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) on the 19 development streams confirms matching causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.96\%$ for \emph{Lite}, $1.05\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}), though the shorter fitting prefix lowers causal F1 ($0.133\to 0.071$).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched calibration and autocorrelation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Splitting the $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ stride-1 windows at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step gap so target steps never overlap. Widening this gap to $G=320$ shifts causal FPR by $<0.25$ points. Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ensures at least $500(1-0.995)=2.5$ raw tail windows above $q_{0.995}$, whereas serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$ via Sokal's $c=5$ estimator) shrinks effective sample size to $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Statistical vs.\ Practical Significance}
Seeds are averaged per stream, with seed standard deviation (SD) $\le 0.008$ on archive causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER, so the stream is the independent unit. We report both \emph{dataset-clustered inference}, using $95\%$ cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip tests, and stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests ($2{,}000$ bootstrap resamples). Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) correction \cite{benjamini1995controlling} is applied across the four primary 118-stream causal contrasts (\emph{Lite} vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D and TS-JEPA on Point-F1 and ER), and we evaluate 12-fold leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) subsets alongside per-dataset breakdowns to separate statistical significance from practical effect size (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Cohorts, Baseline Fairness, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Causal and buffered modes share identical splits per cohort:
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-stage):} Because these streams guided design and the 33-stream suite shares $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for stabilization and thresholds, they test architectural factors rather than out-of-sample calibration. The 19-stream cache across $6$ datasets tests the dial ladder and detector banks in Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and \ref{tab:matched_alert}, while the 33-stream suite ($19$ non-GHL plus $14$ GHL across $7$ datasets) compares external baselines in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}. Hyperparameters were fixed once on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched archive (primary evidence):} Removing those $23$ streams from the $141$-stream archive ($12$ datasets) leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($101$ non-Exathlon, $8$ datasets; Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\noindent\emph{Baseline fairness across three tiers:} (i)~\emph{Matched-backbone controls} include TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D), and \emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} (omitting Steps Q1--Q4). They share identical encoders, window budgets, and seed resets ($\{42,123,456\}$) to isolate scalar SPOT from the breaker panel. Following Wu and Keogh \cite{wu2023flawed}, we also test marginal-preserving cycle-swap and phase-desync anomalies at random interior onsets alongside Gaussian noise injection ($\sigma\in\{0.15,0.30\}$) against a $\max_c|x_c(t)|$ one-liner. (ii)~\emph{System-level aggregators} score the exact same frozen whitened residuals under matched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rates (Tables~\ref{tab:matched_alert}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). (iii)~\emph{External baselines} span TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, PCA Forecaster, Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}. All external models use their published reference configurations ($206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ parameters vs.\ $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable for \emph{Lite}), identical training-only scaling, $W=320$ windowing, matched epoch budgets, $64$-step gaps, and SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) without per-dataset tuning. All runs used PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090 (code: \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and scores $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$ are evaluated step by step without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} (\emph{causal} = live at $\tau$; \emph{buffered} = retrospective review), averaging seeds per stream before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) aggregation:
(1)~\emph{False alarms ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$):} healthy fraction flagged on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and on test steps $y(\tau)=0$ (reported via mean, median, $\text{p90}$, and $\text{p95}$).
(2)~\emph{Step accuracy:} for Point-F1 and precision-recall (PR) AUC without point adjustment, Point-F1 is naturally $0.05\text{--}0.25$ because residuals peak near transitions and rare fault prevalence $\pi$ bounds trivial alarming at $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}=2\pi/(1+\pi)$; PR-AUC measures threshold-free ranking.
(3)~\emph{Episode coverage and speed:} ER is the fraction of contiguous $y=1$ runs hit at least once, compared against Bernoulli chance $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$, while delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ measures steps from onset $t^*$ over detected episodes beyond the $S-1$ causal lag.
(4)~\emph{Operating controls and diagnostics:} \emph{Auto.}\ uses frozen healthy thresholds, $q^*=0.05$ uses $q_{0.95}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, and $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$ matches test FPR post hoc as a ranking diagnostic. Excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ measures $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ tail heaviness, while two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) distance $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) measures nominal drift.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
To isolate which components drive live detection without conflating cached residuals and retrained backbones, we evaluate four controlled ablation tiers across Table~\ref{tab:ablations}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cached dial ladder vs.\ why \emph{Lite} uses $128$ rather than $160$ dials:} On the fixed $19$-stream $M=4$ sequential cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding from $1$ to $224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$). Pruning fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to $160$ position-plus-drift dials reaches $0.098$ Point-F1 at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Replacing the weighted 224-dial score with unweighted $N_{224}$ leaves binary alarms identical and shifts archive PR-AUC by $\le 0.0011$. When retrained end-to-end on the parallel \emph{Lite} backbone in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), however, adding a fourth horizon prefix ($S_3=48$, $160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) yields no Point-F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$) while raising causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.82\%$ to $1.06\%$) and slowing inference by $25.5\%$. On the $118$-stream archive rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), $128$-dial \emph{Lite} likewise matches or exceeds retrained $160$- and $224$-dial panels ($0.078$ causal F1 vs.\ $0.073$ and $0.074$).
    \item \emph{Single-factor encoder, predictor, and panel isolation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Under independent $3$-seed disjoint four-block retraining, four TCN blocks match five on F1 and ER ($p\ge 0.23$) while saving $23.3\text{k}$ parameters, and $64$D pooling heads match $128$D on F1 while lifting causal ER ($55.9\%$ to $63.1\%$). Switching from parallel to sequential rollout at $M=4$ lowers buffered F1 ($0.239$ to $0.195$) and doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%$ to $4.0\%$). Expanding from $160$ to $224$ dials raises ER ($63.6\%$ to $69.0\%$) only by inflating $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, and no panel removes the structural $S-1$ causal lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching omission:} Across a single-seed $118$-stream screen, post-training predictor-row edits (Steps Q1--Q4) leave causal F1 flat ($0.0901$ to $0.0904$). Because they also shift unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS, \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
    \item \emph{Latent dimensionality, damping, and quorum sensitivity:} Retraining across $D \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ ($32$ to $256$ dials, $3$ seeds) shows that \emph{Lite} exceeds Multi-Horizon 1D at every dimension ($56.1\%\text{--}61.0\%$ vs.\ $42.5\%\text{--}48.9\%$ causal ER; $0.062\text{--}0.083$ vs.\ $0.041\text{--}0.054$ F1). Narrow latents ($D=8$, $\operatorname{RankMe}=6.4$) underfit buffered F1 ($0.131$ vs.\ $0.216$ at $D=32$, $\operatorname{RankMe}=25.0$). Dimensions $D\in\{16,32\}$ form a stable causal plateau that peaks at $D=32$ in PR-AUC ($0.269$), whereas $D=64$ ($192\times 192$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) dips to $0.068$ causal F1 under finite-sample covariance estimation. At $D=32$, performance is insensitive to whitening damping across $[10^{-3},10^{-2}]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.093$ F1, $\le 0.75\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) before degrading at $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-1}$. Varying dial tripwires $q_{\text{dial}}\in[0.975,0.990]$ keeps causal F1 within $0.080\text{--}0.090$ because $K_{\text{Lite}}$ rescales automatically ($21.2\to 13.0$). Sweeping quorum safety $\kappa\in[1.00,1.20]$ trades ER ($63.3\%\to 55.1\%$) smoothly against $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.21\%\to 0.55\%$); our default $\kappa=1.12$ holds $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}<1\%$ before detection delay jumps at $\kappa=1.20$ ($98.9\to 149.5$ steps).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate rules fail through three distinct statistical mechanisms on the cached 19-stream residuals (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Cross-coordinate false-alarm compounding:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds marginal errors across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Discrete finite-sample calibration floor:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k at $0.5\%$ across 128 coordinates or BH FDR ($q=0.005$) across 224 dials fails even under stationarity because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot fall below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, collapsing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{Out-of-sample distributional instability vs.\ stable aggregators:} Calibrating an aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) bypasses the discrete floor but exposes out-of-sample tail sensitivity. Discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ from boundary ties, while recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$ under mild cross-block drift. Among distributionally stable aggregators on this cache, the 160-dial panel reaches $0.098$ Point-F1 at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, ahead of 128-coordinate top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.056\text{--}0.072$), while Fisher pooling reaches $0.109$ at $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Independently trained archive comparisons appear in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Synthetic vs.\ Natural Failures}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Separating fault mechanisms clarifies what the latent breaker panel can and cannot catch. The latent encoder reliably flags \emph{cross-channel consistency violations}, \emph{abrupt multi-sensor transients}, and \emph{in-range phase shifts} because they perturb $>K_{\text{Lite}}$ whitened coordinates simultaneously. Conversely, isolated single-sensor hardware faults barely move the 32D summary, whether from stuck-at freezing, variance collapse, rail saturation, quantization steps, packet dropouts, or single-channel bias drift. In these single-sensor cases, $K-1$ healthy channels dilute the input projection by $1/K$ and terminal LayerNorm rescales the remaining perturbation across all $32$ coordinates; on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip.

To test an auxiliary channel-level screen across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$), we threshold three 64-step per-channel statistics at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ with a $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD) floor and $24$-of-$40$ persistence. These statistics track log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, first-order autoregressive (AR) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$. Across $57$ injected $200$-step single-channel events per class, the variance-collapse union lifts recall on collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%\text{--}82.5\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}94.7\%$, and the full branch lifts recall on shocks, linear degradation, and bias from $59.6\%\text{--}75.4\%$ to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$. On \emph{unmodified} test streams, however, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ climbs from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full branch). Because synthetic perturbations omit intermittent telemetry dropouts, quantization coarsening, and coupled hardware degradation, these injections demonstrate synthetic sensitivity only; we claim no real-world sensor-fault coverage and keep the channel branch disabled by default.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End and Observation vs.\ Latent Space}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Retraining with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams ($3$ seeds) reveals a clear channel-count threshold. Shell splitting lifts high-channel spacecraft F1 ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal $0.212 \to 0.321$) but lowers F1 on $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), motivating the $K \ge 25$ gate in \emph{Lite}. Across the 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), raw-observation reconstructors exhibit heavy tails, strong drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$). Adding an observation-space LayerNorm bottleneck cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$), while learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). Because \emph{Lite} and Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) share nearly identical score kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$), the panel's live gain stems from coordinate isolation rather than lighter tails.

\subsection{Computational Complexity and Real-Time Deployment Considerations}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
Operational spacecraft and industrial monitors require bounded memory and deterministic full-pipeline latency across channel standardization, $W=320$ ring-buffer updates, encoder-predictor inference, $96\times 96$ whitening, stabilization blending, and 128-dial quorum evaluation. Across $K \in \{5, 9, 25, 38, 55, 72\}$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} holds $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable parameters ($1.10\text{--}1.18\,\text{MB}$ FP32 weights including the EMA target encoder) plus $44.2\text{--}128.4\,\text{KB}$ of persistent per-stream state. Full-pipeline GPU inference (batch $64$, RTX 5090, $33\text{--}91\,\text{MB}$ peak memory) takes $0.162\text{--}0.253\,\text{ms}$/window, adding only $0.018\text{--}0.051\,\text{ms}$ over the neural forward pass ($0.144\text{--}0.201\,\text{ms}$). On a single CPU thread (batch $1$, embedded-processor proxy), full-pipeline streaming takes $7.11\text{--}7.45\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \le 9$ ($134\text{--}141$ windows/s, $>2\times$ Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate) and $9.76\text{--}10.79\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \in [25, 72]$ ($93\text{--}102$ windows/s, or $7.28\text{--}10.24\,\text{ms}$ on $8$ threads). Under unannounced sensor dropouts on the 19 development streams (mean-imputing dropped channels without retraining), dropping one or two active sensors reduces causal ER gradually from $58.9\%$ to $55.9\%\text{--}54.7\%$ (Point-F1 $0.093$ to $0.066$) while raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $1.5\%$ to $3.4\%\text{--}7.9\%$.

Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$. A separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.151\text{--}0.152$ for TimesNet and TranAD, and $0.082\text{--}0.097$ for DCdetector, NCAD, and Anomaly Transformer). Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

Across target horizons $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five exploratory streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)), both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ while keeping $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}} \le 3.6\%$. Short horizons $S\in\{1,4\}$ yield $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($36.7\text{--}44.1$-step delay at $S=4$), whereas buffered Point-F1 peaks at $S\in\{32,64\}$ ($0.290$ and $0.255$). On the 19 development streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)), post-hoc $f^*=1\%$ ER reaches $56.0\%\text{--}63.1\%$ for the panels versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, though this ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs does not carry over to the untouched archive. On NASA in-range contextual faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ ER at $1.3\%\text{--}2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet. Under Wu and Keogh's benchmark-flaw controls \cite{wu2023flawed} ($19$ dev streams, $3$ seeds, random interior onsets), a $\max_c|x_c|$ one-liner fails on marginal-preserving faults ($0.008\text{--}0.009$ F1). \emph{Lite} catches $50.9\%$ of \emph{cycle-swap} ($0.030$ F1) and $59.6\%$ of \emph{phase-desync} faults ($0.048$ F1) at $1.6\%$ FPR, outperforming Multi-Horizon 1D ($28.1\%$ and $42.1\%$ ER). Adding uncalibrated Gaussian noise ($\sigma\in\{0.15,0.30\}$) preserves \emph{Lite} recall ($61.4\%\text{--}66.7\%$) while raising clean FPR to $6.9\%\text{--}14.3\%$ versus $1.9\%\text{--}6.2\%$ for 1D SPOT.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults that evolve much slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ Point-F1 across all $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). When context and target windows ride a slow ramp together, their latent representations shift in tandem and predictive residuals cancel. Across linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$ ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points). Testing six onset profiles on clean multivariate segments ($3$ seeds: abrupt step, $L\in\{64,256,1024\}$ ramps, exponential degradation $1-e^{-t/512}$, and $0.001\sigma$/step accumulating bias) confirms this mechanism. From abrupt steps to accumulating bias, mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ to $586.7$ steps ($433.9$ steps on exponential degradation) as context-to-target displacement $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened residual RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

To test whether a long-term reference closes this blind spot without altering \emph{Lite} weights, we pair the panel with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ context features and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. On the $14$ real GHL ramp streams ($3$ seeds), this frozen-reference monitor lifts causal ER from $0.8\%$ (\emph{Lite} alone, $0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a two-timescale union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, Point-F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Because GHL's nominal baselines keep wandering after fault episodes, however, uncompensated test FPR rises to $74.4\%$. Sliding-window prediction thus rejects slow nominal drift at the cost of missing slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift catches slow ramps at the cost of flagging benign baseline wander.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams satisfy $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ (median $0.93\%$; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)). However, $14$ streams exceed $5\%$ and push the $95$th percentile to $64.7\%$, concentrated in Spark workload switches on Exathlon, gait-trial changes on Daphnet, and three command-mode shifts on SMAP. Three mechanisms explain these tail failures:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration failure vs.\ concept drift (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Interleaving autocorrelation-preserving blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and serial dependence while removing chronological drift. Across all 118 untouched streams ($3$-seed audit), block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ drops from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ and stays $\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams in Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a) ($1.00 \to 0.00$ on \texttt{SMAP G-6}; $0.75 \to 0.03$ on \texttt{Exathlon 10\_2}). Excess false alarms are thus driven $92.3\%$ by post-calibration \emph{concept drift} rather than tail estimation error.
    \item \emph{Internal drift drivers:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) have nearly the same median $N_{\text{eff}}$ as stable streams ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$; Spearman $\rho=-0.01$) and similar post-LayerNorm latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$). Instead, they suffer a $3.1\times$ larger shift in stabilized residual covariance ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ relative Frobenius norm). This covariance shift inflates marginal dial trip rates ($13.4\times$ vs.\ $1.5\times$ calibration rate) and quorum variance ($4.8\times$ vs.\ $1.4\times$), which quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ tracks closely ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Controlled healthy regime-shift stress test:} Injecting fault-free operating changes halfway through clean nominal segments on the 19 development streams ($3$ seeds) shows which shifts self-heal. Moderate amplitude scaling ($1.5\times, 2.0\times$ on $25\%$ of channels) barely perturbs \emph{Lite} ($2.0\%$ and $3.3\%$ post-shift FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) because terminal LayerNorm absorbs scale changes, while $45^\circ$ correlation rotation raises FPR to $6.8\%$ ($D_{\text{KS}}=0.43$). By contrast, persistent mean steps ($+1\sigma, +2\sigma$), alternating duty cycles, and $1.5\sigma$ baseline walks do \emph{not} self-settle after $W=320$ steps because input standardization and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ remain anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Across those four mean-shift regimes, steady-state FPR stays high at $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ for \emph{Lite} and $10.7\%\text{--}33.8\%$ for Multi-Horizon 1D ($D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$). Triggering live recalibration on the first $400$ post-shift windows when $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ restores mean-step FPR to $4.8\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability of Coordinate and Quorum Calibration}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ median $7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams below $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)\ge 1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). No distribution-free finite-sample tail bound can therefore be claimed from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone. We retain $q_{0.995}$ ($\times 1.12$, floor $3.5$) for two empirical reasons. First, evaluating Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$ resamples of block length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) across all 118 untouched streams shows that `MBB-Median` yields virtually identical thresholds and false-alarm tails ($0.092$ F1, $65.2\%$ ER, $0.92\%$ median and $62.7\%$ p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ and $60.3\%$ deployed in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). Conservative `MBB-UCB90` and binomial $N_{\text{eff}}$ upper bounds trim p95 only to $42\%\text{--}47.7\%$ while sacrificing $8\text{--}17$ ER points, and thinning $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ by $\tau_{\text{int}}$ worsens mean FPR to $10.4\%$. Second, our five-step protocol mitigates calibration uncertainty without test labels. Step~(1) fits weights, $\mathbf{\Omega}$, and (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$. Step~(2) requires $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$; routing shorter streams to 1D SPOT lifts pooled causal F1 to $0.095$, while two-fold cross-fitting on $T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$ cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$. Steps~(3)--(4) audit frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and reject autonomous deployment when $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ ($50$ passing streams reach $0.7\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $0.076$ vs.\ $0.039$ F1 over 1D, $p=3.5\times 10^{-4}$), while Step~(5) monitors live $D_{\text{KS}}$ for recalibration.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Decomposing multi-horizon latent predictive errors into independently calibrated coordinate tripwires (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} at $128$ dials; \emph{Laminar-JEPA} at $224$ dials) yields three conclusions across $33$ design-stage and $118$ untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Coordinate isolation and contextual coverage:} Per-dial tripwires prevent drifting coordinates from inflating a shared scalar threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), catching in-range contextual and phase-desync faults that evade 1D SPOT and $\max_c|x_c|$ baselines \cite{wu2023flawed}. Across all $118$ untouched streams ($12/12$ LODO folds, $9/9$ datasets), \emph{Lite} lifts live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$), halves delay ($69.0$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps) at $93\text{--}141$ windows/s on one CPU thread, and doubles Point-F1 when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$).
    \item \emph{Label-free operating point vs.\ ranking:} At post-hoc matched test FPR, the panel does not outrank scalar scoring. Its gain comes strictly from placing a label-free quorum at higher live episode coverage and shorter delay without test-label tuning.
    \item \emph{Concept-drift attribution and gating:} Block-permutation and Moving-Block Bootstrap audits show that $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms ($7.44\% \to 0.57\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) come from post-calibration concept drift via a $3.1\times$ residual-covariance shift rather than tail estimation error, bounded by our five-step protocol and live $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ recalibration ($24.3\%\text{--}32.0\%\to 4.8\%$ FPR).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Three open directions follow directly from the failure boundaries measured here. First, low-rank or Riemannian streaming updates to $\mathbf{\Omega}$ and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ can track benign operating-mode switches without a $400$-window recalibration buffer. Second, conditioning our frozen-reference EWMA drift monitor on control inputs or multi-rate state priors is needed to retain its $58.5\%$ slow-ramp recall on GHL while suppressing false alarms from benign post-fault baseline wander ($74.4\%$ test FPR). Third, pairing the latent quorum with noise-adaptive coordinate scaling and low-FPR univariate checks can address uncalibrated sensor noise and $1/K$-diluted single-channel flatlines.
