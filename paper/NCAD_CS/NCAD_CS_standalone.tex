\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We study how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a 32-dimensional latent space under distribution shift. We compare an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) against Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, a multi-horizon architecture that taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context batch barycenters, whitens multi-horizon residuals, and scores centered sub-horizon fluctuations via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets (19 non-Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams at five seeds and 14 GHL streams at three seeds), we calibrate thresholds on chronological held-out nominal data using Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) at a 0.1\% design risk. On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent scoring cuts stream-macro held-out false-positive rates (FPRs) three-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (9.3\% to 10.8\% versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD) and ten- to eighty-fold under strictly causal streaming (2.4\% for TS-JEPA and 0.3\% for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%). Similar reductions hold across all 33 streams (0.3\% to 1.5\% causal FPR versus 14.2\% to 16.1\% for reconstructors), and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieves the highest buffered precision-recall area under the curve (0.375) and causal matched-FPR event recall (0.701 at 1\% FPR). Matched-encoder factorial ablations show that Layer Normalization (LayerNorm) bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, learned latent projections and multi-horizon barycenter whitening cut calibration-to-holdout drift, short prediction horizons (one to four steps) eliminate emission latency, and hybrid sensor variance monitors recover quiet single-channel faults at a quantified false-alarm cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains sensor jitter and scale disparities, observation residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), heavy-tailed residuals suffer calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} Predicting the \emph{summary} of a future window, rather than every raw sample, filters out unforecastable sensor jitter. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which forecast a 32-dimensional latent summary of the next $S$ steps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for low-latency tripwires) from the preceding 256 steps without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}. Alongside an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formulate a multi-horizon fluid-dynamic decomposition model, \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}. To capture fast onset transients while removing slow baseline drift, \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context batch barycenters, and whitens prediction residuals across four nested sub-horizons. It then separates the whitened residuals into mean transport and centered sub-horizon fluctuations, scoring wavepacket coherence via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio. Our central contribution is relative false-alarm suppression, reduced calibration-to-holdout drift, and multi-horizon ranking gains rather than solved absolute calibration at $q=10^{-3}$. We evaluate: (i)~how much latent residuals reduce realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ under static, declustered, and rolling calibrators; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent projection, multi-horizon whitening, and soliton concentration isolate the mechanism of improvement; (iii)~how detection varies across abrupt, gradual, and quiet single-channel faults; and (iv)~the trade-off between score-emission latency ($S-1$) and detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Factorial isolation of tail compression vs.\ drift reduction:} Across 12 matched-backbone and non-parametric arms ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ online parameters), a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) bottleneck bounds heavy residual tails ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$). Moving prediction into a learned latent space halves calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in \texttt{TS-JEPA}), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.163$ (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \textbf{Fewer false alarms, leading multi-horizon ranking, and hybrid quiet-fault recovery:} Across the 19 non-GHL streams, latent scoring cuts held-out FPRs three-fold under retrospective buffered attribution ($9.3\%\text{--}10.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD) and ten- to eighty-fold under strictly causal streaming ($0.3\%\text{--}2.4\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves leading buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC $0.375$), point-wise F1 (Point-F1 $0.188$), and causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.701$ at $1\%$ FPR), and pairing latent prediction with a sensor variance monitor raises quiet single-channel event recall from $0.19\text{--}0.28$ to $0.42\text{--}0.50$ at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)).
    \item \textbf{Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration bounds and short-horizon latency control:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that Reynolds-centered multi-horizon fluctuation Gram matrices $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ have an exact rank deficiency ($\lambda_1 \equiv 0$) and obey the tight scale-invariant bound $\frac{1}{M-1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1$, separating isotropic sub-horizon noise ($R_{\text{LT}} = \frac{1}{M-1}$) from rank-1 soliton wavepackets ($R_{\text{LT}} = 1$). Fixed-backbone ablations show that the multi-horizon whitened envelope drives the ranking and drift gains while the post-hoc soliton multiplier satisfies Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence without altering Point-F1, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations} and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Decomposed calibration error across static, declustered, and adaptive rules:} A refitted permutation decomposition across all 19 non-GHL streams shows that inter-block regime drift accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal risk in single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors (with resolved positive confidence intervals (CIs)). In contrast, inter-block drift vanishes ($0.0\%$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.0033$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.0037$) in barycenter-centered \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, and runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles narrow the gap on step and variance shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timesteps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that destabilize extreme-value threshold fitting under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\textbf{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximizes contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent prediction across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ produced by a separate target encoder. Collapse is prevented via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}. How latent predictive residuals behave under unsupervised extreme-value threshold calibration remains unexplored.

\noindent\textbf{Fluid-Dynamic Reynolds Decomposition and Spectral Concentration Priors:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman and operator-theoretic models \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} lift nonlinear dynamics into learned observable spaces. Rather than imposing partial differential equations on raw sensors, we draw on Reynolds decomposition from turbulence modeling to separate multi-horizon latent predictions into a mean trajectory and centered sub-horizon fluctuations. We then measure wavepacket localization across prediction horizons via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio on the centered fluctuation Gram matrix.

\noindent\textbf{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration and Evaluation Rigor:} Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to score exceedances above quantile $u$ to extrapolate a threshold at design risk $q$. However, continuous telemetry violates the classical independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption through short-range serial correlation (quantified by the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}) and inter-block regime drift. Because ``point-adjusted'' F1 severely inflates scores \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}, we evaluate all models using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm operating curves.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. Thirty-three streams enter the primary multi-seed held-out protocol; 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. The suite spans four physical domains and separates natural operational or physiological anomalies from testbed or simulated faults: \emph{spacecraft and solar telemetry} (six SMAP streams, $K{=}25$, $7$ anomaly events; four primary and two short-nominal MSL rover streams, $K{=}55$, $6$ primary events; and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}, $K{=}38$, $6{,}615$ flare events, all natural operational faults); and \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$, $K{=}9$, $72$ natural gait-freezing episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals a $1\,\text{s}$ horizon). In \emph{industrial processes} and \emph{smart infrastructure}, the suite covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ testbed valve and heater faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$, $2$ actuator faults), GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution ($K{=}9$, $37$ natural hydrological anomalies), Metro I-94 hourly highway traffic ($K{=}5$, $25$ natural traffic events), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$, simulated attack flows). Cohort~3 (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}) separately injects six controlled single-channel faults into six real streams.

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$ across the 33 primary streams) and labeled anomaly test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatlines, matching standardized archive subsets in prior benchmarks \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly}.

Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Inclusion in the primary multi-seed protocol required two prespecified criteria: (i)~at least $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows ($M_{\text{val}} = \lceil 0.35\,T_{\text{nom}}\rceil - 319 \ge 1{,}000$, requiring $T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$), and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed runtime ($K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$). Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). Among the 12 reserved streams, five have nominal sequences too short to yield $500$ calibration windows: CalIt2 \texttt{traffic} ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}940, N_{\text{cal}}{=}4$), MSL \texttt{C-2} ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}764, N_{\text{cal}}{=}0$), MSL \texttt{D-16} ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}451, N_{\text{cal}}{=}94$), and Room-Occupancy \texttt{default} and \texttt{1} ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}073$ and $1{,}492$; $N_{\text{cal}}{=}28$ and $101$). Six CICIDS network streams (\texttt{0,2,3,5,6,7}, $K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \in [1.36\times 10^5, 4.15\times 10^5]$) exceed the stride-1 multi-seed runtime envelope, and GECCO \texttt{water\_quality} ($K{=}9$, $1$ held-out event) is used in Cohorts 3--4. All 11 datasets and 130 archive streams appear in the secondary single-seed cross-check (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware platforms ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations (SDs), zero-tuning leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)).

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} (Stage~1), a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the series at index $t$ into: (i)~a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$), establishing the nominal regime; and (ii)~a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$), inspected for trajectory departures.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit leptokurtic sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro} and~\ref{sec:related}), we shift prediction into a 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$, Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. An online context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ into $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while a target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. To prevent constant-vector collapse, target weights $\phi$ are updated as an EMA of online weights $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with cosine momentum $m_k \in [0.995, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures: TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We evaluate two nested latent predictors sharing the same multiscale encoder backbone and coordinate saliency gate: an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and a multi-horizon fluid-dynamic decomposition model (\texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}).

\subsubsection{Unconstrained TS-JEPA Baseline}
In \texttt{TS-JEPA}, a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-horizon prediction $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with standard single-horizon predictive alignment and VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg} ($\gamma_{\text{var}} = 1.0$). Terminal LayerNorm bounds latent representations on the sphere of radius $\sqrt{D}$ ($\|z\|_2 = \sqrt{D}$) and compresses nominal residual kurtosis relative to observation-space reconstructors. However, single-horizon encoding pools the entire $S=64$ target window into one terminal TCN state, smoothing out short-lived sub-horizon transients and leaving cross-horizon residual correlations unwhitened.

\subsubsection{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA (Multi-Horizon Reynolds Decomposition and Lieb--Thirring Concentration)}
\noindent\textbf{Physical Motivation and Structural Design.} In fluid turbulence, Reynolds decomposition separates a velocity field into its time-averaged mean flow and zero-mean fluctuations, while dispersive wave theory characterizes localized, shape-preserving wavepackets (\emph{solitons}) by spectral concentration of the fluctuation operator. \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} brings both principles into latent time-series prediction through two architectural stages before residual scoring:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textit{Multi-depth TCN functional hierarchy taps and synchronized barycenter centering:} Standard TCN encoders read only the deepest dilated block, where wide receptive fields attenuate sharp onset transients. In \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, a multi-depth tap head extracts intermediate feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=64$) across all $L=3$ causal dilated TCN stages, concatenating the temporal mean and terminal state of each stage with the base encoder output into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{cat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times (2 L d_{\text{model}} + D)}$. A two-layer residual projection maps $\mathbf{h}_{\text{cat}}$ onto the unit sphere $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. To eliminate common-mode baseline drift between context and target windows, the online context batch barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} = \operatorname{sg}\bigl(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B \mathbf{u}_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}\bigr)$ is subtracted from both online context and EMA target spherical embeddings prior to final LayerNorm projection.
    \item \textit{Nested causal multi-horizon rollout and decoupled horizon VICReg:} Rather than predicting only the full $S=64$ window, we partition $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefix windows $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ with lengths $S_m = (m/M)S \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$. The EMA target encoder encodes each prefix into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. From $z_{\text{ctx}}$, a sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out intermediate predictions $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ ($\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$) using learnable horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the primary predictor $P_\theta$ forecasts the terminal horizon $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ for $m < M$ and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, the training objective is:
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Sol-Rey}} &= \sum_{m=1}^M w_m \Bigl( 2 - \frac{2}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \bigl\langle \bar{z}_{\text{pred}}^{(b,m)}, \operatorname{sg}\bigl(\bar{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(b,m)}\bigr) \bigr\rangle \Bigr) \\
    &\quad + \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \bigl( \mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}) + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}) \bigr) \\
    &\quad + 0.05\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}(z_{\text{ctx}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{z} = z / \|z\|_2$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}(z) = D^{-1}\sum_{d=1}^D \max(0, 1 - \sigma_d(z))$ enforces unit per-coordinate standard deviation, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}(z) = D^{-1}\|\operatorname{Cov}(z) - \operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{Cov}(z))\|_F^2$ decorrelates coordinates within each horizon $m$ independently, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}(z_{\text{ctx}}) = -D^{-1}\log\det\bigl(\operatorname{Cov}(\bar{z}_{\text{ctx}})\sqrt{D} + 10^{-2}\mathbf{I}_D\bigr)$ prevents dimensional collapse (achieving $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) = 19.8$ vs.\ $11.6$ for single-horizon baselines).

\noindent\textbf{Reynolds Fluctuation Gram Matrix and Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration.}
Physically, nominal background fluctuations disperse energy across multiple uncorrelated sub-horizons, whereas an emerging fault wavepacket concentrates fluctuation energy into a single dominant rank-1 directional mode across horizons. To formalize this distinction, let $\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ stack the $M$ prediction residuals $\mathbf{e}_m = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}$. We fit the empirical shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows to obtain Mahalanobis-whitened residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{e}} = \mathbf{\Omega}(\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{E}) - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$, reshaped into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = [\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_1, \dots, \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Applying Reynolds decomposition across the $M$ horizons separates $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ into its horizon-mean transport $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuations $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. From the $M \times M$ fluctuation Gram matrix $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ with ordered eigenvalues $0 \le \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$, we define the Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio of order $\gamma = 1$:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2} + \epsilon_{\text{LT}}}, \quad \epsilon_{\text{LT}} = 10^{-12}.
    \label{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} establishes the exact algebraic bounds and radial scale invariance of $R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$.

\begin{proposition}[Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration Bounds, Centered Gram Rank Deficiency, and Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathbf{E}' = (\mathbf{I}_M - M^{-1}\mathbf{1}_M\mathbf{1}_M^\top)\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ denote the Reynolds-centered multi-horizon fluctuation matrix for $M \ge 2$ and $\gamma > -1/2$. In the exact limit $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$ for any non-zero fluctuation $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~\emph{Centered Gram nullspace and effective rank $M-1$:} Because $\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$, the constant vector $\mathbf{1}_M$ lies in $\ker(\mathbf{G})$, forcing $\lambda_1(\mathbf{G}) \equiv 0$ identically so that $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{G}) \le \min(M-1, D)$.
(ii)~\emph{Sharp isotropy lower bound and soliton upper bound:} The concentration ratio satisfies the tight two-sided inequality
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{M - 1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1,
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
where the lower bound $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) is attained if and only if fluctuation energy is distributed isotropically across $M-1$ orthogonal sub-horizon modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$), and the upper bound $1$ is attained if and only if $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$ ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0 < \lambda_M$), corresponding to a coherent rank-1 soliton wavepacket.
(iii)~\emph{Exact radial scale invariance:} For any scalar $c > 0$, $R_{\text{LT}}(c\mathbf{E}') = R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$, rendering $R_{\text{LT}}$ invariant to homogeneous amplitude rescaling.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction, $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{E}'(\mathbf{E}'^\top\mathbf{1}_M) = \mathbf{0}$, so $\lambda_1 = 0$ is always an eigenvalue of $\mathbf{G} \succeq \mathbf{0}$, leaving at most $M-1$ positive eigenvalues $\lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ with $\lambda_M > 0$ since $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$. Because $x \mapsto x^{\gamma+1/2}$ is strictly increasing on $x \ge 0$ for $\gamma > -1/2$, $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le \lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$ for all $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$. Summing over the $M-1$ remaining modes gives $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$. Dividing through by $\sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$ yields (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}), with equality on the left iff $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M$ and on the right iff $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0$. Finally, scaling $\mathbf{E}' \mapsto c\mathbf{E}'$ maps $\mathbf{G} \mapsto c^2\mathbf{G}$ and $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \mapsto c^{2\gamma+1}\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$, canceling in numerator and denominator of (\ref{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}) as $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, windows are scored at stride $1$. Let $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denote the dimension-agnostic coordinate saliency gate defined in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). Unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} scores the single-horizon residual $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ via $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$. In \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, we combine the joint $MD$-dimensional whitened residual gate $g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))$ with the peak sub-horizon gate $\max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))$ into the multi-horizon envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, and modulate it by a one-sided median absolute deviation (MAD)-standardized soliton concentration gain and nominal manifold distance gain:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(g_{MD}\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t)\bigr)^2 + \max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t)\bigr)^2\Bigr)}, \label{eq:soliton_env} \\
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{Sol-Rey}}(t) &= \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\Bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{sol}}\max\bigl(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t))\bigr)\Bigr) \nonumber \\
    &\quad \times \Gamma_{\text{bank}}(t), \label{eq:soliton_score}
\end{align}
where $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$ and $z_{\text{LT}}(t) = (R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}'(t)) - m_{\text{LT}}) / (1.4826\,\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}})$ standardizes $R_{\text{LT}}$ against its nominal training median $m_{\text{LT}}$ and MAD $\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}}$. The factor $\Gamma_{\text{bank}}(t) = 1 + 0.15\tanh(0.5\,\tilde{d}_{\text{core}}(t)) + 0.10\tanh(0.5\,\tilde{d}_{\text{null}}(t))$ applies a bounded gain from MAD-normalized $k$-nearest-neighbor coreset distance $\tilde{d}_{\text{core}}$ ($N_{\text{core}}=64, k=5$) and minor principal-component null-space energy $\tilde{d}_{\text{null}}$ ($d_{\text{null}}=8$) fitted on nominal training windows. Because $\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}))$ is one-sided, nominal windows with below-median spectral concentration ($z_{\text{LT}} \le 0$) receive zero amplification. Setting $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0$ and $\Gamma_{\text{bank}} \equiv 1$ recovers the unmodulated multi-horizon envelope control $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ (\texttt{Soliton-Reynolds ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$)}).

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
We formalize online evaluation under three criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, the detector observes only $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$; neither encoder accesses observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ summarizes suspect observations $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$ spanning sample indices $[t, t+S-1]$, score $s_t$ is emitted at the trailing edge $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3), creating a structural emission lag of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO, and $63\,\text{h}$ on hourly Metro). This lag is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$ from onset $t^*$ to the first alarm $t_{\text{alarm}} \ge t^*$. Shortening the horizon to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates emission latency for rapid tripwires (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \textbf{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} \emph{Causal trailing attribution} assigns $s_t$ only to $\tau = t + S - 1$ for zero-lookahead streaming. \emph{Retrospective buffered smear attribution} averages $s_t$ across all suspect timestamps $[t, t+S-1]$, introducing fixed-lag smoothing. Both regimes are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and Scale Equivariance}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
To amplify sparse departures in high-dimensional representations while mitigating channel drowning, we define the coordinate saliency gate $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for any vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    g_d(\mathbf{v}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{d \sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}, \\
    w_i &= \frac{\exp\bigl(|v_i| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^d \exp\bigl(|v_j| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}, \quad \sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau_g = 0.5$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. When $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$, $g_d(\mathbf{0}) = 0$. For non-zero $\mathbf{v}$, rescaling $\mathbf{v} \mapsto c\mathbf{v}$ with $c > 0$ yields $\sigma_{c\mathbf{v}} = c\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon = c\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} + (1-c)\epsilon$. In the operational regime where $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 \gg \epsilon$, the softmax weights $w_i$ are scale-invariant up to an $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/\|\mathbf{v}\|_2)$ perturbation, satisfying approximate degree-1 homogeneity $g_d(c\mathbf{v}) \approx c\,g_d(\mathbf{v})$ and recovering exact degree-1 homogeneity in the limit $\epsilon \to 0^+$. Input channels use uniform weights in the deployed pipeline (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
For multivariate streams ($K > 1$), a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Each projected channel is split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point rFFT grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax scale router. Because smooth Fourier shells overlap at band edges ($\sum \Psi_j \equiv 1$ rather than $\sum \Psi_j^2 \equiv 1$), this acts as a multiscale front-end rather than an orthogonal Parseval wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ from nominal calibration data alone, SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits exceedances $Y = \mathcal{S}(t) - u > 0$ above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with shape $\xi$ and scale $\sigma$ via Grimshaw maximum-likelihood estimation \cite{grimshaw1993computing} with bounded L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10]$). Inverting the GPD tail at target risk $q = 10^{-3}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = \begin{cases}
    u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right], & |\xi| \ge 10^{-5}, \\
    u - \sigma \ln\left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right), & |\xi| < 10^{-5},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:spot_threshold}
\end{equation}
where the lower branch is the continuous exponential-tail limit as $\xi \to 0$, evaluated directly to avoid division by zero. Here $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $N_u$ count post-attribution score timestamps on the calibration block. Primary tables use standard SPOT; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} compares parametric SPOT against non-parametric empirical quantiles, runs-declustered GPD fitting, and rolling thresholds.

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$. Standardization parameters and neural weights are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$.

Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2). Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations provide context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}})$. Splitting $v$ at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H)$ (used to fit the SPOT threshold at $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, excluding $S=64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S)$. Because suspect target windows have length $S=64$, the last calibration target and first evaluation target are $100\%$ disjoint. The first $C=256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. To verify that shared passive context does not bias calibration, we also run a full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320)$ across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$). Stream-macro causal FPR is virtually unchanged: $0.33\% \to 0.36\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $3.02\% \to 3.24\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, versus $12.15\% \to 13.97\%$ for TimesNet and $9.05\% \to 9.73\%$ for TranAD. All primary tables use fixed standard SPOT thresholds during testing.

\paragraph{Independent Statistical Units, Multi-Seed Replication, and Equivalence Bounds}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are evaluated across five random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ observations each) and ablation controls use three seeds ($\{7, 42, 2024\}$). To prevent pseudoreplication, every statistical test specifies its independent unit explicitly: (i)~seeds are averaged within each stream first to quantify within-stream optimization variance ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$), (ii)~stream-level paired tests treat the telemetry stream ($n=33$ primary or $n=19$ non-GHL) as the unit of analysis, and (iii)~domain-level tests aggregate streams within each hardware platform first and treat the dataset family ($N=7$) as the independent cluster. We employ three statistical tools:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and false-discovery-rate adjustment:} The Wilcoxon signed-rank test evaluates whether the median paired difference across streams ($n=33$ or $n=19$) is zero without Gaussian assumptions. We adjust $p$-values across multiple comparisons via the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$), report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples), and cross-check at the dataset-cluster level ($N=7$) via exact sign-flip permutation tests and zero-tuning LODO sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for fixed-backbone regularizer equivalence:} We use TOST to test whether the multiplicative soliton and manifold gain in (\ref{eq:soliton_score}) alters aggregate Point-F1 relative to the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ on the identical trained backbone ($n=19$ streams). TOST tests $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$), establishing equivalence when $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}) < 0.05$ (meaning the $90\%$ paired CI lies inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$) across $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.015, \pm 0.020, \pm 0.030\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Distributional tail and drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} On nominal windows we measure: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, distinguishing heavy-tailed \emph{leptokurtic} ($\gamma_2 > 0$) from light-tailed \emph{platykurtic} ($\gamma_2 < 0$) residuals; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, quantifying distributional shift between calibration ($F_{\text{cal}}$) and held-out nominal ($F_{\text{hold}}$) blocks.
\end{enumerate}

At $K=25$ channels, online trainable parameter counts are closely matched across latent and observation-space backbones: $272{,}266$ online parameters for \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($540{,}148$ total including the stop-gradient EMA target encoder), $311{,}658$ online ($618{,}892$ total including the multi-depth tap head and sub-horizon rollout predictor) for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, $304{,}138$ for matched-encoder \texttt{TCN-ObsPred}, $307{,}402$ for \texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}, $648{,}553$ for TimesNet, and $205{,}746$ for TranAD. Non-finite telemetry entries are zero-imputed with channel standard deviations floored at $10^{-2}$. Causal and retrospective buffered scores receive separately fitted thresholds $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ fitted strictly within their respective calibration windows $v_{\text{cal}}$ as defined in (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}). All primary multi-seed runs converged under the $20$-epoch schedule on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (NVIDIA RTX 5090, $0.60\text{--}0.88\,\text{ms}$ per window, $188\,\text{MB}$ of GPU memory); stream manifests, fitted thresholds, and source code are available at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate models across three tiers. First, \emph{primary calibration and accuracy outcomes} comprise realized held-out nominal FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ relative to design risk $q = 10^{-3}$, unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC. Second, \emph{operating characteristics} evaluate event recall across deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) and across post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). Third, \emph{secondary operational endpoints} report unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Multi-Horizon Whitened Envelope vs.\ Soliton Concentration Modulation}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
To separate the contribution of the multi-horizon barycenter-whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ from the post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton multiplier on the same trained backbone, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) compares \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} against its unmodulated control (\texttt{Soliton-Reynolds ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$)}) across all 19 non-GHL streams. Moving from single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} to the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope raises buffered PR-AUC from $0.331$ to $0.374$ ($+0.043$), cuts strictly causal held-out FPR six-fold ($0.024 \to 0.004$), lowers calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.162$), and increases causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ from $0.395$ to $0.697$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).

Adding the post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration gain ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) and nominal manifold gain on top of $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ yields a null aggregate effect. Buffered held-out FPR shifts slightly from $0.122$ to $0.108$ (paired difference $-0.013$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.035, +0.009]$), while causal FPR ($0.004$ vs.\ $0.003$) and KS drift ($0.162$ vs.\ $0.163$) are unchanged. Similarly, buffered SPOT Point-F1 ($0.188$ vs.\ $0.185$, paired difference $+0.003$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.327$) and PR-AUC ($0.375$ vs.\ $0.374$) satisfy TOST equivalence at $\Delta = \pm 0.030$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0021$) and $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.026$). All measurable gains over \texttt{TS-JEPA} in ranking, causal FPR, and drift suppression thus stem from multi-depth TCN tapping, barycenter centering, and multi-horizon Mahalanobis whitening rather than the post-hoc soliton multiplier.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Fault-Class Breakdown, Quiet-Fault Mechanism, and Hybrid Monitor}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Why do normalized latent encoders miss quiet single-channel faults such as sensor flatlines or dropouts? When a single channel $k$ freezes or shifts by $\delta$ out of $K$ channels, its contribution to the pre-LayerNorm pooled vector scales as $O(1/K)$ and is rescaled onto the $\sqrt{D}$ sphere by LayerNorm along with nominal cross-channel variance. Comparing the auxiliary context-variance input-channel gate against uniform channel weighting on four streams confirms that uniform weighting achieves higher macro Point-F1 ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$), so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. Across six injected single-channel fault types on six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), uniform latent encoding (\texttt{Unif.}) achieves $0.278$ event recall on abrupt $+5\sigma$ spikes ($0.306$ with variance gating). On persistent quiet faults (flatlines, stuck sensors, dropouts) and low-amplitude steps, however, uniform latent encoding reaches only $0.194$ event recall and $0.020\text{--}0.030$ point recall ($0.043\text{--}0.068$ under variance or median absolute deviation (MAD) gating). Because standalone sensor monitors such as a rolling variance-collapse detector (\texttt{lowvar}) and maximum channel $z$-score (\texttt{dev\_maxz}, $0.250\text{--}0.306$ event recall at $14.1\%$ FPR) trigger on different streams than \texttt{TS-JEPA}, combining them in a hybrid union detector (\texttt{TS-JEPA} $\lor$ \texttt{lowvar}) raises point recall to $0.202\text{--}0.318$ and event recall to $0.417\text{--}0.500$ across all six fault classes at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost ($1.9\% \to 17.0\%$).

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
In Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), replacing the standard convolutional stem with the 4-shell Fourier front-end increases macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers calibration residual kurtosis ($1.409 \to 1.069$), while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to conservative threshold tightening on SMAP \texttt{P-3}. The module thus acts as a ranking-conditioning stem rather than a uniform F1 gain.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 12 factorial arms across all 19 non-GHL streams under identical splits, training budgets, and comparable parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Prediction vs.\ reconstruction in observation space (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred} vs.\ \texttt{TCN-ObsRecon}):} Using the exact same \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} backbone, forecasting versus reconstructing raw observations leaves kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 6.10$ vs.\ $6.24$), calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$ vs.\ $0.445$), and causal FPR ($24.7\%$ vs.\ $24.5\%$) virtually unchanged.
    \item \textbf{LayerNorm bottleneck + saliency gate in observation space (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}):} Adding the $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck and coordinate saliency gate to the observation forecaster ($307\text{k}$ parameters) compresses tail kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but leaves calibration-to-holdout drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and lowers causal Point-F1 to $0.084$.
    \item \textbf{Learned latent target projection and multi-horizon barycenter whitening (\texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}):} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ latent space halves calibration-to-holdout drift relative to \texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN} ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.163$ in \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), cuts causal FPR to $2.4\%$ in \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.3\%$ in \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, and raises buffered PR-AUC from $0.257$ to $0.331$ (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and $0.375$ (\texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}).
    \item \textbf{Learned TCN encoder vs.\ linear subspace (\texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN}) and isolation forest:} \texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN} shares $D=32$, LayerNorm, and the saliency gate, achieving $2.1\%$ causal FPR via tail bounding, but its causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ ($0.104$ buffered; \texttt{IsoForest} event recall $0.149$), proving that the learned nonlinear encoder is required for anomaly discriminability.
    \item \textbf{Temporal foresight (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}), EMA target (\texttt{JEPA-NoEMA}), and saliency gate (\texttt{TS-JEPA-NoGate}):} Autoencoding context embeddings (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}) reduces drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, causal FPR $1.7\%$) at lower causal F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.161$); hard-copy targets (\texttt{JEPA-NoEMA}) reach $0.9\%$ causal FPR and $0.146$ causal F1; and replacing the saliency gate with plain $\ell_2$ norm (\texttt{TS-JEPA-NoGate}) yields $1.8\%$ causal FPR and $0.156$ causal F1, confirming that false-alarm reduction does not depend on the saliency gate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, while \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the highest buffered PR-AUC ($0.375$) and causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.701$ at $f^* = 0.01$).

\noindent\textbf{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring cuts held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three-fold under buffered attribution: $9.3\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $10.8\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, versus $30.0\%$ for TimesNet ($p = 0.049$) and $30.7\%$ for TranAD ($p = 0.007$). Under strictly causal streaming, the false-alarm reduction widens to ten- to eighty-fold: $2.4\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.3\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, compared to $24.3\%$ for TimesNet ($p = 0.010$) and $22.2\%$ for TranAD ($p = 0.010$). This advantage holds across all 33 streams ($0.25\%$ causal stream-macro FPR for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} vs.\ $16.11\%$ and $14.23\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, leaving one dataset out at a time keeps causal FPR at $0.2\%\text{--}2.3\%$ ($3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ buffered) for JEPAs versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ causal ($8.4\%\text{--}22.1\%$ buffered) for reconstructors. In Panel~(c), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the lowest causal held-out FPR on every multi-stream family ($0.2\%$ Daphnet, $0.1\%$ GHL, $0.4\%$ MSL, $0.6\%$ SMAP vs.\ $5.6\%\text{--}36.0\%$ for baselines).

\noindent\textbf{Multi-Horizon Ranking Gains vs.\ Unadjusted Point-F1 Trade-offs:} Under buffered attribution on the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and its $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$ control achieve the highest threshold-free PR-AUC ($0.375$ and $0.374$ vs.\ $0.331$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, $0.329$ for TimesNet, and $0.287$ for TranAD). Buffered Point-F1 ($0.188$ and $0.185$, vs.\ $0.206$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) and event recall ($0.496$ and $0.515$) match TimesNet ($0.197$ F1, $0.583$ recall) and beat TranAD ($0.130$ F1, $0.490$ recall) at one-third the false-alarm rate. Across datasets (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), SMAP exceeds the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.370$; PR-AUC $0.576$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and Genesis reaches $0.037$ F1 (vs.\ $0.000$). Conversely, on GHL ($\pi=0.013$), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), illustrating that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (\texttt{TS-JEPA} $\lor$ \texttt{lowvar}) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each component of \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} modifies performance relative to \texttt{TS-JEPA}. First, \emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and barycenter centering} remove common-mode baseline shift, cutting KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.163$) and eliminating the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR ($71.3\% \to 0.0\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Second, \emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and Mahalanobis whitening} ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$) capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, raising buffered PR-AUC ($0.331 \to 0.374$), cutting causal FPR ($2.4\% \to 0.4\%$), and lifting causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ ($0.395 \to 0.697$). Third, \emph{post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) and manifold gain yield a null aggregate effect on the fixed backbone ($0.188$ vs.\ $0.185$ F1, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0021$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.327$; $0.375$ vs.\ $0.374$ PR-AUC), confirming that the multi-horizon whitened representation drives the gains.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows how zero-lookahead streaming shifts the operating threshold. In causal mode, \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} preserves the highest threshold-free ranking (PR-AUC $0.289$ vs.\ $0.278$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.281$ for TimesNet). Without 64-step window averaging, however, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals have a heavier right tail ($\text{median }\xi = -0.182$, $\gamma_2 = 2.31$) than buffered scores ($\xi = -0.473$, $\gamma_2 = 1.58$) or single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($\xi = -0.307$, $\gamma_2 = 1.33$). When SPOT extrapolates that tail to $q=10^{-3}$ in (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}), it places $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ high on the causal score distribution. Consequently, \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} fires quietly on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.2\%$ of test steps (true-positive rate (TPR) $5.2\%$, held-out FPR $0.3\%$), compared to $10.8\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} (TPR $16.6\%$, held-out FPR $2.4\%$) and $24.2\%$ for TimesNet (TPR $34.0\%$, held-out FPR $24.3\%$). Because it flags only the sharpest peak steps inside each anomaly segment rather than staying on throughout the event, its causal Point-F1 drops ($0.060$ vs.\ $0.188$ buffered, and $0.161$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) even though it catches $50.6\%$ of anomaly events ($\Delta t = 166.2$).

To check whether $50.6\%$ event recall could arise by chance on long anomaly segments ($111$ events with median length $L = 139$ and mean $348.1$ across the 18 non-SWAN streams), Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} (Note) compares each detector against an analytical rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$. At \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}'s held-out nominal rate ($p = \text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0033$), random firing catches only $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.288$ of events ($\Delta t_{\text{rand}} = 214.6$ vs.\ $0.506$ and $166.2$ actual). At its overall test firing rate ($\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 0.0220$), unclustered Bernoulli trials reach $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.524$ ($\Delta t_{\text{rand}} = 114.7$) because real model alarms cluster inside detected events rather than firing independently. For TimesNet ($p_{\text{hold}} = 0.243$), independent random firing reaches $0.600$ ($0.801$ at $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}}$), exceeding TimesNet's actual $0.588$. Relaxing the static threshold to the empirical $p_{99.5}$ calibration quantile raises \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}'s causal event recall to $0.612$ at $0.76\%$ held-out FPR.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), \texttt{TS-JEPA} holds causal FPR to $0.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ and $3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$ for TimesNet), comparable Point-F1 at $S=1$ ($0.028$ vs.\ $0.025$), and fast detection ($\Delta t = 57.8$ and $85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases across $\{16, 32, 64\}$, TimesNet causal FPR escalates nearly nine-fold ($6.1\% \to 9.8\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Consequently, $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP, MSL, and GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$). Low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, such as hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) and rapid tripwires instead require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.3\%\text{--}11\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we compare models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), \texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.809$) at lower false-alarm rates than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models, at $f^* = 0.01$ \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.395$) and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($0.701$ causal, $0.621$ buffered) substantially increase event recall compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-FPR regime ($f^* \le 0.05$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample across all streams and attribution modes. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($15\times q$) and $0.25\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($3\times q$), compared to $16.11\%$ for TimesNet ($161\times q$) and $14.23\%$ for TranAD ($142\times q$). Replacing parametric SPOT with a non-parametric empirical 99.5th percentile ($p_{99.5}$) yields nearly identical results ($0.7\%$ causal FPR for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $2.3\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} vs.\ $15.4\%\text{--}17.5\%$ for reconstructors), demonstrating that reconstructor false-alarm flooding is not an artifact of GPD tail fitting. Under $S=64$ buffered attribution, however, smearing each window score across 64 consecutive steps dilates every isolated exceedance into a 64-step cluster, inflating buffered FPR to $5.5\%\text{--}6.3\%$ ($55\text{--}63\times q$) for JEPAs and $19.6\%\text{--}19.9\%$ ($196\text{--}199\times q$) for reconstructors under both rules. Static SPOT is therefore near-calibrated for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} only in strictly causal mode. Two diagnostics explain why causal thresholds hold on latent residuals:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} To separate inter-block regime drift from serial correlation and GPD tail-fit error, we pool nominal calibration and evaluation scores $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ across all $19$ non-GHL streams and \emph{refit} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) under three splits: chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, which removes macro-block drift while preserving intra-block serial correlation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$). Once temporal structure is removed by i.i.d.\ shuffling, every model achieves low false-alarm rates near the $0.1\%$ target ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} = 0.0025$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $0.0033\text{--}0.0035$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, TimesNet, and TranAD). In chronological deployment, inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.0229 \to 0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.2103\text{--}0.2302 \to 0.0427\text{--}0.0464$). In barycenter-centered \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, by contrast, inter-block drift vanishes ($0.0\%$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.0033$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.0037$, difference $-0.0004$).
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability, declustering, and rolling adaptation:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$). Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in only $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Applying Ferro--Segers runs declustering ($r=5$) \cite{ferro2003inference} prior to GPD fitting trims stationary causal FPR from $0.25\%$ to $0.20\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and from $0.72\%$ to $0.44\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}. Under discrete step, variance, and regime shifts across 16 streams, adaptive rolling quantiles hold causal \texttt{TS-JEPA} FPR to $0.06\%\text{--}0.40\%$ (vs.\ $0.8\%\text{--}3.0\%$ for TimesNet), whereas continuous monotonic mean ramps defeat trailing rolling windows ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$ FPR across all models).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a chronological held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Multi-Horizon Whitening:} Matched-encoder ablations ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ online parameters) decouple score compression from representation learning. LayerNorm bottlenecks bound residual kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in \texttt{TS-JEPA}), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.163$ with $0.3\%$ causal FPR. On the fixed backbone, post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation is a null modification satisfying TOST equivalence ($\Delta = \pm 0.020$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.026$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.327$), and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) remove the 63-step emission lag.
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Control, Threshold Trade-offs, and Hybrid Quiet-Fault Recovery:} Latent scoring lowers held-out false alarms three-fold under buffered attribution and ten- to eighty-fold causally. \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves leading buffered PR-AUC ($0.375$) and causal matched-FPR event recall ($f^* = 0.01$: $0.701$ vs.\ $0.217$ for TimesNet), trading lower causal SPOT Point-F1 ($0.060$ at a $2.2\%$ test alert rate vs.\ $0.161$ at $10.8\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) for $0.3\%$ held-out FPR. Pairing \texttt{TS-JEPA} with a rolling variance monitor raises quiet single-channel event recall from $0.19\text{--}0.28$ to $0.42\text{--}0.50$ at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Drift and Adaptive Thresholding:} Static SPOT overshoots $q = 10^{-3}$ by $55\text{--}126\times$ under $S=64$ buffered window smearing and $15\times$ causally for \texttt{TS-JEPA}. In contrast, barycenter-whitened \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} reduces causal static overshoot to $3\times$ ($0.25\%$ stream-macro FPR across 33 streams; $\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} = 0.0025$) and eliminates inter-block drift ($71.3\% \to 0.0\%$). Runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles narrow this gap across step and variance shifts, motivating regime-conditioned conformal calibration.
\end{enumerate}
