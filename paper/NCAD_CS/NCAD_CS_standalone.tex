\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We investigate how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space under distribution shift, alongside an Operator-Entropy variant (OpEntropy JEPA) combining a linear Koopman transition operator, spectral entropy and shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier regularizers, coordinate saliency gating, and a multiscale dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, our primary finding is relative false-alarm suppression rather than universal detection superiority or exact attainment of the 0.1\% nominal design risk. Latent predictive scoring reduces held-out false-positive rates three- to four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (7.4\% to 10.1\% versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.1\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%), while point F1 and precision-recall ranking remain comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled ablations isolate two distinct mechanisms: layer-normalization bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. On a fixed Koopman backbone, equivalence testing confirms that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank without altering point F1 within a 0.01 to 0.03 margin, while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to an unconstrained predictor. Static thresholding still overshoots its 0.1\% design target by 15- to 64-fold out of sample, driven primarily by inter-block distribution shift and serial dependence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments such as NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Operational monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains ambient sensor jitter and channel-scale disparities, observation-space residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and strong serial clustering. When paired with automated extreme-value thresholding such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), these heavy-tailed residuals suffer severe calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} A detector that predicts the \emph{summary} of a future window, rather than every raw sample, ignores sensor jitter that no model can forecast. We therefore study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which forecast a compact 32-dimensional latent summary of the next 64 steps from the preceding 256, without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}. We also formulate an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) whose forecasting operator is regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a shifted Bregman energy barrier. We treat these regularizers as conditioning priors on abstract latent coordinates, and we do not claim that they improve detection accuracy. The evaluation asks three separate questions: (i)~how much do latent residuals reduce false alarms under automated calibration, and does calibration reach its nominal $0.1\%$ risk target; (ii)~does detection quality hold across operating points and fault classes; and (iii)~how long does an operator wait between a fault and an alarm.

\subsection{Contributions}
Each contribution below states one finding in words. The supporting numbers sit in the cited tables and figures.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Why latent scoring lowers false alarms:} Across 12 controlled backbone arms, a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) bottleneck bounds heavy residual tails, and a learned latent projection also keeps held-out residuals close to calibration residuals (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)).
    \item \textbf{Fewer false alarms, comparable detection:} On 33 streams from seven datasets, latent scoring cuts held-out false alarms several-fold relative to reconstruction baselines under both retrospective (buffered) and strictly causal evaluation, and it recovers more faults at a fixed low false-alarm budget. Point-wise F1 (Point-F1) and precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) stay comparable to the baselines, and quiet single-channel faults remain weakly detected (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)).
    \item \textbf{The operator regularizer conditions but does not drive detection:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} shows the Koopman operator cannot collapse to zero. Equivalence tests show that removing the entropy term leaves Point-F1 unchanged, while the full OpEntropy architecture shortens detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \textbf{A measured calibration gap:} Standard extreme-value thresholding still exceeds its $0.1\%$ design risk out of sample. A refitted permutation test attributes most of the excess to distribution shift between calibration and test blocks, and a smaller share to serial dependence (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly operate in raw observation space by training a network to forecast future sensor readings or reconstruct input windows, flagging timesteps where the point-level observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on raw spacecraft telemetry channels. More recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention to amplify association discrepancies; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods so 2D convolutional kernels can capture intra- and inter-period variations.

Despite strong ranking performance on clean benchmarks, observation-space reconstructors share a structural vulnerability under automated thresholding on noisy multi-sensor arrays. Because point-level losses penalize reconstruction error across every channel equally, the network is forced to model high-frequency stochastic noise, quantization jitter, and background environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), aggregating raw reconstruction errors across channels accumulates heavy-tailed residual spikes, referred to statistically as \emph{leptokurtic} tails (excess kurtosis $\gamma_2 > 0$), which destabilize extreme-value threshold fitting and inflate false alarms whenever baseline operating conditions shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\textbf{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} To avoid reconstructing raw sensor noise, self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent embedding space. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximizes contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full window embeddings against context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention branches without a reconstruction loss. However, contrastive classifiers depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology, yielding lower calibrated point detection on diverse continuous telemetry ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent predictive scoring across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate both raw observation decoding and synthetic negative pairs. Rather than reconstructing inputs, a JEPA encodes a historical context window into a latent vector $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicts the target representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$ of a future window produced by a separate target encoder. Because trivial constant embeddings would zero out latent prediction error, non-contrastive architectures prevent representation collapse either through architectural asymmetry (updating the target encoder via an exponential moving average of online weights \cite{grill2020bootstrap}) or through explicit variance-covariance regularization such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}. While prior time-series representation studies evaluate embeddings via post-hoc threshold sweeps, how latent predictive residuals behave under strictly unsupervised extreme-value threshold calibration remains unexplored.

\noindent\textbf{Koopman Operator Theory and Physics-Inspired Priors:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects the underlying trajectories of physical dynamical systems \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize continuous velocity fields in observation space, whereas Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce partial differential conservation laws directly on physical state coordinates. However, rigid differential constraints require known governing equations and suffer from stiff competing gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}.

Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral} provides a softer dynamical prior: nonlinear dynamics can be lifted into an observable embedding space where time evolution is governed by a linear operator. Data-driven Koopman networks \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn neural embeddings jointly with a finite-dimensional transition matrix $\mathbf{K}$. Here we regularize the singular value spectrum of $\mathbf{K}$ via Von Neumann spectral entropy and a smooth Bregman energy barrier, maintaining a strict distinction between \emph{physics-inspired} algebraic priors (which shape abstract learned latent coordinates) and \emph{physics-informed} conservation laws, and testing whether operator conditioning yields downstream detection gains or statistical equivalence relative to an unconstrained predictor.

\noindent\textbf{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration and Evaluation Rigor:} Unsupervised streaming deployment requires converting continuous scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to score exceedances above a high calibration quantile $u$ to extrapolate a threshold at design risk $q$. However, classical extreme-value theory assumes independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples. Continuous telemetry violates this assumption through short-range serial correlation, which causes threshold exceedances to occur in multi-step bursts quantified by the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}, and through non-stationary regime drift between calibration and test blocks. Concurrently, recent benchmark audits \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly} have shown that ``point-adjusted'' F1 (which credits an entire anomaly segment if a single timestep triggers an alarm) severely inflates scores; we therefore evaluate all models using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm operating curves.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP and MSL entities, with two short-nominal MSL streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample threshold tuning, each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Stream inclusion in the primary multi-seed protocol required: (i)~$N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows after splitting, and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed runtime. Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (6 Daphnet, $K{=}9$; 14 GHL, $K{=}16$; 4 MSL, $K{=}55$; 6 SMAP, $K{=}25$; Genesis, $K{=}18$; Metro, $K{=}5$; SWAN, $K{=}38$). The remaining 12 streams (CalIt2, $K{=}2$; two Room-Occupancy, $K{=}5$; two short-nominal MSL; GECCO, $K{=}9$; six 72-channel CICIDS flows) appear in operational incident case studies and a secondary 11-dataset, 130-stream single-seed baseline cross-check (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)); the dyadic front-end ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)) includes SMD \texttt{machine-1-2} ($K{=}38$).

Two external-validity caveats bound interpretation: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented mislabeled segments and point anomalies \cite{wu2023flawed}, so per-dataset scores represent upper bounds on operational detectability; and (ii)~because streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware and environmental characteristics, streams within a dataset are not independent domains ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report unweighted Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations (SDs), leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), restricting conclusions strictly to the evaluated entities and sampling regimes.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a continuous multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:context_target_schematic}(a), a sliding window of total length $W = C + S = 320$ steps advances across the stream at stride $1$ and partitions the local series at split index $t$ into two contiguous blocks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Historical context window ($\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$):} The preceding $C = 256$ observations leading up to index $t$, which establish the current nominal operating regime, phase, and baseline amplitude of the system.
    \item \textbf{Suspect target horizon ($\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$):} The subsequent $S = 64$ observations spanning from $t$ to the online decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, which are inspected to determine whether the system has departed from its expected trajectory.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\columnwidth]{figures/context_target_window_schematic.pdf}
\vspace{-5pt}
\caption{\textbf{(a)}~Sliding window partitioning of $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ into context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ ($C{=}256$) and suspect target $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ ($S{=}64$, decision step $\tau = t + S - 1$). \textbf{(b)}~Empirical 2D latent projection ($\mathbb{R}^{32} \to \mathbb{R}^2$, SMAP \texttt{E-1}) centered at $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K}z_{\text{ctx}}$: nominal calibration ($z_{\text{tgt}}^{\text{cal}}$) and held-out ($z_{\text{tgt}}^{\text{hold}}$) clouds remain inside $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$, whereas fault state $z_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ crosses $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ along $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$.\label{fig:context_target_schematic}}
\vspace{-8pt}
\end{figure}

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit leptokurtic sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro} and~\ref{sec:related}), we shift prediction into a compact 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$, Fig.~\ref{fig:context_target_schematic}(b)) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. An online context encoder $E_\theta$ compresses $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ into a context vector $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while a separate target encoder $E_\phi$ compresses $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into a target vector $z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. Rather than training $E_\phi$ by direct gradient descent (which would allow both encoders to map all inputs to a constant vector), the target weights $\phi$ are updated as an exponential moving average (EMA) of the online weights $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with a cosine momentum schedule $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. By predicting the summary embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$, the model discards uninformative channel-level jitter while preserving structural trajectory departures.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We formulate Operator-Entropy JEPA as our primary transition architecture and evaluate it alongside an unconstrained multi-layer perceptron predictor (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and two comparative physics-inspired variants (Reynolds-Stress and Potential-Flow):

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics and Modal Dispersion)}
\noindent\textbf{Idea.} Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral} represents nonlinear dynamics as a linear map on learned observables. We follow it and forecast in latent space with a single learnable matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, so that $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$.

\noindent\textbf{Problem.} Trained on prediction error alone, $\mathbf{K}$ can fail in two ways. It can put all its energy into a few directions (rank collapse), or it can shrink toward zero (norm collapse). Either way it stops carrying information about the dynamics.

\noindent\textbf{Fix.} We penalize each failure separately. The first penalty keeps energy spread evenly across directions. The second keeps total energy near its starting scale.

\noindent\textbf{Penalty 1: spread energy evenly.}

The singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ of $\mathbf{K}$ measure how strongly it amplifies each orthogonal direction. Each squared value $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ is the energy in direction $i$, and $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i$ is the total energy. Normalizing the energies into a probability distribution $p_i = (\lambda_i + \epsilon_{\log}) / \sum_{j=1}^D (\lambda_j + \epsilon_{\log})$ (with $\epsilon_{\log} = 10^{-12}$ for numerical stability) defines the Von Neumann spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D p_i \log p_i$. We define the \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} as the Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence between the modal distribution $\mathbf{p}$ and the ideal uniform equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
When all $D$ singular modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = 0$; conversely, if $\mathbf{K}$ collapses onto a single rank-1 direction, $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K})$ rises toward $\log D$. Minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K})$ thus encourages a well-conditioned, near-isometric transition matrix.

\noindent\textbf{Penalty 2: keep total energy near one.} A smooth logarithmic Bregman barrier on the energy ratio $r(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / D$ stops $\mathbf{K}$ from shrinking, with no hard gradient clipping:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) &= r(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log\bigl(r(\mathbf{K}) + \epsilon\bigr) \\
    &\quad + \log(1 + \epsilon) \ge 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. This convex barrier equals zero at the unit-energy scale $r(\mathbf{K}) = 1$ (where $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near the identity matrix) and smoothly penalizes both energy decay ($r \to 0$) and runaway amplification ($r \gg 1$). Combining both terms gives the spectral regularizer $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K})$, yielding the training loss:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} &= \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bigl\| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} \bigr\|_2^2 \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{Lyap}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:op_entropy_loss}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the batch size, $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$, and $\beta_{\text{Lyap}} = 0.02$. Here $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ is a discrete Lyapunov covariance-alignment penalty that matches the trace-normalized batch covariance of predicted states $\mathbf{K}\operatorname{sg}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ to that of $z_{\text{tgt}}$, applying a stop-gradient operator $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ to $z_{\text{ctx}}$ so that the operator regularizers condition $\mathbf{K}$ without distorting the encoder representations.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Triviality Under Finite-$\epsilon$ and Singular Barriers]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Consider $\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}}, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$ and empirical context--target cross-covariance $\mathbf{M} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top$:
(i)~Under the finite-$\epsilon$ smooth barrier ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$), the regularizer gradient vanishes at the origin, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$, because $r(\mathbf{K})$ is quadratic in $\mathbf{K}$; however, whenever context and target embeddings exhibit non-zero cross-covariance ($\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$), $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$, which excludes $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ as a stationary point or minimizer.
(ii)~In the singular-barrier limit ($\epsilon \to 0^+$), $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$ unconditionally.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\epsilon > 0$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{2}{D}\Phi'_{\text{energy}}(r)\,\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$, and $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ yields uniform smoothed probabilities $p_i = 1/D$, minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H} \ge 0$ so $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$. Differentiating the prediction term gives $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_\epsilon(\mathbf{0}) = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$ whenever $\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$. As $\epsilon \to 0^+$, $-\log r(\mathbf{K}) \to +\infty$ as $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Scope of Regularization):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} guarantees non-degeneracy of the transition matrix $\mathbf{K}$, not diversity of the encoder representations $z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}}$. Unlike representation regularizers such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg} or Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow} that penalize encoder covariance collapse directly, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ constrains only the transition spectrum; encoder collapse is prevented empirically by the momentum target encoder and stop-gradient decoupling (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).

\subsubsection{Comparative Physical Priors (Reynolds-Stress and Potential-Flow Heads)}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
To test whether alternative continuum-mechanics priors improve anomaly detection, we evaluate two additional latent predictor heads under identical encoder backbones:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Second-Moment Closure Head):} Inspired by fluid-mechanical Reynolds decomposition (which separates mean flow from turbulent covariance fluctuations), this variant augments the mean prediction $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ with a lower-triangular Cholesky factor head $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ ($d_s = 16$) that predicts the projected outer-product second moment $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}$ via $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}$. Training minimizes the sum of the latent prediction MSE and $0.1\operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}})$, and inference adds $0.5\,g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$ to the primary residual score.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA (Curl-Free Flow Matching Head):} Motivated by conservative irrotational vector fields, this variant parameterizes a curl-free velocity field $\hat{v} = -\nabla_{z_t}\Phi_\psi$ as the negative gradient of a scalar potential $\Phi_\psi$ conditioned on flow time $t \in [0,1]$, context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a soft router over $K_r = 4$ orthonormal subspaces. Training uses conditional flow matching along linear paths from Gaussian noise to $z_{\text{tgt}}$, and inference evaluates the velocity residual at the trajectory midpoint. Because curl-free gradient fields cannot represent closed rotational orbits without multi-regime switching, this inductive bias is hypothesized to penalize periodic nominal telemetry (Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, sliding windows are processed at stride $1$. Let $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denote the latent prediction residual, and let $g(\mathbf{e})$ denote the coordinate saliency gate defined in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). Unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} scores windows directly via $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$. Because the final LayerNorm layer projects latent vectors onto the sphere of radius $\sqrt{D}$ and strips radial amplitude, Operator-Entropy JEPA records the pre-LayerNorm radial energy scaled by window RMS for both encoders ($e_{\text{ctx}}, e_{\text{tgt}}$), forms the predicted radial energy $e_{\text{pred}} = e_{\text{ctx}}\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}\|_2 / (\|z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2 + 10^{-6})$, and combines directional and radial discrepancies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{OpEnt}}(t) = g\bigl(\mathbf{e}(t)\bigr) + 0.2\,\frac{|e_{\text{tgt}}(t) - e_{\text{pred}}(t)|}{e_{\text{tgt}}(t) + e_{\text{pred}}(t) + 10^{-6}}.
    \label{eq:opent_score}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
To avoid confounding data access, window buffering, and alarm timing, we formalize online evaluation under three criteria (illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, the detector observes only historical data $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$; neither encoder accesses observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ summarizes the $S$-step target block $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$, window score $s_t$ is emitted at the trailing edge $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3). This creates a structural score-emission latency of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO, and $63\,\text{h}$ on hourly Metro). This structural lag is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$, which measures the elapsed steps from ground-truth anomaly onset $t^*$ to the first timestamp $t_{\text{alarm}} \ge t^*$ whose attributed score exceeds threshold $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$. Shortening the horizon to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates score-emission latency when rapid tripwire response is required (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \textbf{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} Under \emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, window score $s_t$ is assigned only to the decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$ for zero-lookahead streaming. Under \emph{retrospective buffered smear attribution}, $s_t$ is averaged across all steps in $[t+1, t+S]$, which introduces fixed-lag smoothing and can smear post-onset scores up to $S$ steps before $t^*$. Both regimes are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults affecting a single subsystem can be diluted by nominal variance across the remaining coordinates. To amplify sparse latent deviations without losing global Euclidean norm information, we apply a softmax coordinate saliency gate $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e})$ to the residual vector $\mathbf{e} = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \\
    w_d &= \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau_g)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau_g)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with fixed blend $\alpha = 0.5$ and temperature $\tau_g = 0.5$ across all latent models. Input channels are encoded with uniform weights in the deployed pipeline; an auxiliary context-variance input-channel gate is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}.

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Before the dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) backbone, each input channel is split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic frequency shells on a $256$-point real Fast Fourier Transform (rFFT) grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ to within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$). Each shell is processed by a causal 1D convolution with kernel sizes $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$ from coarse to fine scales and recombined via learned softmax routing. Because smooth Fourier shells overlap at band edges, this module acts as a multiscale frequency front-end rather than a strictly orthogonal wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert continuous scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ using nominal calibration data alone, we apply Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}. Rather than assuming the entire score distribution is Gaussian, SPOT isolates the upper tail above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ and fits the exceedances $Y = \mathcal{S}(t) - u > 0$ to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with tail-heaviness shape parameter $\xi$ and scale parameter $\sigma$ via Grimshaw's maximum-likelihood algorithm \cite{grimshaw1993computing}. Inverting the fitted GPD tail at target false-alarm probability $q = 10^{-3}$ (one expected false alarm per $1{,}000$ nominal steps) yields the closed-form threshold:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{cal}}$ is the number of nominal calibration windows and $N_u$ is the number of exceedances above $u$. All primary benchmarks use standard unadjusted SPOT ($\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$). In Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}, we also audit two heuristic kurtosis adjustments: total-threshold scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau_{\text{SPOT}}[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ and excess-only scaling $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau_{\text{SPOT}} - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$. Standardization parameters and neural weights are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$.

Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2). Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations serve as context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}})$. Splitting $v$ at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H)$ (used to fit the SPOT threshold at $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, excluding $S=64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S)$. Because suspect target windows have length $S=64$, the last calibration target and first evaluation target are $100\%$ disjoint; the first $C=256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. A stricter full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320)$ so even passive context is disjoint across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) leaves stream-macro causal FPR virtually unchanged ($2.07\% \to 2.18\%$ for OpEntropy and $3.02\% \to 3.24\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $12.15\% \to 13.97\%$ for TimesNet and $9.05\% \to 9.73\%$ for TranAD). All primary tables use fixed standard SPOT thresholds during testing.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Non-Parametric Hypothesis Testing, and Equivalence Bounds}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are evaluated across five random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ observations each) and ablation controls use three seeds. Because random seeds on the same stream are not independent observations, all metrics are averaged across seeds within each stream first, treating the telemetry stream ($n=33$ or $n=19$) as the independent statistical unit. To compare models without assuming Gaussian error distributions across diverse domains, we employ three statistical tools:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and false-discovery-rate adjustment:} The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is a non-parametric paired hypothesis test that evaluates whether the median difference between two models across the same streams is zero by ranking the absolute magnitudes of their per-stream score differences. Unlike a paired Student's $t$-test, it prevents a single extreme outlier stream from skewing the comparison. When testing multiple model pairs simultaneously, we adjust $p$-values using the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$) to cap the expected proportion of false statistical positives, and report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples).
    \item \textbf{Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for statistical equivalence:} In a standard two-sided test, an insignificant result ($p > 0.05$) indicates only inconclusive evidence rather than equal performance. To test whether a physics-inspired regularizer produces practically negligible change relative to an unregularized control, TOST reverses the null hypothesis by testing whether the true mean difference $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ lies outside a prespecified equivalence margin $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$ via two one-sided tests: $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$). Formal equivalence is established if $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}) < 0.05$ (meaning the $90\%$ paired confidence interval falls strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$). We prespecify $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ Point-F1 for fixed-backbone regularizer controls ($\approx 13\%$ of baseline F1, $<0.09\times$ between-stream standard deviation) and $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ for cross-architecture comparisons, and report narrow-margin sensitivity checks down to $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.010, \pm 0.015, \pm 0.020\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Distributional tail and drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} To quantify why anomaly scores trigger false alarms, we measure two summary statistics on nominal data: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, which measures tail heaviness relative to a Gaussian distribution ($\gamma_2 = 0$), where \emph{leptokurtic} scores ($\gamma_2 > 0$) exhibit heavy outlier-prone tails and \emph{platykurtic} scores ($\gamma_2 < 0$) exhibit light, flat-topped tails compressed by normalization or temporal smoothing; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, which measures the maximum vertical gap between the empirical cumulative distribution functions of the calibration block ($F_{\text{cal}}$) and the held-out nominal evaluation block ($F_{\text{hold}}$), directly quantifying how severely the nominal baseline shifts over time.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Architectures, Fairness Checks, and Cohort Structure}
We separate primary multi-seed conclusions (Cohorts 1--2 across 12 neural, linear, and non-parametric detectors) from secondary single-seed cross-checks. Two fairness audits verify that reconstructive baseline results under the unified $20$-epoch, $W=320$ pipeline are not artifacts of window length or implementation:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate models across three complementary tiers: (i)~\emph{primary calibration and accuracy endpoints}, comprising held-out nominal FPR, unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC; (ii)~\emph{operating characteristics}, evaluating event recall across deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)) and across post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in [0.01, 0.50]$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)); and (iii)~\emph{secondary operational endpoints}, reporting unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating), event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at anomaly prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether physics-inspired regularizers drive anomaly detection accuracy, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) disables each physical penalty on its fixed architecture across all 19 non-GHL streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs.\ $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ (fixed Koopman backbone):} Removing the spectral regularizer leaves buffered Point-F1 ($0.232$ vs.\ $0.235$, paired difference $-0.004$, two-sided $p=0.89$) and PR-AUC ($0.365$ vs.\ $0.365$) virtually identical. Two One-Sided Tests confirm formal equivalence within the prespecified $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ margin ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0007$) and across narrow-margin sensitivity checks down to $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0015$), $\Delta = \pm 0.015$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0024$, with $90\%$ CI $[-0.0112, +0.0029]$ strictly inside $[-\Delta, +\Delta]$), and $\Delta = \pm 0.010$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0088$), as well as within $\pm 0.010$ PR-AUC and $\pm 0.025$ event recall.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs.\ $\alpha_{\text{stress}}{=}0$ (fixed backbone):} Disabling the second-moment loss yields a $+0.013$ Point-F1 shift (two-sided $p=0.59$), confirming TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$) and down to $\Delta = \pm 0.035$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0031$).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs.\ no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling multi-regime routing shifts Point-F1 by $-0.054$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.17$), failing equivalence at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$.
\end{enumerate}
These fixed-backbone regularizer controls must be distinguished from cross-architecture comparisons against unconstrained TS-JEPA (which uses an MLP predictor without radial energy scoring or Lyapunov covariance alignment). On the 19-stream subset, the $+0.028$ Point-F1 difference between OpEntropy JEPA and TS-JEPA ($90\%$ CI $[-0.018, +0.074]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.11$) is statistically inconclusive at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$, whereas across all 33 primary streams OpEntropy JEPA ($+0.016$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.009, +0.045]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$) and Reynolds-Stress ($-0.008$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0017$) satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ (with OpEntropy vs.\ TS-JEPA also satisfying $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.030)$ at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0077$ and $\pm 0.015$ causal FPR at $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0001$).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Factorial Isolation of the Entropy Term and Energy Barrier}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) crosses the spectral-entropy term and Bregman energy barrier across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in [0, 2.0]$ on six streams (three seeds). To quantify spectral diversity, we report the effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe} = \exp(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i) \in [1, 32]$ (which measures how many of the $D=32$ dimensions carry active variance) and the operator condition number $\kappa = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min} \ge 1$ (the ratio of largest to smallest singular values, where $\kappa = 1$ denotes an ideal distance-preserving isometry). Three findings emerge. First, because the Koopman matrix $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near the identity, the unregularized operator is already near full rank ($\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}) = 31.53$ of $32$, condition number $\kappa = 1.437$); increasing $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$ tightens conditioning monotonically ($\kappa = 1.255$ at the deployed $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0.50$, and $\kappa = 1.154$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=2.00$) while lowering training loss variation ($\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}: 0.565 \to 0.545$). Second, because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ applies a stop-gradient to $z_{\text{ctx}}$, encoder effective ranks remain invariant across all regularizer strengths ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}}) \in [5.16, 5.54]$, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) \in [11.54, 11.58]$). Third, detection and false-alarm rates remain stable across arms, with the deployed setting ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0.50, \beta_{\text{norm}}=1$) attaining the highest causal event recall ($0.444$ vs.\ $0.369$ unregularized). On an eight-stream decoupling panel, pairing detached Lyapunov alignment ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0.02$) with the smooth Bregman barrier cuts causal detection delay from $127.0$ steps (TS-JEPA) and $80.2$ steps ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0$) to $58.2$ steps while raising causal Point-F1 from $0.044$ to $0.094$.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating and Quiet-Fault Monitors}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Comparing the auxiliary context-variance input-channel gate against uniform channel weighting on four streams confirms that uniform weighting achieves higher macro Point-F1 ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$), so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. In Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d), injecting six single-channel fault types into six multivariate streams shows that quiet faults (flatlines, stuck sensors, dropouts) yield low point recall ($0.02\text{--}0.07$) across all latent gating variants because LayerNorm embeddings absorb constant single-channel offsets as nominal background. Auxiliary observation-space monitors, namely a rolling variance floor (\texttt{lowvar}) and maximum channel $z$-score (\texttt{dev\_maxz}), recover quiet-fault recall ($0.18\text{--}0.32$) at the cost of inflating nominal FPR eight-fold ($14.1\%\text{--}14.9\%$ vs.\ $1.9\%$).

\subsection{Ablation 4: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
In Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), replacing the standard convolutional stem with the 4-shell Fourier front-end increases macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers calibration residual kurtosis ($1.409 \to 1.069$), while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to conservative threshold tightening on SMAP \texttt{P-3}. The module thus acts as a ranking-conditioning stem rather than a uniform F1 gain.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 12 controlled backbone arms across all 19 non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e) and Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a) separates geometric score compression from representation drift reduction:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Unnormalized observation forecasting (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred}):} Raw observation residuals exhibit heavy tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 6.10$), severe calibration-to-holdout KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$), and high causal false alarms ($24.7\%$).
    \item \textbf{LayerNorm observation bottleneck (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}):} Inserting a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck before decoding mechanically bounds excursions, compressing kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halving causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but leaves calibration-to-holdout drift elevated ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$).
    \item \textbf{Learned latent projection (\texttt{TS-JEPA}, \texttt{OpEntropy}):} Forecasting in learned latent coordinates halves calibration-to-holdout KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ and $0.209$, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$) and reduces causal false alarms an additional five- to eight-fold ($1.7\%\text{--}2.8\%$).
    \item \textbf{Context autoencoding (\texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}) and linear controls:} Autoencoding context embeddings without temporal foresight achieves comparable drift reduction ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, causal FPR $1.7\%$, event recall $0.542$), while linear projection (\texttt{PCA-LinPred-LN}) reaches $2.1\%$ causal FPR at lower F1 ($0.017$) and \texttt{IsoForest} collapses in recall ($0.149$). LayerNorm geometry thus bounds tail kurtosis, while learned latent projection is required to suppress temporal distribution drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns. The main result is a large relative drop in false alarms. Point-F1 and PR-AUC against reconstruction baselines are statistically inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Fewer False Alarms on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring lowers held-out nominal FPR three- to four-fold under buffered attribution ($9.3\%$ and $10.1\%$ for TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA versus $30.0\%$ and $30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under causal streaming ($2.4\%$ and $2.8\%$ versus $24.3\%$ and $22.2\%$). The reduction holds across all 33 streams in paired tests (buffered FPR $-14.4\%$ to $-15.3\%$ versus TimesNet, $p\le 0.006$; $95\%$ CI for OpEntropy $[-24.1\%, -6.5\%]$) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$). Leaving one dataset out at a time keeps buffered FPR at $3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ for JEPAs versus $8.4\%\text{--}18.3\%$ for reconstructors. The exceptions are MSL, where TimesNet has the lower buffered FPR ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $17.7\%$), and Genesis, Metro, and SWAN, where reconstructors emit no alarms at all because their calibration thresholds are too high.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Quality Is Comparable, Not Better:} Lower false-alarm rates do not make latent scoring a better detector. Buffered Point-F1 and event recall for TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA fall in the same range as TimesNet and TranAD (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), and paired differences across all 33 streams are indistinguishable from zero (Point-F1 $+0.012$ and $+0.051$, $p=0.54$ and $0.88$; PR-AUC $-0.000$ and $+0.023$, $p=0.39$ and $0.16$ versus TimesNet and TranAD). Per-dataset standard deviations are as large as the means (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). Only SMAP beats the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.396$), although PR-AUC exceeds base prevalence $\pi$ on both SMAP ($0.504\text{--}0.525$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and MSL ($0.422\text{--}0.434$ vs.\ $\pi=0.255$).

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response, requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring auxiliary variance monitors when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Conditioning vs.\ Detection}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
What does the Operator-Entropy formulation contribute beyond unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? As established by the fixed-backbone equivalence tests in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}, the spectral regularizers condition the operator without altering detection accuracy ($p_{\text{TOST}} < 0.01$ down to $\Delta = \pm 0.010$). However, the OpEntropy transition architecture (combining linear operator $\mathbf{K}$, detached Lyapunov alignment, and radial energy scoring) shortens mean buffered detection delay from $110.3$ to $79.5$ steps ($124.0 \to 112.5$ causally, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b), and $127.0 \to 58.2$ on the decoupling panel). Cross-architecture Point-F1 differences against TS-JEPA satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ across all 33 streams for both OpEntropy ($+0.016$, $p_{\text{TOST}}=0.0019$) and Reynolds-Stress ($-0.008$, $p_{\text{TOST}}=0.0017$), whereas Potential-Flow degrades Point-F1 ($-0.081$, $p=0.033$ unadjusted) because its curl-free gradient parameterization penalizes periodic nominal orbits. The spectral-entropy and Reynolds priors therefore act as well-posed conditioning constraints rather than detection drivers.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} isolates the operational cost of zero-lookahead streaming. Without retrospective smoothing, the lower false-alarm rate ($2.4\%\text{--}2.8\%$) trades onset speed and sensitivity: causal Point-F1 and event recall are more conservative ($0.153\text{--}0.161$ and $0.487\text{--}0.517$, vs.\ $0.209$ and $0.588$ for TimesNet), and mean detection delay increases to $112.5\text{--}124.0$ steps ($62.2$ for TimesNet). Latent scoring thus functions in streaming mode as a conservative filter that prioritizes false-alarm suppression over rapid onset alarm. On five non-stationary incident streams, pairing latent scores with dynamic rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms by up to $21\times$ under regime drift.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
As established in Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}, target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger. To evaluate whether shorter horizons eliminate this latency without sacrificing false-alarm suppression, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero emission lag), OpEntropy JEPA holds causal FPR to $0.5\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving $0.729$ event recall and a detection delay of $\Delta t = 76.3$ steps. As horizon $S$ increases from $1$ to $64$, causal FPR for TimesNet rises nearly nine-fold ($2.5\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas OpEntropy and TS-JEPA remain bounded within $0.3\%\text{--}2.0\%$. Time-critical monitoring applications can therefore operate at $S \in \{1, 4\}$ to eliminate score-emission latency while preserving relative false-alarm suppression.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Reconstruction baselines calibrate to a realized false-alarm rate of $22\%\text{--}31\%$, against $1\%\text{--}10\%$ for latent predictors. Raw recall at one SPOT threshold therefore mixes detector quality with threshold placement. Long anomaly segments make this worse: Daphnet segments average $402$ steps and SMAP segments $1{,}102$, so a detector alarming at random with FPR $30\%$ still touches over $95\%$ of segments, since $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$ approaches one. We compare the models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)):} Thresholds are set at fixed empirical quantiles of the nominal calibration block without test labels. At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$, TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.752$) and higher precision ($0.251$ and $0.259$) at fewer false alarms ($177\text{--}203$ per $1{,}000$ nominal steps) than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall, $262$ alarms) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall, $270$ alarms).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR curves ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)):} Oracle thresholds are chosen to equalize realized test nominal FPR across models. At a stringent $1\%$ realized causal false-alarm budget ($f^* = 0.01$), latent models nearly double event recall ($0.371\text{--}0.431$) compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$). At relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$ causal and across buffered mappings), recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$ over $f^* \in [0.10, 0.50]$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-false-alarm operating regime ($f^* \le 0.01$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Does static extreme-value thresholding achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample? It does not. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro (cell-weighted) causal FPR of $1.51\%$ ($1.71\%$) for TS-JEPA and $1.95\%$ ($2.12\%$) for OpEntropy, which is roughly $15\text{--}20\times$ the $0.1\%$ design target, and buffered stream-macro (cell-weighted) FPR of $5.54\%$ ($6.40\%$) and $6.38\%$ ($7.25\%$), or $55\text{--}64\times$ ($64\text{--}73\times$) the design target. Although this is an order-of-magnitude improvement over TimesNet ($16.11\%$ causal, $19.85\%$ buffered) and TranAD ($14.23\%$ causal, $19.63\%$ buffered; $142\text{--}222\times$ overshoot), a $1.5\%\text{--}6.4\%$ nuisance-alarm rate still falls short of strict $0.1\%$ operational targets. Closing this remaining $15\text{--}64\times$ calibration gap requires pairing latent residuals with (i)~block-declustered GPD fitting (replacing raw correlated exceedances $N_u$ with independent cluster peaks scaled by the Ferro--Segers extremal index $\hat{\theta} \in (0, 1]$ \cite{ferro2003inference}, whose reciprocal $1/\hat{\theta}$ measures the average number of consecutive steps per tail burst), (ii)~online conformal or rolling quantile updates gated by prediction-error variance under discrete regime shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), or (iii)~state-conditioned threshold tables indexed by telemetry operating mode. Three diagnostics in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} explain the static overshoot:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability and quantile/length sensitivity:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ numerical solver fallback and low bootstrap threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in only $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR unchanged ($2.41\%\text{--}2.42\%$ for TS-JEPA), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) raises causal FPR to $3.72\%$ (TS-JEPA) and $4.13\%$ (OpEntropy).
    \item \textbf{Platykurtic collapse under total-threshold kurtosis scaling:} While total-threshold kurtosis scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ reduces buffered FPR on leptokurtic cells ($\gamma_2 \ge 0$, $4.49\% \to 1.11\%$ for TS-JEPA), $64$-step buffered smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of TS-JEPA cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$) with tight relative dispersion ($\sigma_{\text{cal}}/\mu_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.02\text{--}0.05$). Scaling total $\tau$ toward zero pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below the $u=p_{98}$ floor in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating overall buffered FPR from $6.40\%$ to $16.30\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Scaling strictly the tail excess above $u$, $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$, prevents threshold collapse below $u$ and holds 19-stream buffered FPR to $9.19\%$ vs.\ $9.29\%$ unadjusted ($2.40\%$ vs.\ $2.42\%$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)):} Simply permuting held-out evaluation scores against a fixed threshold cannot change the exceedance rate. Instead, our three-stage protocol pools the nominal calibration and evaluation vectors $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{cal}} + N_{\text{hold}}}$ and \emph{refits} the SPOT threshold ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) from scratch on each permuted calibration split: Stage~1 (chronological) fits $\tau_{\text{chrono}}$ on $v_{\text{cal}}$ and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}}$ on $v_{\text{hold}}$; Stage~2 ($L=64$ block permutation) shuffles contiguous $64$-step blocks across $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}}$, splits into permuted calibration ($N_{\text{cal}}$) and evaluation ($N_{\text{hold}}$) halves, \emph{refits} $\tau_{\text{block}}$, and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$ (equalizing macro-block distributions while preserving intra-block autocorrelation); and Stage~3 (pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation) uniformly shuffles all points in $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}}$, splits into calibration and evaluation halves, \emph{refits} $\tau_{\text{point}}$, and evaluates $\text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$. Across the $18$ streams with $N_{\text{cal}}, N_{\text{hold}} \ge 100$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)), inter-block distribution shift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$) accounts for an estimated $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess causal overshoot for JEPAs ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ for reconstructors), and serial dependence ($\text{FPR}_{\text{block}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$) for $29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$. Because latent predictors achieve $\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} = 0$ on 11 of 18 streams (concentrating stream-level variance onto the seven drifting streams so the TS-JEPA and OpEntropy inter-block $95\%$ CIs in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c) span zero), these percentages represent estimated shares of aggregate excess risk rather than invariant per-stream causal proportions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a chronological held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Mechanism Isolation and Regularizer Equivalence:} Comparing 12 controlled backbones decouples score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks bound residual kurtosis and halve false alarms, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.209\text{--}0.214$) and reduce causal false-positive rates to $0.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$. On a fixed Koopman backbone, the spectral-entropy regularizer satisfies TOST equivalence down to $\Delta = \pm 0.010$ Point-F1, functioning as an operator conditioning constraint while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to unconstrained TS-JEPA ($110.3 \to 79.5$ steps buffered; $127.0 \to 58.2$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Control vs.\ Detection Parity:} Latent predictive scoring lowers held-out false alarms three- to four-fold under buffered attribution and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming, and nearly doubles causal event recall at stringent realized false-alarm budgets ($f^* = 0.01$), while overall Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain comparable to observation-space reconstructors.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Drift and Operational Boundaries:} Static SPOT still overshoots its nominal $0.1\%$ design risk by $15\text{--}64\times$ out of sample, driven primarily by inter-block distribution shift ($56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess risk) and serial dependence ($29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$). Future work should pair latent predictive residuals with declustered GPD fitting, online conformal quantile updates, and hybrid variance monitors to close the remaining calibration gap and cover quiet single-channel flatlines.
\end{enumerate}
