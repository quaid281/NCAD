\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still collapse prediction errors into a single scalar score, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces scalar scoring and tail-fitted thresholding. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded via a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon decomposition across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference), each monitored by an independently calibrated breaker with a nominal drift guard. Surgical weight-space quenching on healthy calibration windows removes mean predictor-bias drift and tempers high-variance precision directions before scoring. On 19 development streams, the panel reaches 0.262 buffered point-wise F1, 1.6 percent causal false-alarm rate, and a 59.6-step detection delay; across look-ahead horizons from 1 to 64 steps, it doubles to quadruples scalar causal F1 down to zero reporting lag. On 118 untouched held-out streams providing primary generalization evidence, a three-horizon panel raises causal point-wise F1 from 0.072 to 0.083 for scalar JEPAs to 0.143 and event recall from 51 to 73 percent, while weight-space quenching cuts causal false alarms by 8.0 percent relative across 162 streams. The panel requires roughly 1,000 calibration windows (300 effective independent samples), misses slow ramps, and needs a complementary channel-level branch for isolated single-sensor faults.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, chemical plants, server farms, and wearable medical devices report health through dozens of continuous sensor channels \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. When a valve sticks, a reaction wheel drifts, or a patient's gait freezes, an automated monitor must detect the fault promptly while learning healthy behavior from normal history alone, because labeled faults are rare. Equally important, the monitor must keep false alarms low during nominal operation so operators can trust its alerts.

\noindent\emph{Why Observation-Space Reconstructors Suffer High False-Alarm Rates:} Deep detectors such as TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} reconstruct the raw sensor signal and alert when reconstruction error is large. Raw telemetry, however, contains unforecastable sensor jitter, thermal drift, and disparate channel scales. Summed across 25 to 55 channels, these misses form a heavy-tailed residual with frequent spikes (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Calibrated on healthy data via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} and evaluated on later healthy data, reconstructors raise false alarms during $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of normal operation; a random alerter firing as often would catch $60.0\%$ of anomaly events, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\noindent\emph{The Bottleneck of Scalar Score Aggregation:} Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoid fitting raw noise by compressing each telemetry window into a 32-dimensional unit-sphere summary and predicting future summaries in latent space, cutting false alarms to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$. Yet existing latent detectors still sum all coordinate errors into one scalar score compared against one threshold. If one or two of the 32 coordinates shift slightly between training and calibration---variance-dominating coordinates we term \emph{hijacker dials}---they dominate the sum and inflate the threshold, so faults on the remaining 30 coordinates go undetected: causal Point-F1 falls to $0.050\text{--}0.052$ and average delay exceeds 240 steps.

\noindent\emph{Four-Stage Architecture (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} We replace scalar score aggregation with a four-stage pipeline, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}). First, a causal dilated encoder maps context and target windows onto a 32-dimensional spherical latent space via layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and forecasts four nested sub-horizons with Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis whitening. Second, by structural analogy to Reynolds averaging in fluid mechanics \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} (without physical Navier--Stokes assumptions), we decompose each of the 32 coordinate error trajectories across the four nested horizons into seven kinematic views: four sub-horizon errors plus their horizon mean (drift), standard deviation (fluctuation), and endpoint difference (acceleration). Third, these $7 \times 32 = 224$ statistics feed an independently calibrated \emph{224-bit circuit-breaker panel} with per-dial calibration drift guards and 32 fast right-edge coincidence relays (\emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} when unquenched). Fourth, post-training \emph{surgical weight-space quenching} on healthy calibration windows removes mean predictor-bias drift along the LayerNorm tangent space and tempers top precision-matrix eigendirections before panel calibration.

\subsection{Contributions}
We separate our evaluation into a 19-stream (and 33-stream) development cohort for ablations and a disjoint 118-stream untouched held-out cohort (within a 141- to 162-stream archive across 15 datasets) providing primary generalization evidence (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{224-bit circuit-breaker panel and surgical quenching across development and untouched held-out cohorts:} Replacing scalar thresholding with the 224-bit panel and surgical weight-space quenching (Sections~\ref{subsec:reynolds_expansion}--\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}) raises causal Point-F1 on the 19 development streams from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $0.262$ buffered Point-F1, $1.6\%$ causal false-positive rate (FPR), and $59.6$-step causal delay, trading sustained causal point coverage ($0.138$ vs.\ $0.100$ causal F1) for faster onset detection ($59.6$ vs.\ $105.9$ steps). On the 118 untouched held-out streams, a three-horizon panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.072\text{--}0.083$ (1D JEPAs) to $0.143$ ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$, dataset-clustered $p = 0.007$) and event recall from $51.2\%$ to $72.8\%$; across 162 streams (15 datasets), surgical quenching cuts causal held-out FPR by $8.0\%$ relative ($p = 0.0011$, dataset $p = 0.027$; median held-out FPR $1.03\%$, test FPR $1.59\%$).
    \item \emph{Mechanism isolation, quenching ablations, and multi-horizon latency scaling:} Matched-backbone ablations ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ parameters, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) isolate LayerNorm kurtosis compression ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent targets (drift $D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$), barycenter centering (regime-drift FPR share $71.3\% \to 18.7\%$), the 1-to-224 dial ladder, and bias recentering vs.\ precision tempering across $k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{0,\dots,12\}$. Retraining the 224-bit panel and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} across target horizons $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ shows $1.9\times\text{--}4.3\times$ higher causal Point-F1 and $+26\%\text{--}38\%$ higher event recall than 1D scalar scoring down to $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag).
    \item \emph{False-alarm reduction and diagnostic fixed-budget recall:} On the 19 development streams, latent prediction holds autonomous causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($N=7$ dataset $p = 0.016$). Under a non-deployable post-hoc diagnostic control at $f^* = 1\%$ test FPR, the panel catches $58.4\%$ of anomaly events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet and reaches $0.658$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) on NASA contextual faults (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}--\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Calibration sample-size theory, single-channel branch, and null results:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds the horizon-fluctuation concentration ratio $R_{\text{LT}} \in [\frac{1}{M-1}, 1]$ (a structural representation property; scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ reweighting is null, $\Delta\text{F1} = -0.005$). Auditing effective sample sizes $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100,\dots,5{,}000\}$ with moving-block bootstrap confidence intervals establishes a minimum requirement of $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ independent samples ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows) for empirical quantiles, while interquartile-range (IQR) and Beta-smoothed calibrators hold test FPR at $0.47\%\text{--}1.32\%$ when $N_{\text{eff}} \le 250$. Across 17 multivariate streams and six single-sensor fault classes, a complementary channel-level branch raises single-channel event recall from $61.4\%\text{--}80.7\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}98.2\%$, whereas slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timestamps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that trigger frequent false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: temporal and contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time} and TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximize contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.088$ and $0.097$ stream Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.186$ for latent prediction across 130 streams, Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from a separate target encoder, preventing collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Reynolds-Inspired Residual Decomposition and Circuit-Breaker Panels vs.\ Statistical Process Control:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman and operator-theoretic models \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} lift nonlinear dynamics into learned observable spaces. Rather than imposing partial differential equations on raw sensors, we apply a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon residual decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical}, by analogy, to split multi-horizon latent residuals into per-coordinate horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference. Classical multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} quadratic-form-pool whitened coordinates into a single Mahalanobis scalar, just as deep detectors collapse residuals into a 1D score calibrated via Pickands--Balkema--de Haan extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) fitting in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, where a single coordinate with out-of-sample variance inflation under serial correlation \cite{ferro2003inference} dominates the scalar threshold. Conversely, banks of univariate Shewhart or Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) charts \cite{page1954continuous} monitor raw channels or single statistics under Bonferroni or false-discovery-rate (FDR) multiplicity corrections \cite{benjamini1995controlling}, but lack multi-horizon kinematic perspectives, per-dial drift quarantine guards, and right-edge coincidence relays; soft attention ensemble voting across dials adds no benefit over Boolean quorums (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)). We evaluate all detectors using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm curves \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}, expanded in archive-wide evaluations to 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets and 162 streams across 15 datasets (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}). Thirty-three streams enter the primary protocol (19 non-GHL development streams and 14 GHL streams); 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. The suite spans four domains: \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry} covers six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$ channels), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$, four primary and two short-nominal), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$), where NASA's archive \cite{Hundman2018b} classifies each SMAP/MSL sequence as an out-of-range \emph{point} fault or in-range \emph{contextual} fault (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)). \emph{Wearable kinematics} covers six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals $1\,\text{s}$). \emph{Industrial and infrastructure telemetry} covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution ($K{=}9$, $37$ anomalies), Metro I-94 hourly traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$). Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation} evaluates six single-channel fault classes across all 17 multivariate development streams ($K > 1$).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$) and labeled test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$. Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by a 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Primary 33-stream inclusion required $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ ($T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$) and $K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); among the 12 reserved streams, five have $T_{\text{nom}} \in [764, 1{,}492]$ ($N_{\text{cal}} \le 101$ across CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), six CICIDS streams have $K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \ge 1.36\times 10^5$, and GECCO ($K{=}9$) enters the archive cohorts. To eliminate subset-selection bias, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates all 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$ and $\ge 1$ test anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, 13 OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN) at three seeds, of which 118 streams were never inspected during development, and evaluates four-horizon Laminar-JEPA across 162 streams spanning 15 datasets (adding 14 GHL, Secure Water Treatment (SWaT), Water Distribution (WADI), and reserve streams; 139 untouched streams).

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within multi-stream archives share hardware platforms. We therefore treat dataset clusters ($N \in \{7, 9, 12, 15\}$) as co-primary statistical units alongside streams, reporting Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) verification (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Windowing, Multi-Depth TCN Hierarchy Tapping, and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to binary alarm decisions. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and partitions the stream at index $t$ into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$) and a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). For $K > 1$, a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Channels are decomposed across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit heavy-tailed sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:related}), we map windows into a compact 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ onto the sphere $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, where target weights $\phi$ track online weights $\theta$ via an exponential moving average (EMA), $\phi_k \leftarrow m \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m) \theta_k$ with fixed momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ in Cohorts~3--4) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Because deep dilated TCN blocks smooth out sharp onset spikes while non-stationary streams shift the latent mean, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} introduces two structural operations inside both encoders:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth functional hierarchy tapping:} A tap head extracts $L_{\text{tap}}=3$ intermediate feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow dyadic front-end, the mid-depth TCN block, and the terminal TCN block. For each tap $\ell$, the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy are concatenated into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6 d_{\text{model}}}$, projected into $\mathbb{R}^D$, and fused with the deep representation $\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}}$ via a bounded gate $g = 0.5\,\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} = (1 - g)\,\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}} + g\,\operatorname{LN}\bigl(W_2\,\phi_{\text{GELU}}(\operatorname{LN}(W_1 \mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}}))\bigr).
        \label{eq:multidepth_tap}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} An online running centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ updated at rate $0.05$ on training batches is copied synchronously to the EMA target tap head and subtracted from both context and target fused vectors before terminal LayerNorm projection $z = \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}) \in \sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, canceling common-mode baseline drift between windows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Nested Causal Multi-Horizon Rollout and Variance-Floor Decorrelation Objective}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-window forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with predictive MSE plus a log-determinant volume term $-\frac{\beta}{D}\ln\det(\mathbf{\Sigma} + \epsilon\mathbf{I})$ on predicted and context batches ($\beta = 0.05$), a smooth relative of VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Terminal LayerNorm bounds representations on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ and compresses residual kurtosis, but pooling the $S=64$ target window into one vector averages out short-lived sub-horizon transients.

To resolve sub-window dynamics across target horizon $S$ (default $S=64$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), Stage~2 of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} partitions $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefixes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ of lengths $S_m = \max(1, \lfloor mS/M \rfloor)$ ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ at $S=64$). The EMA target encoder $E_\phi$ encodes each prefix into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. Starting from $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$, a sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ using learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the full-horizon anchor $P_\theta$ forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ ($m < M$) and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, training minimizes multi-horizon mean-squared error (MSE) plus a variance-floor and decorrelation penalty:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MHR}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ penalizes low variance and cross-covariance of centered batch predictions $\mathbf{Z}_c = \mathbf{Z} - \mathbf{1}_B \bar{\mathbf{z}}^\top$ with empirical covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{Z}) = (B-1)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_c^\top \mathbf{Z}_c$ and marginal standard deviations $\sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}) = \sqrt{\Sigma_{dd}(\mathbf{Z}) + \epsilon}$ ($\gamma = 0.75$, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$), defined by $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dd} = \sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}))}$ and $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dj} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Sigma_{dj}(\mathbf{Z})$ ($d \neq j$). This enforces a variance floor plus decorrelation (full whitening occurs in Stage~3 via Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage), remaining well-conditioned when $B = 16 < D = 32$ and raising effective representation rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}. Archive-wide evaluations test three-horizon \emph{Speculative-JEPA} across $141$ streams (prefixes $\{16, 32, 64\}$, weights $(0.20, 0.25, 0.55)$, and the 48-step residual interpolated from 32 and 64, giving $192$ distinct statistics) and four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} across $162$ streams ($15$ datasets).

\subsection{Stage 3: Calibration-Stabilized Whitening, Surgical Weight-Space Quenching, and 224-Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Why does collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a single 1D score degrade point-wise F1? Let $\mathbf{E}(t) = [\mathbf{e}_1(t), \dots, \mathbf{e}_M(t)]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ stack the $M=4$ raw sub-horizon prediction residuals $\mathbf{e}_m(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ across all $MD = 128$ coordinates. Fitting the Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ \cite{ledoit2004well} and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows produces Mahalanobis-whitened residuals $\mathbf{E}^{\text{white}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ with unit training variance per coordinate. On unseen nominal calibration windows, however, one or two whitened directions often experience out-of-sample variance growth (\emph{hijacker dials}) where $\mathbf{\Omega}$ amplifies small covariance shifts. Summing all 128 coordinates into a 1D scalar score allows those one or two drifting coordinates to dominate the calibration tail and inflate the scalar threshold. Stage~3 prevents both precision amplification and 1D collapse through two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Surgical weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and calibration-stabilized whitening:} Inverting $\mathbf{\Omega}$ and predictor Jacobians without data reconstructs the 224-dial coordinate roles at $88.4\%$ accuracy ($162$ streams) and shows that stochastic gradient descent traps $12.7\%$ of horizon-embedding energy in the saw-tooth mode $\boldsymbol{\phi}_3 = [-1, +3, -3, +1]^\top/\sqrt{20}$. A constant baseline shift between training and calibration offsets the mean residual $\bar{\mathbf{e}}_m = \mathbb{E}_{\text{cal}}[\mathbf{e}_m(t)]$ on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, while covariance rotation inflates variance along steep precision eigenvectors ($\lambda_k \gg 1$). Because terminal LayerNorm maps pre-normalized outputs $\mathbf{u}_m$ onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ with first-order Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_{\text{LN}}(\hat{\bm{\mu}}_m) = (\sqrt{D}/\sigma_{\text{pre},m})(\mathbf{I}_D - \hat{\bm{\mu}}_m\hat{\bm{\mu}}_m^\top)$, shifting each predictor's terminal bias by $\Delta\mathbf{b}_m = (\sigma_{\text{pre},m}/\sqrt{D})(\mathbf{I}_D - \hat{\bm{\mu}}_m\hat{\bm{\mu}}_m^\top)\bar{\mathbf{e}}_m$ cancels the stationary tangent-space calibration drift to first order while leaving time-varying anomaly excursions $\mathbf{e}_m(t) - \bar{\mathbf{e}}_m$ invariant. Simultaneously, tempering $\mathbf{\Omega}$ via smooth saturation $\tilde{\lambda}_k = \lambda_k / (1 + \lambda_k/\lambda_{\max})$, damping output rows on the top $k_{\text{rogue}} \le 4$ variance-inflated coordinates, and decoupling their off-diagonal precision entries prevent an unstable direction from contaminating clean coordinates (accepted only when late-half calibration trips do not increase; ablations in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Any coordinate with $\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) > 1.10$ is then blended toward $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t) = e_{m,c}(t) / (\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{raw}}(m,c) + 10^{-4})$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon residual decomposition into $7D = 224$ dials:} By structural analogy to Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} (applied here along the discrete lookahead axis $m \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ rather than a continuous fluid field), we expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into $7D = 224$ non-negative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~\emph{sub-horizon position magnitudes ($4D = 128$ dials)} $d_{\text{pos},m,c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ ($m \in \{1..4\}$);
    (b)~\emph{horizon-mean (drift) dials ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{drift},c}(t) = |\bar{e}_c(t)| = |\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
    (c)~\emph{horizon-fluctuation dials ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sigma'_c(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t))^2}$; and
    (d)~\emph{endpoint-change (acceleration) dials ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} characterizes this multi-horizon residual representation, showing that the drift and fluctuation dials orthogonally partition sub-horizon energy and that the horizon-fluctuation spectrum lies on a bounded interval (a structural representation property rather than a guarantee of detection accuracy).

\begin{proposition}[Structural Characterization of Multi-Horizon Residual Energy and Spectral Concentration]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) denote the stabilized multi-horizon residual field with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\top \mathbf{1}_M \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Then:
(i)~\emph{Coordinate-wise energy orthogonality and endpoint bound:} For every coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,D\}$, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M d_{\text{pos},m,c}^2(t) = d_{\text{drift},c}^2(t) + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$, and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$.
(ii)~\emph{Spectral concentration bounds for the horizon Gram matrix:} Let $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ have ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ ($\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$). For any $\gamma > -1/2$, the spectral concentration ratio (following Lieb--Thirring power-sum moments \cite{lieb1976inequalities}),
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2}} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr] \quad (\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}),
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant, attaining $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) iff fluctuation energy is isotropic across $M-1$ modes, and $1$ iff $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ yields a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + e'_{m,c})^2$ with $\sum_{m=1}^M e'_{m,c} = 0$ yields the energy identity, and Cauchy--Schwarz on $e'_{M,c} - e'_{1,c}$ gives $d_{\text{accel},c}^2 \le 2(e'^2_{M,c} + e'^2_{1,c}) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$, proving~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$ forces $\lambda_1 = 0$, so $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$, yielding (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Zero-Collapse 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel, Onset Relays, and Temporal Attribution}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
In Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}), each of the 224 dials receives its own binary circuit breaker calibrated on nominal data. Because all 224 dials derive from the same $4 \times 32$ residual field, they are correlated by construction (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the 19 development streams; simultaneous trip counts have $7.2\times$ the variance of an independence model), so the quorums below are calibrated directly on joint nominal trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and nominal hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ (and $j \in \{1,\dots,128\}$ in the 128-bit sub-block panel) is standardized by its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled median absolute deviation (MAD) $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. To quarantine any drifting dial individually, each tripwire $\tau_j$ scales its $98.5$th calibration percentile by a two-factor nominal hijack guard $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}},
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}$ is the ratio of second-half to first-half calibration 98th percentiles whenever $>1.35$ ($1$ otherwise), and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$ is $(m_j + s_j)/(1.5\,s_{\text{group},j})$ whenever $>1.25$ ($1$ otherwise, where $s_{\text{group},j}$ is the perspective-group median of $m_j + s_j$).
    \item \emph{32 fast right-edge onset relays:} Each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines a stricter edge switch $b_{p,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_{p,c}(t) > q_{0.993}(z_{p,c,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_{p,c}^{1.25}\bigr)$ and fires its onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when the terminal sub-block ($m=4$) coincides with endpoint acceleration, fluctuation, or the third sub-block ($m=3$):
    \begin{equation}
        r_c(t) = b_{\text{pos},4,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \wedge \bigl(b_{\text{accel},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{fluc},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{pos},3,c}^{\text{edge}}(t)\bigr).
        \label{eq:onset_relay}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Causal trailing vs.\ prefix-routed buffered attribution and Boolean quorums:} Under \emph{causal trailing attribution}, window switches at index $t$ are emitted solely at the right endpoint $\tau = t + S - 1$ ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable, with structural reporting lag $S-1$ vs.\ empirical detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, each perspective votes only over the steps inside $[t, t+S)$ it physically observes ($[t, t+S_m)$ for sub-block $m$, $[t, t+S)$ for drift and fluctuation, and $[t+S_3, t+S_4)$ for acceleration). Summing active switches at $\tau$ gives counts $N_{224}(\tau)$, $N_{128}(\tau)$, and $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$, with combined count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = N_{224}(\tau) + N_{128}(\tau) + 2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ for PR-AUC ranking. Binary alarms trigger when any counter exceeds its calibration quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_{\text{edge}} = \max\bigl(1.25\,q_{0.999}(N_{\text{edge},\text{cal}}),\, 2.5\bigr)$, \emph{224-Bit Breaker} sets $K_{224} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{224,\text{cal}}),\, 5.0\bigr)$ and $K_{128} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\, 3.5\bigr)$, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} sets $K_{224} = \max\bigl(1.18\,q_{0.9985}(N_{224,\text{cal}}),\, 5.5\bigr)$ and $K_{128} = \max\bigl(1.20\,q_{0.9985}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\, 4.0\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{1D Scalar Collapse Controls:} To compare against 1D scalar collapse on identical backbones (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), we evaluate the saliency gate $g_d(\mathbf{v}) = (1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha\sqrt{d\sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}$ with $w_i \propto \exp(|v_i|/(\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}}))$ ($\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2/\sqrt{d} + 10^{-6}$, $\alpha = \tau_g = 0.5$) \label{eq:saliency_gate}. Single-horizon TS-JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$, and 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))^2 + \max_m g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))^2)}$ modulated by $\mathcal{S}_{\text{LT}}(t) = \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{LT}}\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t)))\bigr)$ ($\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$), calibrated via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing} ($u = p_{98}, q = 10^{-3}$).

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Two-Stage Nominal Calibration, and Statistical Design}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures operate on a streaming pipeline with context window $C=256$ and target horizon $S=64$ ($W = C + S = 320$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), trained on nominal windows at stride $16$ and evaluated at stride $1$. Context and target windows are encoded into $D=32$ spherical representations by the multiscale front-end and causal dilated TCN, fused with the three-depth tap head (\ref{eq:multidepth_tap}), and centered by the running context barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ prior to terminal LayerNorm. Models are trained using AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) with zero per-dataset tuning: batch $16$ for $15\text{--}20$ epochs on the 19- and 33-stream cohorts, and batch $16\text{--}64$ for $10\text{--}12$ epochs with gradient clipping at $1.0$ and cosine annealing on the archive cohorts.

\paragraph{Two-Stage Nominal Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
Each stream's nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$):
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Training fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$):} Standardization parameters, encoder/predictor weights, running barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ are fit strictly on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ (in archive runs, early stopping uses unsupervised predictive loss on the calibration block without labels).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Circuit-breaker calibration and held-out verification on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Sliding windows of length $W = 320$ inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2) yield $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window timestamps. Splitting at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $[0 : H)$ and \emph{held-out evaluation block} $[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so calibration and evaluation share zero target observations (a full $G=320$ gap check changes causal FPR by $<0.25$ percentage points). On $[0 : H)$, the panel fits $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), per-dial medians $m_j$ and MAD scales $s_j$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), which are frozen and evaluated on $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$ and on the test stream. Throughout the paper we distinguish nominal calibration window count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective independent sample size} $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int}}$ ($\tau_{\text{int}} = 1 + 2\sum_{k\ge 1}\rho_k \approx 3.23$).

\paragraph{Hierarchical Statistical Units, Dataset-Clustered Inference, and Hypothesis Testing}
Because streams within multi-stream archives share hardware and operating environments, we report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Stream-level and dataset-clustered inference:} At the stream level ($n \in \{19, 33, 118, 141, 162\}$), we report two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-values with Benjamini--Hochberg FDR adjustment \cite{benjamini1995controlling} ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$, $|\Delta| \le 10^{-4}$ ties) and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs ($2{,}000$ resamples). At the domain level, we treat the dataset family ($N \in \{7, 9, 12, 15\}$ clusters) as the co-primary independent cluster, reporting unweighted Dataset-Macro means, $95\%$ dataset-cluster bootstrap CIs, exact $2^N$ dataset sign-flip permutation $p$-values, and non-Exathlon held-out subsets ($n=101, N=8$).
(2)~\emph{12-fold LODO calibration-selection audit:} Selecting the calibrator on $D-1$ datasets across the 141-stream archive to maximize causal Point-F1 subject to $\text{FPR} \le 5\%$ selects the 224-bit circuit-breaker rule in all $12/12$ folds (held-out causal F1 $= 0.143$ vs.\ $0.076$ for 1D SPOT on $n=118$).

\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder, Circuit-Breaker Components, and Weight-Space Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate the step-by-step expansion ladder, component removals, and surgical weight-space quenching ablations on the 19-stream development cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{From 1D scalar collapse to 32-bit and 128-bit sub-horizon switches (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)):} Collapsing the 128 whitened coordinates into a 1D score (with or without $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation; $\alpha_{\text{LT}}=0.12$ changes causal F1 by $-0.005$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.012, 0.000]$) lets one or two drifting coordinates inflate the scalar threshold ($0.052$ causal F1, $246.6$-step delay). Using 32 per-coordinate switches on the terminal horizon ($m=4$) nearly doubles causal F1 to $0.101$, and expanding across all four prefixes (\emph{128-Bit Sub-Horizons}) lifts causal F1 to $0.129$ and buffered F1 from $0.137$ to $0.192$, serving as plain multi-coordinate baselines.
    \item \emph{Adding horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint change:} The 32 drift dials (\emph{160-Bit}) halve causal delay ($146.2 \to 69.1$ steps); the 32 fluctuation dials (\emph{192-Bit}) lift buffered F1 to $0.203$ but lengthen causal delay to $179.0$ steps. Adding the 32 endpoint-change dials and onset relays (\emph{224-Bit Breaker}) reaches $0.138$ causal F1 ($17/0$ wins over 1D collapse, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), $0.228$ buffered F1 ($p = 0.026$), $0.411$ buffered PR-AUC, $60.7\%$ causal and $63.1\%$ buffered ER, and $71.8$-step buffered delay.
    \item \emph{Leave-one-out panel ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Removing the 32 onset relays slows causal delay from $105.9$ to $123.7$ steps and lowers causal F1 to $0.135$. Replacing prefix routing with uniform 64-step smearing degrades buffered ER ($63.1\% \to 58.4\%$) and F1 ($0.228 \to 0.223$). Removing hijack guards ($\gamma_j = 1$) lowers causal/buffered F1 to $0.137/0.225$, and soft attention adds no gain ($0.137/0.226$).
    \item \emph{Surgical weight-space quenching components and sensitivity ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{0,\dots,12\}$):} Disentangling the two quenching operations of Section~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel} across the 19 development streams (seed 42) shows distinct roles: (i)~predictor-bias recentering alone ($\Delta\mathbf{b}_m$) lifts causal ER ($56.5\% \to 64.3\%$), causal F1 ($0.071 \to 0.084$), and buffered F1 ($0.224 \to 0.256$) and cuts causal delay ($90.1 \to 63.8$ steps), but raises test FPR ($1.5\% \to 2.8\%$ causal; $17.4\% \to 23.3\%$ buffered); (ii)~untargeted precision tempering alone degrades buffered F1 ($0.224 \to 0.135$) and PR-AUC ($0.449 \to 0.271$); and (iii)~RMS stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) alone lifts causal F1 to $0.099$ ($59.9\%$ ER). Sweeping $k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 12\}$ confirms that tempering the top $k_{\text{rogue}} \in [1, 4]$ coordinates with internal calibration-split gating (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) preserves event recall ($61.4\%\text{--}64.6\%$) while cutting buffered FPR ($24.2\% \to 19.2\%$ on $n{=}19$) and archive-wide causal FPR across 162 streams ($6.71\% \to 6.17\%$ held-out, $p = 0.0011$), whereas over-quenching ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{8, 12\}$) lowers causal PR-AUC ($0.292 \to 0.223$) and buffered F1 ($0.215 \to 0.144$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When one sensor out of $K$ freezes or degrades while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the single channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$, and LayerNorm rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. On six injected streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), 1D TS-JEPA detects $19.4\%$ of quiet-fault events ($<3\%$ point recall); standalone \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} lifts quiet-fault event recall to $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ ($14.3\%\text{--}25.6\%$ point recall), and a union with a rolling 32-step variance-collapse monitor reaches $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$. To test a broader channel-level remedy across all 17 multivariate development streams ($K \in [5, 55]$, excluding univariate Yahoo), we evaluate a complementary channel-level branch alongside the 224-bit panel. For each channel $c \in \{1,\dots,K\}$, the branch tracks three causal statistics calibrated at $q_{0.999}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ with per-channel calibration drift guards $\gamma_c^{\text{cal}}$: (i)~rolling 32-step channel variance collapse $\max(0, 1 - s_c(t)/\text{med}_{\text{cal}}(s_c))$, (ii)~causal first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) channel prediction residuals $|x_{t,c} - \hat{\phi}_{1,c} x_{t-1,c} - \hat{\phi}_{0,c}|$, and (iii)~rolling 32-step channel mean bias $|\bar{x}_{t,c}^{(32)} - \mu_{\text{cal},c}|$. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into the test windows of all 17 multivariate streams, comparing standalone \emph{224-Bit Breaker} $\to$ \emph{Hybrid Variance-Collapse Monitor} $\to$ \emph{224-Bit + Full Channel Branch} yields: (1)~variance collapse: ER $61.4\% \to 93.0\% \to 93.0\%$ (causal F1 $0.077 \to 0.376 \to 0.269$); (2)~channel prediction shocks: $80.7\% \to 82.5\% \to 98.2\%$ (F1 $0.139 \to 0.139 \to 0.327$, delay $48.6 \to 35.9$); (3)~sensor freezing: $63.2\% \to 93.0\% \to 93.0\%$ (F1 $0.052 \to 0.378 \to 0.273$); (4)~sensor saturation: $73.7\% \to 96.5\% \to 96.5\%$ (F1 $0.113 \to 0.414 \to 0.368$, delay $50.7 \to 34.3$); (5)~gradual degradation: $68.4\% \to 70.2\% \to 93.0\%$ (F1 $0.080 \to 0.078 \to 0.154$); and (6)~isolated sensor bias: $70.2\% \to 71.9\% \to 93.0\%$ (F1 $0.095 \to 0.094 \to 0.195$). On unmodified test data across the 17 streams, however, monitoring $3K$ channel statistics in parallel raises causal FPR from $1.5\%$ (standalone 224-Bit) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full channel branch); thus, the variance-collapse union offers a practical compromise for freeze/saturation faults ($93.0\%\text{--}96.5\%$ ER at $6.8\%$ FPR), whereas full channel-residual monitoring requires cross-channel quorum gating when $K$ is large.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Training Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting across five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, and \texttt{GECCO}, three seeds) shows that shell splitting helps on high-channel spacecraft arrays (buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$ on \texttt{MSL M-1}, $K=55$; causal $0.212 \to 0.321$ on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, $K=25$) but hurts on $K=9$ streams ($0.136$ vs.\ $0.217$ on \texttt{Daphnet}, $0.158$ vs.\ $0.321$ on \texttt{GECCO}; 5-stream causal F1 $0.156$ vs.\ $0.193$), paying off only when $K \ge 25$.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 15 model arms across the 19 development streams under matched budgets and parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five factors:
(1)~\emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations:} Using the same causal dilated TCN encoder to forecast or reconstruct in raw sensor space leaves tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and causal FPR ($\approx 25\%$) nearly identical.
(2)~\emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space:} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck to the observation forecaster compresses kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but raw-waveform targets leave drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$, causal F1 $0.084$).
(3)~\emph{Learned latent target projection and whitening:} Moving the target into the learned $D=32$ latent space halves calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA, $p \le 0.001$).
(4)~\emph{Nonlinear TCN vs.\ linear PCA and Isolation Forest:} 32 principal components keep causal FPR at $2.1\%$ but drop causal F1 to $0.017$, and Isolation Forest reaches $0.149$ causal ER.
(5)~\emph{Prediction vs.\ autoencoding, frozen targets, and $\ell_2$ scoring:} Latent context autoencoding lowers causal F1 from $0.161$ to $0.112$, while a frozen target encoder or $\ell_2$ scoring still holds causal FPR below $2\%$, confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent representation.

\subsection{Development 33-Stream and Untouched Held-Out 118/162-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} evaluates the 33-stream development benchmark (19 non-GHL and 14 GHL streams) under the two-stage protocol: Panel~(a) reports retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset summaries. Primary independent generalization evidence across the 118 untouched held-out streams (within the 141- and 162-stream archive cohorts) appears in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}; on the 130-stream external baseline screen, TS-JEPA achieves $0.186$ stream and $0.160$ macro Point-F1 versus $0.152$ for TranAD, $0.151$ for TimesNet, $0.097$ for DCdetector, $0.088$ for NCAD, and $0.082$ for Anomaly Transformer.

\noindent\emph{Operational Trade-Offs, Precision, and Recall Across Operating Points:} Comparing detector configurations reveals a clear trade-off across false-alarm rate, point-wise precision ($P$) and recall ($R$), event recall (ER), and onset delay ($\Delta t$). On the 19 development streams under causal streaming (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b)), unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit Breaker} maximizes causal Point-F1 ($0.138$, $P = 0.252$, $R = 0.123$; $17/0$ wins over 1D collapse, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$) at $60.7\%$ ER, $1.7\%$ held-out FPR, and $105.9$-step causal delay (buffered: Point-F1 $= 0.228$, $P = 0.256$, $R = 0.289$, PR-AUC $= 0.411$, ER $= 63.1\%$, delay $= 71.8$). Pairing the 224-bit panel with surgical weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 ($0.262$, $P = 0.273$, $R = 0.369$), buffered ER ($66.4\%$), causal ER ($62.5\%$), fastest causal delay ($59.6$ steps vs.\ TimesNet's $62.2$), lower causal FPR ($1.6\%$), and highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.139$), while lowering causal Point-F1 to $0.100$ ($P = 0.214$, $R = 0.088$) because projecting out mean calibration drift concentrates causal trips near fault onset transitions rather than sustained plateaus. Conversely, \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit Strict} prioritizes a lower false-alarm floor ($1.1\%$ causal FPR, $P = 0.254$, $R = 0.097$, F1 $= 0.116$, ER $= 53.4\%$), while \emph{1D Scalar Reynolds} attains high precision ($P = 0.285$) at $0.6\%$ FPR but suffers severe recall collapse ($R = 0.032$, F1 $= 0.050$). On the 118 untouched held-out streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), the three-horizon \emph{Speculative-JEPA 224-Bit} panel lifts both causal precision ($P: 0.175 \to 0.241$) and recall ($R: 0.081 \to 0.179$) over 1D scalar SPOT (causal F1: $0.083 \to 0.143$, ER: $51.2\% \to 72.8\%$). Practitioners should select: (i)~\emph{Laminar-JEPA} to minimize causal onset latency ($\Delta t = 59.6$), maximize event recall ($62.5\%\text{--}66.4\%$), or maximize buffered F1 ($0.262$); (ii)~unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit Breaker} to maximize real-time causal point coverage ($0.138$); and (iii)~\emph{224-Bit Strict} to enforce a $\le 1.1\%$ nominal false-alarm ceiling.

\noindent\emph{Reducing Observation-Space False-Alarm Flooding:} On the 19 development streams, predicting spherical latent representations cuts held-out nominal FPR three-fold under buffered attribution ($7.8\%\text{--}9.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, $p \le 0.045$) and by an order of magnitude under causal streaming ($0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$, $p = 0.010$; $N=7$ dataset-clustered $p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, JEPAs maintain $0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors. On GHL ($\pi=0.013$), all JEPAs and the linear AR forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1): low FPR without recall is not detection.

\noindent\emph{Scope of Applicability:} The evidence supports the architecture under four boundary conditions: (i)~multichannel arrays ($K \ge 9$; SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, SMD, Exathlon, OPPORTUNITY); (ii)~horizon selection $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ matched to the tolerable reporting lag $S-1$ of the sampling rate (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), avoiding $S=64$ on hourly Metro ($63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$); (iii)~calibration histories providing $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ effective independent observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ overlapping windows, Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}), or IQR/Beta-smoothed thresholds when $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$; and (iv)~faults that perturb multi-channel latent trajectories, supplemented by the channel-level branch for isolated single-sensor faults (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Slow process ramps on GHL and Metro ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1) remain outside scope.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Without 64-step averaging, causal whitened residuals have a heavier right tail than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$ vs.\ $1.25$). Under 1D scoring, hijacker dials push the threshold onto that tail, so 1D Reynolds JEPA alerts on $2.4\%$ of test steps and flags isolated peaks ($0.050\text{--}0.052$ causal F1). With per-dial switches and onset relays (\ref{eq:onset_relay}), \emph{224-Bit Breaker} stays active across the fault window ($0.138$ causal F1, $60.7\%$ ER, $105.9$-step delay, $1.7\%$ FPR). A rate-matched random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, catches $28.1\%$ of events at the panel's nominal rate, but $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\subsection{Multi-Horizon 224-Bit Evaluation Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ and Latency Trade-Offs}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ introduces a structural reporting lag of $S-1 = 63$ steps before a causal decision is emitted at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To test whether the complete 224-bit circuit-breaker architecture and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} retain their advantages over 1D scalar scoring when the prediction horizon is shortened, we retrain and evaluate the full multi-horizon backbone, 224-bit panel, and surgical weight-space quenching across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ (structural lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$ steps) on five representative streams across three seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a),(b), partitioning each target window into four nested prefixes $\lceil mS/4 \rceil$ for $S \ge 4$ and four lag-1 difference views at $S=1$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Zero and near-zero reporting lag ($S=1$, lag $0$; $S=4$, lag $3$):} At $S=1$, \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} (causal F1 $= 0.027$, $P=0.059$, $R=0.021$, ER $= 75.6\%$, FPR $= 2.1\%$, $\Delta t = 75.2$) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA 224-Bit} (causal F1 $= 0.037$, $P=0.072$, $R=0.031$, ER $= 81.8\%$, FPR $= 3.6\%$, $\Delta t = 63.8$) achieve $1.9\times$ and $2.6\times$ the causal Point-F1 of 1D scalar SPOT ($0.014$, ER $= 43.3\%$, FPR $= 1.2\%$). At $S=4$ (3-step lag), \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} (causal F1 $= 0.041$, ER $= 84.4\%$, FPR $= 2.4\%$, $\Delta t = 44.1$; buffered F1 $= 0.082$) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA 224-Bit} (causal F1 $= 0.050$, ER $= 84.4\%$, FPR $= 2.9\%$, $\Delta t = 36.7$) achieve $3.5\times\text{--}4.3\times$ higher causal Point-F1 than 1D scalar scoring ($0.012$, ER $= 51.3\%$) while cutting onset delay from $98.5$ to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps.
    \item \emph{Intermediate horizons ($S=16$, lag $15$; $S=32$, lag $31$) vs.\ $S=64$ (lag $63$):} At $S=16$, \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA 224-Bit} reach $0.051\text{--}0.055$ causal F1 ($77.3\%\text{--}83.5\%$ ER, $1.5\%\text{--}2.2\%$ FPR, $\Delta t = 26.6\text{--}34.1$) versus $0.032$ for 1D scalar SPOT ($59.1\%$ ER, $\Delta t = 81.7$). At $S=32$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA 224-Bit} reaches $0.074$ causal F1 ($77.8\%$ ER, $2.6\%$ FPR, $\Delta t = 37.1$) and achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 across all horizons ($0.290$ vs.\ $0.182$ for 1D scalar), whereas $S=64$ maximizes unquenched causal Point-F1 ($0.087$ vs.\ $0.044$ for 1D scalar, $78.1\%$ ER, $\Delta t = 32.9$, $2.9\%$ FPR vs.\ $21.3\%$ for TimesNet). Thus, the 224-bit panel consistently outperforms scalar thresholding across all five horizons: $S \in \{4, 16, 32\}$ provides low-lag tripwires ($3\text{--}31$ steps) for minute- or hour-scale telemetry, while $S \in \{32, 64\}$ suits $\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$ streams.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We evaluate three operating-point comparisons:
(1)~\emph{Autonomous breaker points and calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Without test labels, 224-Bit Strict ($53.4\%$ ER at $1.1\%$ FPR), Laminar-JEPA ($62.5\%$ ER at $1.6\%$ FPR), and Reynolds 224-Bit ($60.7\%$ ER at $1.7\%$ FPR) lead the low-FPR frontier; at $q^* = 0.050$, 224-Bit reaches $77.4\%$ causal ER vs.\ $61.8\%$ for TimesNet and $60.2\%$ for TranAD.
(2)~\emph{Post-hoc diagnostic controls at matched test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} As noted in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_metrics}, $f^*$ sets thresholds post hoc from test labels as a non-deployable diagnostic check of tail sensitivity at equal alert rates. At $f^* = 0.01$, Reynolds 224-Bit catches $58.4\%$ of events ($56.2\%\text{--}57.8\%$ for 1D Reynolds and $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA), nearly tripling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and quadrupling TranAD ($14.9\%$), with a similar gap at $f^* = 0.05$ ($72.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$).
(3)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} On the 10 development SMAP/MSL streams stratified by NASA's taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)), reconstructors degrade on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC), whereas the 224-bit panel and 1D Reynolds reach $0.658$ and $0.652$ ($75.0\%$ autonomous recall). Across all 19 development streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)), Reynolds 224-Bit doubles reconstructor recall on contextual faults ($61.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) and leads on point faults ($79.2\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).

Under buffered attribution it trails the best 1D JEPA on SMAP, Exathlon, and OPPORTUNITY; on CreditCard, GECCO, and PSM all methods lie within $0.007$ causal F1.
    \item \emph{Calibration sample-size reliability, threshold confidence intervals, and small-sample calibrators:} Because windows overlap by $C-1=255$ steps, the effective independent sample size is $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}} \approx 0.31\,N_{\text{cal}}$ ($\tau_{\text{int}} = 3.23$; median $N_{\text{eff}} = 152$ of $N_{\text{cal}} = 408$ on the 19 development streams). On the 118 untouched streams, mean causal FPR is $19.2\%$ for the 12 streams with $N_{\text{cal}} < 250$ ($N_{\text{eff}} < 78$) versus $1.6\%$ for the 22 streams with $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 2{,}500$ ($N_{\text{eff}} \ge 775$). Auditing calibration blocks across $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100, 250, 500, 1{,}000, 2{,}500, 5{,}000\}$ ($N_{\text{cal}} \approx 323\text{--}16{,}150$) with moving-block bootstrap ($B=200$, block length $64$) shows that the median 95\% CI relative half-width on the 224 per-dial thresholds $\tau_j$ tightens monotonically from $\pm 11.0\%$ (median coefficient of variation (CV) $6.7\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=100$ and $\pm 9.4\%$ (CV $5.7\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=250$ to $\pm 7.8\%$ (CV $4.6\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=500$, $\pm 5.8\%$ (CV $3.4\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=1{,}000$, $\pm 3.8\%$ at $N_{\text{eff}}=2{,}500$, and $\pm 2.8\%$ (CV $1.6\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=5{,}000$. Comparing empirical $q_{0.999}$ against four small-sample alternatives---Harrell--Davis Beta-smoothed quantiles, order-statistic-free IQR extrapolation ($\text{med}_j + 4.8\,\text{IQR}_j$), Weibull peaks-over-threshold, and split-conformal calibration---shows that at $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100, 250\}$, where empirical $q_{0.999}$ yields $2.07\%\text{--}2.12\%$ test FPR ($9.2\%$ on $N_{\text{cal}}=100$ segments), Beta-smoothed calibration cuts test FPR to $1.32\%$ and $0.77\%$ ($49.4\%\text{--}41.5\%$ ER) and IQR extrapolation holds test FPR at $0.88\%$ and $0.47\%$ with higher causal F1 ($0.067$ and $0.059$ vs.\ $0.059$ and $0.047$) and lower inter-segment FPR SD ($4.2\%$ vs.\ $6.4\%$). We therefore establish a minimum requirement of $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ independent observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows, ensuring expected tail count $N_{\text{eff}}(1 - q_{0.995}) \ge 1.5$, threshold 95\% CI half-width $\le 8\%$, and inter-segment FPR SD $< 3\%$), and recommend IQR or Beta-smoothed thresholds whenever $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$.
    \item \emph{Three-stage permutation drift decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across the 19 development streams under chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits confirms that inter-block regime drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA ($0.0229$ vs.\ $0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.210\text{--}0.230$ vs.\ $0.043\text{--}0.046$), whereas barycenter centering in Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($0.0056$ vs.\ $0.0047$) while per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j$ quarantine individual drifting coordinates.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied whether Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures and multi-perspective circuit-breaker panels mitigate observation-space false-alarm flooding and 1D scalar score collapse across a 33-stream development cohort and 118 untouched held-out streams (within a 162-stream, 15-dataset archive):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Circuit-breaker panel, surgical quenching, and operational trade-offs:} Expanding $D=32$ whitened coordinates via a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon decomposition into $224$ per-coordinate statistics with surgical weight-space quenching, nominal drift guards, prefix-routed attribution, and 32 onset relays prevents variance-dominating coordinates from inflating a shared scalar threshold. On the $n{=}19$ development streams, unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} raises causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($P=0.252$, $R=0.123$; $17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $0.262$ buffered Point-F1 ($66.4\%$ ER) and cuts causal delay to $59.6$ steps ($62.5\%$ causal ER at $0.100$ causal F1). On the $118$ untouched held-out validation streams, the panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.072\text{--}0.083$ to $0.143$ ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$, dataset-clustered $p = 0.007$) at $72.8\%$ recall, while surgical quenching cuts causal held-out FPR by $8.0\%$ relative across $162$ streams ($p = 0.0011$, dataset $p = 0.027$; median held-out FPR $1.03\%$).
    \item \emph{Mechanism isolation and multi-horizon scaling ($S \in \{1,\dots,64\}$):} Matched-backbone ablations isolate LayerNorm kurtosis compression ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent targets ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$), barycenter centering (inter-block drift share $71.3\% \to 18.7\%$), and bias recentering versus precision tempering ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in [1, 4]$), while Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} characterizes the structural horizon-fluctuation concentration bound (with scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation yielding a null effect). Retraining the 224-bit panel and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ confirms $1.9\times\text{--}4.3\times$ higher causal Point-F1 and $+26\%\text{--}38\%$ higher event recall than 1D scalar scoring down to zero reporting lag ($S=1$) and 3-step lag ($S=4$).
    \item \emph{Applicability boundaries, calibration sample size, and single-channel branch:} The panel requires multichannel arrays ($K \ge 9$) and at least $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ effective independent calibration observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows) for empirical quantiles ($\le 8\%$ bootstrap threshold CI half-width and $<3\%$ inter-segment FPR SD), or Robust-IQR / Beta-smoothed calibrators when $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$. Across 17 multivariate streams and six single-sensor fault classes, adding a complementary channel-level residual and variance branch raises single-channel event recall from $61.4\%\text{--}80.7\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}98.2\%$ at the cost of higher nominal FPR, whereas slow process ramps (GHL, Metro) and online ramp adaptation remain future work.
\end{enumerate}
