\begin{abstract}
Reconstructive time-series anomaly detectors score raw observations, making them sensitive to benign sensor jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) forecast normalized latent window summaries, yet collapsing coordinates into one scalar residual allows drifting coordinates to inflate thresholds. We introduce two multi-horizon circuit-breaker models: Laminar-JEPA, a four-horizon, 224-dial panel combining sub-horizon magnitudes, drift, fluctuation, endpoint differences, and 32 onset relays with optional nominal weight-space quenching; and Laminar-JEPA-Lite, a distilled three-horizon, 128-dial position-plus-drift detector using a parallel single-normalization predictor and channel-gated spectral splitting with zero weight edits. Laminar-JEPA-Lite cuts parameters by 35 to 52 percent and accelerates inference by 2.43 times. On the 33-stream development benchmark, Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite hold autonomous causal false-positive rate (FPR) at 1.3 and 1.0 percent versus 22.2 to 24.3 percent for reconstructors, reaching buffered point-wise F1 of 0.244 and 0.242 and seven-dataset macro F1 of 0.133 and 0.171. On the 19-stream residual cache, the 224-dial panel lifts causal F1 from 0.013 to 0.071 over scalar collapse, and a 160-dial subset reaches 0.098. Across 118 untouched held-out streams under disjoint checkpoint selection, Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal F1 from 0.074 to 0.085 and event recall from 49.5 to 67.0 percent over scalar multi-horizon scoring while remaining positive across all 12 leave-one-dataset-out folds on 141 streams, whereas quenching lowers four-horizon causal FPR across seven of nine datasets. Calibration precision depends on autocorrelation-adjusted sample size; causal ramp detection vanishes at four context windows, and single-sensor sensitivity raises false alarms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Telemetry monitors track dozens of channels simultaneously \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. Because labeled faults are scarce, a streaming detector must learn nominal behavior from healthy history alone, flag anomalies promptly, and keep false alarms low during normal operation.

\noindent\emph{Why Observation-Space Reconstructors Suffer High False-Alarm Rates:} Reconstructors such as TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} score raw sensor error. Because raw telemetry mixes jitter, thermal drift, and disparate channel scales, summing errors across 25--55 channels produces heavy-tailed spikes. Calibrated on healthy windows via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, reconstructors fire during $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of later healthy data; a rate-matched coin flip catches $60.0\%$ of anomaly events, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\noindent\emph{The Bottleneck of Scalar Score Aggregation:} Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} compress each window into a 32-dimensional normalized summary and forecast future summaries in latent space, cutting nominal false alarms to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$. Yet scalar latent detectors still sum all coordinate errors against one threshold: if one or two coordinates shift slightly between training and calibration (\emph{hijacker dials}), they inflate the global threshold and mask faults on the other 30 coordinates (causal Point-F1 $0.050\text{--}0.055$, delay $138\text{--}154$ steps).

\noindent\emph{Multi-Horizon Coordinate Circuit Breakers (\emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} We pair multi-horizon JEPA forecasting with calibrated Boolean circuit-breaker panels in two tiers (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}): (1)~\emph{Laminar-JEPA} maps context and target windows into 32D layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} summaries with resolvent residual whitening, expands each coordinate's four-horizon error trajectory into $7\times32=224$ dials plus 32 right-edge onset relays, and applies optional nominal split-gated weight-space quenching; and (2)~\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, distilled from a layer-by-layer activation and component autopsy, replaces sequential four-horizon rollout and post-hoc weight editing with a parallel three-horizon single-LayerNorm predictor, channel-count-gated spectral splitting ($K\ge 25$), and $4\times32=128$ position-plus-drift breakers under a single nominal quorum, cutting parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$ and accelerating windowed inference by $2.43\times$.

\subsection{Contributions}
We separate evaluation into a 33-stream development cohort (plus a 19-stream residual cache) and a disjoint 118-stream untouched held-out cohort within a 141-stream archive across 12 datasets under disjoint checkpoint selection (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Flagship and distilled panels:} On the 33-stream benchmark, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reach buffered Point-F1 of $0.244$ and $0.242$ and causal event recall (ER) of $67.3\%$ and $65.0\%$ at $1.3\%$ and $1.0\%$ held-out false-positive rate (FPR), with \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leading $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.133$). On the 19-stream cache, the 224-dial panel lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ over 1D collapse ($p=0.0023$), and a 160-dial subset reaches $0.098$. Across $118$ untouched streams ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal Point-F1 from $0.074$ to $0.085$ ($p=0.0034$; $+0.025$ over TS-JEPA, $p=1.6\times 10^{-4}$), lifts causal ER from $49.5\%$ to $67.0\%$ across $8/9$ datasets (sign-flip $p=0.012$), and stays positive in all $12/12$ leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) folds on $n=141$, while nominal quenching lowers 4H causal FPR across $7/9$ datasets ($p=0.016$).
    \item \emph{Autopsy distillation, mechanisms, and latency:} Matched-backbone controls (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)) isolate LayerNorm tail compression and barycenter drift reduction while showing scalar spectral reweighting is null. Layer activation traces, LayerNorm spillover audits, and a $K \ge 25$ spectral threshold motivate \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}. Across $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ on five exploratory streams, both panels reach $2.1$--$4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 at $S=1,4$ without establishing cross-domain latency rules.
    \item \emph{False-alarm control and contextual faults:} On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent prediction holds autonomous causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($N=7$ dataset $p = 0.016$). At diagnostic budget $f^* = 1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ and $56.0\%$ of events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and reach $0.519\text{--}0.532$ buffered precision-recall (PR) AUC with $77.5\%\text{--}87.5\%$ autonomous ER on NASA contextual faults (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}--\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Calibration sample size, single-channel branch, and limits:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds horizon-fluctuation concentration $R_{\text{LT}} \in [\frac{1}{M-1}, 1]$, while scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ reweighting is null ($\Delta\text{F1} = -0.005$). Sokal autocorrelation ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$) and block-bootstrap audits show $N_{\text{eff}}=300$ does not guarantee $99.9$th-percentile precision; interquartile-range (IQR) and Beta-smoothed quantiles remain exploratory. Across 19 streams, an optional channel branch lifts injected single-sensor ER to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$ while raising nominal causal FPR from $1.5\%$ to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$; natural sensor failures and slow ramps ($L \ge 4C$) remain limitations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$: autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly; TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches---temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}---depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults ($0.088$ and $0.097$ stream Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.186$ for latent prediction on 130 streams, Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}; we instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Conversely, univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories, whereas matched-rate per-coordinate and recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal rules inflate under coordinate drift (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite spans 45 continuous telemetry streams across 11 datasets from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}, expanded in archive-wide evaluations to 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}). Thirty-three streams enter the development protocol (19 non-GHL streams and 14 GHL streams) and 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry} covers six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$ channels), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$, four primary and two short-nominal), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$), where NASA \cite{Hundman2018b} labels each SMAP/MSL sequence as an out-of-range \emph{point} fault or in-range \emph{contextual} fault (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)). \emph{Wearable kinematics} covers six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals $1\,\text{s}$). \emph{Process and infrastructure telemetry} covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ slow-ramp faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry ($K{=}9$, $37$ anomalies), Metro I-94 traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$). Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation} evaluates six injected single-channel fault classes across all 19 multivariate development streams ($K \in [5, 55]$).

Each stream provides nominal history ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$) and labeled test data ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$), standardized per channel on the weight-fitting prefix ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$) and split chronologically into training ($65\%$, further split $50\%/15\%$ into weight-fitting and checkpoint-selection prefixes under disjoint selection; Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), a 64-step isolation gap, and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$). Development 33-stream inclusion required $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ ($T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$), $K \le 55$, and $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2{\times}10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); the 12 reserved streams comprise five short-nominal streams ($T_{\text{nom}} \in [764, 1{,}492]$, $N_{\text{cal}} \le 101$: CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, two Room-Occupancy), six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$), and GECCO ($K{=}9$, included in archive cohorts). Archive cohorts evaluate all 141 qualifying streams across 12 datasets ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$, $\ge 1$ anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, 13 OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN; $118$ untouched by design).

Two caveats bound external validity: SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams within multi-stream archives share hardware platforms. Dataset clusters ($N \in \{7, 9, 12\}$) are therefore treated as co-primary units alongside streams via Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and LODO audits (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Windowing, Multi-Depth TCN Hierarchy Tapping, and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($K$ channels over $T$ timesteps) to binary alarm decisions. A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time, splitting each position $t$ into a historical \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of $C = 256$ steps and a future \emph{target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of $S = 64$ steps (ending at the causal decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). Before mixing channels, multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise 1D convolution ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass) and are split across four smooth dyadic frequency shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid. The shells form an amplitude partition of unity within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$, separating slow baseline drift from high-frequency vibration before per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$) and a softmax router feed a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Rather than reconstructing raw sensor waveforms, a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path} maps context and target windows into compact $D=32$-dimensional layer-normalized summaries via dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders. Target weights track online weights via an exponential moving average (EMA) at momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on archive cohorts) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}, placing summaries near radius $\sqrt{D}$ up to the normalization epsilon. Deep dilated TCN stacks can smooth out brief onset spikes, while operating-regime shifts displace the latent mean. Both encoders address these two failure modes directly:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth hierarchy tapping:} Each encoder taps three intermediate feature maps ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow spectral stem, mid-depth TCN block, and final TCN block. Concatenating the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy of each tap, projecting to $\mathbb{R}^D$ through a two-layer GELU head, and blending with the deep TCN output through a bounded gate ($g \le 0.5$) preserves fast transients alongside long-range context.
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} During training, an online centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ updates at rate $0.05$, copies synchronously to $E_\phi$, and is subtracted from both context and target summaries prior to final LayerNorm. Centering reduces learned common-mode offsets without guaranteeing invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Nested Causal Multi-Horizon Rollout and Variance-Floor Decorrelation Objective}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single 64-step forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant volume penalty ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Pooling an entire 64-step target window into one vector can average out short-lived faults, and LayerNorm bounds vector length without bounding temporal tail heaviness.

To track how prediction error evolves across the target window, Stage~2 of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} encodes $M=4$ nested target prefixes of lengths $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ at $S=64$ (clamped to at least two steps when $S>1$; at $S=1$, all four prefixes share the single target step and differ only through predictor rollout). Starting from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ with learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a recurrent sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential LayerNorm forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M-1)}$ while an anchor head $P_\theta$ predicts the full horizon $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$. Assigning half the loss weight to $m=M$ and splitting the other half equally across $m < M$, training minimizes multi-horizon MSE plus a non-collapse penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Unlike log-determinant penalties that require batch size $B > D$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ penalizes collapse directly on small batches: its diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$ enforce a per-coordinate standard-deviation floor $\gamma = 0.75$ (using batch-biased variance plus $10^{-4}$), and its off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize cross-coordinate sample covariance ($B-1$ divisor). At $B=16 < D=32$, effective latent rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ rises from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}.

\subsection{Stage 3: Calibration-Stabilized Whitening, Optional Weight-Space Quenching, and 224-Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a single scalar score creates a bottleneck: if nominal drift inflates even a few coordinates between training and calibration, those coordinates dominate the scalar norm. Stacking the $M=4$ signed residuals $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ forms $\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$ ($M\!D=128$ entries). On nominal training windows, eigendecomposing the residual covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ defines a damped resolvent precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying centered residuals by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ yields whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ while suppressing near-null eigenmodes instead of amplifying them. Stage~3 then insulates coordinates against nominal calibration drift and expands the horizon trajectory:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration-stabilized whitening and optional quenching:} If a coordinate's whitened RMS on nominal calibration windows exceeds $1.10$, it blends smoothly toward its RMS-standardized raw residual $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)$ so no drifting eigenmode can blow up downstream tripwires:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    Full \emph{Laminar-JEPA} also includes an optional zero-gradient post-training \emph{weight-space quench} fit strictly on nominal training and calibration windows. Coordinates are ranked by an instability score standardized via the median and median absolute deviation (MAD), combining train-to-calibration mean/scale shifts, calibration half-split drift, residual autocorrelation, and a negative guard for isolated nominal spikes. For candidate top-$k$ drifting coordinates ($k\in\{4,2\}$), four steps run sequentially: \emph{(Q1)}~damping the selected predictor output rows/LayerNorm gains and shifting $\mathbf{p}_m$ toward calibration means (which induces unselected spillover through 32D LayerNorm, audited in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}); \emph{(Q2)}~blending residual centers $75\%$ toward post-edit calibration means; \emph{(Q3)}~attenuating off-diagonal precision couplings by $0.15$ and rescaling whitened calibration RMS; and \emph{(Q4)}~retaining the edit (preferring $k=2$ on ties) only if nominal half-split FPR improves while preserving at least $92\%$ of pre-edit quorum thresholds, reverting otherwise.
    \item \emph{Multi-horizon expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} Inspired algebraically by the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} across the $M=4$ horizons of each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$, we separate $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into its horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Taking magnitudes yields $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Removing the horizon mean splits each coordinate's squared error orthogonally into persistent offset plus within-window fluctuation, and confines the $M \times M$ horizon Gram matrix to the $(M-1)$-dimensional subspace orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}_M$ (a geometric property of the representation rather than an accuracy guarantee):

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant concentration ratio for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Zero-Collapse 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel, Onset Relays, and Temporal Attribution}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Instead of adding all 224 dials into one global score, Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) assigns each dial its own binary circuit breaker on nominal calibration data. Because the 224 dials are derived from a single $4 \times 32$ residual tensor, their trips are correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort, with joint trip-count variance $7.2\times$ independent trials). Alarm quorums are therefore calibrated directly on the empirical distribution of simultaneous nominal trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized against its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled MAD $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into a nonnegative score $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. To quarantine coordinates that drift during calibration, its $98.5$th-percentile tripwire $\tau_j$ is multiplied by two nominal hijack guards ($\gamma_j^{\text{cal}} \ge 1$ when second-half calibration 98th percentiles exceed the first half by $>1.35\times$, and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}} \ge 1$ when dial scale exceeds $1.5\times$ its perspective-group median by $>1.25\times$):
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 right-edge onset relays:} When an abrupt fault first enters the right edge of the $S$-step target window, earlier prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover nominal data. To catch these transitions promptly, each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge switches ($\gamma^{1.25}$ guard) and fires a binary onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ whenever a terminal-prefix trip ($m=4$) coincides with an acceleration, fluctuation, or $m=3$ trip on the same coordinate.
    \item \emph{Causal vs.\ buffered attribution and quorum voting:} Under \emph{causal trailing attribution}, active breakers on window $[t,t+S)$ emit a real-time decision strictly at its right endpoint $\tau=t+S-1$ (structural lag $S-1$, empirical delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, position votes route to their corresponding prefixes $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation votes span $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint-difference votes route to $[t+S_3,t+S)$ (empty at $S=1$). Summing active trips yields $N_{224}(\tau)$, position sub-panel count $N_{128}(\tau)$, and relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ (summed as $N_{\text{tot}} = N_{224} + N_{128} + 2N_{\text{edge}}$ for continuous PR-AUC), triggering a binary alarm whenever any counter exceeds its nominal quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with nominal calibration quorums $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Autopsy-Distilled Architecture: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Layer-by-layer activation tracing and component ablations of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}) motivate three simplifications that cut trainable parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$ ($141.9\text{k}$ at $K < 25$; $222.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$ vs.\ $295.8\text{k}$) and accelerate windowed GPU inference by $2.43\times$ ($3.14\times$ at $K < 25$) with zero post-training weight editing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Four-block TCN and $K \ge 25$ spectral gating:} Because a fifth dilated block damps short-prefix anomaly divergence without improving effective rank, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses four TCN blocks ($d \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $64\text{D}$ pooling heads, and activates the four-shell rFFT front-end only on high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), using a direct causal projection when $K < 25$.
    \item \emph{Parallel three-horizon single-LayerNorm predictor:} Instead of sequential four-horizon unrolling and stacked predictor normalizations, a single residual two-layer GELU MLP predicts $M=3$ nested prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$ at $S=64$; $96\times 96$ precision $\mathbf{\Omega}$) in parallel from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m$ with one terminal LayerNorm, avoiding sequential lag and LayerNorm cross-coupling.
    \item \emph{128-dial position-plus-drift panel and single quorum:} Retaining only the $96$ sub-horizon magnitudes $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and $32$ horizon-mean drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ ($4D=128$ breakers calibrated via (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire})) removes fluctuation/endpoint dials, onset relays, and weight quenching, firing under one quorum $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,4.0\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit) and Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128-Bit)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Full},\text{Lite}\}$, attribution mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Per-timestep binary alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 1: Disjoint Training \& Resolvent Whitening}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically into $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \text{gap}_S, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$ and standardize channels using $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ alone.
\State Set nested prefixes $S_{1:4}=\{16,32,48,64\}$ (Full) or $S_{1:3}=\{16,32,64\}$ with $K\ge 25$ rFFT gate (Lite).
\For{stride-$16$ training batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ and targets $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$ with barycenter shift $-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$.
    \State Roll out sequential $\{P_{\text{sub}}, P_\theta\}$ (Full) or parallel single-LayerNorm predictor (Lite); update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (or fixed epochs) and fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_+^{MD})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\If{tier $=$ Full and $q_{\text{wsp}} = 1$}
    \State Rank drifting coordinates on $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}})$; run Steps Q1--Q4 for $k\in\{4,2\}$ and keep edit iff nominal split and $0.92$-quorum gates pass.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 2: Nominal Circuit-Breaker Calibration on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$}
\State Extract stabilized whitened residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ via (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).
\If{tier $=$ Full}
    \State Expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ into $7D=224$ dials (position, drift, fluctuation, endpoint) and $32$ right-edge onset relays.
    \State Fit MAD scales $(m_j,s_j)$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\Else \Comment{Distilled Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
    \State Extract $4D=128$ position-plus-drift dials $(|\tilde{e}_{m,c}|, |\bar{e}_c|)$, fit $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and set single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}=\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},4.0)$.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 3: Online Inference on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$}
    \State Compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \to \mathbf{d}(t)$ and binary trips $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(z_j(t)>\tau_j)$ (plus relays $r_c(t)$ if Full).
    \State Attribute active counts to right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ (\text{causal}) or prefix spans $[t,t+S_m)$ (\text{buffered}).
    \State Emit $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ when active counts exceed $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) or $K_{\text{Lite}}$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Disjoint Nominal Calibration Protocol, Boundary Isolation, and Cross-Fitting}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$):
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Weight fitting vs.\ checkpoint selection):} Standardization statistics, encoder/predictor weights, running barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and precision $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$ are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$. Because monitoring early-stopping loss on nominal calibration windows can make empirical calibration quantiles mildly optimistic, all archive benchmarks ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) enforce a strictly \emph{disjoint checkpoint-selection protocol} that splits $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ chronologically into weight-fitting $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 50\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) and checkpoint-selection $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($\max(W{+}64,\lfloor 0.15\,T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor)$ steps), leaving threshold calibration completely untouched by model training and selection (development cohorts train for fixed epochs on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ without validation early stopping). On short histories where carving out $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ shrinks training support, a \emph{two-fold chronological cross-fitting} check swaps $[0:H)$ and $[H+S:M_{\text{val}})$ across two calibration folds; on the 19-stream cache, consensus voting ($\hat{y}^{(1)}\wedge\hat{y}^{(2)}$) lowers stream-mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $1.50\%$ to $0.92\%$ (median $1.07\%\to 0.38\%$) while retaining $66.7\%$ median ER.
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Circuit-breaker calibration and boundary isolation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Stride-1 windows of length $W = 320$ inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ yield $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window indices. Splitting at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $[0 : H)$ and \emph{held-out nominal evaluation block} $[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so calibration and evaluation share zero target observations (a full $G=320$ window gap changes causal FPR by $<0.25$ percentage points). On $[0 : H)$ alone, the panel fits $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), dial medians $m_j$ and MAD scales $s_j$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or single $N_{128}$ quorum in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), frozen on $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$. We distinguish raw calibration window count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective sample size} $N_{\text{eff},s} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int},s}$ using stream- and statistic-specific Sokal initial-positive-sequence estimators: across the 19 development streams, median $\tau_{\text{int}}$ is $2.51$ (IQR $[1.52, 4.04]$) for individual dials $z_j$, $1.88$ ($[1.22, 2.73]$) for $N_{\text{edge}}$, $5.58$ ($[3.07, 14.09]$, max $206.0$) for $N_{224}$, and $6.75$ ($[5.10, 16.88]$) for 1D scalar scores (legacy lag-64 Bartlett pooled summary $2.66$ median, $3.23$ trimmed cohort summary; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Hierarchical Statistical Units, Dataset-Clustered Inference, and Hypothesis Testing}
Because streams within multi-stream archives share hardware platforms, we report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Dataset-clustered and stream-level inference:} Because stream-level $p$-values treat same-spacecraft channels as independent, \emph{dataset-clustered inference} ($95\%$ cluster-bootstrap CIs and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip permutation tests) is emphasized alongside stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs ($2{,}000$ resamples). For multiplicity reporting on the 118 untouched streams, the focal causal contrasts comparing \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} against Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) and TS-JEPA yield Benjamini--Hochberg \cite{benjamini1995controlling} adjusted stream-level $p=0.0034$ on Point-F1 (raw $p=0.0034$ and $1.6\times 10^{-4}$) and adjusted dataset sign-flip $p=0.039$ on ER (raw $p=0.012$ and $0.039$, positive on $8/9$ datasets); other quoted $p$-values are raw and descriptive. Non-Exathlon held-out subsets ($n=101$, $8$ datasets) verify insensitivity to archive composition.
(2)~\emph{12-fold LODO audit:} Across all $12/12$ LODO folds on the 141-stream archive, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} retains a positive causal Point-F1 gain over Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) ($\Delta\in[+0.002,+0.039]$, $\max p=0.015$) and TS-JEPA ($\Delta\in[+0.023,+0.034]$, $\max p=3\times 10^{-4}$), and selecting the detector maximizing causal Point-F1 subject to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} \le 6\%$ on $D-1$ datasets chooses \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} in all $12/12$ folds.

\subsection{Consolidated Cohort Organization, Development vs.\ Held-Out Separation, and Baseline Fairness}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Across all evaluations, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} denotes the sequential four-horizon 224-bit architecture with nominal split-gated quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} omits Steps Q1--Q4), and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} denotes the parallel three-horizon 128-bit position-plus-drift architecture without weight editing. (1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-inspected):} A recomputed 19-stream residual cache across $6$ datasets evaluates the dial ladder, component ablations, and conformal/SPC comparators on identical 4H unquenched residuals (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b), Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}); a separately trained 33-stream development benchmark across $7$ datasets ($19$ non-GHL matched-backbone streams in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) plus $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) pairs \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, 1D JEPAs, and observation-space baselines; and tripwire/quenching constants were fixed on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, and \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).

\subsection{Multi-Tier Operational Evaluation Metrics and Aggregation}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To prevent point-adjustment inflation \cite{kim2022towards} and aggregation ambiguity, all binary false-alarm and Point-F1 metrics are computed \emph{per valid timestep $\tau$} (never point-adjusted and never per-window without timeline attribution): $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ averages $\hat{y}(\tau)$ over valid timesteps in $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ averages $\hat{y}(\tau)$ over nominal test timesteps ($y(\tau)=0$), and Point-F1, Precision, Recall, and PR-AUC (from $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$) score all valid test timesteps against $y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$. Event-level ER measures the fraction of contiguous $y=1$ segments with $\ge 1$ alarm, and delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ is the step lag from segment onset $t^*$ to first in-segment alarm. Unless explicitly labeled \emph{median} or \emph{Dataset-Macro}, cohort headline numbers report \emph{stream-level arithmetic means} of within-stream seed averages, paired with stream medians for skewed FPR distributions and non-deployable post-hoc test-FPR controls ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) strictly for diagnostic comparison.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder, Circuit-Breaker Components, and Weight-Space Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate perspective expansion and panel components on the 19-stream residual cache (not paired to the separately trained run in Panel~(c)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational trade-off between 160 and 224 dials:} While the 224-dial panel lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ over 1D scoring ($p=0.0023$; buffered $+0.033$, $p=0.53$), more dials are not automatically better: the 160-dial position-plus-drift subset ($128$ sub-horizons $+$ $32$ horizon means, single quorum, no relays) achieves higher causal Point-F1 ($0.098$), higher causal ER ($59.1\%$ vs.\ $56.5\%$), shorter causal delay ($74.8$ vs.\ $90.1$ steps), and lower nominal FPR ($0.55\%$ held-out, $0.87\%$ test vs.\ $0.76\%$, $1.50\%$) at $29\%$ lower dial cost, whereas all 224 dials lead only in retrospective buffered Point-F1 ($0.224$ vs.\ $0.198$). This directly motivates the $M=3$ position-plus-drift 128-dial panel in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}. Scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation remains null in Panel~(c).
    \item \emph{Relays, single-quorum voting, and hijack guards:} Removing the 32 onset relays lowers causal Point-F1 slightly ($0.071 \to 0.068$), and replacing the three-counter rule (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) with a single 224-dial quorum lowers causal Point-F1 to $0.063$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0055$; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}). Conversely, removing hijack guards ($\gamma_j=1$) raises causal Point-F1 to $0.073$ and shortens delay to $70.1$ steps on this cache, showing that drift guards can damp useful fault responses when calibration drift is mild. None removes the $S-1$ causal reporting lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching step isolation and LayerNorm coupling:} On single-seed 4H checkpoints across all $118$ untouched streams (unquenched causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 6.76\%$, $\text{F1} = 0.0901$, $\text{ER} = 63.8\%$), $k=4$ predictor row damping (Step Q1) shifts unselected coordinates by a median $11.1\%$ of nominal RMS ($45.5\%$ shifting $>10\%$) via 32D LayerNorm coupling without lowering $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($7.06\%$), while horizon-embedding shifts lower raw RMS on $68.2\%$ of edited coordinates ($4.1\%$ spillover). Buffer recentering and precision decoupling alone (Steps Q2--Q3) drive the FPR drop ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 6.71\%$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}} = 14.75\%$) at lower causal $\text{F1}$ ($0.0826$), whereas adding the nominal split gate (Step Q4, accepted on $39/118$ streams) reaches $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 6.62\%$ while preserving causal $\text{F1}$ ($0.0904$, $\text{ER} = 63.3\%$). Because ungated edits lower $k=2$ causal $\text{F1}$ on $11/19$ development streams ($0.141\to 0.049$) and $k\in\{8,12\}$ cuts PR-AUC ($0.292\to 0.223$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} omits weight editing altogether.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Even when flatlining shifts the normalized summary ($0.64$ vs.\ $0.77$ relative divergence for a variance-matched harmonic flip on \texttt{SMAP P-3}), terminal LayerNorm spreads static error thinly across all $32$ coordinates ($12.5$ vs.\ $62.1$ switches; $6.3\%$ vs.\ $97.7\%$ window alerts), and single-sensor faults suffer further $O(1/K)$ dilution across $K-1$ nominal channels. Extending Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) ($n{=}6$), we evaluate a causal channel branch across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$) without per-stream or test-label tuning. Per channel, three 64-step rolling statistics---log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, training-fit first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$---are thresholded at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ on nominal validation windows (floor $6.0$ MAD, $1.25\times$ half-split drift multiplier) with a $24$-of-$40$-step persistence gate. On the highest-variance active channel $c^\star=\arg\max_{c:\sigma_c^{\text{tr}}>10^{-8}}\sigma_c^{\text{tr}}$, a deterministic 9-point grid over $[320,N_{\text{test}}-520]$ (stride-50 fallback) places three nonoverlapping $L_{\text{inj}}=200$-step faults ($57$ events/class, gap $\ge 264$) inside clean segments ($y^{\text{orig}}\equiv 0$ on $[s-64,s+264)$). For $s\le t<s+200$ using training $(\mu_c,\sigma_c,q_{.995,c})$:
\begin{equation}
\small
x'_{t,c^\star}=
\begin{cases}
\mu_{c^\star} & \text{variance collapse},\\
\mu_{c^\star}+1.35(-1)^{t-s}\sigma_{c^\star} & \text{prediction shock},\\
x_{s-1,c^\star} & \text{sensor freezing},\\
\max(q_{.995,c^\star},\mu_{c^\star}+2.2\sigma_{c^\star}) & \text{saturation},\\
x_{t,c^\star}+2.5\sigma_{c^\star}(t-s)/199 & \text{gradual degradation},\\
x_{t,c^\star}+1.5\sigma_{c^\star} & \text{isolated bias}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:single_channel_injection}
\end{equation}
Outside $[s,s+200)$, all channels remain unchanged. Injected-event recall rises from $63.2\%$ (standalone panel) to $93.0\%$ for variance collapse, $63.2\%$ to $94.7\%$ for freezing, and $82.5\%$ to $94.7\%$ ($96.5\%$ full branch) for saturation under the variance-collapse union; the full channel branch raises recall from $75.4\%$ to $96.5\%$ on prediction shocks, $59.6\%$ to $80.7\%$ on gradual degradation, and $68.4\%$ to $86.0\%$ on isolated bias. However, on the \emph{unmodified} test streams, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union, $\text{F1}=0.070$) and $19.4\%$ (full branch, $\text{F1}=0.129$). Synthetic single-channel injections do not establish detection on natural sensor failures, and the full union's $19.4\%$ false-alarm rate makes it unsuitable as a default detector.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, \texttt{GECCO}; three seeds) reveals a clear channel-count threshold: shell splitting improves high-channel spacecraft detection ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$) by separating thermal drift from actuator chatter, but lowers F1 at $K{=}9$ (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), setting the $K \ge 25$ spectral gate in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 16 matched-budget arms in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five mechanisms: (1)~raw-observation forecasting and autoencoding share heavy tails ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), large drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and $\approx 25\%$ causal FPR; (2)~a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves FPR, yet raw targets keep drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$); (3)~learned $D{=}32$ latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179\text{--}0.190$ across multi-horizon JEPA tiers, $p \le 0.001$); (4)~linear PCA and Isolation Forest keep low FPR but miss nonlinear faults; and (5)~frozen targets and $\ell_2$ scoring retain $<2\%$ causal FPR, whereas latent context autoencoding lowers causal F1.

\subsection{Development 33-Stream and Untouched Held-Out 118/141-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following the cohort hierarchy in Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the 33-stream development benchmark under retrospective buffered attribution (Panel~(a)), causal streaming (Panel~(b)), and per-dataset stratification (Panel~(c)), while Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} reports the strictly disjoint-selection ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$) benchmark across all 118 untouched held-out streams and the 141-stream archive. On a separate 130-stream baseline screen, TS-JEPA reaches stream-mean Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$. The observed reduction in nominal false alarms with latent scoring is specific to these protocols; spherical normalization alone does not prove a distributional guarantee.

\noindent\emph{Operational trade-offs and configuration guidance:} On the 33-stream benchmark, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} edges out \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} on unweighted buffered Point-F1 ($0.244$ vs.\ $0.242$) and causal ER ($67.3\%$ vs.\ $65.0\%$), whereas \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.1\%$ vs.\ $12.8\%$), leads $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.133$), and cuts parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$ and inference latency by $2.43\times$. In practice, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} is the recommended default for streaming and buffered deployment, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} suits settings prioritizing peak buffered F1 or nominal 4H drift damping, short horizons ($S\le 4$) address sub-window reporting lag, and raw-level variance or trend charts should be paired only when targeting single-sensor freezes or slow monotonic process ramps.

\noindent\emph{Scope boundaries and controlled slow-ramp sweep:} Observation-space reconstructors show high nominal false-alarm rates here, whereas learned latent targets lower false alarms without guaranteeing light tails on arbitrary data. Conversely, because the encoder summarizes only the preceding $C=256$ steps, gradual drift that shifts negligibly across one context window causes context and target summaries to co-move, explaining the $0.000$ Point-F1 on the 14 GHL slow-ramp streams (low false alarms without recall is not detection). To map this boundary, we injected linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ (slopes $a/L$ from $6.25{\times}10^{-2}$ to $1.22{\times}10^{-4}\sigma$/step, plus a $256$-step plateau; $15$ events/cell across $10$ clean test segments on $\lceil 0.3K\rceil$-channel and $1$-channel subsets). For steep multi-channel ramps ($L=64$, $a\in[1,4]\sigma$), the 224-bit panel reaches $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ causal detection probability at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay and $1.7\%\text{--}1.9\%$ false-alarm rate ($+5.7\text{--}7.7$ percentage points above the clean-segment Poisson episode baseline; $+19.4\text{--}24.1$ points for 1D SPOT at $60\text{--}67$-step delay). By $L=256$ ($=C$), panel excess causal detection narrows to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points ($50\text{--}71$-step delay), and for $L\ge 1024$ (slope $\le 3.9{\times}10^{-3}\sigma$/step) raw hit rates ($87\%\text{--}91\%$) reflect background episodes over $1{,}280\text{--}4{,}352$-step spans, with chance-corrected detection falling to $[-4.8,+0.8]$ points across all JEPA detectors ($[-5.9,-1.0]$ on single-channel ramps). Fixed-window JEPAs thus target abrupt to medium-onset faults rather than gradual industrial drift.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent detectors degrade under real-time causal streaming. Buffered attribution averages residuals across overlapping predictions covering each timestamp ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$), whereas causal streaming evaluates a single right-edge step without temporal averaging ($\gamma_2 = 0.92\text{--}2.33$). Summing whitened coordinates into a 1D score allows one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the global SPOT threshold onto that heavier tail, missing sustained faults. Replacing the scalar sum with independently calibrated coordinate breakers isolates drifting coordinates. Against a rate-matched Bernoulli alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, random guessing catches only $28.1\%$ of events at our $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, whereas at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ causal FPR, coin flipping catches $60.0\%$ of events by chance alone, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$ recall.

\subsection{Multi-Horizon Scaling Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ and Latency Trade-Offs}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Because a target window of length $S$ emits its causal decision at $\tau=t+S-1$, $S=64$ imposes a 63-step reporting lag. We retrain on five selected streams at $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ to examine the latency trade-off. At $S=1$, all predictor states are compared with the same one-step target, so this setting has no distinct target-horizon decomposition; at $S=4$, the shortest encoded prefix is two steps. In the buffered $S=1$ setting, endpoint-difference votes have no routed span. These short-horizon results therefore test the implemented detectors, not distinct horizon views.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a), both \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT across all five horizons while holding test FPR below $3.6\%$ ($\le 2.4\%$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, held-out FPR $\le 1.2\%$, vs.\ $2.5\%\to 21.3\%$ for TimesNet). At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037$ and $0.050$ vs.\ $0.014$ and $0.012$) with $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ ($0.030$ and $0.036$) with $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER; at $S=4$, detection delay drops to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps. Buffered Point-F1 peaks at $0.290$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($S=32$) and $0.255$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($S=64$) versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot establish a deployment rule across sampling rates or datasets.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine detection across operating budgets and fault taxonomies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Autonomous vs.\ diagnostic operating budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Along the causal recall--FPR frontier, the autonomous points of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} occupy the $\le 1.3\%$ held-out FPR regime without test-label tuning. At post-hoc diagnostic test-FPR controls ($f^* \in \{1\%, 5\%\}$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events at $f^* = 1\%$ versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD.
    \item \emph{In-range contextual vs.\ point faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Under NASA's spacecraft taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Panel~(a)) and a $5\sigma$ amplitude split across all 19 development streams (Panel~(b)), out-of-range point spikes ($>5\sigma$) are flagged by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike, whereas in-range contextual faults decouple cross-channel phase or velocity inside normal amplitude bounds: on NASA contextual sequences, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} achieve $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR ($84.0\%$ and $80.0\%$ at $f^*=1\%$; $0.532$ and $0.519$ buffered PR-AUC) versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR ($56.0\%$ at $f^*=1\%$, $0.317$ PR-AUC) for TimesNet.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and its autopsy-distilled counterpart \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} across 33 development and 118 untouched held-out streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Flagship vs.\ distilled circuit-breaker panels:} On the 33-stream benchmark, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reach $0.244$ and $0.242$ buffered Point-F1 and $67.3\%$ and $65.0\%$ causal ER at $1.3\%$ and $1.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} cuts parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$, speeds up inference by $2.43\times$, and leads $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$). On the 19-stream cache, the 224-dial panel ($0.071$, $p=0.0023$), 160-dial subset ($0.098$), and training-whitened $\ell_2$ conformal rule ($0.072$) outperform 1D collapse ($0.013$). Across $118$ untouched streams ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.074$ to $0.085$ ($p=0.0034$) and causal ER from $49.5\%$ to $67.0\%$ across $8/9$ datasets ($p=0.012$; $12/12$ LODO folds on $n=141$), while nominal quenching lowers 4H causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ across $7/9$ datasets ($p=0.016$).
    \item \emph{Mechanisms and horizon latency:} Matched-backbone controls confirm LayerNorm tail compression and barycenter drift reduction while scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation is null, and five-stream horizon sweeps ($S\in\{1..64\}$) retain $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 gains down to $S=1$.
    \item \emph{Calibration, single-sensor, and slow-ramp boundaries:} Sokal autocorrelation ($\tau_{\text{int}}\le 206$) leaves $1.5$ ($q_{0.995}$) and $0.3$ ($q_{0.999}$) expected tail points at $N_{\text{eff}}=300$. An optional channel branch lifts injected single-sensor ER to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$ at $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$ unmodified $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$, and excess causal detection vanishes on slow ramps once $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$); pairing latent panels with raw-level trend charts and testing natural sensor failures remain future priorities.
\end{enumerate}
