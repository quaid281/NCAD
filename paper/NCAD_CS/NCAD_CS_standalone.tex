\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry requires setting operational thresholds automatically via statistical calibration, such as Extreme Value Theory (EVT/SPOT), without ground-truth labels. While reconstructive baselines like TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive ranking (PR-AUC $0.29\text{--}0.34$), heavy-tailed observation residuals cause severe false-alarm flooding, yielding nominal false-positive rates of $24\text{--}30\%$. We investigate whether forecasting in latent representation space via Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) conditions residuals for automated thresholding. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical domains evaluated under held-out calibration (up to five seeds on the multi-seed subset), unconstrained latent prediction (TS-JEPA) suppresses nominal false-positive rates roughly four-fold down to $7.6\%$ (paired difference $-0.153$ against TimesNet, $p=0.002$). However, overall Point-F1 ($0.234$ vs. $0.197$, $p=0.54$) and PR-AUC ($0.323$ vs. $0.329$, $p=0.45$) remain statistically indistinguishable from baselines, while detection latency increases relative to the fastest reconstructive baseline ($\Delta t = 80$ vs. $48$ steps) and causal event recall drops ($10.7\%$ vs. $32.9\%$). Furthermore, evaluating three candidate physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds stress, and conservative potential flow) reveals domain-specific trade-offs rather than universal gains: linear Koopman operators excel on spacecraft telemetry (PR-AUC $0.591$ on SMAP), but curl-free priors fail on cyclic kinematics, and on aggregate, physical regularizers do not outperform unconstrained latent prediction ($p \ge 0.61$). Finally, EVT calibration systematically misses its nominal design risk ($q=10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry, performing comparably to empirical percentiles. Latent prediction thus provides a more conservative detector that suppresses false alarms, but does not improve ranking fidelity or resolve tail calibration drift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments --- NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, wearable biometric arrays --- dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs \cite{cuellar2024explainable}.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
Multi-sensor telemetry reflects a physical state trajectory $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$, yet modeling dynamics in raw observation space faces four obstacles: estimating derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \Delta x / \Delta t$ on noisy discrete streams amplifies high-frequency noise; telemetry captures only a noisy projection of unmeasured states, violating full-state observability; in large arrays ($K \ge 25$) cross-channel nonlinear interactions resist rigid functional forms; and discrete operational mode transitions (orbital day/night cycling, thruster firings, gait freezing) induce continuous distribution drift.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Deep detectors face three limitations. First, generative models (autoencoders, VAEs, masked Transformers) minimize pointwise $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}, so reconstruction loss allocates capacity to high-frequency jitter rather than nominal dynamics. Second, contrastive frameworks such as NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} train on synthetic perturbations whose geometry distorts the embedding space, inducing artificial decision boundaries and false alarms. Third, reconstruction errors $\|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2$ accumulate residual variance across dozens of irrelevant channels; under streaming thresholding (SPOT), the resulting heavy-tailed residuals defeat extreme-value calibrators and trigger false-alarm flooding.

\subsection{Investigating Latent Prediction and Inductive Priors}
We study whether latent-space prediction mitigates observation-space noise drowning without synthetic contrastive perturbations. A JEPA maps context $x_{\text{ctx}}$ into a representation space and predicts target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ directly \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}, discarding unpredictable stochastic jitter and potentially conditioning residuals for tail calibration. Alongside an unconstrained predictor (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formalize and test three physics-inspired regularizers: \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (spectral-entropy regularization of a latent Koopman transition matrix), \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} (second-moment fluctuation-covariance alignment), and \textit{Potential-Flow JEPA} (curl-free gradient dynamics $\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi$). All share a coordinate-saliency scoring gate and a dyadic frequency front-end under SPOT calibration; a strictly causal input-channel gating variant is evaluated separately in a fault-type study.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\noindent\textbf{1) Quantifying the Latent Residual Conditioning Effect:} Forecasting in abstract representation space rather than reconstructing raw waveforms produces lighter-tailed residuals, lowering held-out nominal FPR from $30.0\text{--}30.7\%$ to $7.6\%$ (paired $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet, $p{=}0.002$; $-0.151$ vs.\ TranAD, $p{=}0.003$). Point-F1/PR-AUC remain statistically indistinguishable, detection latency rises ($\Delta t{=}80$ vs.\ $48$ steps), and causal event recall drops ($10.7\%$ vs.\ $32.9\%$): latent prediction is a conservative low-false-alarm operating point, not universal dominance.

\noindent\textbf{2) Rigorous Evaluation of Physics-Inspired Inductive Priors:} Across 33 streams, neither Operator-Entropy ($+0.002$ Point-F1, $p{=}0.71$) nor Reynolds-Stress ($-0.015$, $p{=}0.61$) beats unconstrained TS-JEPA, and Potential-Flow is significantly worse ($-0.081$, $p{=}0.033$): Koopman operators excel on quasi-linear spacecraft telemetry (SMAP PR-AUC $0.591$) but curl-free flows fail on cyclic kinematics. Fixed-backbone ablations show no significant change when the physical terms are disabled ($p \ge 0.375$).

\noindent\textbf{3) Empirical Analysis of Threshold Calibration Failures:} EVT/SPOT systematically misses its design risk ($q{=}10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry --- nominal FPR $5\text{--}8\%$ for JEPAs, $\approx 22\%$ for baselines --- and kurtosis adjustments degrade rather than correct out-of-sample risk control, performing comparably to simple empirical quantiles.

\noindent\textbf{4) Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark:} 33 matched streams across seven datasets under a strict held-out calibration protocol (65/35 split with isolation gap, up to five seeds, bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon tests); all code, checkpoints, and logs released.

Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work; Section~\ref{sec:dataset} the benchmark suite; Section~\ref{sec:proposed_method} the formulations; Sections~\ref{sec:exp}--\ref{sec:results} the protocol, ablations, and results; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes. Supplementary material contains proofs, extended diagnostics, and case-study detail.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Dynamical Systems, Reconstruction, and Forecasting Detectors}
Physical modeling of multi-sensor telemetry frames system behavior as continuous trajectories $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize velocity fields in raw observation space, where integrating through high-frequency sensor noise induces numerical stiffness and jitter sensitivity. Koopman theory and DMD \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} instead learn linear operators on observable embeddings; physical disruptions then manifest as spectral decoherence. Our Operator-Entropy variant transfers this idea to a latent transition operator regularized by spectral entropy during training.

The predominant detection paradigm learns nominal patterns via raw-signal reconstruction: LSTM prediction with dynamic thresholding for NASA telemetry \cite{Hundman2018b}, association-discrepancy attention in Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, 2D FFT-periodicity decomposition in TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and adversarial sub-series attention in TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}. These share a vulnerability: point-level losses bias networks toward fitting stochastic noise, and sustained anomalies contaminate the context window. Forecasting alternatives such as DeepAR \cite{salinas2020deepar} avoid bidirectional memorization but accumulate variance across irrelevant channels in high-dimensional arrays.

\subsection{Threshold Calibration, Contrastive, and Self-Supervised Detection}
Unsupervised detectors require an automated rule converting residual scores to binary alarms without test labels. Non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} and Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}---fitting a Generalized Pareto tail above a high quantile under design risk $q$---are standard; however, EVT's asymptotic guarantees assume i.i.d.\ tail samples, which telemetry autocorrelation and operational drift violate.

To avoid reconstructing noise, contrastive methods learn discriminative representations: NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} trains TCNs against injected synthetic anomalies (which distort the physical manifold and miss subtle real faults), and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} maximizes cross-scale consistency via dual-attention contrasting. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead predict target representations in latent space, with non-contrastive collapse prevention \cite{zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening, bardes2021vicreg}. Time-series extensions \cite{yue2022ts2vec, eldele2021time, zhang2023self} typically evaluate ranking metrics or post-hoc threshold sweeps without studying how latent residuals behave under automated tail calibration---the gap this work addresses.

\subsection{Physics-Inspired Inductive Biases}
PINNs \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian/Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} embed dynamics as hard constraints; their stiff competing gradients \cite{wang2021understanding} and closed-form-PDE requirements fit poorly to uncalibrated multi-sensor telemetry. We instead test whether \emph{inspired} structures---spectral entropy, second-moment closure, conservative potential flow---improve latent residuals.

\subsection{Clarification of Technical Terminology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physics-inspired vs.\ physics-informed:} the latter enforces known governing equations as loss residuals; the former adopts physics-motivated structures as exploratory regularizers, without claiming latent coordinates are physical state variables.
    \item \textbf{Causal preprocessing vs.\ causal decision-time access:} the former computes features strictly from history ($\partial \mathbf{g}/\partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$); the latter specifies whether alarms are issued with $0$-lookahead or after an $S$-step buffer.
    \item \textbf{Conservative vs.\ dissipative:} autonomous gradient flows $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi$ satisfy $\frac{d}{d\tau}\Phi \le 0$ and preclude periodic orbits.
    \item \textbf{Operator non-degeneracy vs.\ representation anti-collapse:} the transition operator $\mathbf{K}$ not collapsing to zero is distinct from the encoder not collapsing representations to a constant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Positioning}
This investigation bridges joint-embedding predictive learning and multivariate anomaly detection: forecasting latent $z_{\text{tgt}}$ rather than reconstructing $\hat{x}$ avoids noise drowning; three candidate physics-inspired regularizers are evaluated empirically against unconstrained latent controls; latent coordinate-saliency gating and dyadic frequency shells preprocess residuals; and EVT/SPOT calibration is evaluated on strictly held-out nominal telemetry to quantify the gap between design risk and empirical false-alarm rates.

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the UCI Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP Earth-observation \texttt{A-1}/\texttt{A-2}/\texttt{A-7}/\texttt{D-3}/\texttt{P-3}/\texttt{E-1} power, thermal, and attitude subsystems; MSL rover \texttt{C-1}/\texttt{C-2}/\texttt{D-14}/\texttt{D-15}/\texttt{D-16}/\texttt{M-1} actuator and environmental channels, with two short-nominal streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet FoG streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 GHL gasoil heating-loop streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample calibration, each stream's nominal sequence is partitioned chronologically into training (65\%), calibration (17.5\%), and held-out evaluation (17.5\%) blocks separated by an isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). The held-out protocol spans 33 streams across seven datasets (6 Daphnet, 14 GHL, 4 MSL, 6 SMAP, Genesis, Metro, SWAN); streams whose nominal blocks are too short for disjoint partitions (CalIt2, Room-Occupancy, two MSL) or where multi-seed stride-1 evaluation over $10^6$ steps was prohibitive (GECCO, CICIDS) are reserved as case studies.

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dataset_specs} specifies the suite: $2{,}079{,}507$ nominal training and $2{,}986{,}574$ test timesteps ($>$5.06M observations), with per-dataset domain, channel dimension $K$, sequence lengths, contamination ratio $\pi\%$, anomaly typologies, and partition protocols.


A machine-readable manifest (\texttt{reports/stream\_manifest.csv}, generated by \texttt{scripts/build\_stream\_manifest.py}) enumerates every stream's identifier, channel dimension, split lengths, held-out anomaly counts, benchmark role, and evaluated seed set as recorded in the released artifacts. All counts in this section, the abstract, and the results tables reconcile against it: 33 primary held-out streams (19 non-GHL at five seeds, 14 GHL at three to five seeds depending on size) and 12 case-study streams.

\section{Methodology: Latent Representation Forecasting and Structured Inductive Priors}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

This section formalizes the mathematical formulation, latent predictors, and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a multivariate stream partitioned into context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize pointwise error $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}\| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2$, where ambient jitter dominates the Frobenius norm and capacity is spent reconstructing noise (noise drowning). We instead adopt the JEPA framework \cite{lecun2022path}: a context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an EMA momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}}$, with $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$, $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$. The objective forecasts target representations in latent space, testing whether structured priors condition prediction residuals for tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
To examine whether incorporating structured dynamical inductive biases stabilizes representation forecasting, we evaluate an unconstrained latent predictor alongside three candidate formulations inspired by physical dynamical principles:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, in which nonlinear trajectories are analyzed via linear operators on observable functions, we parameterize the predictive latent transition via a learned operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent collapse onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or to zero (energy collapse), the regularizer decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote the singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T$, and $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ the total energy. For $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) > 0$, the scale-free spectral distribution over modes is $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2$, $\sum_i p_i = 1$. We omit isotropic diagonal loading from the Gram matrix before normalization --- it would give $\mathbf{K} \to \mathbf{0}$ an artificial identity spectrum with maximal entropy, spuriously rewarding collapse. The Von Neumann spectral entropy uses the convention $0 \log 0 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i: p_i > 0} p_i \log p_i.
\end{equation}
The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} quantifies the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform modal equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^T$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Here, $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while dimensional collapse to rank 1 yields maximal deficiency $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \log D$.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) and prevent explosive gain, we introduce a non-negative Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge 0, \quad r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) = \max\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2}{\gamma D}, \epsilon\right),
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ provides a numerical floor. For the identity operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$, $\|\mathbf{I}\|_F^2 = D$, yielding $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{I}) = 0$ exactly. We emphasize that the implemented barrier is \emph{floored}: it attains a finite maximum penalty $\epsilon - 1 - \log \epsilon \approx 12.8$ (before $\beta_{\text{norm}}$ weighting) throughout $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 < \epsilon\gamma D$, rather than diverging; divergence to $+\infty$ is a property only of the unfloored idealization $\Phi_{\text{energy}}^{\ast} = r - 1 - \log r$. The operator is initialized near identity ($\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$), so $r_{\text{safe}} \approx 1$ and training starts with a vanishing energy penalty; since LayerNorm gives $\mathbb{E}[\|z\|_2^2] \approx D$, the isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ matches the natural energy of unitary transitions. In sensitivity checks across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$ the model converged stably, with $0.5$ balancing spectral spread against predictive regression. The composite spectral regularizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$. The total training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$ in all experiments. All shared representation components (saliency gating, dyadic frequency shells, LayerNorm) are held fixed across models and controls, isolating the transition-operator loss. We emphasize that $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts on the operator matrix, enforcing a non-collapsed operator spectrum; it provides no analytical guarantee against encoder representation collapse, which is mitigated empirically by the EMA target encoder and stop-gradient operations \cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Triviality Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$, and non-degenerate context--target coupling $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^T \neq \mathbf{0}$. Then (i) the zero operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is strictly not a local or global minimizer of $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K})$ under the implemented floored barrier, and (ii) under the unfloored idealization ($\epsilon \to 0^+$, $\Phi_{\text{energy}}^{\ast} = r - 1 - \log r$), additionally $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ the floored barrier is saturated and contributes zero gradient, while $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}|_{\mathbf{0}} = -\frac{2}{B}\sum_b z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}{z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^T \neq \mathbf{0}$, so $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ is non-stationary; (ii) follows from $-\log r \to +\infty$ while $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} \to E_{\text{tgt}} < \infty$. Full proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Operator Non-Degeneracy vs. Representation Collapse):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} concerns only the transition operator: it does not prevent encoder collapse. If $E_\theta$ mapped all inputs to a constant $z^{(b)} = \mathbf{c}$, then $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ would attain zero prediction error and zero spectral penalty while encoding no information; prevention of that failure mode rests empirically on the stop-gradient momentum dynamics, not on Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse}.

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Second-Moment Closure Head)}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Reynolds decomposition splits a turbulent velocity field into a mean flow and a fluctuation component, where the Reynolds stress tensor captures the second moment of velocity fluctuations. We adapt this second-moment formulation as a latent-space inductive bias; the latent coordinates are learned embeddings, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is not a physical momentum-transport tensor.

A two-layer MLP predicts the conditional mean target latent $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a learned linear projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$ ($d_s = 16$) applied to the EMA target representation yields the empirical second-moment matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^T + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}.
\end{equation}
Because $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}$ is computed from an instantaneous latent vector rather than averaged over the target horizon $S$, it forms a rank-one outer product with a ridge term enforcing membership in $\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$; statistically it is an auxiliary second-moment projection penalty. The closure head $h_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})$ outputs a lower-triangular $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ with positive diagonal $L_{ii} = \operatorname{softplus}(a_{ii}) + \epsilon$, and the predicted covariance is reconstructed via Cholesky factorization:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s},
\end{equation}
confining predictions to $\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$. The training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}).
\end{equation}
At inference time, the anomaly score combines the mean latent discrepancy and the fluctuation tensor residual via the coordinate-saliency gate (formalized in Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). The stress term enters both the training loss and the test score; its downstream contribution is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a).

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory and Flow-Matching Derivation)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, a smooth field $v \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R}^D)$ splits into a curl-free potential gradient and a solenoidal field, $v = -\nabla \Phi + \nabla \times A$. As dissipative systems relax toward local minima on potential-energy surfaces, we model latent evolution as the dissipative gradient flow $\dot{z}(\tau) = -\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z(\tau))$ for a scalar potential network $\Phi_\psi$ with smooth (SiLU) activations.

\begin{proposition}[Absence of Periodic Attractors in Gradient Flows]
\label{prop:no_limit_cycles}
Let $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$ be an autonomous gradient flow on $\mathbb{R}^D$ where $\Phi \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R})$. There exist no non-constant periodic orbits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Along a closed orbit, $0 = \Phi(\gamma(T_p)) - \Phi(\gamma(0)) = -\int_0^{T_p}\|\nabla\Phi(\gamma(t))\|_2^2 dt$, forcing $\nabla\Phi \equiv \mathbf{0}$ and hence $\dot{\gamma} \equiv \mathbf{0}$, contradicting non-constant periodicity. Full proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Continuous Velocity Field and Multi-Subspace Regime Routing}
The velocity field is the negative gradient of a parameterized scalar potential, $\hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}})$, where $\Phi_\psi$ is a three-layer residual MLP (LayerNorm, SiLU) conditioned on state $z_t$, diffusion time $t \in [0,1]$ (sinusoidal embedding), context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a discrete regime projection $p_{\text{regime}}$; the raw gradient is tangent-projected via $v_\perp = v - \langle u, v\rangle u$, $u = z_t/\|z_t\|_2$, bounding integration curvature.

Discrete operational modes use $K_r = 4$ learnable orthonormal subspace projections $\mathbf{U}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d}$ ($d{=}8, D{=}32$); the active regime is selected by maximal captured context energy, $k^* = \arg\max_k \|\mathbf{U}_k^\top z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2^2$, $p_{\text{regime}} = \mathbf{U}_{k^*} \mathbf{U}_{k^*}^\top z_{\text{ctx}}$, with an orthogonality separation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{subspace}} = \frac{2}{K_r(K_r-1)} \sum_{i < j} \|\mathbf{U}_i^\top \mathbf{U}_j\|_F^2$. Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations} includes a single-regime control ($K_r{=}1$) with routing disabled.

\paragraph{Flow-Matching Objective, Numerical Midpoint Evaluation, and Dynamical Misspecification}
Proposition~\ref{prop:no_limit_cycles} governs the autonomous continuous flow $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$; the empirical objective instead trains $\hat{v}$ via conditional flow matching (OT-CFM) along linear paths between noise $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$: $z_t = (1-t)z_{\text{noise}} + t z_{\text{tgt}}$, $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$, with target field $v_t^* = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}$ and loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \left[ \| \hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2 \right].
\end{equation}
Along the deterministic mean trajectory ($z_{\text{noise}} = \mathbf{0}$), $z_t = t\,z_{\text{tgt}}$ and $v_t^* = z_{\text{tgt}}$ is constant.

At inference, rather than integrating an ODE over $t \in [0,1]$, the detector queries the field at the trajectory midpoint:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}}, 0.5, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - z_{\text{tgt}}.
\end{equation}
Since the reference velocity along the mean path is $v_{0.5}^* = z_{\text{tgt}}$, this midpoint query directly measures the discrepancy between the learned potential gradient and the nominal target, matching the dimensionality ($D{=}32$) and scale of the unconstrained TS-JEPA residual. The anomaly score applies the saliency gate: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$.

\noindent\textbf{Dynamical Misspecification on Non-Conservative Telemetry:} A curl-free velocity field ($\nabla \times \hat{v} \equiv \mathbf{0}$) forces circulation along any closed path $\mathcal{C}$ to vanish, $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \hat{v} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$. Valid for purely dissipative equilibria, this is dynamically misspecified for the non-conservative cyclic dynamics common in telemetry (motor revolutions, diurnal GECCO consumption cycles, periodic Daphnet gait kinematics): normal circulating orbits are penalized as anomalous. This predicts degraded detection on cyclic telemetry, tested in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed at stride 1. For TS-JEPA, Operator-Entropy, and Potential-Flow, the score gates the latent discrepancy through $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}): $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t))$. For Reynolds-Stress, $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(z_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. All shared representation stages are held fixed across variants and ablation controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\textbf{Operational Distinction: Zero-Lookahead Causal Filtering vs. Buffered Predictive Early Warning.}
Two regimes exist: (1) zero-lookahead filtering, emitting $\hat{y}_t$ from observations up to $t$; and (2) buffered predictive monitoring across lookahead $S$. TS-JEPA uses regime (2): given context $x_{\text{ctx}}$, it projects $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ over $[t, t+S]$, so an anomaly at $t^*$ can raise discrepancy up to $S{=}64$ steps before onset. Under strict unadjusted point-level evaluation (stride 1), warnings during $[t^*-S, t^*]$ score as false alarms --- predictive foresight incurs a retrospective metric penalty for early warning. Incident analysis (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}) also evaluates \textit{Middle} alignment ($t + S/2$); all baselines share identical windowing, horizon, and standardization.


\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and the Auxiliary Channel-Gate Variant}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults frequently originate in a localized subsystem while the remaining channels exhibit ambient noise, diluting localized anomalies in isotropic representations. The deployed detector mitigates this in latent space via an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate on error vectors $\mathbf{e} = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{pred}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) = (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \quad w_d = \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)},
\end{equation}
amplifying sparse coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$ while remaining exactly $\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$ under isotropic errors. This is the discrepancy gate used in every experiment: input channels are encoded unweighted, and the gate acts only on the $D$-dimensional predictive residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary Input-Channel Gate (Evaluated Variant)}
We also formulate a strictly causal \textit{Causal Channel Saliency Gate} as an auxiliary variant (full specification and the fault-type study in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}): each channel is weighted by the sigmoid of its standardized context-window variance share, $g_k = \sigma\!\left((s_k - \bar{s})/(\sigma_s + \epsilon)\right)$, applied symmetrically to context and target encoders so that $\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$ (zero target leakage). For $K{=}1$ the implementation short-circuits to identity $\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{1}$ (the naive formula degenerates to $0.5$); equal variance shares yield the neutral value $0.5$ --- a \emph{relative} reweighting absorbed by downstream LayerNorm, not a normalized partition --- and a constant channel is suppressed toward $0$, not removed. Boundary cases verified numerically: $K{=}1 \Rightarrow g \equiv 1.0$; i.i.d.\ eight-channel input gives mean $0.50 \pm 0.22$; a $10\times$-variance channel gates to $0.93$ vs.\ $0.41$ for the rest; a constant channel to $0.08$; and an abrupt mid-context variance shift gates identically to a persistent one ($0.93$), since shares pool over the context window.

\paragraph{Zero-Leakage Data Provenance and Temporal Isolation}
We enforce strict temporal isolation across the training, nominal-calibration, and held-out evaluation partitions (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}): standardization statistics come only from the training partition, EVT tail parameters $(u, \sigma, \xi)$ only from the calibration partition, and evaluation proceeds chronologically with frozen weights and thresholds. The auxiliary gate's weights depend only on $x_{\text{ctx}}$; the deployed pipeline weights inputs uniformly.

\paragraph{Gating Boundary Conditions and Failure Modes}
Three boundary conditions apply to variance-based gating. First, intrinsically quiet channels keep low $s_k$ and remain attenuated even during small absolute-magnitude anomalies; streams are therefore standardized by nominal statistics before gating. Second, quiet faults (flatlines, stuck values, $v_k^{\text{ctx}} \to 0$) cannot be isolated by variance amplification; the fault-type study (Section~\ref{subsec:gate_fault_study}) confirms they are weakly detected under every gating regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$), a limitation of the latent discrepancy signal rather than of gating --- flatlines are instead caught, only partially, through predictive discrepancy when the faulted channel contributes to the aggregate residual. Third, abrupt regime transitions transiently perturb variance shares until the $C{=}256$ context window refills.

\subsubsection{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Anomalies span temporal scales from sensor spikes to thermal drifts; the encoder front-end therefore decomposes inputs into $J{=}4$ dyadic frequency bands. Continuous wavelet theory defines the Littlewood--Paley partition of unity as:
\begin{equation}
    |\phi(\omega)|^2 + \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} |\psi(2^j \omega)|^2 = 1, \quad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi],
\end{equation}
discrete numerical implementations require exact partition enforcement on a finite frequency grid. Over an $N_{\text{fft}} = 256$ discrete real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid yielding $F = 129$ non-negative frequency bins $\omega_m = m / N_{\text{fft}}$ for $m \in \{0, \dots, N_{\text{fft}}/2\}$, dyadic center frequencies are positioned at $c_j = 2^{j - J + 1}$ for $j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. The unnormalized shells are constructed in three branches: a half-Gaussian profile anchored at DC for $j = 0$, log-frequency octave bells $\tilde{\Psi}_j(\omega_m) = \exp\left(-\frac{(\log_2 \omega_m - \log_2 c_j)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$ with bandwidth $\sigma = 0.6$ for interior shells $j \in \{1, 2\}$, and a plateau rising toward the Nyquist edge for $j = J-1$. To strictly preserve the partition property across all discrete frequencies, filter weights are normalized bin-wise into an exact partition of unity:
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_j[\omega_m] = \frac{\tilde{\Psi}_j(\omega_m)}{\sum_{l=0}^{J-1} \tilde{\Psi}_l(\omega_m)}, \quad \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1.0, \quad \forall m \in \{0, \dots, F-1\}.
\end{equation}
The frequency-domain split is applied directly via $\mathbf{x}_j = \mathrm{irfft}(\Psi_j \odot \mathrm{rfft}(\mathbf{x}))$, which is numerically exact: measured partition-of-unity deviation is below $1.2 \times 10^{-7}$ and reconstruction error below $5 \times 10^{-8}$. Each shell is processed by a full (non-depthwise) causal 1D convolution with kernels $k \in \{15, 9, 5, 3\}$ (coarse-to-fine) and left-side causal padding $p = k-1$, then recombined by a learned softmax scale router over pooled per-shell energies, a $1 \times 1$ projection, residual scaling $\gamma = 0.1$, and LayerNorm. The normalized shells overlap smoothly at band edges, so band energies are correlated ($\sum_j \|\mathbf{x}_j\|^2$ recovers $\approx 70\%$ of signal energy) and the finest shell concentrates near Nyquist. Comparing each band's spectral mass inside its ideal dyadic octave $[2^{-(j+1)}, 2^{-j}]$ against Mallat-form Haar and Daubechies-4 octave responses (\texttt{reports/lp\_filterbank\_diagnostics.csv}): our shells place $\{0.50, 0.33, 0.20, {\approx}1.00\}$ in-band versus $\{0.78, 0.50, 0.55, 0.82\}$ (Haar) and $\{0.86, 0.63, 0.64, 0.87\}$ (DB4) --- the front-end is \emph{less} frequency-localized than either standard wavelet bank, trading octave isolation for smooth, exactly-reconstructing amplitude partitions and learned scale routing. We therefore characterize the block as a Fourier-domain multiscale front-end with an exact discrete partition of unity---not as an exact Littlewood-Paley octave decomposition---and rely on the matched on/off ablation in Section~\ref{sec:ablations} (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b) to measure its empirical contribution. This decomposition isolates transient high-frequency perturbations from low-frequency diurnal baselines before latent projection.

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}: excesses over a high threshold $u$ (98th percentile) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via an empirical heuristic factor governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ rises to suppress volatile-noise false alarms; on light-tailed ones it tightens for sensitivity.

We emphasize that this is an engineering heuristic for pre-asymptotic finite-sample tails, not an asymptotic EVT theorem. In the controlled calibrator ablation (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) it does not improve out-of-sample risk control: on held-out data the multiplier raises TS-JEPA nominal FPR from $5.26\%$ to $17.68\%$ with no statistically distinguishable Point-F1 change ($0.1694$ vs.\ $0.1577$).

More broadly, EVT does not eliminate threshold-calibration difficulty: under $q = 10^{-3}$ (ideal asymptotic FPR $0.1\%$), measured held-out nominal FPR settles at $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ for the JEPAs and $\approx 22\%$ for TimesNet/TranAD, owing to finite-sample variance, residual autocorrelation, and non-stationary drift between nominal training and deployment. The calibrated finding of this paper is narrower: latent predictive residuals produce substantially fewer false alarms than observation-space residuals under identical calibration, reflecting lighter tails at the cost of lower causal recall. Thresholds use static EVT on held-out nominal splits; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

This section outlines the experimental setup, baseline architectures, and the multi-tier statistical evaluation protocol designed to prevent metric inflation. The master experiment matrix enumerating all evaluation suites (stream counts, train/calibration/test partitions, horizons, calibrators, and metrics) is provided in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:master_matrix}.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted using a causal dilated Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) comprising 3 residual blocks with channel depth $F=48$, kernel size $K=5$, and dilation factors $d \in \{1, 2, 4\}$. While the convolutional receptive field across the 3 dilated blocks spans $1 + 2(K-1)\sum_l d_l = 57$ timesteps, global temporal average pooling across the resulting feature maps aggregates information across the entire context window $C=256$, condensing the sequence into a fixed-dimensional latent representation $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ regularized by Layer Normalization and dropout ($p=0.20$). The momentum target encoder parameters $\theta_{\text{tgt}}$ are updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) tracking: $\theta_{\text{tgt}} \leftarrow m \theta_{\text{tgt}} + (1-m) \theta_{\text{ctx}}$, where momentum coefficient $m$ follows a cosine schedule from $m_{\text{base}}=0.996$ to $m_{\text{max}}=0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's nominal telemetry is partitioned into three disjoint chronological blocks: \emph{training} (first 65\%; representation weights only), \emph{nominal calibration} (next 17.5\%; withheld from training, used solely to fit per-stream GPD tail parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}0.98$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$, with $\gamma_2$ estimated strictly within this block), and \emph{nominal evaluation} (final 17.5\%, separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap; held-out FPR measured on data the threshold never saw). Thresholds then remain fixed during test evaluation to measure static EVT stability under drift; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}).

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment Across Experiments}
To avoid ambiguity, we specify the calibrator per experiment. \textbf{Primary held-out benchmark} (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, \ref{tab:causal_benchmark}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}; all paired statistics): standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier); headline metrics are the ``SPOT'' column of Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, with calibrator variants compared on identical score streams. \textbf{Ablations and controlled studies} (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}): SPOT with the $\tanh(\gamma_2)/6$ kurtosis multiplier (the released \texttt{adaptive\_kurtosis} flag). \textbf{Early-warning and EVT mechanism studies}: standard SPOT unless the calibrator is the object of comparison. \textbf{Incident metrics} (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}): rolling SPOT. No experiment mixes calibrators across models within a table.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Multiplicity Controls}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are trained across five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 large GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) use three seeds (five on the two smallest) for cost, and ablation controls use three. Two co-primary endpoints: (i) \emph{nominal FPR reduction} on held-out nominal data, testing whether latent prediction stabilizes EVT bounds; and (ii) \emph{detection parity} via unadjusted Point-F1 and PR-AUC, testing whether suppression degrades discrimination.

Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, moving-block bootstrap 95\% CIs (block $L{=}64$), and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The independent unit of analysis is the telemetry stream ($n{=}19$ non-GHL at five seeds; $n{=}33$ where GHL enters at available coverage): seeds are averaged within stream and repeated windows aggregated to one stream-level score, so nothing is double-counted. Because nonsignificant tests do not establish equivalence, we make no noninferiority claim: paired differences are reported with effect sizes and CIs for readers to judge practical margins. $p$-values across comparisons are Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted at $q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$; CIs spanning zero or adjusted $p > 0.05$ are treated as inconclusive, not as equivalence.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Parameter Capacities}
To isolate algorithmic principles from external implementation discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline under identical windowing, standardization, and EVT calibration:

\noindent\textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Multi-periodic reconstructive architecture mapping 1D series to 2D variation spaces via FFT period discovery and Inception blocks ($648{,}553$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $574{,}529$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Adversarial reconstructive Transformer with two-stage self-conditioning and sub-series attention ($205{,}746$ at $K{=}25$; $201{,}090$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TS-JEPA (Unconstrained Control):} Standard JEPA predicting $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ via a 2-layer MLP without physical priors ($289{,}061$ at $K{=}25$; $218{,}309$ at $K{=}9$; only the input projection scales with $K$ at $+48$ parameters per channel).

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Formulations:} Operator-Entropy ($285{,}733$ at $K{=}25$; its explicit $D{\times}D$ Koopman matrix replaces the MLP predictor, $-1.15\%$ capacity), Reynolds-Stress ($295{,}953$, $+2.4\%$ for the covariance closure head), and Potential-Flow ($338{,}117$, $+17.0\%$ for the potential network). These specifications confirm performance differences do not stem from parameter scaling.

\noindent\textbf{Linear AR Forecaster (Non-Neural Baseline):} Ridge-regularized linear map from flattened context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \cdot K}$ to target windows $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \cdot K}$, scored by observation-space prediction error under identical held-out EVT --- isolating whether latent representation learning is necessary at all.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Critical surveys \cite{kim2022towards, schmidl2022anomaly, wu2023current} document severe reporting vulnerabilities in this literature --- point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, threshold gaming --- so we report metrics across complementary tiers:

\noindent\textbf{Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (Primary Metric):} Unweighted mean of Point-F1 across datasets:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Macro-F1}_{\text{DS}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|} \sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \left( \frac{1}{|C_d|} \sum_{c \in C_d} \text{F1}_{c} \right)
\end{equation}
where $C_d$ is the channel set of dataset $d$; this prevents high-stream datasets (GHL, 14 streams) from dominating and weights each physical domain equally.

\noindent\textbf{Pooled Micro Point-F1:} Global pooling of TP/FP/FN over all test timesteps, $\text{Micro-F1} = 2\sum_c \text{TP}_c / (2\sum_c \text{TP}_c + \sum_c \text{FP}_c + \sum_c \text{FN}_c)$; it exposes models that flood long streams with false alarms. \textbf{Channel-Macro Point-F1} is the standard per-channel average. \textbf{PR-AUC} (average precision over all score thresholds) evaluates raw ranking independent of calibration. \textbf{Event Recall} is the fraction of contiguous anomaly segments $[t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]$ in which at least one alarm fires. \textbf{Mean Detection Delay} $\Delta t$ is the onset-to-first-alarm lag averaged over detected events (undetected events enter via Event Recall, not an arbitrary penalty). \textbf{Trivial all-positive reference}: a constant alarm scores $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at base rate $\pi$; models below it provide less value than an uncalibrated alarm. \textbf{Contiguous PA-F1}: point adjustment within contiguous ground-truth segments only; we also report test-sweep PA-F1 (best post-hoc threshold) to quantify the residual calibration gap.

\subsection{Evaluation Regimes: Zero-Lookahead Causal vs. Buffered Inference}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered_protocol}
To keep early-warning capability separate from future-observation access, we evaluate two regimes: \textbf{strictly causal online detection} (at decision time $T$ only $\{X_\tau\}_{\tau \le T}$ is accessible; scores use trailing attribution; zero lookahead) and \textbf{buffered window detection} ($S{=}64$ future timesteps are buffered before scoring, capturing full trajectory evolution at a deliberate $64$-step latency). Both record Mean Detection Delay, Event Recall within delay budgets ($\Delta t \le 10, 64$), and false alarms per 1,000 steps ($\text{FAR}_{1\text{k}}$).

\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

To isolate the individual contributions of each mathematical and architectural component, we conduct controlled ablation experiments across multiple telemetry regimes.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$:} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.241 \pm 0.014$ vs. $0.244 \pm 0.024$; difference $-0.003$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.011, +0.005]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.62$); PR-AUC stays $0.347\text{--}0.348$.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$:} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.203 \pm 0.059$ vs. $0.208 \pm 0.071$ ($+0.005$, $[-0.006, +0.016]$, $p=0.41$). The null is also insensitive to the covariance estimator: per-sample temporal (\texttt{Reynolds-tempcov}) or batch (\texttt{Reynolds-batchcov}) covariances in place of the rank-1 outer product shift Point-F1 by $-0.009$/$-0.010$ and FPR by $+0.004$/$-0.042$ over nine streams --- the second-moment term carries no signal beyond ordinary latent prediction.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing \emph{improves} Point-F1 to $0.129 \pm 0.061$ from $0.096 \pm 0.026$ ($-0.033$, $[-0.060, -0.010]$, $p=0.048$); both underperform TS-JEPA ($0.234 \pm 0.030$, $p=0.013$).
\end{enumerate}
Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating Variant}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling, a non-causal target-only leaky control, and independent context/target gating under identical TS-JEPA configuration (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:gating_variants}, \ref{tab:fault_recall}; full regime definitions and per-fault-type results in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}). The effect is stream-dependent (MSL $0.047 \to 0.227$, SMAP $0.280 \to 0.019$); macro Point-F1 favors uniform weighting ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$), and the leaky control's small margin ($+8.6\%$) shows the effect stems from quiescent-noise suppression rather than target statistics. The fault-type study finds gating roughly doubles point recall on transient faults at $\sim$$3\times$ the nominal FPR, while quiet faults (flatline, stuck sensor, dropout) remain weakly detected under \emph{every} regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$). Input gating is therefore reported as an evaluated variant; the deployed pipeline retains uniform input weighting.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Monolithic convolutions either over-smooth transients or miss long-period cycles (e.g., GECCO's diurnal consumption alongside abrupt pressure drops). As reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells ($\mathbf{x}_j = \mathrm{irfft}(\Psi_j \odot \mathrm{rfft}(\mathbf{x}))$) plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a modest, stream-dependent effect under identical TS-JEPA configuration: macro buffered Point-F1 improves from $0.088$ to $0.131$ ($+49.6\%$ relative) across five multivariate streams, concentrated on MSL ($0.002 \to 0.052$) and SMD ($0.000 \to 0.177$), with a small SMAP regression ($0.294 \to 0.261$) and near-zero GECCO detection either way. Two caveats: the block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters (part of the gain may be capacity), and measured shell overlap (Section~\ref{sec:proposed_method}) means octave isolation is not attained. The front-end is a modest contributor, not the primary driver.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Threshold Calibration Rules on Nominal Splits}
\label{subsec:ablation_calibration}
The complete calibrator comparison across all 33 streams and all learned architectures is reported in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} and Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}; we summarize the two findings most relevant to the ablation suite here. First, simple non-parametric rules match parametric SPOT: on TS-JEPA, a validation-maximum rule attains held-out FPR $0.0553$ versus $0.0526$ for standard SPOT at nearly identical Point-F1. Second, the kurtosis-adaptive multiplier degrades calibration, inflating TS-JEPA held-out FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$ while leaving Point-F1 statistically indistinguishable ($0.1694$ vs.\ $0.1577$). Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} decomposes this failure into extremal clustering, chronological drift, and GPD goodness-of-fit breakdown.

\subsection{Controlled Backbone Comparison: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
A foundational hypothesis is that predicting in a contracted latent space produces residuals better conditioned for EVT thresholding than observation-space reconstruction or forecasting. Comparing full JEPA models against public baselines confounds receptive field, depth, and normalization; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:controlled_backbone} therefore evaluates three formulations under a unified \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, 3-layer dilated causal TCN, LayerNorm, GELU, temporal pooling): \textbf{TCN-Obs-Recon} (reconstruction MSE), \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} (observation forecasting residual), and \textbf{TS-JEPA} (latent prediction against an EMA target), trained identically and calibrated via kurtosis-adaptive EVT on a held-out 20\% nominal split.



\noindent\textbf{Observation Reconstruction vs. Observation Forecasting:} The matched-backbone results resolve whether false-alarm reduction stems from forecasting or from latent representation: TCN-Obs-Recon and TCN-Obs-Pred exhibit virtually identical tails ($\gamma_2$: $6.09$/$6.00$ val, $57.26$/$55.66$ test; clean FPR $27.29\%$/$27.57\%$) and are statistically indistinguishable across the 15 streams (Wilcoxon clean FPR $p = 0.6784$; Point-F1 $p = 1.0000$). Forecasting in raw observation space does not condition residual tails because residuals directly reflect sensor jitter.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Space Projection and Tail Compression:} Latent-space prediction fundamentally reshapes the residual distribution: test excess kurtosis drops from $55.66$ to $1.77$ ($>31\times$), and clean empirical FPR falls from $27.57\%$ to $11.80\%$ (Wilcoxon $p = 0.0479$, $\approx 6{,}500$ fewer false alarms per stream). Under $0$-dB noise injection, observation-space FPR surges past $50\%$ while TS-JEPA holds at $13.93\%$ ($p = 0.0026$).

\noindent\textbf{The Operational Sensitivity Trade-Off:} This stability costs detection sensitivity: clean Point-F1 declines from $0.1916$ (TCN-Obs-Pred) to $0.0785$ ($p = 0.0054$), with recall dropping $0.4315 \to 0.1460$ --- localized point anomalies that do not perturb macroscopic state are filtered with the noise. Latent prediction thus acts as a conservative state detector.


\noindent\textbf{Investigation of Native Window Horizons:} A secondary hypothesis is that baselines suffered under EVT calibration only because of the long $W{=}320$ window. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:native_window_baselines} refutes this: native horizons \emph{worsen} tail conditioning. TranAD at $W{=}10$ spikes to $\gamma_2 = 448.25$ (vs.\ $29.93$ at $W{=}320$) --- micro-windows lack the context to average out sensor noise --- and TimesNet at $W{=}96$ shows $\gamma_2 = 98.14$ with $44.09\%$ empirical FPR. TS-JEPA retains well-conditioned residuals ($\gamma_2 = 3.14$, FPR $22.57\%$): observation-space tail corruption is inherent to raw reconstruction, not a window-length artifact.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

We report evaluations across 33 matched telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol: whether latent prediction conditions residuals for automated thresholding, the empirical behavior of the physics-inspired regularizers, operational latency, and the reliability of EVT calibration on real telemetry.

\input{sections/tab_core_benchmark.tex}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} presents the empirical comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams evaluated under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$).

Several primary empirical findings emerge from Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}:

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076$--$0.086$ versus $0.300$ (TimesNet) and $0.307$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams (seeds averaged), the paired FPR reduction is statistically significant: TS-JEPA $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet ($95\%$ CI $[-0.247, -0.071]$, Wilcoxon $p{=}0.002$) and $-0.151$ vs.\ TranAD ($[-0.247, -0.070]$, $p{=}0.003$); Operator-Entropy shows $-0.144$ vs.\ TimesNet ($p{=}0.006$) and $-0.142$ vs.\ TranAD ($p{=}0.006$). Latent residuals avoid observation-level noise, yielding better-conditioned score distributions (isolated under the same-backbone control, Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}).

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Parity:} False-alarm suppression does not translate into statistically distinguishable detection gains. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences are inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$).

\noindent\textbf{Event Recall and Detection Conservatism:} Lower FPR accompanies lower event recall: on non-GHL streams TimesNet captures $58.6\%$ of events and TranAD $48.1\%$, versus $49.8\%$ (TS-JEPA), $39.8\%$ (Reynolds), $33.1\%$ (Potential-Flow). Subtle onsets that do not shift dominant latent coordinates are filtered with the noise.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Delay Trade-Offs ($\Delta t$):} Mean detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) varies widely: TimesNet responds fastest ($\Delta t{=}48$ steps), TS-JEPA averages $80$, and the physics variants are slower still ($133$--$192$); TranAD is slowest ($234$). Because latent encoders smooth observation noise, alarms trigger only once anomalies propagate into dominant latent coordinates --- deployments trade ~4$\times$ fewer nominal false alarms for added initial latency.

\noindent\textbf{Necessity of Nonlinear Representation Learning (Linear AR Control):} A ridge-regularized linear AR forecaster under identical calibration attains FPR $0.006$ but collapses to Point-F1 $0.057$ (precision $4.35\%$): observation-space linear dynamics cannot separate faults from background variance. Nonlinear latent representation is necessary to combine controlled false alarms with usable detection.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-Specific Telemetry Dynamics:} Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:all_datasets}--\ref{tab:dataset_prauc} report the per-dataset Point-F1/PR-AUC breakdown. Relative performance depends on the physical sensing regime: TimesNet leads on Daphnet periodic kinematics ($0.245$ F1, $0.343$ PR-AUC vs.\ TS-JEPA $0.155$/$0.137$), where 2D Fourier decomposition captures multi-frequency gait structure; latent prediction leads on spacecraft telemetry (SMAP: TS-JEPA $0.441$ F1 / OpEntropy $0.591$ PR-AUC; MSL: OpEntropy $0.281$ F1 / $0.445$ PR-AUC, versus TimesNet $0.304$/$0.159$ F1). On SWAN, TimesNet triggers zero test detections under severe threshold miscalibration ($0.000$ F1 despite $0.292$ PR-AUC). On GHL and Metro, all models struggle ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1): subtle actuator drifts against stationary backgrounds and extreme traffic surges evade static EVT bounds respectively.

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data refute this:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. TS-JEPA:} paired difference $+0.002$ Point-F1 (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$), $+0.002$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.86$), $+0.009$ FPR ($p{=}0.75$); leads on only 9 of 33 streams.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. TS-JEPA:} $-0.015$ Point-F1 ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$), $-0.007$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.13$), $+0.001$ FPR ($p{=}0.92$); leads on 11 of 33.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. TS-JEPA:} significantly worse Point-F1 ($-0.081$, $[-0.160, -0.018]$, $p{=}0.033$) and nominally lower PR-AUC ($-0.067$, $p{=}0.58$); leads on 7 of 33.
\end{enumerate}

These findings align with the matched-capacity regularizer controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}, where disabling each physical loss term on a fixed backbone produces changes well within seed noise. We conclude that the physical regularizers provide no measurable anomaly detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. Performance differences between models stem from shared representation conditioning rather than the physical loss terms.


The dynamical diagnostics evaluating \emph{why} each regularizer fails (operator rank preservation without detection gain, covariance-shift insensitivity, and curl-free misspecification on cyclic telemetry) are reported in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics} (Tables~\ref{tab:operator_entropy_validation}--\ref{tab:potential_flow_validation}).

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

Event-level (incident) metrics, operational latency under dense vs.\ buffered inference, and rolling-SPOT adaptation case studies on Room-Occupancy, SMAP \texttt{P-3}, CalIt2, and a five-stream causal-vs-buffered panel are reported in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational} (Tables~\ref{tab:operational_incident_metrics}, \ref{tab:causal_vs_buffered}): dynamic rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at the cost of event recall (100\% $\to$ 63.6\%), and TS-JEPA causal false-positive rate ($4.18\%$) is an order of magnitude below reconstructive baselines ($20.8\%$--$30.1\%$).
\input{sections/tab_causal_benchmark.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark} reports the complete 33-stream benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams, unconstrained TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ (versus $0.2427$ for TimesNet and $0.2219$ for TranAD), an out-of-sample false-alarm suppression of roughly $18$-fold; on the 14 GHL streams causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs and $0.04\text{--}0.06$ for baselines, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ for TS-JEPA and $0.1093 \pm 0.0085$ for Operator-Entropy, whereas TimesNet achieves $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$. Event recall likewise declines from $58.82\%$ (TimesNet) to $46.55\%$ (TS-JEPA). This confirms that without a prospective lookahead buffer to observe multi-step trajectory evolution, latent predictive scoring functions as an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of reduced detection sensitivity.

\paragraph{Buffered Early-Warning Lead Time}
To separate prospective warning capability from detection fidelity, we measure the fraction of events first alerted before their labeled onset under buffered inference across five streams. TS-JEPA warns $12.3\%$ of events pre-onset ($7$ of $57$ pooled), with a median lead time of $180$ steps and $23.8$ false warnings per $1{,}000$ nominal steps. TimesNet warns $73.7\%$ of events pre-onset at a comparable median lead ($204$ steps) but emits $206.4$ false warnings per $1{,}000$ nominal steps; on GECCO it alerts $37$ of $37$ events early while generating $873.6$ false warnings per $1{,}000$ nominal steps, approaching a constant-alarm regime. Pre-onset coverage is therefore confounded by the underlying alarm rate: at its conservative operating point, TS-JEPA trades early-warning coverage for an order-of-magnitude lower nuisance-alarm rate.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

A critical requirement for autonomous telemetry monitoring is whether automated extreme-value thresholding adheres to theoretical design risk parameters. When practitioners specify a design risk of $q=10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), the operational expectation is that nominal false alarms will occur at or below this frequency.

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares threshold calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under buffered inference. Four realities emerge:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Systematic design-risk overshoot:} SPOT ($u{=}0.98, q{=}10^{-3}$) never achieves its design risk out-of-sample --- held-out nominal FPR averages $5.26\%$ (TS-JEPA), $6.17\%$ (OpEntropy), $22.17\%$ (TimesNet), $22.17\%$ (TranAD), a $53$--$222\times$ overshoot.
    \item \textbf{High failure rate:} 95\% bootstrap CIs exclude $q$ in $61\%$/$65\%$/$86\%$/$82\%$ of runs (TS-JEPA/OpEntropy/TimesNet/TranAD); non-stationary drift violates EVT's stationary-tail assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Parity with empirical quantiles:} on TS-JEPA, validation-maximum gives FPR $0.0553$ / F1 $0.1591$ and empirical p99.5 gives $0.0682$ / $0.1598$ --- parametric GPD tail modeling confers no demonstrable benefit.
    \item \textbf{Kurtosis-adaptive failure:} the multiplier inflates TS-JEPA FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$ (CI-exclusion $61\% \to 82\%$) with indistinguishable F1 ($0.1694$ vs.\ $0.1577$); calibration-split kurtosis does not forecast out-of-sample tail shifts.
\end{enumerate}

To separate the candidate causes of calibration failure, we recorded per-stream tail-fit diagnostics on a nine-stream non-GHL subset (TS-JEPA and TimesNet, three seeds each; \texttt{reports/evt\_tail\_diagnostics.csv}). Three observations disentangle the mechanism. First, tail-sample scarcity is genuine: the median exceedance count above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and the Grimshaw estimator returns the degenerate boundary shape $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits, so tail-estimation error is real. Second, goodness-of-fit is nonetheless not the dominant failure: a Kolmogorov--Smirnov test of the excesses against each fitted GPD rejects at $p < 0.05$ in only $6/27$ (TS-JEPA) and $8/27$ (TimesNet) cells. Third, realized held-out FPR varies from $0.0$ to $0.67$ across seeds on the \emph{same} stream under fits that pass the KS test --- direct evidence that inter-block distribution drift, not tail mis-specification, drives the overshoot. Exceedances are also strongly clustered (mean of $10$--$22$ exceedances per cluster, more pronounced for TimesNet, whose residuals spike in sustained bursts), which inflates the effective variance of the FPR estimate beyond what an i.i.d.\ tail model assumes. Finally, the empirical 99.5th-percentile rule achieves an equal or lower held-out FPR than SPOT in roughly half the cells ($13/27$ TS-JEPA, $16/27$ TimesNet), confirming that parametric tail extrapolation confers no reliability advantage under this drift regime.


\paragraph{Extended calibration diagnostics}
The multi-parameter EVT sensitivity sweep ($u$, $q$, kurtosis coefficient $\beta$) and the four-mechanism failure decomposition (extremal clustering via the Ferro--Segers extremal index, two-sample KS drift, GPD goodness-of-fit rejection, and empirical risk overshoot) are reported in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:evt_detail} (Tables~\ref{tab:evt_sensitivity} and~\ref{tab:evt_failure_decomposition}). The decomposition attributes the design-risk overshoot primarily to inter-block distribution drift and extremal clustering rather than tail mis-estimation, consistent with the per-stream tail diagnostics above.

Computational profiling (parameters, VRAM, inference latency, throughput) is reported in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}: the JEPA family uses $183.8\text{--}185.3$~MB peak memory (nearly $7\times$ less than TranAD) at $0.60\text{--}0.84$~ms per-window inference.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigated empirically whether JEPA-style latent prediction and physics-inspired regularizers resolve the false-alarm vulnerability of multivariate anomaly detection, evaluating 33 telemetry streams across seven physical domains under a strictly held-out calibration protocol. Three conclusions follow.

First, prediction in a contracted latent space conditions residual distributions: filtering observation-space jitter cuts held-out nominal FPR roughly four-fold versus reconstructive baselines ($-0.153$ vs. TimesNet, $p{=}0.002$; $-0.151$ vs. TranAD, $p{=}0.003$, five-seed subset), while Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain statistically indistinguishable ($p \ge 0.13$). Under strictly causal streaming, latent prediction suppresses false alarms to $1.0\%\text{--}1.9\%$ at the cost of lower F1 and delayed event recall.

Second, hypothesis testing refutes a detection advantage for the physics-inspired regularizers: neither Operator-Entropy nor Reynolds-Stress outperforms TS-JEPA ($p \ge 0.61$) across all 33 streams, and Potential-Flow is significantly worse ($p{=}0.033$), unable to represent circulating orbits. Matched-capacity controls confirm that disabling each physical term shifts Point-F1 by only $+0.001$ to $+0.006$ ($p \ge 0.58$); the formulations enforce their intended mathematics but real telemetry does not obey the idealized laws.

Third, SPOT calibration systematically overshoots its design risk on non-stationary telemetry ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ nominal FPR for JEPAs, $\approx 22\%$ for baselines at $q{=}10^{-3}$; CIs exclude $q$ in 61--86\% of runs). Parametric tail modeling offers no advantage over empirical quantiles, and kurtosis adjustment degrades rather than corrects the drift.

\textbf{Scope.} All evaluations are \emph{within-dataset}: models are trained, calibrated, and tested on disjoint chronological partitions of the same stream. We claim nothing about cross-dataset transfer or unseen fault types (cf.\ Section~\ref{subsec:gate_fault_study}). Extensions include solenoidal components via Helmholtz decomposition, memory-bounded online drift adaptation, accelerated streaming inference kernels, and integer quantization for edge deployment.
