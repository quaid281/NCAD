\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry is central to operational safety across aerospace systems, industrial processes, cyber-physical infrastructure, and smart environments. While reconstructive architectures such as TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive continuous ranking performance (PR-AUC $0.2585$--$0.2612$), their observation-space residuals destabilize automated streaming threshold calibration, generating over $1.05\times 10^6$ false positives. To address this calibration failure, we evaluate Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) that forecast dynamics in regularized latent representation space. We introduce three physics-informed inductive regularizers grounded in dynamical systems theory, continuum mechanics, and field theory: 
(1) \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, which parameterizes a latent Koopman transition matrix and regularizes dynamic spectral entropy; 
(2) \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, which adapts hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition to align second-order cross-channel fluctuation covariances; and 
(3) \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA}, which parameterizes latent trajectory transitions via an irrotational velocity potential ($\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$). 
To handle heterogeneous sensor arrays, the framework incorporates coordinate saliency gating for active channel isolation, dyadic wavelet shells for multi-scale frequency decomposition, and kurtosis-adapted Extreme Value Theory (EVT) for streaming threshold calibration. Evaluated on a matched benchmark of 45 continuous telemetry channels across 11 datasets, latent prediction suppresses false alarms while capturing true physical anomalies. Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves the highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.2066$, a +43.2\% relative improvement over TimesNet and +28.4\% over TranAD) and is the only architecture to exceed the analytical All-Positive baseline ($0.1893$). In Pooled Micro Point-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA achieves $0.3458$ ($3.2\times$ higher than TranAD and $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet) while reducing false positives by an order of magnitude ($130{,}237$ vs $1{,}047{,}144$). These results show that latent predictive architectures yield well-conditioned residual distributions that stabilize streaming threshold calibration, with physical inductive priors providing targeted performance gains in specialized dynamical regimes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments (such as the NASA Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) satellite, Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rovers, industrial IoT facilities, and wearable biometric sensor arrays), dozens to hundreds of continuous telemetry channels concurrently track electrical power buses, thermal gradients, mechanical vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. When sensor faults or operational breakdowns emerge at the edge, monitoring systems must identify dynamical anomalies before hardware damage occurs \cite{cuellar2024explainable}.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
From a physical perspective, multi-sensor telemetry reflects the state trajectory of an underlying continuous physical system $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$. However, attempting to model dynamical equations directly in raw observation space presents four main challenges on edge devices:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Numerical Instability in Empirical Differentiation:} Estimating instantaneous time derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ directly on discrete, noise-corrupted sensor streams amplifies high-frequency measurement noise, destabilizing numerical trajectory tracking.
    \item \textit{Partial Observability \& Unmeasured Internal States:} Remote sensing telemetry captures only a noisy surface projection of unmeasured thermodynamic, electrical, and environmental states, violating full-state observability.
    \item \textit{High-Dimensional Cross-Sensor Coupling:} In multi-sensor arrays ($K \ge 50$), cross-channel dependencies and non-linear interactions cannot be adequately represented by rigid functional forms in raw signal space.
    \item \textit{Non-Stationary Downlink \& Operational Drift:} Edge IoT and satellite systems undergo discrete operational mode transitions (e.g., orbital day/night thermal cycling, thruster firings, Parkinsonian gait freezing, cloud server garbage collection) that cause continuous distribution drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Deep learning architectures provide data-driven alternatives to explicit equation modeling, yet they face three primary limitations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Signal-Level Noise Memorization:} Generative models (e.g., autoencoders, VAEs, masked Transformers) minimize point-level $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}. Because reconstruction loss penalizes every discrepancy equally, autoencoders allocate capacity to fitting high-frequency sensor jitter rather than learning nominal dynamical transitions.
    \item \textit{Limitations of Synthetic Contrastive Perturbations:} Contrastive frameworks such as Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} train encoders via Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE) using heuristic synthetic perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise spikes, sequence splicing). However, real physical faults manifest as subtle deviations from nominal dynamics. Synthetic injections distort embedding geometry, inducing artificial decision boundaries that generate false alarms on continuous telemetry.
    \item \textit{Scale Distortion and Threshold Fragility in Observation Space:} In high-dimensional observation space, reconstruction residual magnitude is dominated by high-variance background sensors. Under automated streaming thresholding such as Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT), this distortion causes extreme-value calibrators to fit to ambient sensor noise rather than anomalous state changes, triggering false alarm flooding in operational telemetry.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Approach: Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures}
To eliminate signal-space noise memorization without relying on synthetic contrastive perturbations, we formulate a family of \textit{Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures} (PI-JEPA) for multi-sensor telemetry. Instead of reconstructing raw, jitter-corrupted sensor observations $\hat{x} \approx x$, a JEPA maps past observations $x_{\text{ctx}}$ into an abstract representation space and predicts target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ directly \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}. By operating in latent space, the architecture discards high-frequency stochastic sensor noise that lacks long-range predictability.

However, unconstrained latent prediction alone can be underspecified in multi-sensor telemetry. To incorporate physical domain priors into self-supervised representation learning, we introduce three physics-informed JEPA architectures featuring inductive dynamical regularizers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Mechanics):} Parameterizes a latent linear Koopman transition matrix and monitors spectral stability via normalized dynamic singular-value entropy. Discrepancies between predicted and target spectral profiles isolate anomalous damping and spectral decoherence.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Covariance Alignment):} Inspired by hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition ($z = \bar{z} + z'$), this architecture aligns the second-order cross-channel fluctuation covariance tensors $\mathbf{R}_{ij} = \mathbb{E}[z'_i z'_j]$. Structural anomalies disrupt multi-sensor covariance balance.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory):} Enforces conservative latent trajectory transitions by parameterizing the transition field as the gradient of a scalar potential ($\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$). Because gradient fields are irrotational by construction, non-conservative dynamical anomalies produce large predictive discrepancies.
\end{enumerate}

To stabilize learning across heterogeneous high-dimensional sensor arrays, all three architectures incorporate Coordinate Saliency Gating to isolate active channels, Dyadic Wavelet Shells to decouple high-frequency transients from baseline drift, and a Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) calibrator that modulates tail thresholds against non-Gaussian kurtosis.

\subsection{Contributions}
The contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Framework:} We formulate an unsupervised anomaly detection framework that embeds physical regularizers (spectral dissipation, turbulent momentum transfer, conservative flow) directly into latent representations, avoiding raw-space noise reconstruction.
    \item \textit{Physics-Informed JEPA Formulations:} We derive and implement three physics-informed architectures (\texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, and \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA}) and benchmark them alongside an unconstrained control (\texttt{TS-JEPA}).
    \item \textit{Inductive Architectural Components:} We introduce Coordinate Saliency Gating for cross-channel fault localization, Dyadic Wavelet decomposition for multi-resolution temporal analysis, and Kurtosis-Adaptive EVT tail calibration to provide automated streaming alarms without transductive test tuning.
    \item \textit{Multi-Tier Benchmark Evaluation:} We evaluate performance across 11 telemetry datasets (45 matched channels), reporting Dataset-Macro, Channel-Macro, Pooled Micro-F1, and Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) alongside an analytical All-Positive reference baseline. We show that latent prediction resolves threshold calibration collapse, achieving up to \textbf{+43.2\% relative improvement} in Dataset-Macro F1 and a \textbf{3.2$\times$ to 5.3$\times$ gain} in Pooled Micro-F1 while reducing false positives by an order of magnitude compared to reconstructive baselines.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Unsupervised time-series anomaly detection spans dynamical equation discovery, reconstructive generative modeling, contrastive representation learning, and predictive world models. This section reviews these approaches and positions our Physics-Informed JEPA framework.

\subsection{Dynamical Systems Modeling and Koopman Operators}
The physical modeling of multi-sensor telemetry has long sought to capture system behavior as continuous dynamical trajectories governed by differential equations $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}:} Parameterize continuous velocity fields using neural networks $\frac{dx}{dt} = f_\theta(x(t), t)$ and solve trajectories via numerical ODE integrators. While Neural ODEs learn flexible vector fields, fitting them directly in raw sensor space forces numerical solvers to integrate through high-frequency stochastic sensor noise, leading to numerical stiffness, slow training, and extreme sensitivity to measurement noise.
    \item \textit{Koopman Operator Theory and Dynamic Mode Decomposition \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa}:} Analyzes non-linear dynamical systems by learning linear transition operators acting on observable functions. Modern deep Koopman frameworks (e.g., Koopa \cite{liu2023koopa}) learn observable embeddings for non-stationary forecasting. In anomaly detection, physical disruptions manifest as spectral decoherence or sudden changes in modal dissipation. Rather than operating on uncalibrated raw signals, our framework parameterizes latent linear transitions and monitors dynamic spectral entropy in representation space.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reconstruction-Based Detection}
The predominant deep learning approach in time-series anomaly detection relies on learning nominal patterns through raw signal reconstruction. Hundman et al. \cite{Hundman2018b} introduced LSTM-based prediction coupled with non-parametric dynamic thresholding for NASA spacecraft telemetry (SMAP/MSL). Modern architectures incorporate specialized attention and frequency-domain transformations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}:} Introduces Anomaly-Attention to quantify the \textit{Association Discrepancy} between local prior Gaussian kernel associations and global series self-attention. While effective for isolated point anomalies, it couples discrepancy with point-level waveform reconstruction.
    \item \textit{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Decomposes 1D multivariate time series into 2D temporal variations using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periodicity discovery and 2D Inception blocks. However, optimizing point-level 2D reconstruction makes it susceptible to over-fitting high-frequency sensor noise.
    \item \textit{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Employs a two-phase adversarial Transformer with sub-series attention and focus scoring to amplify subtle reconstruction errors, but remains vulnerable to phase-drift in continuous dynamical systems.
\end{itemize}

Reconstructive methods share a common vulnerability: \textit{signal-level reconstruction memorization}. Because loss functions optimize point-level $L_1$ or $L_2$ differences in raw signal space, reconstructive networks are biased toward fitting high-frequency stochastic noise rather than learning invariant dynamical laws. In addition, during sustained anomalies, corrupted observations contaminate the context window, causing the reconstructor to adapt and reconstruct the anomaly as the "new normal."

\subsection{Contrastive Learning and Representation-Based Detection}
To avoid reconstructing raw noise, contrastive methods learn discriminative representations by separating nominal sequences from anomalous perturbations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}:} Uses Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) trained with Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE). Artificial synthetic anomalies (Gaussian noise, point spikes, trend shifts) are injected into training windows to create negative pairs. However, synthetic injections distort the physical dynamical manifold and fail to generalize to subtle real-world physical faults (e.g., gradual thermal dissipation or gait freezing).
    \item \textit{DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}:} Replaces reconstruction with multi-scale dual-attention (patch-wise and channel-wise) contrastive representation learning, maximizing cross-scale representation consistency. While DCdetector eliminates artificial injection heuristics, contrastive objectives require careful scale selection and representation pooling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Non-Contrastive Learning and Time-Series JEPAs}
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), originally proposed by LeCun \cite{lecun2022path} and demonstrated in computer vision by I-JEPA \cite{assran2023self} and V-JEPA \cite{bardes2024vjepa}, predict target representations from context in latent space. Non-contrastive regularizers such as Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, W-MSE \cite{ermolov2021whitening}, and VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg} prevent representation collapse without negative mining.

Recent works have extended joint-embedding predictive principles to time-series sequences:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Time-Series JEPAs:} SC-JEPA / MTS-JEPA \cite{he2026scjepa} stabilizes latent predictive learning for time-series anomaly prediction using a multi-scale soft-codebook dictionary. T-SAR-JEPA \cite{woldesenbet2026tsarjepa} applies self-supervised latent prediction to Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) amplitude time series. CF-JEPA \cite{lee2026cfjepa} employs mask-free forward prediction with asymmetric encoders for time-series representation learning. In cyber-physical and automotive domains, Girgis et al. \cite{girgis2026timeseries} explore time-series JEPAs for predictive remote control over capacity-constrained IoT channels, while Fertig et al. \cite{fertig2026online} investigate JEPA embeddings for online automotive sensor monitoring.
\end{itemize}
However, existing time-series JEPA adaptations rely on generic unconstrained Euclidean loss or discrete codebooks without physical domain regularizers, leaving latent trajectories unanchored to physical conservation principles.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Deep Learning and Structured Inductive Biases}
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics} integrate differential equations into deep models by penalizing residual PDE errors via automatic differentiation. While effective for forward and inverse problems in fluid and solid mechanics, standard PINNs face two primary limitations in high-dimensional sensor telemetry:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Gradient Pathologies in Multi-Objective Optimization:} Jointly optimizing data fidelity and PDE residual loss leads to stiff, competing gradient dynamics, often causing training instability or failure to converge in high noise regimes \cite{wang2021understanding}.
    \item \textit{Unobservable System Dynamics:} Complex IoT and aerospace telemetry (e.g., SMAP, MSL, and cyber-physical water networks) rarely follow a single closed-form PDE whose analytical derivatives can be evaluated across dozens of uncalibrated sensors.
\end{enumerate}
Rather than enforcing analytical differential equations through auxiliary training penalties, our framework incorporates physical conservation, turbulent transport, and spectral dissipation directly into the latent representations and coordinate projections. This embeds physical inductive constraints directly, avoiding multi-loss balancing conflicts.

\subsection{Positioning of the Proposed Framework}
The proposed Physics-Informed JEPA framework bridges joint-embedding predictive learning and physical domain mechanics through four distinct structural designs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Latent Predictive Forward Learning Without Reconstruction Traps:} By forecasting future representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ rather than raw observations $\hat{x}$, our models circumvent high-frequency noise drowning and sensor jitter memorization that hobble reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD).
    \item \textit{Physics-Informed Inductive Formulations:} We introduce three physics-informed inductive formulations: \texttt{Operator-Entropy} (Koopman spectral dissipation), \texttt{Reynolds-Stress} (turbulent momentum transport), and \texttt{Potential-Flow} (conservative irrotational field theory), addressing cyclic, turbulent, and conservative physical dynamics.
    \item \textit{Multi-Resolution and Saliency Stabilizers:} We incorporate Coordinate Saliency Gating to prevent active channel dilution in high-dimensional arrays, and Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelets to isolate transient impulses from low-frequency operational drift.
    \item \textit{Kurtosis-Adaptive Statistical Calibration:} We deploy Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} modulated by dynamic sample kurtosis, establishing decision boundaries on operational telemetry without transductive test-label tuning.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

To evaluate our Physics-Informed JEPA framework across diverse edge, cyber-physical, and orbital sensing dynamics, our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 strictly matched continuous telemetry channels sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the PhysioNet biomedical database \cite{bachlin2010wearable}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. We group these channels into four IoT and remote sensing domains:

\subsection{Domain 1: Satellite and Spacecraft Telemetry (18 Channels)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{NASA SMAP Earth Observation Satellite \cite{Hundman2018b} (8 evaluated channels):} Continuous power, thermal, and attitude telemetry from the Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) spacecraft tracking solar array currents, bus voltages, battery temperatures, and thruster status subject to radiation upsets and operational mode shifts.
    \item \textit{NASA MSL Curiosity Rover \cite{Hundman2018b} (9 evaluated channels):} Environmental and robotic actuator telemetry from the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rover monitoring drive motor currents, joint angles, arm kinematics, and power subsystem faults during planetary exploration.
    \item \textit{SWAN-SF Space Weather Telemetry \cite{angryk2020multivariate} (1 channel):} Solar photospheric vector magnetogram telemetry from NASA SDO/HMI tracking active region magnetic field perturbations during solar eruptive events.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Domain 2: Wearable Biometrics and Kinematics (9 Channels)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Daphnet Wearable Body Sensor Network \cite{bachlin2010wearable} (9 channels):} High-frequency tri-axial accelerometry from wearable body sensor nodes (placed on the shank, thigh, and trunk; 9 continuous dimensions per subject) tracking Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) breakdown events in Parkinson's patients.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Domain 3: Industrial IoT and Cyber-Physical Systems (8 Channels)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{GHL Industrial Separation Plant (6 channels):} Multi-phase gas-oil-water separation industrial process telemetry monitoring tank levels, flow rates, and valve control faults.
    \item \textit{Genesis Automation Testbed (1 channel):} Industrial manufacturing testbed telemetry tracking automated handling machinery faults.
    \item \textit{GECCO Water Distribution IoT (1 channel):} Physical water distribution process telemetry monitoring chemical and hydrological sensor measurements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Domain 4: Smart Infrastructure and Environmental Sensing (10 Channels)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Room-Occupancy Environmental IoT \cite{candanedo2016accurate} (2 channels):} Multi-sensor ambient environmental IoT sensing ($\text{CO}_2$, light, temperature, humidity) tracking room-occupancy state switches.
    \item \textit{CICIDS Edge Network Telemetry (6 channels):} High-throughput network flow telemetry capturing edge gateway protocol dynamics and security anomalies (DDoS bursts and port scans).
    \item \textit{CalIt2 Building People-Flow Telemetry \cite{asuncion2007uci} (1 channel):} Continuous pedestrian counts via optical beam sensors at the UC Irvine Calit2 facility entrance.
    \item \textit{Metro Highway Traffic (1 channel):} Interstate highway traffic volume and environmental condition telemetry.
\end{enumerate}

Each benchmark channel provides pre-split training sequences (consisting exclusively of nominal operational behavior) and testing sequences containing labeled ground-truth anomalies. Continuous numeric features are standardized via training statistics ($\bm{\mu}_{\text{train}}, \bm{\sigma}_{\text{train}}$), flooring standard deviations at $\max(\bm{\sigma}, 10^{-2})$ to prevent division by zero during flat-line operational phases.

This section formalizes the mathematical formulation and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Prediction Paradigm}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a continuous multivariate time-series sequence consisting of $K$ observed sensor variables over $T$ timesteps. The sequence is partitioned into historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect/target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, where $C$ and $S$ denote the respective temporal horizons.

Existing deep learning baselines (e.g., TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) frame anomaly detection as pointwise reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \mathbb{E}\left[ \| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In complex multi-sensor telemetry, ambient high-frequency stochastic jitter dominates the Frobenius norm, causing models to waste representational bandwidth reconstructing sensor noise rather than modeling system state dynamics (leading to noise drowning).

To overcome this, we adopt the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm \cite{lecun2022path}. A context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, updated via:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k, \quad m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995].
\end{equation}
The core objective is to predict target representations in latent space under physical dynamical constraints.

\subsection{The Physics-Informed JEPA Triad}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Rather than relying on unconstrained Euclidean regression, we incorporate physical domain regularizers through three complementary geometric formulations:

\subsubsection{1. Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Dynamics)}
In dynamical systems theory, non-linear trajectories can be analyzed via linear operators acting on observable functions. In our architecture, the encoder maps the observation window to a finite-dimensional latent observable vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^D$. We define the transition between context and target latent states via a learned transition matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}.
\end{equation}
To monitor spectral stability across modes, we evaluate the singular value spectrum $\sigma_i(\mathbf{K})$, which governs the energy amplification and contraction of the latent map. We define the normalized spectral entropy as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D \tilde{\sigma}_i \log \tilde{\sigma}_i, \quad \tilde{\sigma}_i = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sum_{j=1}^D \sigma_j + \epsilon}.
\end{equation}
During nominal periodic or quasi-periodic operation, energy is concentrated along leading spectral modes (low entropy). Non-stationary anomalies that disrupt modal coherence trigger sudden spectral diffusion. The Operator-Entropy JEPA training objective regularizes the linear predictive transition alongside spectral entropy:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{2. Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Covariance Alignment)}
In multi-channel telemetry representing coupled flows (thermal, convective, hydraulic), channel interactions are governed by transport phenomena. Inspired by the hydrodynamic \textit{Reynolds decomposition}, we decompose latent state vectors into a baseline component and dynamic fluctuations:
\begin{equation}
    z(t) = \bar{z}(t) + z'(t),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{z}(t) = \frac{1}{\tau}\int_0^\tau z(t-s)ds$ represents the rolling background state, and $z'(t)$ denotes temporal fluctuations. 

The inter-coordinate momentum transport is characterized by the second-moment fluctuation covariance matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R} = z'(t) {z'(t)}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}.
\end{equation}
Anomalies trigger turbulent shear divergence across latent coordinates, disrupting the nominal cross-channel covariance balance. The Reynolds-Stress JEPA loss aligns the predicted mean trajectory and target fluctuation covariance:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \| \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_R \| \mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} \|_F^2.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{3. Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
Under the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition theorem, any sufficiently smooth vector field can be uniquely resolved into an irrotational (curl-free) conservative potential gradient and a solenoidal (divergence-free) rotational field. 

In nominal physical equilibria, conservative dynamical systems conserve potential energy along phase trajectories. We parameterize the latent transition dynamics through the gradient of a scalar potential network $\Phi_\psi(z)$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{v}(z) = \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z), \quad \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = z_{\text{ctx}} + \hat{v}(z_{\text{ctx}}).
\end{equation}
Because the curl of the gradient of any $C^2$ scalar field is identically zero ($\nabla \times (\nabla \Phi) \equiv \mathbf{0}$), parameterizing the velocity field as $\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$ guarantees that the predicted trajectory field is \textit{strictly irrotational by construction}. Physical anomalies that introduce non-conservative work, dissipative friction, or unmodeled circulation diverge from any integrable potential field, producing large residual discrepancies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \| \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{ctx}}) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Sequential Window Mapping}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, sliding context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect target windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed causally with a stride of 1 timestep. The predictive core computes the latent discrepancy between the predicted target representation $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ and the target encoder representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(t) = \| \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}
For Operator-Entropy JEPA, the anomaly score is augmented by the relative spectral entropy generation $|\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}_{\text{tgt}}) - \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}_{\text{ctx}})|$. Each timestep $t$ is mapped directly to its calibrated anomaly score $\mathcal{S}(t)$.

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{1. Coordinate Saliency Gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 50$), anomalous signals frequently manifest in only a small subset of channels while remaining channels exhibit ambient noise. To prevent dimensional drowning, we introduce a parameter-free \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gate} within the encoder:
\begin{equation}
    s_k = \frac{\text{Var}_t(x_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \text{Var}_t(x_j) + \epsilon}, \quad \tilde{x}_k = x_k \cdot \sigma\left(\frac{s_k - \bar{s}}{\sigma_s}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic sigmoid, and $\bar{s}, \sigma_s$ denote the mean and standard deviation of channel variances. This dynamically prioritizes informative non-stationary coordinates without discarding quiet sensor channels.

\subsubsection{2. Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Real-world physical anomalies operate across disparate temporal scales, ranging from high-frequency electrical spikes to gradual thermal drifts. We integrate Littlewood-Paley dyadic frequency decomposition into the encoder front-end. The input sequence is decomposed into dyadic octave bands via bandpass filters $\psi_j(\omega) = \psi(2^j \omega)$ satisfying the partition of unity:
\begin{equation}
    |\phi(\omega)|^2 + \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} |\psi(2^j \omega)|^2 = 1, \quad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi].
\end{equation}
This multi-scale frequency decomposition enables the model to isolate high-frequency transient anomalies from low-frequency baseline diurnal oscillations without expanding parameter count.

\subsubsection{3. Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT)}
Following scoring, decision alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT). In standard SPOT, the extreme threshold $\tau$ is determined by fitting a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$ to excesses over an initial threshold $u$ at risk probability $q$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_u}{N}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right].
\end{equation}
To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via the empirical excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ increases, raising the threshold to suppress false alarms from volatile noise bursts; on light-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 < 0$), the threshold tightens to maximize detection sensitivity.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

This section outlines the experimental setup, baseline architectures, and the multi-tier statistical evaluation protocol designed to prevent metric inflation.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous, fair comparison across all models, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Sliding Window Geometry:} Past context duration $C=256$, suspect/target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across testing streams.
    \item \textit{Encoder Backbone:} Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted using a causal dilated Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) with 3 residual blocks, channel depth $F=48$, kernel size $K=5$, and exponentially increasing dilation factors $d \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ regularized by Layer Normalization and dropout ($p=0.20$).
    \item \textit{Target Encoder Update:} The target encoder parameters $\theta_{\text{tgt}}$ are updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum tracking: $\theta_{\text{tgt}} \leftarrow m \theta_{\text{tgt}} + (1-m) \theta_{\text{ctx}}$, with $m$ following a cosine schedule from $m_{\text{base}}=0.996$ to $m_{\text{max}}=0.9995$.
    \item \textit{Optimization Protocol:} AdamW optimizer with initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences.
    \item \textit{Statistical Calibration:} Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) calibration fits Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails to the top $2.0\%$ ($u=0.98$) of nominal training residuals under extreme risk parameter $q=10^{-3}$, dynamically modulated by the empirical sample kurtosis of the channel anomaly stream.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Unified Reimplementation}
To isolate algorithmic principles from external codebase discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline under identical windowing, standardization, and EVT calibration:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Multi-periodic reconstructive architecture that transforms 1D time series into 2D variation spaces via 1D-FFT periodic frequency discovery and 2D Inception blocks.
    \item \textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Adversarial reconstructive Transformer featuring two-stage self-conditioning and sub-series attention to amplify subtle reconstruction errors.
    \item \textbf{TS-JEPA (Unconstrained Control):} Standard Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture predicting target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without physical inductive priors, isolating the effect of latent-space forecasting.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Informed Formulations:} Three physics-informed architectures (\texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, and \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA}) incorporating spectral Koopman dissipation, turbulent momentum transport, and conservative curl-free potential constraints respectively.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Recent critical surveys in time-series anomaly detection \cite{kim2022towards, schmidl2022anomaly, wu2023current} demonstrate that standard evaluation protocols are plagued by severe reporting vulnerabilities, including point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, and arbitrary threshold gaming. To ensure transparency and reproducibility, we report evaluation metrics across multiple complementary tiers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (Primary Metric):} We compute the unweighted arithmetic mean of Point-F1 scores across datasets:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Macro-F1}_{\text{DS}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|} \sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \left( \frac{1}{|C_d|} \sum_{c \in C_d} \text{F1}_{c} \right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{D}$ is the set of benchmark datasets and $C_d$ is the set of channels in dataset $d$. This prevents datasets with numerous channels from dominating the aggregate benchmark score.
    \item \textbf{Pooled Micro Point-F1:} We pool all raw True Positives, False Positives, and False Negatives globally across all test timesteps:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Micro-F1} = \frac{2 \sum_{c} \text{TP}_c}{2 \sum_c \text{TP}_c + \sum_c \text{FP}_c + \sum_c \text{FN}_c}
    \end{equation}
    Micro-F1 directly penalizes models that flood long streams with hundreds of thousands of false alarms, exposing reconstructive noise drowning.
    \item \textbf{Channel-Macro Point-F1:} The standard per-channel average $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \text{F1}_i$ across all evaluated channels.
    \item \textbf{Precision-Recall Area Under the Curve (PR-AUC):} Average Precision computed over all possible continuous anomaly score thresholds:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{PR-AUC} = \sum_{k} (R_k - R_{k-1}) P_k
    \end{equation}
    PR-AUC evaluates raw score ranking capability entirely independent of the threshold calibration mechanism.
    \item \textbf{Trivial All-Positive Reference Baseline:} We report the analytical performance of a constant all-positive predictor ($\hat{y}_t = 1, \forall t$):
    \begin{equation}
        \text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = \frac{2 \cdot P}{2 \cdot P + N} = \frac{2 \pi}{1 + \pi}
    \end{equation}
    where $\pi = P / (P+N)$ is the anomaly base rate. Any model failing to exceed $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}$ provides less discriminative value than an uncalibrated constant alarm.
    \item \textbf{Contiguous Point-Adjusted F1 (PA-F1):} Standard segment adjustment where detecting any single anomaly point expands to the entire contiguous anomaly segment. To prevent boundary bleeding, point adjustment is computed strictly within contiguous ground-truth segments before metric calculation. We also report the \textit{Test-Sweep PA-F1} (best post-hoc threshold scan) to quantify the remaining calibration gap.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

To isolate the individual contributions of each mathematical and architectural component in our Physics-Informed JEPA framework, we conduct systematic ablation experiments across representative multi-domain telemetry types:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{High-Dimensional Aerospace Telemetry (NASA SMAP / MSL, $K \ge 50$):} High-dimensional sensor pods with strong inter-channel thermal and electrical cross-coupling.
    \item \textit{Periodic Biomechanical Kinematics (Daphnet FoG):} Fast multi-axial accelerometry exhibiting repeating limit-cycle dynamics and abrupt gait freezing.
    \item \textit{Transient Cyber-Physical Hydraulics (GECCO / SWAN):} Continuous environmental and hydrodynamic streams with sharp, turbulent anomalies.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physics-Informed Inductive Biases vs. Unconstrained Prediction}
Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) isolate the impact of embedding physical domain constraints into latent representations compared to unconstrained latent forecasting (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and observation-space reconstruction (\texttt{TimesNet}, \texttt{TranAD}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Reconstructive Observation Space:} Reconstructive models suffer from severe false alarm flooding (TimesNet accumulates over $10^6$ false positives, yielding a Pooled Micro-F1 of only $0.0647$).
    \item \textit{Unconstrained Latent Prediction (\texttt{TS-JEPA}):} Moving prediction to latent space eliminates noise memorization, lifting Micro-F1 to $0.2860$ and achieving $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro F1 with $0.3449$ PA-F1.
    \item \textit{Physics-Informed Formulations:} \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} attains the highest Dataset-Macro F1 ($0.2066$) and Channel-Macro F1 ($0.1743$), while \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} achieves the highest overall Micro-F1 ($0.3458$) and reduces false alarms by 8-fold ($130{,}237$ vs $1{,}047{,}144$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Coordinate Saliency Gating in High-Dimensional Pods}
In high-dimensional telemetry ($K \ge 50$), anomalous faults typically originate in a localized subsystem (e.g., a single valve or battery cell). In standard latent pooling:
\begin{equation}
    z_{\text{pool}} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K z_k
\end{equation}
the localized anomalous signal is diluted by ambient normal noise across the remaining 49 quiescent channels. 

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b), disabling Coordinate Saliency Gating ($\mathbf{g}_t = \mathbf{1}$) causes a substantial drop in Point-F1 on SMAP ($0.285 \to 0.198$) and MSL ($0.265 \to 0.174$). The saliency gate $\mathbf{g}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_g \text{Var}_t + \mathbf{b}_g)$ dynamically isolates high-variance, non-stationary coordinates, suppressing ambient quiescent dimensions and maintaining high signal-to-noise ratio.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Real-world industrial and environmental telemetry contains compound temporal dynamics operating simultaneously across disjoint frequency bands. For instance, in municipal water infrastructure (\texttt{GECCO}), sensors record 24-hour diurnal consumption cycles alongside sub-second hydraulic valve transients.

Standard monolithic convolutions either over-smooth high-frequency transients or fail to capture long-period cycles. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), replacing standard convolutions with Littlewood-Paley dyadic wavelet projections:
\begin{equation}
    z^{(j)} = \Psi_j \ast x, \quad j \in \{0, 1, \dots, J-1\}
\end{equation}
improves Point-F1 on \texttt{GECCO} from $0.1429$ to $0.2904$ on \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, while lifting Point-Precision on \texttt{Daphnet} accelerometry from $0.245$ to $0.342$. Decoupling frequency octaves prevents low-frequency power from masking subtle high-frequency phase disruptions.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Kurtosis-Adaptive vs. Static Extreme Value Calibration}
Standard Extreme Value Theory (SPOT) fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution to residuals exceeding an initial quantile $u$. However, telemetry noise profiles exhibit substantial variation in tail heaviness across sensor channels. In leptokurtic distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$, heavy tails), static thresholds trigger frequent false alarms; in platykurtic distributions ($\gamma_2 < 0$), static thresholds miss low-amplitude structural faults.

Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d) compares our kurtosis-adaptive EVT calibrator against static calibration baselines:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Static 3-Sigma Gaussian Threshold:} Yields poor detection ($0.082$ Macro Pt-F1) due to pervasive non-Gaussianity and heavy tails in raw telemetry residuals.
    \item \textit{Standard EVT (Unweighted SPOT):} Achieves $0.174$ Macro Pt-F1, but suffers from false positive bursts during non-stationary operational phases.
    \item \textit{Kurtosis-Adaptive EVT (Ours):} By scaling the effective tail threshold $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left(1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)\right)$, the calibrator raises decision margins during heavy-tailed regimes while sharpening sensitivity on calm channels, achieving $0.2066$ Macro Pt-F1.
\end{enumerate}

To isolate the contribution of the physical inductive biases from the predictive latent paradigm itself, we evaluate \texttt{ts\_jepa} as an unconstrained control. \texttt{ts\_jepa} achieves $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro Point-F1, $0.1442$ Channel-Macro Point-F1, and $0.3066$ Channel PA-F1. While substantially outperforming reconstructive baselines (+27.4\% in Dataset-Macro F1 over TimesNet), it trails \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} in Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.1838$ vs $0.2066$) and Channel Point-F1 ($0.1442$ vs $0.1743$), and trails \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} in Micro-F1 ($0.2860$ vs $0.3458$) and Channel PA-F1 ($0.3066$ vs $0.3436$). This indicates that while latent representation prediction mitigates noise drowning, domain-aligned physical conservation laws provide beneficial inductive structure.

\subsection{Impact of Architectural Enhancements}
Ablation of the three internal inductive layers confirms their collective role in stabilizing high-dimensional inference:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gating:} On high-dimensional multi-sensor streams ($K \ge 50$ in NASA SMAP and MSL telemetry), unweighted latent pooling causes informative anomalous dimensions to drown in ambient channel noise. Saliency gating dynamically prioritizes high-variation informative channels, enabling \texttt{reynolds\_stress\_jepa} and \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} to detect localized subsystem faults across complex sensor arrays without manual channel sub-selection.
    \item \textit{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells:} Multi-scale temporal decomposition within the encoder allows the networks to isolate fast transient spikes from low-frequency baseline drifts. On \texttt{GECCO} (water infrastructure with simultaneous daily consumption cycles and high-frequency valve faults), dyadic filtering doubled \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} performance from $0.1429$ to $0.2904$ Point-F1.
    \item \textit{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory:} Real-world anomaly score tails frequently deviate from standard Gaussianity. By dynamically scaling the EVT threshold via excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$, the calibrator suppresses false alarms during high-entropy volatile bursts while boosting overall PA-Recall to $67\%-71\%$ across the benchmark.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented a Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (PI-JEPA) framework for unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate sensor telemetry. By replacing raw observation-space reconstruction with forward prediction in latent representation space, our approach eliminates the threshold calibration collapse that causes reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) to flood continuous streams with hundreds of thousands of false alarms under ambient sensor noise.

We embedded physical domain principles directly into latent representations through three formulations:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA:} Quantifying spectral stability and dissipation via dynamic spectral entropy across latent Koopman transition modes.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA:} Aligning cross-channel second-moment fluctuation covariance tensors inspired by hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA:} Enforcing conservative, irrotational trajectory dynamics by parameterizing the transition field as a scalar potential gradient.
\end{enumerate}
Together with Coordinate Saliency Gating, Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells, and Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory tail calibration, the proposed framework stabilizes streaming threshold calibration. Across 11 telemetry datasets (45 matched channels) evaluated under identical protocols, Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves the highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.2066$, a \textbf{+43.2\% relative improvement} over TimesNet and \textbf{+28.4\%} over TranAD) and clears the analytical all-positive baseline ($0.1893$). In Pooled Micro-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA attains \textbf{0.3458} (over $3.2\times$ higher than TranAD and $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet) while reducing false alarms by \textbf{8-fold}.

Future work includes:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Non-Conservative Field Extensions:} Relaxing strict irrotational constraints with parameter-controlled solenoidal vorticity components for dissipative systems with limit cycles.
    \item \textit{Continuous Online Streaming Adaptation:} Developing memory-efficient, test-time momentum adaptation to track orbital thermal cycles and environmental seasonal drift without catastrophic forgetting.
    \item \textit{Multi-Seed Uncertainty Quantification:} Scaling evaluations across extensive multi-seed benchmarks with confidence intervals and range-based evaluation metrics (e.g., VUS-PR).
    \item \textit{Edge Hardware Quantization and Acceleration:} Compiling PI-JEPA encoders into 8-bit integer (INT8) formats for deployment on radiation-hardened spacecraft microcontrollers and ultra-low-power wearable edge devices.
\end{itemize}
