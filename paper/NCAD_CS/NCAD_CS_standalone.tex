\begin{abstract}
Monitoring spacecraft and industrial machines demands fast fault detection without constant false alarms, yet waveform reconstructors fire often on jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) predict compact learned summaries instead, though single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one threshold. We combine multi-horizon residuals, coordinate whitening, per-coordinate tripwires, and a quorum. Underlying score ranking matches scalar JEPAs. Instead, the principal contribution is automated label-free operating-point selection across streams fitting only on their healthy prefix. Across 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets, unscreened Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA and cuts stream-paired co-detected onset latency by 74.6 steps (41.4 on shared segments). However, mean held-out false-positive rate (FPR) rises from 4.4 to 6.4 percent and prospective test FPR rises from 8.8 to 17.0 percent. Overall Point-F1 shifts from 0.076 to 0.086 across datasets and trails 1D at 1 percent test FPR, while TimesNet and TranAD need 24.6 to 25.6 percent held-out FPR for higher F1. Three-stage screening accepts 46 streams at 6.8 percent mean test FPR (2.9 percent median). Routing the rest yields a complete hybrid that trades 10.0 vs. 8.8 percent test FPR for 59.4 percent ER and 0.091 Point-F1, plus quarantined recalibration.
\end{abstract}

Anomaly detection, Multivariate time series, Representation learning, Self-supervised learning, Statistical process control.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace scalar scoring with an integrated multi-horizon circuit-breaker system, like a breaker box in which each circuit trips its own switch regardless of the total load. Across dyadic prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, whitened coordinates and their cross-block mean receive individual healthy tripwires and fire when a correlation-aware quorum trips together. Under matched calibration and operational scaling, wider variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), automatically shifting the label-free operating point toward higher coverage when scalar calibration under-triggers, without claiming superior intrinsic ranking (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational compromise): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $128$ position-plus-drift dials within $0.97$ megabytes (MB) FP32 under one quorum (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224-dial reference): monitors $M=4$ horizons across $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays; \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points against this reference for lower mean and buffered false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our primary contribution is automated label-free operating-point selection across a complete multi-horizon detector and gated workflow, supported by two motivating propositions. Each stream fits to its own healthy prefix ($K\in[2,55]$), testing zero-tune architecture transfer across sensor suites (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Label-free operating-point selection and paired latency:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} states idealized matched-scale equivariance before upstream coupling and additive floors. Across 118 untouched streams, unscreened \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} shifts the operating point over 1D JEPA, lifting causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$). Stream-paired co-detected onset latency drops by $74.6$ steps ($41.4$ on exact shared segments; conditional means $133.5\to 69.0$). Higher recall raises mean held-out FPR ($4.4\%\to 6.4\%$, $0.93\%$ median) and prospective test FPR ($8.8\%\to 17.0\%$), trading precision ($0.165\to 0.146$) for recall ($0.134\to 0.205$). Overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) drops on three of nine datasets with cluster confidence intervals (CIs) crossing zero, gains for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($p=0.047$), and trails 1D in Point-F1 at $1\%$ test FPR ($0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$).
    \item \emph{Whitening structure and prospective trade-off:} Horizon-preserving Kronecker zero-phase component analysis (ZCA) matches dense ZCA across $118$ streams ($+0.010$ F1, CI $[-0.012,+0.027]$, $p=0.76$; three-seed $[-0.006,+0.029]$), while consistent principal component analysis (PCA) recovers the causal raw-axis blending drop ($0.086$ vs.\ $0.090$ F1, $p=0.33$). Archive-informed two-stage ($n=52$, $11.0\%$ test FPR) and drift-gated three-stage ($n=46$, $D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$, $6.8\%$ mean and $2.9\%$ median test FPR) rules are verified via nine-fold nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) screening. The complete 118-stream three-stage hybrid trades $10.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$ test FPR ($12.9$ vs.\ $8.8$ episodes per $1{,}000$ steps) against complete 1D SPOT for $59.4\%$ vs.\ $52.5\%$ ER and $0.091$ vs.\ $0.076$ F1. Healthy-block permutation cuts archive FPR by $92.3\%$.
    \item \emph{External baselines and triage:} Under SPOT, TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1) reach higher raw F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ held-out FPR, falling to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR vs.\ $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}. CPU streaming sustains $93\text{--}141$ windows/s within $128\,\text{KB}$, and leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) masking hits $85.7\%$ Top-1 synthetic subset sensitivity.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds error energy with a null reweighting effect, while serial dependence leaves $105$ of $118$ streams without tail guarantees. Auxiliary channel guards raise clean FPR ($6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$), and frozen-reference drift on slow industrial ramps yields $74.4\%$ test FPR.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically reconstruct windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$. Long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, and Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)-period TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} fit raw jitter, causing heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) channel-sum spikes across $K \ge 25$ channels under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Contrastive encoders \cite{zhang2023self} including temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} rely on synthetic perturbations matching test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target latents $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without decoding, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn physical laws, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties suffer stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}; we borrow Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as residual algebra. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse coordinates into one scalar, as do extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Coordinate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and split-conformal Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) rules \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ floor on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Evaluations omit point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy history followed by a labeled test segment. Two cohorts play distinct roles. From a 45-stream development pool across 11 datasets, 33 qualifying streams formed the primary development cohort, while 141 archive streams across 12 datasets (118 never inspected during design) provide the primary evaluation.

\noindent\emph{Development pool and qualifying cohort:} Drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}, the 45-stream pool spans $K\in[2,72]$ channels across three domains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar:} six SMAP ($K{=}25$) and six MSL ($K{=}55$) streams divided by NASA into out-of-range \emph{point} and in-range \emph{contextual} faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, plus SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$).
    \item \emph{Wearables:} six $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes, where $S{=}64$ spans $1\,\text{s}$).
    \item \emph{Industrial and infrastructure:} 14 slow-ramp Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), Genesis ($K{=}18$), GECCO ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three qualifying streams ($19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL across $7$ datasets, $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$, $K\le 55$, $\le 1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps) form the primary dev cohort (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), while short or $K{=}72$ streams stay aside for archive tests.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The 141-stream archive spans 12 datasets ($\ge 100$ calibration windows and one fault; $1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ healthy steps): SMAP ($47$), Daphnet ($22$), MSL ($18$), SMD ($18$), Exathlon ($17$), OPPORTUNITY ($13$), plus single-stream GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN. Excluding the $23$ tuning streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across nine sets ($37$ zero-tuning external streams, $1$ GECCO stream, and $80$ unseen streams from tuned sets; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(b)).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and caveats:} Each stream fits only on its own nominal prefix, transferring the frozen architecture, damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum across $K\in[2,55]$ without cross-asset weights (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Because SMAP and MSL contain labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed} and same-facility streams share hardware, evaluations combine dataset-clustered inference ($7$ dev, $9$ archive sets), nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) tuning, and 9-fold exclusion sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-7pt}
\caption{End-to-end streaming pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($128$ dials across $M{=}3$ prefixes) and the \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reference ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays across $M{=}4$ prefixes). Raw telemetry windows pass through online and EMA target encoders (Stage~1), parallel multi-horizon prediction and coordinate whitening (Stage~2), nonnegative position-plus-drift dial expansion (Stage~3), and a healthy-calibrated quorum alarm (Stage~4).\label{fig:triad_architecture}}

\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Stage~1 compresses each raw telemetry window into a bounded $32$-number summary while retaining both slow baselines and fast transients. A length-$320$ window advances one step at a time, splitting at index $t$ into a $256$-step context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and a $64$-step target $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ whose causal window timestamp is $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ channel mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features split into four smooth dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose weights sum to one at every frequency, pass through depthwise causal convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombine via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN, dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$) \cite{bai2018empirical}. Streams with $K < 25$ feed directly into the TCN. For short targets ($T<S$), the TCN adapts dilations to $\min(2^\ell,\max(1,\lfloor T/8\rfloor))$, bypasses the depthwise stem at $T<3$ and the rFFT block at $T<4$, and zeros standard deviation and first differences at $T=1$.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and exponential moving average (EMA) target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$, momentum $m = 0.996$, or $0.995$ on 1D controls) map each window to $D=32$ coordinates without collapsing across $10\text{--}20$ epochs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. To capture both waveform level and roughness, each encoder summarizes TCN features via three temporal statistics (last step, max, and mean) and three variability statistics (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, and mean absolute first difference), projected through $d_{\text{head}}$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads ($d_{\text{head}}=32$ in canonical models; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)). A final affine-free LayerNorm projects embeddings onto the $\sqrt{D}$-sphere to tame heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$), though dividing by total feature variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines by $O(1/\sqrt{KD})$ (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two mechanisms preserve sharp edges and remove static offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Deep TCN layers smooth out sudden spikes, so both encoders also read the front-end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, blending mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) features ($\mathbb{R}^{288}\to d_{\text{head}}\to 32$) into the output at weight $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} Subtracting a running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, copied into $E_\phi$) before terminal LayerNorm removes constant training offsets. However, post-calibration regime shifts still require live monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction and Residual Whitening}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Predicting a single $64$-step target summary, as in the baseline \emph{TS-JEPA} (a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a $\beta=0.05$ log-determinant term \cite{bardes2021vicreg}), averages out brief transients. Instead, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ nested target prefixes from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$ through one parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP behind a single terminal LayerNorm, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} adds $S_3=48$ ($M=4$). For general target length $S$ at $M=3$, prefix lengths clamp to integers $S_m = \min(S, l_m)$ with $(l_1,l_2,l_3)=(\max(2,\lfloor S/4\rfloor),\max(4,\lfloor S/2\rfloor),S)$, generating nested $\{S/4,S/2,S\}$ horizons for $S\ge 16$, merging to $\{2,4,4\}$ at $S=4$, and collapsing to single-step targets ($\{1,1,1\}$, or four ones for $M=4$) at $S=1$. Weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$, the training loss pairs MSE with a batch anti-collapse term $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Acting at batch size $B=16<D=32$ without log-determinant inversion, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ pushes each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma=0.75$ via diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d)}$ and penalizes off-diagonal sample covariance $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ (divisor $B-1$):
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 ,
\end{equation}
raising target effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

Raw prediction errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ ($\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$) have unequal variances and correlate across coordinates, so Stage~2 centers and decorrelates them via damped symmetric ZCA whitening. Flattening $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($96$ at $M{=}3$, $128$ at $M{=}4$), we fit its mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and unbiased sample covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^\top$ (divisor $N_{\text{fit}}-1$) on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Clamping $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$ defines damped precision $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ and symmetric root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_k}/(\lambda_k + 10^{-3})\bigr)\mathbf{U}^\top$. On the $66$ of $118$ archive streams where $N_{\text{fit}} < 96$ leaves $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}$ rank-deficient, resolvent damping maps zero eigenvalues to exact zeros along unobserved directions, whereas a shared $D{\times}D$ Kronecker factor pools $3N_{\text{fit}} \ge 96$ samples.

Unlike eigenbasis PCA whitening, symmetric ZCA minimizes rotation away from input $(m,c)$ axes, keeping entrywise correlation $r=0.64\text{--}0.67$ with raw errors (vs.\ $r\approx 0$ for eigenbasis PCA). Because nested prefixes overlap in time, $29.2\%\text{--}31.3\%$ of $\|\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}\|_F^2$ couples across horizon blocks ($m\neq m'$), making reshaped $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ \emph{ZCA-anchored coordinates}. Replacing $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ with shared Kronecker ZCA $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$ removes cross-horizon mixing, commutes with horizon averaging, and matches dense ZCA across all $118$ archive streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Coordinates whose stabilized norm on disjoint $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$ shrink toward index-matched marginals $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=(e_{m,c}(t)-\bar{\mu}_{\text{res},m,c})/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$, frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (strictly within horizon under Kronecker ZCA; predictor-row quenching Q1--Q4 gave no archive gain and is omitted):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
    w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 3: Coordinate Dial Expansion and Horizon Algebra}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Splitting $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t)$ \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} into cross-block mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ ($r=0.63\text{--}0.70$ with pure horizon drift; exact under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$ expands whitened errors into $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
(a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{ZCA-anchored positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
(b)~$D = 32$ \emph{cross-block drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
(c)~$D = 32$ \emph{block fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$; and
(d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} states two algebraic properties of any reshaped $M\times D$ matrix (exact horizon energy under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$): mean and fluctuation partition squared energy orthogonally, and $\mathbf{F}(t)$ spans at most $M-1$ modes.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ (so the RMS horizon error equals $\sqrt{|\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)}$, not $|\bar{e}_c(t)| + d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$) and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
In plain terms, multivariate residual scoring faces three design options across $J$ coordinates or dials $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$. Global quadratic aggregation $S_{\text{glob}}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$ sums all coordinates into one threshold, whereas a marginal union $\max_j \mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$ compounds per-coordinate false alarms across $J$ tests. A correlation-aware coordinate quorum instead assigns each dial its own quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and sets a joint trip-count threshold $K_q$ based on healthy calibration data. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} states a multiplicative scale-equivariance property motivating this choice: when $k\ll J$ ``hijacker'' coordinates share a persistent post-training scale factor across both $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and operational data, global quadratic thresholds grow unboundedly while per-coordinate quantile tripwires stay invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Matched-Scale Quorum Equivariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$ denote nominal calibration and operational residual vectors, and assume a matched positive diagonal scaling $\mathbf{S}_\eta = \operatorname{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_J)$ with $s_j=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta\ge 0$) on a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k=|\mathcal{H}|<J$ coordinates and $s_j=1$ for $j\notin\mathcal{H}$ acts jointly on both calibration and operational data ($\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}=\mathbf{S}_\eta\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbf{S}_\eta\mathbf{u}(t)$):
(i)~under global scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-calibration quantile satisfies $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2\bigr)$, growing unboundedly in $\eta^2$ so any live fault $\mathbf{u}(t^*)$ supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected when $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous tripwires $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}|)$, every operational binary trip $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)})$ is invariant to $\eta$ for all $\eta\ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate dependence, leaving $N_{\text{cal}}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J b_{j,\text{cal}}(t)$, $K_q=\kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$, and $N(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J b_j(t)$ unchanged and guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $\sum_{j=1}^J (u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2$ pointwise, monotonicity and positive homogeneity of quantiles give~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ yields $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}(t^*))=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2\le\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity gives $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=s_j\,\tau_j^{(0)}$ for all $j$; dividing $|s_j u_j(t)|>s_j\tau_j^{(0)}$ by $s_j>0$ yields $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=b_j^{(0)}(t)$ identically on both $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and operational $t$, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$, $K_q$, and $N(t)$, while $m$ active coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ yield $N(t^*)\ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration and scope limits:} For $J=32$ standard normal coordinates at $\alpha=0.005$ and $\beta=0.015$, matched $\eta=5$ scaling on $k=2$ hijackers raises $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$, masking a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault ($144.4 < 252.4$) while $K_q=3.5$ stays invariant and trips $10 > 3.5$. Three practical qualifications separate this idealized property from the deployed detector. First, (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) adds a fixed $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor ($\tau_j = q_{0.985} + 10^{-4}$), breaking exact scale equivariance near threshold when $s_j \ne 1$ since $s_j q_{0.985}+10^{-4} \ne s_j(q_{0.985}+10^{-4})$; switching to multiplicative $\tau_j = q_{0.985}(1+10^{-4})$ restores exact positive homogeneity and agrees with (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) on $99.96\%$ of healthy test steps ($|\Delta\text{ER}|=0.055\%$, Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}). Second, upstream LayerNorm, ZCA whitening, (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) shrinkage, and horizon averaging couple raw channels prior to $\mathbf{d}(t)$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} describes diagonal scaling on formed dials rather than raw sensors. Third, if variance shifts \emph{after} $\tau_j$ freeze on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, or if bias or covariance rotates, $b_j(t)$ is not invariant, requiring empirical screening and quarantined recalibration (Section~\ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}), while detection requires multi-dial coverage ($m > K_q$).

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) implements this quorum on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ using linear quantile interpolation. Because dials share the $M \times 32$ error matrix, trips correlate strongly (median effective rank $65/224$; count variance $7.2\times$ binomial independence), so quorums are fitted directly to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus an additive $10^{-4}$ floor (giving approximate rather than exact scale equivariance near zero):
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 separate onset relays:} When a sudden fault enters the target window's right edge while $m \in \{1,2\}$ still see healthy data, coordinate $c$ drives an auxiliary binary relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ (excluded from the $224$-dial vector $\mathbf{d}(t)$) under $q_{0.993} + 10^{-4}$, firing when $m=4$ position trips jointly with endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$.
    \item \emph{Causal target-window offset vs.\ onset latency:} For target window $[t,t+S)$, live \emph{causal attribution} emits its decision strictly at the right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ without lookahead. Relative to window start $t$, this creates a fixed $S-1=63$-step window offset rather than a mandatory $63$-step fault delay: a fault starting at $t^*$ enters the right edge at $\tau_{\min}=t^*$ (zero minimum post-onset lag), and reported onset-to-alarm latency $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^* \ge 0$ can be below $63$ steps on sharp faults. Retrospective \emph{buffered attribution} maps block trips across $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation across $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint across $[t+S_3,t+S)$.
    \item \emph{Quorum decision:} Summing the $224$ dials gives $N_{224}(\tau)$ (with $128$-dial position count $N_{128}(\tau)$, and $N_{224}(\tau)$ used for ranking checks), while summing the $32$ relays gives $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. An alarm fires when any count crosses its healthy quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with calibration quorums $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$ (linear quantile interpolation, strict inequalities).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both archive tiers share the $32$D-head backbone (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)) with four TCN blocks ($\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), depthwise rFFT shells at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbb{R}^{32} \to 64 \to 32$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and $96$ position plus $32$ drift dials under $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$.
    \item \emph{High-recall $7D=224$ reference (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) with $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}); dev tables also show its $5$-block sequential predecessor (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite, Gated Fallback, and Quarantine\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Routed alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active dial count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\State Partition $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$ or $S_{1:4}{=}\{16,32,48,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), linear-quantile tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (or $(K_{224},K_{128},K_{\text{edge}})$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum})).
\State Screen frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; if $N_{\text{cal}}{<}500$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}{>}0.05$, or (strict tier) $D_{\text{KS}}{>}0.50$, route stream to 1D SPOT fallback.
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t){=}\mathbb{I}(d_j(t){>}\tau_j)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (causal) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (buffered); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ when active count exceeds quorum.
    \State Monitor sliding $D_{\text{KS}}$; if $D_{\text{KS}}{\ge}0.50$, quarantine $400$ windows under frozen thresholds until mode switch is confirmed.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (RMS of full-tensor and peak-horizon scores, $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$) blend $\ell_2$ norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\subsection{Setup and Calibration Protocol}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures use context $C=256$, target horizon $S=64$ ($W=320$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), latent dimension $D=32$, nominal training stride $16$, and evaluation stride $1$. Models are trained with AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) and zero per-dataset tuning. Development cohorts train at batch $16$ for $15\text{--}20$ fixed epochs, while archive cohorts train at batch $16\text{--}64$ for up to $12$ early-stopped epochs with gradient clipping ($1.0$) and cosine annealing.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
To prevent threshold leakage, each stream's healthy history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ splits chronologically into five target-disjoint blocks separated by $64$-step isolation gaps (allowing past-context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ overlap across block cuts unless widened to $W=320$; Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}), freezing all weights and thresholds before evaluation:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Fitting, selection, and stabilization):} Reusing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for early stopping or stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) biases tripwires downward. In the primary archive, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits channel scaling, encoder-predictor weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the early-stopped checkpoint; and $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($160\text{--}2{,}048$ windows) freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched. Fixed-epoch development suites use $65\%$ for training, paired with a disjoint $4$-block check ($50\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$) in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Calibration and serial dependence):} The final $35\%$ of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ splits in half across a $64$-step gap into $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$. Widening all post-fit gaps to $W=320$ across $49$ eligible streams preserves causal ER ($+0.0004$), Point-F1 ($+0.0010$), and precision ($+0.0004$, all $p\ge 0.66$). Held-out FPR shifts by $+0.84$ points ($p=0.11$; post-$\mathbf{X}_q$ $n=60$: $\Delta\text{F1}=+0.0004$). Under independence, $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ windows would yield $2.5$ expected exceedances above $q_{0.995}$. Because sliding windows correlate strongly ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$ via Sokal's $c=5$), median effective sample size drops to $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}} = 65$, or $0.325$ independent exceedances, making $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ an empirical operating cutoff rather than a statistical tail guarantee (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}).

\paragraph{Statistical vs.\ Practical Significance}
Seeds average within each stream first, with seed standard deviation (SD) $\le 0.008$ on causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER, treating the stream as the unit of analysis. We report both \emph{dataset-clustered inference}, using $95\%$ cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) and exact $2^D$ sign-flip tests, and stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests ($2{,}000\text{--}4{,}000$ resamples) under Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} across the four primary contrasts (\emph{Lite} vs.\ 1D and TS-JEPA on F1 and ER). Generalization checks separate zero-tuning datasets from partially tuned sets and combine nested LODO hyperparameter selection with 9-fold exclusion sensitivity (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Cohorts, Baseline Fairness, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Causal and buffered splits align across two cohorts: the \emph{development cohort} spans the 19-stream residual cache ($6$ sets; Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:matched_alert}) and 33-stream primary suite ($7$ sets; Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), with hyperparameters frozen once on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, and \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}). Excluding those $23$ streams leaves the $118$-stream archive across nine datasets (Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit}).

Baselines span three tiers: \emph{matched-backbone controls} (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon 1D, and Wu--Keogh cycle-swap, phase-desync, and noise checks vs.\ $\max_c|x_c(t)|$ \cite{wu2023flawed}); \emph{system aggregators} scoring frozen residuals; and \emph{external baselines} covering TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}, with Linear PCA, TranAD, and TimesNet also run across all $118$ untouched streams ($3$ seeds, SPOT $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$; code at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}).

\paragraph{Protocol Chronology and Archive Provenance}
To distinguish pre-specified design from archive-informed policy, choices fall into three stages:
(1)~\emph{Frozen before first archive run} ($23$ tuning streams and dev checks): backbone, $M{=}3$ prefixes, damping $10^{-3}$, (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) cutoff $1.10$, and $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$, alongside tripwire $q_{0.985}$, quorum ($\kappa{=}1.12$, floor $3.5$), and live monitor $D_{\text{KS}}{\ge}0.50$.
(2)~\emph{Post-hoc archive diagnostics} (frozen detector unchanged): block permutation, matched budgets, eight-arm PCA/ZCA checks, $W{=}320$ gap checks, and multiplicative floors.
(3)~\emph{Archive-informed routing policies} added after observing archive tail drift: pre-deployment $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}{\le}5\%$ and $D_{\text{KS}}{\le}0.50$ gates to 1D SPOT fallback, cross-checked via nine-fold nested LODO screening (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Stepwise predictions without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} average seeds per stream before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) aggregation. Healthy alarm burden reports pointwise $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and prospective $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ (mean, median, p90, p95) alongside contiguous false-alarm episodes per $1{,}000$ healthy test steps. Step precision, recall, Point-F1 (against the per-stream mean all-positive baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}$), and precision-recall (PR) AUC measure accuracy and ranking. Event coverage ER is compared against $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$. For onset latency $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^* \ge 0$, \emph{stream-paired} comparisons pair stream-mean delays across streams where both detectors trigger at least once (possibly on different events), \emph{exact segment-paired} restricts strictly to identical fault segments co-detected by both models within a seed, and \emph{detection-conditioned} averages over each detector's triggered streams. Autonomous thresholds (\emph{Auto.}) are contrasted against $q^*=0.05$ and upper-order quantile post-hoc $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$, paired with kurtosis $\gamma_2$ and Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Before evaluating the untouched archive, we test which parts of the detector carry the live gain and which can be simplified without loss (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Position-plus-drift dials suffice at $M=3$:} On the fixed $19$-stream cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding $1\to 224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$), and keeping only position and drift ($160$ dials) reaches $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (unweighted $N_{224}$ shifts archive PR-AUC by $\le 0.0011$). Under end-to-end \emph{Lite} retraining (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), adding a fourth prefix ($160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) gives no F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$) while raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.82\%\to 1.06\%$) and slowing inference by $25.5\%$, matching the $118$-stream matched-rate rerun ($0.078$ vs.\ $0.073\text{--}0.074$ F1).
    \item \emph{Slimmer depth and parallel prediction hold up:} Under four-block retraining (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), four TCN blocks match five on F1 and ER ($p\ge 0.23$, saving $23.3\text{k}$ params), and $64$D heads lift causal ER over $128$D ($55.9\%\to 63.1\%$). Sequential rollout at $M=4$ drops buffered F1 ($0.239\to 0.195$) and doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%\to 4.0\%$), whereas adding fluctuation and relay dials ($160\to 224$) trades higher causal ER ($63.6\%\to 69.0\%$) against higher $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.06\%\to 1.45\%$ causal; $6.8\%\to 11.9\%$ buffered).
    \item \emph{Whitening structure and fair PCA comparison:} Replacing dense $96{\times}96$ ZCA with horizon-preserving Kronecker ZCA ($\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) yields comparable archive accuracy rather than proven equivalence. Single-seed causal F1 ($s=42$) shifts by $+0.010$ (cluster CI $[-0.012,+0.027]$, $p=0.76$; ER $64.4\%$ vs.\ $62.9\%$), and three-seed F1 shifts by $+0.014$ (cluster CI $[-0.006,+0.029]$, $p=0.40$). Block-diagonal and dial ZCA match causal F1 ($0.095$ and $0.098$) while raising buffered ER by $+6.2$ and $+5.7$ points ($p\le 0.048$). Disentangling PCA eigenbasis rotation from (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) index mismatch across three archive seeds clarifies the prior drop ($0.091\to 0.071$). Pure PCA matches pure ZCA on causal scores ($0.087$ vs.\ $0.086$ F1, $p=0.60$). Consistent eigenbasis shrinkage recovers the mismatched penalty ($0.086$ vs.\ $0.078$, stream CI $[+0.002,+0.014]$) and matches dense ZCA causally ($p=0.33$). Under buffered attribution, dense ZCA still leads consistent PCA ($0.166$ vs.\ $0.146$ F1, $p=0.005$), while Kronecker PCA matches Kronecker ZCA ($p\ge 0.68$).
    \item \emph{Weight-space quenching is redundant:} On a $118$-stream screen, predictor-row edits (Q1--Q4) leave causal F1 unchanged ($0.0901$ vs.\ $0.0904$) while perturbing unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS, so \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
    \item \emph{Hyperparameters plateau across wide ranges:} Retraining $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ beats Multi-Horizon 1D at every width ($56.1\%\text{--}61.0\%$ vs.\ $42.5\%\text{--}48.9\%$ ER), plateauing at $D\in\{16,32\}$ (peak PR-AUC $0.269$ at $D=32$) while $D=8$ underfits buffered F1 ($0.131$) and $D=64$ dips to $0.068$ causal F1. Accuracy stays flat across damping $\lambda_{\text{damp}}\in[10^{-3},10^{-2}]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.093$ F1) and dial percentile $q_{\text{dial}}\in[0.975,0.990]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.090$ F1 as $K_{\text{Lite}}$ adapts from $21.2$ to $13.0$), while quorum safety $\kappa=1.12$ within $[1.00,1.20]$ keeps $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}<1\%$ before detection delay rises at $\kappa=1.20$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Why simpler coordinate rules fail (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(a)):} On the cached 19-stream residuals, thresholding $128$ coordinates without a quorum compounds marginal errors into $40.6\%\text{--}66.5\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Conversely, \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) and BH FDR ($q=0.005$) collapse recall ($\text{F1}\le 0.001$) because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot drop below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$. Matching each stream's $0.34\%$ nominal rate after aggregation avoids that floor, yet discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $21.1\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on ties and Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$. Among stable rules, the $160$-dial quorum ($0.098$ F1, $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) and Fisher pooling ($0.109$ F1, $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) lead top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.056\text{--}0.072$).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Synthetic vs.\ Natural Failures}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The latent panel flags \emph{cross-channel consistency violations}, \emph{abrupt multi-sensor transients}, and \emph{in-range phase shifts} because they perturb $>K_{\text{Lite}}$ coordinates simultaneously. Conversely, isolated single-sensor faults (freezing, variance collapse, saturation, quantization, dropouts, or single-channel bias) barely move the 32D summary: $K-1$ healthy channels dilute the input projection by $1/K$ and terminal LayerNorm rescales the remainder across all $32$ coordinates (on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials vs.\ $62.1$ for a harmonic flip).

To test an auxiliary channel-level screen across all $19$ dev streams ($K\in[5,55]$), we threshold 64-step log-variance drop, first-order autoregressive (AR) residual RMS, and standardized mean shift at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ with a $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD) floor and $24$-of-$40$ persistence. Across $57$ injected $200$-step single-channel events per class, the variance-collapse union lifts recall on collapse, freezing, and saturation ($63.2\%\text{--}82.5\%\to 93.0\%\text{--}94.7\%$), and the full branch lifts recall on shocks, linear ramps, and bias ($59.6\%\text{--}75.4\%\to 80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$). On \emph{unmodified} test streams, however, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ climbs from $1.5\%$ ($\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ and $19.4\%$. Because synthetic injections omit real hardware coupling, we claim no field sensor-fault coverage and keep the channel branch disabled.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End and Observation vs.\ Latent Space}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Retraining with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five exploratory streams ($3$ seeds) motivates our spectral gate: shell splitting lifts high-channel spacecraft F1 ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal $0.212 \to 0.321$) but lowers F1 on $K{=}9$ wearable and water-quality streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}). Because this five-stream comparison is small and confounded with sensor domain, $K \ge 25$ is treated as a pragmatic design heuristic rather than proof that channel count alone drives the difference.

Across the 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), raw-observation reconstructors suffer heavy tails, strong drift, and high false alarms ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, causal FPR $\approx 25\%$). An observation-space LayerNorm bottleneck curbs kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$), while learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). Because \emph{Lite} and Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) share nearly identical score kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$), the panel's live gain comes from coordinate isolation rather than lighter tails.

\subsection{Computational Complexity and Real-Time Deployment Considerations}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
Real-time inference stays compact in both memory and step latency. Canonical $32$D-head \emph{Lite} uses $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable parameters ($0.90\text{--}0.97\,\text{MB}$ FP32 with the target encoder; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)). Across $K \in [5,72]$, the wider $64$D-head upper bound ($1.10\text{--}1.18\,\text{MB}$ FP32 including whitening buffers, plus $44\text{--}128\,\text{KB}$ state) runs in $0.16\text{--}0.25\,\text{ms}$/window on a batched GPU (RTX 5090) and $7.1\text{--}10.8\,\text{ms}$/window on one CPU thread ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s, $>2\times$ Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate). On $19$ dev streams with unannounced sensor dropouts, mean imputation retains $55.9\%\text{--}54.7\%$ ER (vs.\ $58.9\%$ clean) while $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ to $3.4\%\text{--}7.9\%$.

\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark, Attribution Regimes, and Horizons}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} explains why scalar scores degrade when moving from buffered review to live streaming. Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$ and causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $1.2\%$ to $0.7\%$ ($p=0.0025$). In return, \emph{Lite} trades lower causal ER ($62.2\%$ vs.\ $70.1\%$, $p=0.0029$) and causal Point-F1 ($0.142$ vs.\ $0.165$, $p=0.035$) against the $224$-dial reference. Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

\input{sections/tab_operating_and_horizon.tex}

Across target horizons $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five exploratory streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)), both panels beat 1D SPOT at every horizon ($\text{FPR}_{\text{test}} \le 3.6\%$). Causal F1 peaks at $S\in\{1,4\}$ ($36.7\text{--}44.1$-step delay at $S=4$), and buffered F1 peaks at $S\in\{32,64\}$ ($0.290$ and $0.255$). By Stage~2 clamping (Section~\ref{subsec:triad_formulations}), $S=4$ merges prefixes to $\{2,4,4\}$, whereas $S=1$ collapses all prefixes to identical single-step targets ($\{1,1,1\}$ in \emph{Lite}; four ones at $M=4$) with standard-deviation and difference features zeroed. On the 19 dev streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)), $f^*=1\%$ ER reaches $56.0\%\text{--}63.1\%$ versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, though ranking parity holds with scalar JEPAs on the untouched archive. On NASA contextual faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ ER at $1.3\%\text{--}2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet. Under Wu--Keogh controls \cite{wu2023flawed} ($19$ dev streams, $3$ seeds), $\max_c|x_c|$ fails ($0.008\text{--}0.009$ F1), whereas \emph{Lite} catches $50.9\%$ of \emph{cycle-swap} ($0.030$ F1) and $59.6\%$ of \emph{phase-desync} faults ($0.048$ F1) at $1.6\%$ FPR (vs.\ $28.1\%$ and $42.1\%$ for 1D). Uncalibrated Gaussian noise ($\sigma\in\{0.15,0.30\}$) preserves \emph{Lite} recall ($61.4\%\text{--}66.7\%$) while raising clean FPR to $6.9\%\text{--}14.3\%$ (vs.\ $1.9\%\text{--}6.2\%$ for 1D).

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ F1 across $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), because context and target latents shift together and cancel. Across linear ramps ($a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$, duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$), chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$. Across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds), mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $433.9$ on exponential $1-e^{-t/512}$ and $586.7$ on $0.001\sigma$/step bias as $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

Pairing \emph{Lite} with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ lifts real GHL causal ER from $0.8\%$ ($0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Post-fault GHL baseline wander, however, drives uncompensated test FPR to $74.4\%$: sliding prediction misses slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift flags benign wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned ZCA-anchored latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across block indices ($m\in\{1,2,3\}$; exact horizons under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and cross-block drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $m\in\{2,3\}$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking provides \emph{controlled perturbation attribution} across input channels. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under injected $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Because latent features mix sensors nonlinearly and real field datasets lack ground-truth root-cause labels, this validates synthetic perturbation sensitivity rather than causal fault localization on natural failures, and $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines remain sub-quorum.

Losses on SMAP ($-0.043$), OPPORTUNITY ($-0.029$), and MSL ($-0.014$) leave the cluster CI spanning zero ($[-0.026,+0.069]$, $p_{\text{str}}=0.0009$). Point-F1 doubles on $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=0.047$; $+20.4$ ER points), while 1D leads on shorter prefixes ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$). \noindent\emph{2) Trade-off against the 224-dial reference:} The $224$-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reference reaches higher causal ER ($69.4\%$, $\Delta=-4.8$ points, cluster CI $[-6.6,-1.3]$, $p=0.016$) and lower p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($49.3\%$ vs.\ $64.7\%$), at similar Point-F1 ($0.091$, $p=0.15$). In return, \emph{Lite} lowers mean ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $7.0\%$, $p=1.3{\times}10^{-7}$), median ($0.93\%$ vs.\ $1.4\%$), and buffered ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $21.0\%$, $p=9.2{\times}10^{-10}$) $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ while lifting the $\le 5\%$ pass rate ($88.1\%$ vs.\ $84.7\%$).

\noindent\emph{3) External reconstructors and matched-budget parity:} Under untouched-archive SPOT, \emph{Lite} beats Linear PCA Forecaster by $+34.5$ ER points and $+0.063$ F1 ($p\le 8.2{\times}10^{-12}$). TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1, both $0.236$ PR-AUC) attain higher raw F1 only at $25.6\%$ and $24.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Against scalar JEPAs, archive PR-AUC matches ($0.152$ vs.\ $0.162$ causal; $0.229$ vs.\ $0.230$ buffered). At matched $r=0.5\%$ calibration budget (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)), \emph{Lite} incurs higher held-out FPR ($7.3\%$ vs.\ $5.8\%$ for 1D). Under the canonical linear $99$th-quantile protocol used alongside TimesNet and TranAD, \emph{Lite} reaches $51.2\%$ ER vs.\ $55.6\%$ for 1D. By contrast, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b) applies upper-order quantiles to discrete dial counts in a separate three-seed rerun ($52.9\%$ vs.\ $54.9\%$ ER; $0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$ F1, with rerun precision $0.179$ vs.\ $0.171$ and recall $0.068$ vs.\ $0.042$). Because four independent clusters limit an exact two-sided dataset sign-flip test to $p_{\min}=2/2^4=0.125$ ($0.0625$ across five sets with GECCO), dataset-level uncertainty remains high despite unanimous wins, while $80$ unseen streams from tuned sets trade $+10.5\%$ ER ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0080$) against $-0.016$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}=0.55$). Nine-fold nested LODO ($s=42$) selects $(0.990, 1.06)$ in every fold ($69.5\%$ vs.\ $52.8\%$ ER; $0.094$ vs.\ $0.076$ F1). Under nine-fold exclusion, ER stays positive ($+9.7\%\text{--}17.8\%$, $p_{\text{clus}}=0.0078$), while unweighted $\Delta\text{Pt-F1}$ dips when removing Exathlon ($-0.005$) due to SMAP.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Chronological distribution shift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration variance vs.\ chronological nonstationarity (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and within-block dependence while breaking chronological regime order. Across $n=118$, healthy-block permutation reduces measured archive $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ by $92.3\%$ ($7.44\%\to 0.57\%$; $\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams). Because shuffling exposes $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ to operating modes absent in chronological prefixes, this drop reflects sensitivity to uncalibrated regime evolution rather than an exact variance decomposition.
    \item \emph{Associated drift correlates:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) match stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$), whereas stabilized residual-covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), strongly correlating with quorum inflation ($4.8\times$) and $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In $3$-seed fault-free dev stress tests, terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and keeps $45^\circ$ basis rotations moderate ($6.8\%$ FPR). Mean steps, duty-cycle shifts, and baseline walks do not self-settle, raising FPR to $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ alongside $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$. Blindly auto-recalibrating on $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, however, absorbs persistent faults: fitting $400$ post-shift windows during a persistent $+2.5\sigma$ fault cuts post-buffer recall to $9.1\%$. Our conditional policy therefore flags $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ under frozen weights, quarantines candidate windows, and permits threshold updates only after operator or control-state confirmation ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall preserved; $4.8\%$ FPR on confirmed shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability and Two-Stage Deployment Funnel}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite calibration histories cannot provide distribution-free tail guarantees. Because sliding windows correlate strongly (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$), $105$ of $118$ untouched streams have $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) tests sensitivity to serial dependence without inventing unobserved extremes: across $n=118$, \texttt{MBB-Median} matches the unbootstrapped quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR; $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1), whereas upper-confidence-bound (UCB) \texttt{MBB-UCB90} and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds cost $8\text{--}17$ ER points and thinning inflates mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

Autonomous deployment pairs empirical screening with fallback routing, noting the archive-informed origin of these gates (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59$ streams, and two-stage screening ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$) accepts $52$ ($35$ meeting $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$ at $0.9\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $14.5\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$). Because $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ selection carries finite-sample optimism (median $5.8\%$ Wilson UCB at $N_{\text{eff,hold}}=66.7$), prospective $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ on those $52$ streams reaches $11.0\%$ mean and $3.9\%$ median ($53.8\%$ staying $\le 5\%$). In our three-seed rerun, accepted streams emit $18.1$ mean ($13.9$ median) false-alarm episodes per thousand healthy steps, vs.\ $16.8$ ($12.6$) unscreened and $8.8$ ($4.4$) for 1D. Adding a third drift gate ($D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$) retains $46$ streams at $6.8\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($2.9\%$ median, $60.9\%$ keeping $\le 5\%$). On that subset, \emph{Lite} rescues 1D SPOT under-triggering, lifting ER from $32.1\%$ to $49.8\%$ and Point-F1 from $0.030$ to $0.068$ ($p\le 0.0004$), at $6.8\%$ vs.\ $3.8\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$. Across all $118$ streams on equal footing, complete three-stage routing ($46$ \emph{Lite} plus $72$ 1D) poses a prospective trade-off against complete 1D SPOT rather than lowering false alarms. Complete hybrid routing raises ER ($52.5\%\to 59.4\%$, cluster CI $[+1.7,+16.7]$, $p=0.0004$) and Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.091$, $p=0.0003$), at $10.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ and $12.9$ vs.\ $8.8$ rerun episodes per thousand healthy steps ($p<10^{-4}$). Nine-fold nested LODO screening reproduces this identical $46$-stream out-of-fold split, staying stable ($42\text{--}46$ streams, $10.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) under tighter $3\%$ screening cuts alongside live $D_{\text{KS}}$ quarantine monitoring.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Operational deployment pairs healthy-prefix fitting ($K\in[2,55]$, no cross-asset weights) with archive-informed gating validated via nested LODO screening. Two-stage screening retains $52$ streams ($11.0\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) and drift screening ($D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$) retains $46$ ($6.8\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$), routing remaining streams to 1D SPOT while holding live $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ shifts in a $400$-window quarantine buffer. Post-alarm LOCO masking provides synthetic perturbation triage rather than field localization.

Overall, null regularizer findings, matched-FPR ranking parity, and high raw FPR for TimesNet and TranAD show that the panel's benefit rests on automated label-free operating-point selection, rescuing scalar under-triggering on stable long histories ($49.8\%$ vs.\ $32.1\%$ ER across $46$ accepted streams) rather than superior intrinsic ranking. Deployment spans three tiers: (1)~\emph{standalone fit} on abrupt/contextual faults passing $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$, and $D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$; (2)~\emph{complementary guards required} on diluted single-sensor flatlines, slow ramps, and uncalibrated switches; and (3)~\emph{1D SPOT fallback} when prefixes are short or drift screens fail.

\vspace{-4pt}
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Combining multi-horizon JEPA residuals, whitening, per-dial tripwires, and fallback routing across $33$ dev and $118$ archive streams supports three conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Label-free operating-point selection:} Rather than better underlying discrimination, the quorum shifts the label-free operating point. Idealized equivariance holds approximately under additive floors, Kronecker ZCA matches dense ZCA within CIs, and consistent PCA recovers causal shrinkage drops. Unscreened \emph{Lite} lifts causal ER ($52.5\%\to 64.6\%$, $p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$) and cuts segment-paired delay ($-41.4$ steps; stream-paired $-74.6$), while raising test FPR ($8.8\%\to 17.0\%$) and leaving dataset Point-F1 mixed ($0.076\to 0.086$).
    \item \emph{Matched budgets and baselines:} Matched-FPR checks confirm ranking parity with scalar JEPAs (1D leads F1 at $f=1\%$), whereas TimesNet and TranAD gain F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ FPR. LOCO masking hits $85.7\%$ Top-1 synthetic subset sensitivity.
    \item \emph{Complete prospective trade-off:} Healthy-block permutation reduces archive FPR by $92.3\%$. Evaluated completely across $118$ streams, three-stage routing trades $10.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$ test FPR ($12.9$ vs.\ $8.8$ episodes per thousand healthy steps) against complete 1D SPOT for $59.4\%$ vs.\ $52.5\%$ ER and $0.091$ vs.\ $0.076$ F1, paired with quarantined drift monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Next steps include field root-cause validation, lightweight online $\mathbf{\Omega}_D$ updates, control-conditioned ramp guards, and flatline monitoring.
