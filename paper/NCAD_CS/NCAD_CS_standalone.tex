\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise far fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs sum every coordinate into one number, letting drifting coordinates inflate the threshold and hide faults elsewhere. We replace that scalar score with per-coordinate circuit breakers: each coordinate's error across future horizons receives its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and an alarm fires when a quorum trips. We study Laminar-JEPA-Lite, a 128-breaker operational architecture, and Laminar-JEPA, a 224-breaker ablation reference. Primary evaluation uses 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets, separating model fitting, checkpoint selection, adaptation, and calibration onto disjoint healthy blocks. Under buffered attribution, Laminar-JEPA-Lite raises event recall from 59.0 to 67.4 percent and Point-F1 from 0.143 to 0.156 over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA while lowering held-out false-positive rates from 19.5 to 14.3 percent. Under live causal reporting, it raises event recall from 52.5 to 63.8 percent across all nine datasets, halves delay, and beats system-matched coordinate aggregators; pooled causal Point-F1 rises modestly from 0.076 to 0.079 (doubling from 0.040 to 0.079 when calibration reaches 500 windows), while causal held-out false-positive rates are right-skewed (0.78 percent median, 7.0 percent mean from short calibration histories). Design-stage streams show 1 versus 22 to 24 percent false-positive rates against reconstructors; slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream readings from dozens of sensors at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A detector watching this telemetry has two jobs: raise an alarm soon after something breaks, and stay quiet the rest of the time. Faults are rare and seldom labeled, so the detector must learn what normal looks like from healthy history alone. Operators lose trust quickly when a monitor alarms several times a day on healthy data, and an ignored monitor catches nothing.

The most common approach trains a network to reproduce raw sensor signals and raises an alarm when the reproduction is poor; TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} follow this recipe. In practice, much of raw telemetry cannot be copied at all. Random sensor jitter leaves an irreducible error, and benign thermal drift or duty-cycle changes degrade the copy even when nothing is broken. Summing errors across 25--55 channels measured in different units allows one noisy channel to dominate, producing heavy-tailed spikes on healthy data.

These spikes defeat threshold calibration. Setting thresholds on healthy windows with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} still yields $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) for reconstruction detectors on later healthy data across our 19 design-stage development streams, roughly one step in four. Because faults span many steps, alarming at random $24\%$ of the time already hits $60.0\%$ of fault episodes; TimesNet catches $58.8\%$, reflecting frequent alarms rather than selective fault recognition.

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoids copying raw signals. An encoder compresses each window into a 32-number summary, and a predictor forecasts the summary of the next window. Discarding unpredictable jitter yields lighter error tails and cuts healthy false alarms on those development streams by an order of magnitude (Contribution~3).

Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse the 32 prediction errors into one scalar score and one threshold. When one or two summary features drift slightly as a machine warms up or switches load, their growing errors push the shared threshold upward. A fault that shifts the remaining 30 features moderately then stays below that raised threshold and goes missed. We call these drifting features \emph{hijacker dials}. On our development streams, single-score JEPAs reach causal Point-F1 (step-level alarm accuracy) of only $0.050\text{--}0.055$ and need $138\text{--}154$ steps to flag a fault.

Two evaluation shortcuts also inflate published numbers: point adjustment credits an entire fault segment if one step inside it is flagged \cite{kim2022towards}, and thresholds tuned with test labels or calibrated on adaptation data understate false alarms. We score every step without point adjustment, fit thresholds on healthy data only, separate model fitting, checkpoint selection, adaptation, and calibration into disjoint healthy blocks on our primary archive, and reserve 118 streams never inspected during design.

We replace the single scalar alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, like a building breaker box where a short in one circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across target prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, each of the 32 coordinates receives separate \emph{dials} tracking horizon error size and persistent drift, each with its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and the system alarms when a quorum of dials trips together. Drifting dials widen only their own thresholds instead of blinding the rest of the panel. Targeting abrupt to medium-onset faults (within roughly one $256$-step context window), we study two variants (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, our preferred operational architecture, predicts three horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $4\times32=128$ position-plus-drift dials under one quorum ($35\%\text{--}52\%$ fewer parameters and $2.43\times$ faster inference).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, a 224-dial ablation reference variant, scores four views of each coordinate's error plus 32 onset relays and optional weight editing to isolate which views aid live causal versus buffered attribution; more dials do not imply a better operational detector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Thirty-three development streams (where adaptation and thresholds share calibration data) provide exploratory design-stage evidence, while 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets (of 141 streams across 12 datasets) under a disjoint five-block protocol provide primary evidence of generalization and calibration (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}). We report live \emph{causal} streaming separately from \emph{buffered} after-the-fact attribution:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event coverage, buffered accuracy, and archive false-alarm behavior:} On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises buffered event recall (ER) from $59.0\%$ to $67.4\%$ and buffered Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.156$ ($p=0.022$) over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA while lowering held-out buffered FPR ($19.5\%\to 14.3\%$), retaining a $+10.9$-point recall lead at a matched $5\%$ test FPR. Under live causal reporting, it raises ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ across all $9$ datasets (sign-flip $p=0.0039$; all $12$ leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) folds), halves delay ($70.8$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps), and beats system-matched coordinate aggregators. Pooled causal Point-F1 rises modestly from $0.076$ to $0.079$ ($95\%$ stream confidence interval (CI) $[-0.019,+0.023]$, $p=0.014$), doubling from $0.040$ to $0.079$ ($p=2.7\times 10^{-5}$) on adequately calibrated streams ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, $n=59$). Archive causal held-out FPR is right-skewed: median $0.78\%$ ($0.61\%$ for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$), $87.3\%$ of streams $\le 5\%$, and mean $7.0\%$ driven by short calibration histories.
    \item \emph{Contributing factors:} Design-stage matched-backbone ablations indicate that LayerNorm and barycenter centering contribute to lighter tails and less drift under these protocols, whereas spectral reweighting has a null effect.
    \item \emph{Design-stage comparison with reconstructors:} On 19 development streams, latent prediction holds causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors, and at a matched $1\%$ test FPR \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ and $56.0\%$ of events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet.
    \item \emph{Explicit limits:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} gives geometric bounds whose scalar score reweighting is null; autocorrelation limits extreme-quantile precision on short calibration blocks; an optional channel branch lifts recall on synthetic single-sensor faults ($80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$) only by raising test FPR from $1.5\%$ to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$; and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$) and natural sensor failures remain out of reach.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$: autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly; TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts Gaussian prior and series-level attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel-wise reconstruction losses fit stochastic jitter, so summing errors across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes and high false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive approaches---temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} with synthetic anomalies, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}---depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without observation decoding or negative pairs, avoiding collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects underlying system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured dynamics, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties require known laws and exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}; we instead borrow the algebraic mean-plus-fluctuation identity of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as a multi-horizon residual summary. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse whitened coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate alternatives such as univariate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and per-coordinate split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete calibration floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All methods are evaluated without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case in this paper is a \emph{stream}: one recording from one system, with $K$ sensor channels sampled over time. Every stream has a healthy (nominal) history, used for training and calibration, followed by a labeled test segment that contains the faults. We use two collections of streams. A 45-stream development suite drawn from 11 datasets guided every design decision and supplies the main development benchmark. A larger 141-stream archive drawn from 12 datasets supplies the final evaluation, and 118 of its streams were never inspected during design.

\noindent\emph{Development suite:} The 45 streams come from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. They cover three kinds of systems with channel counts from 2 to 72 and very different fault types:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry:} six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$). NASA labels each SMAP and MSL fault as either an out-of-range \emph{point} fault, where a value leaves its normal range, or an in-range \emph{contextual} fault, where values stay in range but behave abnormally together \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)).
    \item \emph{Wearable motion sensing:} six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} recorded at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes), so the $S{=}64$-step target window spans one second.
    \item \emph{Industrial processes and infrastructure:} 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$) whose $26$ faults are slow ramps, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three of these streams form the development benchmark: 19 non-GHL streams and the 14 GHL streams, spanning 7 datasets. Inclusion required enough healthy data for calibration ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ windows), at most 55 channels, and at most $1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). The other 12 streams are reserved: five have very short healthy histories ($764$--$1{,}492$ steps; CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), the six CICIDS streams exceed the channel limit, and GECCO is evaluated in the archive instead.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive contains every qualifying stream from 12 datasets, requiring at least $100$ calibration windows and at least one labeled anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, and 13 OPPORTUNITY streams, plus single streams from GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN. Healthy histories range from $1{,}920$ to $132{,}481$ steps and test segments from $2{,}158$ to $114{,}001$ steps. Removing the 23 streams used to tune the method leaves 118 untouched streams from 9 datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{How each stream is split:} Every healthy history is divided in time order. The first $65\%$ fits the model, and its first part also sets the per-channel standardization ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$). The next $17.5\%$ calibrates alarm thresholds ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), and after a 64-step gap the last $17.5\%$ measures false alarms on healthy data the thresholds have never seen ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$). Archive runs further divide the model-fitting block (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams from the same archive often share hardware, so they are not fully independent. We therefore also treat whole datasets as units of analysis (7 for the development benchmark, 9 for the untouched streams, 12 for the full archive), using Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and leave-one-dataset-out audits (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.77\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{Overview of the four-stage \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and compact \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} pipeline: \textbf{(1)}~sliding window ($C{=}256, S \in \{1..64\}$) and dual encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) with conditional rFFT splitting ($K{\ge}25$ in Lite), barycenter centering, and LayerNorm; \textbf{(2)}~multi-horizon rollout ($M{=}4$ sequential in full \emph{Laminar-JEPA}; $M{=}3$ parallel single-LayerNorm in \emph{Lite}) under variance-floor and decorrelation loss plus resolvent whitening into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 32}$; \textbf{(3)}~horizon expansion into $7 \times 32 = 224$ dials (full) or $4 \times 32 = 128$ position-plus-drift dials (\emph{Lite}); and \textbf{(4)}~per-dial Boolean circuit breakers emitting causal ($\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$) or prefix-buffered alarms.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-5pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} shows the pipeline from raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($K$ channels over $T$ steps) to alarms. In plain terms, it has four stages: (1)~encode each window of recent data into a short summary vector; (2)~predict the summaries of the next few stretches of data; (3)~turn the prediction errors into a set of \emph{dials}, each measuring one aspect of the error; and (4)~give every dial its own threshold and raise an alarm when enough dials trip together. The networks are trained on healthy data only; stabilization weights and alarm thresholds are then fitted on later healthy blocks and frozen.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time. At each position $t$ it splits into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of the past $C = 256$ steps and a \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the next $S = 64$ steps, which ends at the decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Telemetry mixes slow trends with fast vibration, so the encoder first separates time scales. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal per-channel convolution and are split into four smooth frequency bands (dyadic shells of a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT), forming an amplitude partition of unity). Each band has its own causal convolution (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and a softmax router feeds the bands into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}. Single-channel inputs skip the band split.

The encoders follow the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}: instead of reconstructing raw waveforms, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and a target encoder $E_\phi$ map context and target windows to $D=32$-dimensional summaries, each normalized by LayerNorm to length close to $\sqrt{D}$. The target encoder is a slowly updated copy of the online one, an exponential moving average (EMA) with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on archive cohorts) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Two problems arise in practice. Deep dilated TCNs blur brief spikes at fault onset, and changes in operating regime shift all summaries together. Two encoder components address them:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth tapping (keeps fast transients):} Each encoder also reads three intermediate feature maps ($d_{\text{model}}=48$): the spectral stem, a middle TCN block, and the final TCN block. For each tap, the last time step and the root-mean-square (RMS) energy over time are projected to $\mathbb{R}^D$ by a two-layer GELU head and blended with the deep TCN output through a bounded gate ($g \le 0.5$).
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering (removes common offsets):} During training, a running mean of the context summaries $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (update rate $0.05$, shared with $E_\phi$) is subtracted from both context and target summaries before the final LayerNorm. Centering removes offsets learned in training; it does not guarantee invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The simplest JEPA detector, the single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA}, uses a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to predict one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the whole 64-step target window, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant penalty ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Summarizing all 64 steps in one vector can average away a short fault. \emph{Laminar-JEPA} therefore predicts how the target unfolds: it summarizes $M=4$ nested prefixes of the target window, the first $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ steps. Starting from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ plus learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a recurrent predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ produces $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M-1)}$ in sequence, and an anchor head $P_\theta$ predicts the full-window summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$. The loss gives the full window weight $w_M=1/2$ and each shorter prefix $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ ($1/6$ in the full model, $1/4$ in Lite), and adds a penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$ that keeps the summaries from collapsing to a constant:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$ and the penalty acts on the predicted summaries of each batch. Log-determinant penalties need batches larger than $D$; $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ works on small batches. Its diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$ push each coordinate's standard deviation above $\gamma = 0.75$ ($\sigma_d$ from biased batch variance plus $10^{-4}$) and its off-diagonals $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize sample covariance (divisor $B-1$, both symmetric entries counted), so $\frac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^D \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \frac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2$. At $B=16 < D=32$, this lifts target effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first \emph{whitened}: rescaled to unit variance and decorrelated using statistics from healthy training windows. We flatten $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into a vector of length $MD$ ($128$ in the full model, $96$ in Lite), estimate its mean and unbiased covariance on the $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ windows, and with $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ ($\mathbf{U},\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{MD\times MD}$) use the damped precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying centered errors by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$; the damping constant $10^{-3}$, fixed across all cohorts, shrinks directions with almost no training variance instead of amplifying their noise.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$, never on the block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ that later sets alarm thresholds (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), and blend them toward a simpler per-coordinate scaling, the unwhitened error divided by its root-mean-square on $\mathbf{X}_q$, $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=e_{m,c}(t)/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    The full \emph{Laminar-JEPA} can also run an optional gradient-free \emph{weight-space quench}, which edits the few predictor outputs that drift most on healthy data and keeps the edit only if false alarms fall. It uses only $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_q$ (legacy fixed-epoch development runs without $\mathbf{X}_q$ use $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$; Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}). Coordinates are ranked by a median-absolute-deviation (MAD) standardized instability score that combines mean and scale shifts from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ to $\mathbf{X}_q$, drift between the halves of $\mathbf{X}_q$, and residual autocorrelation, with a negative term for isolated spikes. For the top $k\in\{4,2\}$ coordinates, four steps run in order. \emph{(Q1)}~Damp the selected predictor output rows and LayerNorm gains and shift $\mathbf{p}_m$ toward the $\mathbf{X}_q$ means; through the shared 32D LayerNorm this also moves unselected coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). \emph{(Q2)}~Move residual centers $75\%$ of the way toward the post-edit $\mathbf{X}_q$ means. \emph{(Q3)}~Scale off-diagonal precision couplings by $0.15$ and rescale whitened $\mathbf{X}_q$ RMS. \emph{(Q4)}~Keep the edit (preferring $k=2$ on ties) only if the $\mathbf{X}_q$ half-split FPR improves while at least $92\%$ of the pre-edit quorum thresholds are preserved; then freeze all parameters.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

The proposition below states two exact properties of this split. Part~(i) says that drift and fluctuation measure non-overlapping parts of the position energy, and that the endpoint dial cannot exceed a multiple of the fluctuation dial. Part~(ii) says the fluctuation pattern lives in at most $M-1$ directions and defines $R_{\text{LT}}$, which is $1$ when one pattern dominates and $1/(M-1)$ when the energy is spread evenly. These are geometric facts about the representation, not accuracy guarantees.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Instead of adding all 224 dials into one score, Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) gives each dial its own threshold, learned from the healthy calibration block. A dial \emph{trips} when it exceeds its threshold, and an alarm fires when enough dials trip at once. Because all dials come from the same $4 \times 32$ error matrix, their trips are correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort; the variance of the trip count is $7.2\times$ that of independent dials). We therefore do not assume independence; the alarm quorums are set from the trip counts actually observed on healthy calibration data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds and hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized by its calibration median $m_j$ and scaled MAD $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ (the $10^{-4}$ floor handles zero-MAD dials), giving $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. Its tripwire $\tau_j$ is the $98.5$th calibration percentile of $z_j$, widened by $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}} \ge 1$ when its calibration tail grows (second-half $q_{0.98}$ above $1.35\times$ the first half) and by $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}} \ge 1$ when its scale exceeds $1.875\times$ its perspective-group median:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 onset relays:} When a sudden fault first enters the right edge of the target window, the short prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover healthy data, so few position dials trip. To catch this moment, each coordinate $c$ has stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($\gamma^{1.25}$ guard) and fires a relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when its full-window position dial ($m=4$) trips together with its endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$ dial.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing and quorum voting:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, the trips from window $[t,t+S)$ are reported at its right end $\tau=t+S-1$, the earliest moment all of its data exist; this fixes a reporting lag of $S-1$ steps, separate from the detection delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$ after fault onset $t^*$. Under \emph{buffered attribution}, used for after-the-fact review, each trip is assigned to the steps it describes: position trips to their prefix $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation trips to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint trips to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. Counting active trips at each step gives $N_{224}(\tau)$, the position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and the relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. For ranking metrics, the continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = N_{224}(\tau) + N_{128}(\tau) + 2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ counts position trips and relays twice (fixed on development streams before held-out testing; Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). The binary alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with quorums set on calibration counts: $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$. All quantiles use linear interpolation, the safety factor is applied before the floor, quorums stay real-valued, and both trips and alarms use strict inequalities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Compact Variant: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps the same four stages but makes three simplifications, each supported by the ablations in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}. Together they cut trainable parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$ ($141.9\text{k}$ at $K < 25$ and $222.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$, vs.\ $295.8\text{k}$), speed up windowed GPU inference by $2.43\times$ ($3.14\times$ at $K < 25$), and need no weight editing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Smaller encoder:} A fifth dilated block blurred short-lived anomalies without making the summaries richer, so \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses four TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$) and $64$D pooling heads. The frequency-band front-end is used only on high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), where it helped; smaller streams use a direct causal projection.
    \item \emph{Parallel three-horizon predictor:} One residual two-layer GELU MLP predicts $M=3$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$) in parallel from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m$, with a single final LayerNorm. This removes the lag of sequential prediction and the coupling between stacked normalizations, and shrinks $\mathbf{\Omega}$ to $96\times 96$.
    \item \emph{128-dial panel with one quorum:} Only the $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and the $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ are kept, each calibrated by (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}). Fluctuation and endpoint dials, onset relays, and quenching are removed. Steps are ranked by the plain count $N_{128}(\tau)$, and the alarm uses one quorum, $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit) and Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128-Bit)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Full},\text{Lite}\}$, attribution mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Per-timestep binary alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 1: Disjoint Training, Whitening \& Adaptation ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q$)}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$ and standardize channels using $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ alone.
\State Set nested prefixes $S_{1:4}=\{16,32,48,64\}$ (Full) or $S_{1:3}=\{16,32,64\}$ with $K\ge 25$ rFFT gate (Lite).
\For{stride-$16$ training batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ and targets $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$ with barycenter shift $-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$.
    \State Roll out sequential $\{P_{\text{sub}}, P_\theta\}$ (Full) or parallel single-LayerNorm predictor (Lite); update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (or fixed epochs) and fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_+^{MD})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\If{tier $=$ Full and $q_{\text{wsp}} = 1$}
    \State Rank drifting coordinates on $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; run Steps Q1--Q4 for $k\in\{4,2\}$ and keep edit iff $\mathbf{X}_q$ split and $0.92$-quorum gates pass.
\EndIf
\State Fit and freeze stabilization scales and blending weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 2: Untouched Circuit-Breaker Calibration on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$}
\State Compute frozen stabilized residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ via (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).
\If{tier $=$ Full}
    \State Expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ into $7D=224$ dials (position, drift, fluctuation, endpoint) and $32$ right-edge onset relays.
    \State Fit MAD scales $(m_j,s_j)$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\Else \Comment{Preferred Deployment Tier: Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
    \State Extract $4D=128$ position-plus-drift dials $(|\tilde{e}_{m,c}|, |\bar{e}_c|)$, fit $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and set single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}=\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 3: Online Inference on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$}
    \State Compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \to \mathbf{d}(t)$ and binary trips $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(z_j(t)>\tau_j)$ (plus relays $r_c(t)$ if Full).
    \State Attribute active counts to right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ (\text{causal}) or prefix spans $[t,t+S_m)$ (\text{buffered}).
    \State Emit $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ when active counts exceed $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) or $K_{\text{Lite}}$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} To measure what the breaker panel adds over a single score on the same backbone (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), the 1D controls pool a residual vector into one number: an equal blend of its $\ell_2$ norm and a softmax-weighted coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$). Single-horizon TS-JEPA pools $\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$; 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA combines the pooled full-tensor and peak-horizon scores by root-mean-square (with optional $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$). Both are thresholded with SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) into a model-fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a held-out nominal suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint weight fitting, checkpoint selection, and adaptation):} Reusing calibration windows for early stopping, blending (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), or quenching can make nominal thresholds optimistic. Archive benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) therefore use a disjoint \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$) inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits standardization, network weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the checkpoint; and $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$ stride-1 windows isolated from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ by $S=64$ steps) fits and freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and optional quenching (Steps Q1--Q4) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched. Fixed-epoch development runs (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) omit $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$, and the 19-stream cache skips (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and quenching. A four-block check ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) on the 19 development streams gives causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ of $0.96\%$ (\emph{Lite}) and $1.05\%$ (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}), matching Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, though the smaller $50\%$ fitting block lowers causal F1 ($0.133\to 0.071$, $0.144\to 0.076$). On short histories, \emph{two-fold cross-fitting} swaps the halves of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ and requires $\hat{y}^{(1)}\wedge\hat{y}^{(2)}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched calibration and boundary isolation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Stride-1 windows of length $W = 320$ in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ give $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ indices. Splitting at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step gap $[H : H+S)$ so target observations never overlap (a $G=320$ gap shifts causal FPR by $<0.25$ points). On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone, the frozen model fits all dial scales, guards, tripwires (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$. We separate raw count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective sample size} $N_{\text{eff},s} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int},s}$ via Sokal estimators ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Statistical Inference}
We report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Dataset-clustered and stream-level inference:} \emph{Dataset-clustered inference} ($95\%$ cluster-bootstrap CIs and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip permutation tests) is emphasized alongside stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs ($2{,}000$ resamples). For the four focal causal contrasts on the 118 untouched streams (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) and TS-JEPA, on Point-F1 and ER), Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) \cite{benjamini1995controlling} adjusted $p$-values are $0.014$ and $3.6\times 10^{-4}$ (stream-level Point-F1) and $0.0078$ and $0.012$ (dataset sign-flip ER, positive on $9/9$ and $8/9$ datasets); other quoted $p$-values are raw and descriptive, and non-Exathlon subsets ($n=101$, $8$ datasets) verify sensitivity to archive composition.
(2)~\emph{12-fold LODO audit:} Across all 12 LODO folds of the 141-stream archive, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps a positive gain on causal ER ($+0.099$ to $+0.141$ vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D), buffered Point-F1, and causal Point-F1 vs.\ TS-JEPA ($\max p=0.0054$); its causal Point-F1 gain over Multi-Horizon 1D holds in $9/12$ folds.

\subsection{Cohorts and Baselines}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} omits Steps Q1--Q4. Causal and buffered modes share identical splits per cohort (except a single-horizon 130-stream buffered screen in Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}):
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-stage, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}):} Because these streams were inspected during design and the 33-stream suite shares $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for stabilization and thresholds, they compare design choices rather than out-of-sample calibration. The 19-stream cache ($6$ datasets) tests the dial ladder, panel components, and conformal/SPC banks on shared unquenched residuals (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b), Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}); the 33-stream benchmark ($7$ datasets: $19$ non-GHL in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) plus $14$ GHL in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) compares all baselines. Tripwire and quench constants were fixed on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched held-out cohorts (primary evidence):} Removing those $23$ tuning streams from the $141$-stream archive ($12$ datasets, five-block protocol) leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($101$ non-Exathlon across $8$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), plus a single-seed Q1--Q4 isolation audit (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\emph{Three baseline tiers:} (i)~\emph{Matched-backbone controls} (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D)) fix the representation and test one SPOT score against the panel. (ii)~\emph{System-level aggregators} fix the whitened residuals and replace the panel with maximum $z$, top-$k$ voting, $\ell_2^2$ conformal, Fisher/Stouffer $p$-pooling, multiplicity-controlled conformal banks, or SPC charts, calibrated to the panel's $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rate unless noted (Tables~\ref{tab:matched_alert}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). (iii)~\emph{External baselines} (TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, PCA Forecaster, Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) share splits, training-only scaling, gaps, and SPOT ($u=p_{98}$, $q=10^{-3}$), using $206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ parameters (panels add none). Runs used PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090 ($0.15\,\text{ms}$/window \emph{Lite}, $0.38\,\text{ms}$ \emph{Laminar-JEPA}; $188\,\text{MB}$ peak); code: \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Every alarm $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$ is scored step by step against $y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}. \emph{Causal} numbers show what an operator sees live at step $\tau$; \emph{buffered} numbers reflect retrospective review after the target window finishes and are not live performance. Headline values average seeds per stream before averaging across streams (or unweighted across datasets in dataset-macro Buffered Point-F1, $\text{DS-F1}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{False-alarm rates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$):} Fraction of healthy steps flagged on the untouched pre-test check block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ ($[H+S:M_{\text{val}})$) and on healthy test steps ($y(\tau)=0$). Because a few drifting streams skew the mean, we also report stream medians and $90$th percentiles ($\text{p90}$).
    \item \emph{Step-level accuracy (Point-F1, precision-recall (PR) AUC):} Point-F1 (Pt-F1) is the harmonic mean of step precision (fraction of alarm steps that are truly faulty) and step recall (fraction of faulty steps flagged). Without point adjustment, Pt-F1 is naturally modest ($0.05\text{--}0.25$, not $0.90$): residuals peak near regime changes rather than across every step of a long fault, and when faults occupy a small fraction $\pi$ of the stream, even a $1\%$ false-alarm rate adds many false-positive steps (alarming at every step scores $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}=2\pi/(1+\pi)$). PR-AUC (Buf.\ PR when buffered) scores ranking quality across all thresholds without inflation from healthy steps.
    \item \emph{Episode catch rate and speed (ER, Delay $\Delta t$):} Event recall (ER) is the fraction of contiguous faulty episodes ($y=1$ runs) with at least one alarm; it must be read with FPR because random alarms at rate $p$ still hit long episodes at rate $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$. Detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ counts steps from fault onset $t^*$ to the first in-segment alarm over \emph{detected} episodes (distinct from the $S-1$ causal lag), so catching an extra late-developing fault can raise mean $\Delta t$.
    \item \emph{Budget controls and score diagnostics:} \emph{Auto.}\ uses the deployed healthy-only threshold (quorums or SPOT), $q^*=0.05$ sets thresholds at the $95$th $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ percentile ($q_{1-q^*}$), and diagnostic controls $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$ use test labels post-hoc to match a $1\%$ or $5\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$. On nominal scores, excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ ($\gamma_2=0$ for Gaussian tails) measures $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ spike heaviness, and the two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) distance $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0,1]$ ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) measures healthy drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Panel Components and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate perspective expansion and panel components on the 19-stream residual cache (not paired to the separately trained run in Panel~(c)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Scientific role of the 224-dial reference vs.\ compact panels:} More dials do not make a better operational detector; we keep 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as a diagnostic reference because scoring all four views on one residual tensor isolates which views aid live causal versus buffered attribution. Against 1D scoring on this cache, 224 dials raise causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$; buffered $+0.033$, $p=0.53$), whereas pruning fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to 160 position-plus-drift dials raises causal Point-F1 to $0.098$, ER to $59.1\%$, and cuts delay ($90.1\to 74.8$ steps) and $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.76\%\to 0.55\%$) while lowering buffered Point-F1 ($0.224\to 0.198$). Retraining on the 118 untouched archive streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)) tests 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, same-run 160-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, and 3-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (128 dials) on identical splits: pruning $224\to 160$ dials cuts buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ by $6.2$ points ($19.2\%\to 13.0\%$, $p=3.7\times 10^{-15}$) at near-identical causal Point-F1 ($0.073$ vs.\ $0.074$), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} matches or exceeds both ($0.078$ causal, $0.154$ buffered Point-F1) at lower cost.
    \item \emph{Relays, PR-AUC score weighting, quorum rules, and hijack guards:} Removing the 32 onset relays lowers causal Point-F1 ($0.071 \to 0.068$), and replacing the three-counter quorum (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) with one 224-dial quorum lowers causal Point-F1 to $0.063$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0055$; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}). Replacing the double-position continuous score $N_{\text{tot}} = N_{224} + N_{128} + 2N_{\text{edge}}$ with unweighted $N_{224}+N_{\text{edge}}$ or $N_{224}$ leaves binary alarms unchanged and changes PR-AUC by at most $0.004$ on this cache and $0.0016$ on the 118 untouched streams (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses unweighted $N_{128}$). Removing hijack guards ($\gamma_j=1$) raises causal Point-F1 to $0.073$ ($70.1$-step delay) on this cache when nominal drift is mild, so the guards are a safeguard against drift rather than a source of accuracy. No panel component removes the $S-1$ reporting lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching step isolation and LayerNorm coupling:} We tested each quench step in isolation on single-seed \emph{Laminar-JEPA} checkpoints across all $118$ untouched streams (unquenched: causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 6.76\%$, $\text{F1} = 0.0901$). Damping $k=4$ predictor rows (Step Q1) shifts unselected coordinates by a median $11.1\%$ of nominal RMS through 32D LayerNorm coupling and does not lower $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($7.06\%$). Recentering and decoupling (Steps Q2--Q3) lower it to $6.71\%$ but cut causal F1 to $0.0826$. The split gate (Step Q4, accepted on $39/118$ streams) reaches $6.62\%$ with F1 unchanged ($0.0904$). Because ungated edits hurt $k=2$ causal $\text{F1}$ on $11/19$ development streams ($0.141\to 0.049$) and $k\in\{8,12\}$ cuts PR-AUC ($0.292\to 0.223$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} omits weight editing altogether.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate alarms either fire constantly without a quorum, almost never fire under multiplicity correction on short calibration sets, or lose their calibration out of sample. We compare three calibration regimes on the same 19-stream whitened residuals without test-label tuning (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Per-coordinate marginal union:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or even at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds false alarms across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Family-wise and false discovery rate (FDR) multiplicity control:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) across 128 coordinates or BH FDR ($q=0.005$) across 224 dials hits the discrete conformal $p$-value floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, paralyzing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{System-level matched nominal alarm rate:} Calibrating the aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) shows that discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ still jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ out of sample from boundary ties, and recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$. Among stable rules on this cache, the 160-dial panel ($\text{F1}=0.098$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=0.55\%$) beats 128-coordinate top-$k$ ($0.068$), $\max_j z_j$ ($0.056$), and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.072$), whereas Fisher pooling reaches higher Point-F1 ($0.109$; Stouffer $0.095$) at nearly double the held-out FPR ($1.05\%$ vs.\ $0.55\%$). On the 118 untouched archive streams using \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}'s frozen residuals (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($0.078$ F1, $65.0\%$ ER, $7.1\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) beats Fisher ($0.077$ F1, $61.1\%$ ER at $8.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; $+3.9$ ER points on $9/9$ datasets, sign-flip $p=0.0078$), Stouffer ($0.057$ F1, $55.8\%$ ER), raw top-$k$ ($0.069$ F1, $58.1\%$ ER; $+6.9$ ER points on $9/9$ datasets), $\max_j z_j$ ($0.063$ F1, $58.8\%$ ER), $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.071$ F1, $55.6\%$ ER), and BH-FDR conformal ($0.024$ F1, $14.6\%$ ER).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
A frozen or flatlined sensor barely moves the latent summary: terminal LayerNorm spreads the small change across all $32$ coordinates, so on \texttt{SMAP P-3} a flatline trips $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip, and a single faulty sensor is further diluted by the $K-1$ healthy channels. Extending Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) ($n{=}6$), we evaluate a causal channel branch across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$) without per-stream or test-label tuning. Per channel, three 64-step rolling statistics---log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, training-fit first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$---are thresholded at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ on nominal validation windows (floor $6.0$ MAD, $1.25\times$ half-split drift multiplier) with a $24$-of-$40$-step persistence gate. On the highest-variance active channel $c^\star=\arg\max_{c:\sigma_c^{\text{tr}}>10^{-8}}\sigma_c^{\text{tr}}$, we place three nonoverlapping $200$-step faults per stream inside clean test segments ($57$ events per class). For $s\le t<s+200$ using training $(\mu_c,\sigma_c,q_{.995,c})$:
\begin{equation}
\small
x'_{t,c^\star}=
\begin{cases}
\mu_{c^\star} & \text{variance collapse},\\
\mu_{c^\star}+1.35(-1)^{t-s}\sigma_{c^\star} & \text{prediction shock},\\
x_{s-1,c^\star} & \text{sensor freezing},\\
\max(q_{.995,c^\star},\mu_{c^\star}+2.2\sigma_{c^\star}) & \text{saturation},\\
x_{t,c^\star}+2.5\sigma_{c^\star}(t-s)/199 & \text{gradual degradation},\\
x_{t,c^\star}+1.5\sigma_{c^\star} & \text{isolated bias}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:single_channel_injection}
\end{equation}
Outside $[s,s+200)$, all channels remain unchanged. The variance-collapse union lifts injected-event recall for variance collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%$--$82.5\%$ to $93.0\%$--$94.7\%$, and the full channel branch lifts recall on shocks, gradual degradation, and bias from $59.6\%$--$75.4\%$ to $80.7\%$--$96.5\%$. However, on the \emph{unmodified} test streams, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union, $\text{F1}=0.070$) and $19.4\%$ (full branch, $\text{F1}=0.129$). Synthetic single-channel injections do not establish detection on natural sensor failures, and the full union's $19.4\%$ false-alarm rate makes it unsuitable as a default detector.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, \texttt{GECCO}; three seeds) shows a channel-count threshold: shell splitting improves high-channel spacecraft detection ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), but lowers F1 on both $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), setting the $K \ge 25$ spectral gate in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 16 matched-budget arms in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) on the development streams separates five contributing factors under these protocols (not universal causes): (1)~raw-observation forecasting and autoencoding share heavy tails ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), large drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and $\approx 25\%$ causal FPR; (2)~a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves FPR, yet raw targets keep drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$); (3)~learned $D{=}32$ latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179\text{--}0.190$ across multi-horizon JEPA tiers, $p \le 0.001$); (4)~linear PCA and Isolation Forest keep low causal FPR ($0.021$ and $0.039$) but reach only $0.017$ and $0.041$ causal Point-F1; and (5)~frozen targets and $\ell_2$ scoring retain $<2\%$ causal FPR, while latent context autoencoding lowers causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.161$ for TS-JEPA). The panels' gains do not come from lighter tails alone: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps roughly the same kurtosis as Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$).

\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark and Scope Boundaries}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports exploratory design-stage results on the 33-stream development suite (where stabilization and thresholds share $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} provides our primary generalization and calibration evidence under the disjoint five-block protocol across 118 untouched streams and all 141 archive streams. On a separate 130-stream buffered screen, TS-JEPA reaches stream-mean Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$. Spherical normalization alone gives no distributional guarantee; lower false alarms with latent scoring hold specifically under these protocols.

\noindent\emph{Design-stage configuration guidance:} Both tiers reach nearly identical unweighted buffered Point-F1 ($0.244$ vs.\ $0.242$) and causal ER ($67.3\%$ vs.\ $65.0\%$) on the 33-stream suite. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, however, halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.1\%$ vs.\ $12.8\%$) and leads $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.133$). We deploy \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} operationally and keep \emph{Laminar-JEPA} for ablation.

\noindent\emph{Slow-ramp boundary:} A $C=256$-step encoder cannot see drift slower than its window. Context and target summaries move together, yielding $0.000$ Point-F1 on all 14 GHL slow-ramp streams. Injecting linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and length $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ ($15$ events per cell) maps the cutoff: steep multi-channel ramps ($L=64$, $a\ge 1\sigma$) reach $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ ER at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay ($5.7\text{--}7.7$ points above background chance), $L=C$ falls to $0.5\text{--}2.5$ points above chance, and for $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$) chance-corrected detection vanishes ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points) across every JEPA variant.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Live streaming is much harder than retrospective review. Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent scores degrade under causal reporting: buffered attribution averages residuals across every overlapping window covering a timestamp, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step with no averaging, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the shared SPOT threshold (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). On these development streams, a rate-matched Bernoulli alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ hits $28.1\%$ of events at $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Exploratory Horizon Sweep Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Shorter horizons trade buffered smoothing for lower live lag. Because a target window of length $S$ reports causally at $\tau=t+S-1$ ($63$-step lag at $S=64$), we retrain on five exploratory streams across $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a); at $S=1$ all prefixes coincide, and at $S=4$ the shortest prefix spans two steps), where both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ with test FPR $\le 3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037,0.050$ vs.\ $0.014,0.012$; $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ ($0.030,0.036$; $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER), cutting delay at $S=4$ to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps, whereas buffered Point-F1 peaks at $0.290$ ($S=32$) and $0.255$ ($S=64$) versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot set a general rule.

\subsection{Design-Stage Operating Budgets and Fault Taxonomies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine the 19 development streams across budgets and fault types:
(1)~\emph{Diagnostic budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At a post-hoc matched $f^*=1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD.
(2)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range spikes ($>5\sigma$) are caught by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike, whereas in-range contextual faults alter cross-channel phase or velocity within normal bounds; on NASA contextual sequences \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet.

\subsection{Primary Held-Out Generalization, False-Alarm Tails, and Calibration Limits}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c) establish three primary findings under the disjoint five-block protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event-level gains, operating-point reconciliation, and calibration-length split (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}):} Across all 118 untouched streams (three seeds; on all 141 archive streams \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $.076/.156$ Point-F1 and $.636/.658$ ER vs.\ $.075/.146$ and $.512/.573$ for Multi-Horizon 1D), buffered and causal modes differ in how gains arise. Under \emph{buffered} attribution, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises ER from $59.0\%$ to $67.4\%$ ($+8.4$ points, cluster CI $[+2.3\%,+16.6\%]$) and Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.156$ ($p=0.022$) while \emph{lowering} buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($19.5\%\to 14.3\%$, median $8.7\%\to 4.8\%$, $p=0.0067$), and keeps a $+11.0$-point ER advantage at a post-hoc matched $5\%$ test FPR ($53.3\%$ vs.\ $42.2\%$, $p=1.2\times 10^{-5}$, $7/9$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Under live \emph{causal} streaming, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises autonomous ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ ($+11.3$ points, cluster CI $[+5.1\%,+21.8\%]$, $9/9$ datasets, sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and halves detection delay ($70.8$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps; $66$ vs.\ $136$ in the rerun, $p=4.8\times 10^{-4}$), but pooled causal Point-F1 improves only modestly ($0.076\to 0.079$, $\Delta=+0.003$, stream CI $[-0.019,+0.023]$, cluster CI $[-0.033,+0.060]$, $70/43$ wins, $p=0.014$) alongside higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($4.4\%\to 7.0\%$). Matching $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rates (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} still catches $+12.4$ points more causal events than rate-matched 1D ($65.0\%$ vs.\ $52.6\%$, $p=1.7\times 10^{-5}$), whereas at post-hoc equalized test FPR ($f^*\in\{1\%,5\%\}$) causal ER matches 1D ($72.6\%$ vs.\ $75.2\%$ at $5\%$, $p=0.20$). Splitting at the development-suite threshold $N_{\text{cal}}=500$ explains the modest pooled Point-F1: on adequately calibrated streams ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, $n=59$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} doubles causal Point-F1 over 1D ($0.079$ vs.\ $0.040$, $\Delta=+0.039$, CI $[+0.016,+0.064]$, $p=2.7\times 10^{-5}$; $+16.0$ ER points) and still wins without Exathlon ($n=43$: $0.051$ vs.\ $0.031$, $p=0.0050$, mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=1.56\%$), whereas on short histories ($N_{\text{cal}}<500$, $n=59$) 1D leads causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.080$, $p=0.31$; explaining why pooled non-Exathlon $n=101$ favors 1D on causal F1, $0.073$ vs.\ $0.060$, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered F1 $0.148$ vs.\ $0.139$ and causal ER $58.8\%$ vs.\ $50.6\%$). Disjoint quenching barely changes causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.90\%\to 6.83\%$, $p=0.55$) while costing $3.5$ ER points.
    \item \emph{Held-out false-alarm tail distribution, operational budgets, and autocorrelation (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a), Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c)):} Unlike the $\approx 1\%$ development results, archive causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ is strongly right-skewed across $n=118$ streams. Mean, median, $90$th, and $95$th percentiles are $(4.2\%,0.38\%,7.0\%,20.9\%)$ for TS-JEPA, $(4.4\%,0.39\%,6.8\%,23.7\%)$ for Multi-Horizon 1D, $(6.8\%,1.26\%,7.6\%,38.3\%)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, and $(7.0\%,0.78\%,10.6\%,50.2\%)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}. Stream counts exceeding $(1\%,2\%,5\%,10\%)$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ are $(29,21,14,10)$ for TS-JEPA, $(23,16,13,11)$ for Multi-Horizon 1D, $(67,41,16,12)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, and $(54,33,15,13)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}; thus $54.2\%$ of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} streams meet a $\le 1\%$ budget ($66.1\%$ when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$) and $87.3\%$ meet a $\le 5\%$ budget ($88.1\%$ when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$). Calibration size correlates inversely with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (Spearman $\rho=-0.32$, $p=5.2\times 10^{-4}$): $N_{\text{cal}}<250$ ($n=12$) averages $16.0\%$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ versus $1.05\%$ (median $0.48\%$) for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 2{,}500$ ($n=22$). Autocorrelation drives this finite-sample variance: on the 19 development streams, median Sokal $\tau_{\text{int}}$ is $2.51$ ($z_j$), $1.88$ ($N_{\text{edge}}$), $5.58$ ($N_{224}$, max $206.0$), and $6.75$ (1D), leaving median $N_{\text{eff}}$ of $(154.6,67.1,195.9)$ at median $N_{\text{cal}}=408$ and only $(2.32,0.34,0.20)$ expected tail exceedances ($12.7\%\text{--}25.0\%$ block-bootstrap CI half-widths). On short histories ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$, $n=18$), two-fold cross-fitting cuts causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$ at $69.9\%$ ER.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit:} Comparing chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits links excess FPR primarily to inter-block drift without ruling out tail misfit ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}-\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$ accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors vs.\ $18.7\%$ with centering). Within-checkpoint traces ($n{=}6$, $K\in\{9,25,55\}$) show tap fusion and centering lift anomaly divergence from $0.06\text{--}0.16$ to $0.70\text{--}0.94$ and cut mean coordinate $D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.140$ to $0.096$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied multi-horizon circuit-breaker panels (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for operational deployment; 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as an ablation reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 exploratory design-stage streams and 118 primary untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event coverage, buffered accuracy, and operating points:} Under the five-block archive protocol ($n=118$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises buffered ER ($59.0\%\to 67.4\%$) and Point-F1 ($0.143\to 0.156$) at lower held-out FPR ($19.5\%\to 14.3\%$), and raises live causal ER ($52.5\%\to 63.8\%$) across all nine datasets ($p=0.0039$), halves detection delay, and beats system-matched coordinate aggregators at $35\%\text{--}52\%$ lower parameter cost and $2.43\times$ speedup over \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. Pooled causal Point-F1 rises modestly ($0.076\to 0.079$), doubling on adequately calibrated streams ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$: $0.040\to 0.079$, $p=2.7\times 10^{-5}$), while weight quenching yields no reliable benefit.
    \item \emph{Contributing factors and latency:} Design-stage matched-backbone controls indicate that LayerNorm and centering contribute to lighter tails and lower drift under these protocols, and an exploratory five-stream sweep preserves $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 gains at $S\in\{1,4\}$.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tails, single-sensor, and ramp limits:} Archive causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has a low median ($0.78\%$; $87.3\%$ of streams $\le 5\%$) but a $7.0\%$ mean driven by short autocorrelated calibration blocks (two-fold cross-fitting cuts short-stream $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$). An optional channel branch helps synthetic single-sensor faults only at elevated FPR, and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL at $0.000$ Point-F1) and natural sensor failures remain open priorities.
\end{enumerate}
