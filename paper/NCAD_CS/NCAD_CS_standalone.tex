\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We investigate the Operator-Entropy Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (OpEntropy JEPA), which forecasts future telemetry states in an abstract latent space using a linear Koopman transition operator constrained by scale-free Von Neumann spectral entropy and a shifted logarithmic Bregman energy barrier, paired with causal coordinate saliency gating and a Fourier-domain dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, latent predictive scoring suppresses false alarms four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (7.6% to 8.6% nominal false-positive rate versus 30.0% for TimesNet and 30.7% for TranAD) and twelve- to eighteen-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.4% to 1.9% versus 22.2% to 24.3%). This operational false-alarm advantage does not establish general detection superiority: point-level F1, precision-recall ranking, and common-support matched-operating-point recall remain statistically comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled factorial and regularization-strength ablations show that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank and near-unit condition numbers without improving downstream detection, confirming statistical equivalence to unconstrained latent prediction within a 0.05 F1 margin. Static extreme-value calibration also fails to maintain its 0.1% design risk under deployment shift; under our exchangeable permutation protocol, inter-block distribution drift accounts for 84% to 96% of this overshoot. Latent residual conditioning suits supervisory monitoring of structured multi-sensor dynamics, whereas single-step horizons, quiet flatlines, and baseline ramps require complementary low-variance monitors or adaptive recalibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments (NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT, and biometric arrays), dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, where faults must be identified without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains ambient sensor jitter and channel-scale disparities, observation-space residuals exhibit severe excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with automated extreme-value thresholding such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), these heavy-tailed residuals suffer severe calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Novelty Positioning:} To suppress false-alarm flooding without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}, we formulate the \textit{Operator-Entropy Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture} (OpEntropy JEPA), which scores predictive discrepancies in a low-dimensional latent space \cite{lecun2022path}. Physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman operator entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) are evaluated strictly as algebraic priors on abstract latent coordinates, not as physical conservation laws. The contributions are listed below.

\subsection{Contributions}
This study makes four focused contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Operational False-Alarm Control Under Held-Out Calibration:} Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets (19 non-GHL at five seeds; 14 GHL at three to five seeds), latent predictive scoring via OpEntropy JEPA and unconstrained TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal false-positive rates four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution ($7.6\%\text{--}8.6\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ TimesNet and $30.7\%$ TranAD; paired reductions $-0.142$ to $-0.153$, $p \le 0.006$) and $12\times\text{--}18\times$ under strictly causal trailing streaming ($1.4\%\text{--}1.9\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). We report matched-budget operating characteristics ($q^* \in \{0.001, 0.005, 0.010, 0.050\}$) and common-support realized-FPR contrasts ($[0.10, 0.50]$), establishing that false-alarm suppression trades off against sensitivity on subtle faults and does not yield statistically significant point-F1 or PR-AUC superiority.
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy Theory and Factorial Regularizer Ablations:} We prove that the shifted Bregman barrier and non-degenerate cross-covariance prevent $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ collapse (Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse}) while distinguishing operator non-degeneracy, encoder representation diversity, and anomaly separability. Controlled factorial ablations (Entropy-Only, Barrier-Only, Both across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in [0.05, 2.00]$, Neither, plus Lyapunov covariance alignment and Cayley orthogonal-dissipative operators) show that spectral regularization drives operator effective rank to $31.97\text{--}32.00/32$ and condition number to $\kappa(\mathbf{K}) = 1.03\text{--}1.08$, but yields no aggregate Point-F1 gain over unconstrained TS-JEPA, confirming formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ F1 ($p \le 0.0054$).
    \item \textbf{Calibration Failure Under Shift and Drift Decomposition:} We demonstrate that static EVT calibration at design risk $q=10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) still suffers an empirical calibration failure under non-stationary deployment ($5.3\%\text{--}6.2\%$ buffered and $1.0\%\text{--}1.5\%$ causal FPR on 33 streams vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ and $16\%\text{--}18\%$ for reconstructors). Under our tested exchangeable permutation protocol, inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\%\text{--}96\%$ of this overshoot relative to sample scarcity and serial autocorrelation, without implying an identical drift fraction in every external deployment.
    \item \textbf{Horizon-Latency Trade-Offs, Mechanism Isolation, and Fault-Type Boundaries:} Evaluating prediction horizons $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ quantifies the trade-off between zero-wait emission ($S{=}1$) and multi-step smoothing ($S{=}64$). Controlled backbone and fault-type experiments show that low-dimensional LayerNorm bottlenecks mechanically compress kurtosis, learned representations govern threshold transfer, and quiet flatline faults ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ recall) are recovered by a complementary variance monitor only at about eight times the nominal FPR.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Dynamical Systems, Reconstruction, and Forecasting Detectors:} Multi-sensor telemetry models system dynamics as state trajectories $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$ \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ODEs \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize velocity fields in raw observation space, where integrating through high-frequency sensor noise induces numerical stiffness. Koopman theory and dynamic mode decomposition \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn linear operators on observable embeddings, identifying disruptions via spectral decoherence; our proposed Operator-Entropy JEPA architecture transfers this principle to a latent transition operator regularized by spectral entropy.

Reconstructive detectors model nominal patterns via observation reconstruction: LSTM forecasting with dynamic thresholding for spacecraft telemetry \cite{Hundman2018b}, association-discrepancy attention in Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, 2D FFT-periodicity variation in TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and adversarial sub-series attention in TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}. These share a vulnerability: point-level losses bias models toward stochastic noise, accumulating residual variance across irrelevant channels in high-dimensional arrays.

\noindent\textbf{Threshold Calibration, Contrastive, and Self-Supervised Detection:} Unsupervised monitoring requires automated rules to map residual scores to binary alarms without labels. Non-parametric thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} and Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fit Generalized Pareto tails under design risk $q$; however, EVT asymptotic guarantees assume i.i.d.\ samples, which temporal autocorrelation and operational drift violate.

To avoid reconstructing noise, contrastive methods train discriminative representations: NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} injects synthetic anomalies, yielding lower calibrated point detection than predictive models ($0.058$ vs.\ $0.160$ macro Point-F1 across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} maximizes dual-attention consistency ($0.070$ macro Point-F1). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target representations in latent space with non-contrastive collapse prevention \cite{zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening, bardes2021vicreg}. Time-series extensions \cite{yue2022ts2vec} evaluate ranking or post-hoc threshold sweeps without studying how latent residuals behave under automated tail calibration.

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Priors and Conceptual Positioning:} PINNs \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian/Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} embed physical conservation laws as rigid differential constraints; their stiff competing gradients \cite{wang2021understanding} and known-PDE requirements fit poorly to uncalibrated multi-sensor telemetry. We strictly distinguish \emph{physics-inspired} algebraic regularizers (where latent coordinates are abstract learned features, not physical state variables) from \emph{physics-informed} conservation constraints; \emph{causal data access} from \emph{score-emission latency}; and \emph{operator non-degeneracy} from \emph{encoder representation diversity} and \emph{anomaly separability}.

\section{Multi-Sensor IoT and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams sourced from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the UCI Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}: 33 streams are evaluated under the primary multi-seed held-out calibration protocol and 12 reserved for operational case studies. Streams span four physical domains: \emph{spacecraft telemetry} (SMAP and MSL entities, with two short-nominal MSL streams reserved; SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}); \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet FoG streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable}: 64~Hz tri-axial accelerometry for Freezing-of-Gait events, so $S{=}64$ is a 1-s horizon); \emph{industrial processes} (14 GHL gasoil heating-loop streams, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed, and GECCO water-distribution chemistry/hydrology at 1-minute resolution); and \emph{smart infrastructure} (Room-Occupancy environmental sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate}, six CICIDS edge network-flow streams, CalIt2 people-flow \cite{asuncion2007uci}, and Metro I-94 highway traffic).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences and labeled anomaly test sequences; channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatline phases. We evaluate the standardized subsets studied in the literature \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly} (of 55 SMAP and 27 MSL archive entities) for strict matching and reproducible runtime.

To eliminate circular in-sample threshold tuning, each stream's nominal sequence is partitioned chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Stream inclusion in the primary multi-seed held-out protocol was governed by two pre-specified criteria prior to model evaluation: (i)~a nominal sequence long enough to yield at least $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows after the $65\%/17.5\%/17.5\%$ split, and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed training and evaluation runtime. Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (6 Daphnet, $K{=}9$; 14 GHL, $K{=}16$; 4 MSL, $K{=}55$; 6 SMAP, $K{=}25$; Genesis, $K{=}18$; Metro, $K{=}5$; SWAN, $K{=}38$). The remaining 12 streams (CalIt2, $K{=}2$; two Room-Occupancy, $K{=}5$; two short-nominal MSL; GECCO, $K{=}9$; and six 72-channel CICIDS network flows) are evaluated in operational incident case studies and in a broader 11-dataset, 130-stream single-seed baseline cross-validation panel (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). The dyadic front-end ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)) uses a five-stream panel that includes one diagnostic SMD stream (\texttt{machine-1-2}, $K{=}38$).

Two external-validity caveats bound interpretation. First, SMAP and MSL contain documented mislabeled segments and near-trivial point anomalies \cite{wu2023flawed}; their per-dataset scores represent upper bounds on operational detectability. Second, because multiple streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share sensor hardware and environmental telemetry characteristics, streams within a dataset are not independent application domains. We therefore report unweighted Dataset-Macro means, per-dataset between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations, leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) domain sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), and restrict conclusions strictly to the evaluated entities and sampling regimes rather than extrapolating to entire mission archives.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a multivariate stream partitioned into context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize pointwise error $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}\| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2$, which can weight channels with different noise scales unequally; whether this consumes model capacity is not tested here. We instead adopt the JEPA framework \cite{lecun2022path}: a context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an EMA momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}}$, with $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$, $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$. The objective forecasts target representations in latent space to filter out stochastic sensor noise and condition prediction residuals for automated tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We formulate Operator-Entropy JEPA as our primary architecture, and evaluate it alongside an unconstrained baseline (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and two comparative physical formulations (Reynolds-Stress and Potential-Flow):

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, where nonlinear trajectories are mapped via linear operators on observable functions, we parameterize the predictive latent transition via a learned operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent collapse onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or to zero (energy collapse), the regularizer decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ eigenvalues of $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^\top$, and $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ total energy. To ensure collapsing modes ($\lambda_i \to 0^+$) receive strictly negative gradients ($\partial \Delta\mathcal{H}/\partial \lambda_i < 0$) without sign reversal, we form the smoothed modal distribution $p_i = (\lambda_i + \epsilon_{\log}) / \sum_{j=1}^D (\lambda_j + \epsilon_{\log})$ ($\epsilon_{\log} = 10^{-12}$) and Von Neumann spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D p_i \log p_i$. The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} measures Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while rank-1 collapse yields $\Delta\mathcal{H} \approx \log D$. Minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ drives $\mathbf{K}$ toward isometric modal equipartition rather than low-rank truncation, acting directly on learned embeddings.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) without explosive gain or zero-gradient clamping, we impose a smooth shifted Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log\bigl(r(\mathbf{K}) + \epsilon\bigr) + \log(1 + \epsilon) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $r(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / (\gamma D)$ and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. This barrier is $C^\infty$ smooth for $r \ge 0$, vanishes at $r = 1$, caps zero-energy penalty at $\approx 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$, and has radial derivative $\partial\Phi_{\text{energy}}/\partial r|_{r=0} = 1 - 1/\epsilon < 0$. With $\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$, $r \approx 1$ and initial barrier penalty vanishes. The composite regularizer is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
and the training objective minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b}\| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{Lyap}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}}$ ($\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0, \lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5, \beta_{\text{Lyap}} = 0.02$). The optional discrete Lyapunov defect aligns batch covariances, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyap}} = \|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{tgt}}\|_F^2 / \bigl(D\,(\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{tgt}}/D + 10^{-6})^2\bigr)$, where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{tgt}}$ are the empirical covariances of $\mathbf{K}z_{\text{ctx}}$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$ over the batch. Shared representation stages are fixed across controls. $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts on $\mathbf{K}$ only; encoder collapse is mitigated empirically by EMA target dynamics and stop-gradient operations \cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Triviality and Barrier Behavior]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$, and non-degenerate coupling $\mathbf{M} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$. Then: (i) under the shifted barrier ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$), the penalty at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ is finite ($\approx 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$) with $\partial\Phi_{\text{energy}}/\partial r|_{r=0} = 1 - 1/\epsilon < 0$, and $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is neither a stationary point nor a local or global minimizer; and (ii) as $\epsilon \to 0^+$, $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) At $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$, $r=0$ and $\Phi_{\text{energy}} = -1 - \log\epsilon + \log(1+\epsilon) \approx 12.8$. Because $r$ depends on $\mathbf{K}$ only through $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{K}}\Phi_{\text{energy}} = 2\Phi'(r)\,\mathbf{K}/(\gamma D)$ vanishes at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ even though $\Phi'(0) < 0$. The entropy deficiency is the relative entropy of the density matrix $\boldsymbol{\rho} = (\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^\top + \epsilon_{\log}\mathbf{I})/\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^\top + \epsilon_{\log}\mathbf{I})$ to $\mathbf{I}/D$; it is smooth in $\mathbf{K}$, nonnegative, and zero at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$, so its gradient vanishes there. The data term has gradient $\frac{2}{BD}\sum_b(\mathbf{K}z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}){z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top = -2\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$ at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$. The total gradient is therefore nonzero, so $\mathbf{0}$ is not stationary, hence not a local or global minimizer. (ii) For $\epsilon = 0$, $\Phi_{\text{energy}} = r - 1 - \log r \to +\infty$ as $r \to 0^+$, while the data term and $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ are nonnegative, so $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) \ge \lambda_{\text{ent}}\beta_{\text{norm}}\Phi_{\text{energy}} \to +\infty$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Three Distinct Properties):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} concerns \emph{operator non-degeneracy} only. It does not establish \emph{representation diversity} (the effective rank of $z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}}$) or \emph{anomaly separability}. If $E_\theta$ maps all inputs to a constant, $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ attains zero loss with a perfectly uniform operator spectrum. Equipartition of the singular values of $\mathbf{K}$ also does not force the encoder to spread trajectories across all $D$ coordinates: a flat operator spectrum is compatible with latent trajectories concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace, which is what we measure ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}}){\approx}5.2$ and $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}){\approx}10.1$ of $32$; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}c). Representation non-collapse rests empirically on stop-gradient momentum dynamics, and Section~\ref{sec:ablations} reports operator rank, condition number, and representation rank for every regularizer arm.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary Quadratic Second-Moment Head}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Inspired by Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence, which separates mean flow from velocity fluctuations, we evaluate an auxiliary quadratic second-moment head in latent space. The observed matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$ ($d_s = 16$, via projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$) is an instantaneous rank-1 projected outer product plus ridge penalty, not a physical turbulent covariance. A closure head outputs lower-triangular $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ reconstructing predicted covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}$. The objective minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}})$. At test time, scoring adds the second-moment discrepancy: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$.

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory and Flow Matching)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition a smooth velocity field splits into a curl-free gradient and a solenoidal part. We parameterize $\hat{v} = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi$ via a residual MLP conditioned on state $z_t$, flow time $t \in [0,1]$, context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a regime projection selected among $K_r = 4$ orthonormal subspaces ($d{=}8, D{=}32$) with tangent projection $v_\perp = v - \langle u, v\rangle u$, $u = z_t/\|z_t\|_2$. Training uses conditional flow matching along linear paths from noise $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ to $z_{\text{tgt}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \| \hat{v} - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2$, and inference queries the field at the trajectory midpoint, $\mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}}, 0.5, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - z_{\text{tgt}}$. The curl-free bias is hypothesized to penalize cyclic nominal orbits and degrade detection on periodic kinematics (Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors}). 

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed at stride 1. For TS-JEPA and Potential-Flow, the score gates the residual $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ (or whitened $\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{1/2}(\mathbf{e}(t) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_r)$) via $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}): $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$. Because final LayerNorm projects embeddings to radius $\sqrt{D}$, Operator-Entropy JEPA tracks pre-LayerNorm radial energy scaled by window RMS ($e_{\text{ctx}}, e_{\text{tgt}}$) with $e_{\text{pred}} = e_{\text{ctx}}\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}\|_2 / (\|z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2 + 10^{-6})$, scoring $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t)) + 0.2\,|e_{\text{tgt}}(t) - e_{\text{pred}}(t)| / (e_{\text{pred}}(t) + 10^{-6})$. For Reynolds-Stress, $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t)) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. Shared stages are fixed across controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
To resolve the methodological confounding between data availability, decision latency, and evaluation attribution, we formalize detection under three operational criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$, the model observes only history $\mathbf{X}_{:\tau} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:\tau, :]$. Neither encoder accesses future observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps):} Because suspect representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$ encodes the horizon $\mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :]$, window score $s_t$ cannot be computed until timestep $\tau = t + S - 1$. This imposes an inherent emission latency of $S - 1 = 63$ steps: $0.98\,\text{s}$ on Daphnet ($64\,\text{Hz}$), $10.5\,\text{min}$ on SMAP, MSL, and GHL under standard 10-s ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$) sampling \cite{Hundman2018b, zhou2026mtsbench}, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO ($1\,\text{sample/min}$), and $63\,\text{h}$ on Metro ($1\,\text{sample/h}$). This latency guarantees zero future lookahead, but must not be confused with instantaneous detection at fault inception: batch scoring over horizon $S{=}64$ fits supervisory health monitoring and slow subsystem drift where anomaly persistence distinguishes faults from noise, but cannot provide sub-second emergency tripwires on low-frequency telemetry. Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency} quantifies how shortening $S$ trades this latency against false-alarm control.
    \item \textbf{Alarm attribution convention:} We distinguish two attribution regimes: (1)~\emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, where score $s_t$ is emitted and evaluated strictly at $\tau$, providing zero-lookahead online monitoring; and (2)~\emph{retrospective buffered smear attribution}, where $s_t$ is attributed across the entire suspect window $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$. Under unadjusted point evaluation, buffered alarms in $[t^*-S, t^*]$ register as false alarms: buffering provides fixed-lag detection, not early warning. The two regimes represent distinct operational realities and are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}a,b).
\end{enumerate}




\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, the latent JEPA architectures incorporate shared inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and the Auxiliary Channel-Gate Variant}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults frequently originate in a localized subsystem while the remaining channels exhibit ambient noise, diluting localized anomalies in isotropic representations. The deployed detector mitigates this in latent space via an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e})$ on error vectors $\mathbf{e} = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \\
    w_d &= \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)}, \nonumber
\end{align}
amplifying sparse coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$.  We fix blend parameter $\alpha = 0.5$ and softmax temperature $\tau = 0.5$ across all latent predictors. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) evaluate unweighted observation errors and do not use the gate. This is the discrepancy gate used in every latent experiment: input channels are encoded unweighted, and the gate acts only on the $D$-dimensional predictive residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary Input-Channel Gate (Evaluated Variant)}
A strictly causal gate weights each channel by the sigmoid of its standardized context-window variance share, $g_k = \sigma\!\left((s_k - \bar{s})/(\sigma_s + \epsilon)\right)$, applied symmetrically to both encoders so that $\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$ (identity at $K{=}1$). It cannot isolate quiet faults (recall $0.02$--$0.07$ under every gating regime, Section~\ref{sec:ablations}); the deployed pipeline weights inputs uniformly.



\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Anomalies span temporal scales from sensor spikes to thermal drifts; the encoder front-end therefore splits each channel into $J{=}4$ smooth dyadic shells on an $N_{\text{fft}}=256$ rFFT grid, normalized bin-wise into an exact amplitude partition of unity, $\sum_j \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ (measured deviation $< 1.2\times10^{-7}$). Each shell feeds a causal 1D convolution ($k \in \{15,9,5,3\}$ coarse-to-fine), recombined by learned scale routing. Because the shells overlap smoothly at band edges, the front-end is \emph{less} frequency-localized than standard wavelet banks. We therefore describe it as a Fourier-domain multiscale front-end, not an exact Littlewood--Paley octave decomposition, and measure its empirical contribution by matched ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b).

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}: excesses over a high threshold $u$ (98th percentile) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we evaluate an empirical heuristic multiplier $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot [ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) ]$ governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals: on heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ rises to suppress volatile noise; on light-tailed ones, it tightens for sensitivity.

The multiplier is an engineering heuristic for pre-asymptotic tails, not an EVT theorem. The primary pipeline uses standard SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and evaluates the multiplier only as a sensitivity arm (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}), where it degrades out-of-sample risk control ($5.26\% \to 17.68\%$ FPR).

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted via a multiscale dyadic front-end and causal dilated TCN (6 residual blocks, $F{=}48$, kernel $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$); multi-statistic pooling (terminal, mean, max, and channel statistics) projects the $C{=}256$ context through a linear bottleneck recording pre-LayerNorm radial energy before LayerNorm and dropout ($p{=}0.20$). The momentum target encoder uses cosine EMA $m_{\text{base}}{=}0.996 \to m_{\text{max}}{=}0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's nominal telemetry is partitioned into three disjoint chronological blocks: \emph{training} (first 65\%), \emph{nominal calibration} (next 17.5\%), and \emph{nominal evaluation} (final 17.5\%, separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap). The training block optimizes representation weights alone. Withheld from training, the nominal calibration block fits per-stream GPD tail parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}0.98$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$, estimating $\gamma_2$ strictly within this interval. The nominal evaluation block then measures held-out FPR on telemetry unobserved during threshold selection. Thresholds remain fixed during test evaluation to measure static EVT stability under drift; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis (Section~\ref{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}).

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment}
All experiments use standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier) on held-out nominal splits, except the rolling-SPOT incident analysis and the calibrator comparison itself (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}); no table mixes calibrators across models.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Multiplicity Controls}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are trained across five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 large GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) use three seeds (five on the two smallest) for cost, and ablation controls use three. We report held-out nominal FPR, Point-F1, and PR-AUC as distinct outcomes; nonsignificant detection differences are not evidence of parity. 

Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Within each stream, per-cell FPR intervals use a moving-block bootstrap ($L{=}64$) over the false-alarm indicator sequence; across streams, paired contrasts resample stream differences (2{,}000 iterations, seeds averaged within stream). The independent statistical unit is the telemetry stream ($n{=}19$ non-GHL at five seeds; $n{=}33$ where GHL enters at available coverage), with dataset-clustered checks reported to account for shared sensor environments. For general detector contrasts, nonsignificant tests do not establish equivalence; instead, we report paired differences with effect sizes and CIs. Across general contrasts, $p$-values are Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted at $q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$; CIs spanning zero or adjusted $p > 0.05$ are treated as inconclusive. For physical regularizer variants, where hypotheses test whether additions alter baseline performance, we complement Wilcoxon tests with formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) against TS-JEPA: the null hypothesis $H_0: |\mu_1 - \mu_2| \ge \Delta$ tests non-equivalence against $H_1: |\mu_1 - \mu_2| < \Delta$ under an equivalence margin of $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ Point-F1. Every reported test is stated in the text with its endpoint, cohort, number of paired streams, raw and adjusted $p$, and effect-size interval.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Parameter Capacities}
To isolate algorithmic principles from implementation-specific discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline with identical windowing ($C=256, S=64$), standardization, and EVT calibration. TimesNet and TranAD are in-house re-implementations configured to published architectural depths and evaluated under a shared optimization budget (AdamW, $\eta=10^{-3}$, cosine decay, $20$ epochs); no method received per-model hyperparameter tuning beyond the shared configuration, ensuring that differences reflect representation and scoring dynamics rather than disparate tuning budgets. While this standardized budget ensures equal training exposure, it is a controlled benchmarking standard rather than bespoke per-model search, and three checks bound its influence. (i)~\emph{Training-budget sensitivity} (7 streams, 3 seeds, held-out SPOT): halving training from 20 to 10 epochs moves the JEPA-versus-reconstructor mean Point-F1 from $0.048$ vs.\ $0.154$ to $0.038$ vs.\ $0.158$ and held-out FPR from $0.092$ vs.\ $0.333$ to $0.087$ vs.\ $0.333$, so the false-alarm gap does not depend on the epoch count. (ii)~\emph{Native windows}: at the baselines' native horizons (TranAD $W{=}10$, TimesNet $W{=}96$) tail heaviness persists (excess kurtosis $\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and $98.14$, TimesNet FPR $44.09\%$), against $\gamma_2 = 3.14$ for TS-JEPA, so the long window does not explain it. (iii)~\emph{Published-score replication}: under the same pipeline the baselines replicate published Point-Adjusted F1 scores on SMD and MSL, and Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) extends the comparison to NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer on 130 streams. Per-window inference takes $0.60$--$0.84$~ms for the JEPAs at $183.8$--$185.3$~MB peak memory on an RTX 5090.

\noindent\textbf{Compared Models:} Operator-Entropy JEPA ($285{,}701$ parameters at $K{=}25$, $-1.16\%$ vs.\ the unconstrained TS-JEPA at $289{,}061$); physical controls Reynolds-Stress ($+2.4\%$) and Potential-Flow ($+17.0\%$); observation-space baselines TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} ($648{,}553$) and TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} ($205{,}746$); a ridge-regularized Linear AR Forecaster; and non-parametric and score-bound mechanism controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}).

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Critical surveys \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards} document severe reporting vulnerabilities in this literature (point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, threshold gaming), so we report metrics across complementary tiers. \emph{Exploratory operating characteristics}: event recall over nominal calibration budgets and over a realized-test-FPR interval $[0.10,0.50]$, interpolated within stream--seed curves only where both bounds are observed. \emph{Secondary endpoints}: Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (unweighted mean across datasets, preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating); channel-macro Point-F1; PR-AUC (average precision, ranking quality independent of calibration); event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments containing $\ge1$ alarm); mean detection delay $\Delta t$ (onset-to-first-alarm lag over detected events only); and the trivial all-positive reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at base rate $\pi$; models below it underperform a constant alarm. Pooled micro-F1 exposes flood-prone models; contiguous PA-F1 and test-sweep PA-F1 quantify the residual calibration gap.

\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone. Because controls run three seeds against the five-seed principal runs, the paired control means below are computed on the shared three-seed subset and differ slightly from the five-seed Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) rows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$:} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.241 \pm 0.014$ vs. $0.244 \pm 0.024$; difference $-0.003$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.011, +0.005]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.62$); PR-AUC stays $0.347\text{--}0.348$.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$:} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.203 \pm 0.059$ vs. $0.208 \pm 0.071$ ($+0.005$, $[-0.006, +0.016]$, $p=0.41$).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing shifts Point-F1 to $0.129$ from $0.096$ on the shared three-seed subset ($p_{\text{raw}}=0.048, p_{\text{BH}}=0.24$; $0.089$ at five seeds in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)); both underperform TS-JEPA ($0.234$, $p_{\text{raw}}=0.013, p_{\text{BH}}=0.24$, non-significant under FDR adjustment). 
\end{enumerate}
Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction. Formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) reject non-equivalence ($H_0: |\Delta| \ge 0.05$) and confirm statistical equivalence to TS-JEPA within a tight $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ Point-F1 margin: Operator-Entropy achieves $p = 0.0009$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.014, +0.021]$, also passing $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ with $p=0.0283$) and Reynolds-Stress achieves $p = 0.0054$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.042, +0.007]$). These null results establish practical equivalence rather than an empirical advantage.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Factorial Isolation of the Entropy Term and Energy Barrier}
\label{subsec:factorial}
A single on/off control cannot separate the spectral-entropy term from the energy barrier or show how either scales. Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) crosses the two terms (neither, entropy only, barrier only, both) and sweeps $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00\}$ with the barrier on, on six streams at three seeds with a $D{=}32$ operator. Each arm reports operator rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K})$, condition number $\kappa(\mathbf{K})$, representation rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}})/\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$, the coefficient of variation $\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}$ of the training loss as a stability proxy, and held-out calibration and detection outcomes in both attribution regimes.

Three observations follow. First, the unconstrained operator is already near-isotropic ($\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}){=}31.83$ of $32$, $\kappa{=}1.235$) because $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized at the identity; the regularizers tighten it only marginally ($31.97$ and $\kappa{=}1.083$ at the deployed $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0.50$, $\kappa{=}1.034$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}2.00$) and lower $\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}$ from $1.013$ to $0.917$. Second, the encoder representation is unaffected: $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ stays within $5.15\text{--}5.34$ and $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ within $9.92\text{--}10.07$ of $32$ in every arm. Operator non-degeneracy, representation diversity, and anomaly separability are therefore separate properties, and only the first moves. Third, no arm or strength improves detection: buffered Point-F1 spans $0.076\text{--}0.094$ and causal Point-F1 $0.022\text{--}0.028$ with no monotone trend in $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$, buffered FPR spans $0.066\text{--}0.104$, and the unconstrained arm has the lowest buffered FPR of all arms ($0.066$) and the second-highest causal event recall ($0.516$, behind $0.544$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}1.00$). The entropy term is a well-posed constraint that preserves operator conditioning, not a demonstrated source of detection or calibration gain.

\noindent\textbf{Decoupling.} On eight streams (causal trailing), adding covariance coupling ($\alpha_Z{=}0.005$) and the Lyapunov defect ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}{=}0.02$) cut delay from $112.0$ (single-operator OpEntropy) to $30.1$ steps at event recall $0.433$ and FPR $0.021$, against $85.1$, $0.376$, and $0.018$ for unconstrained TS-JEPA, at some cost in Point-F1 ($0.050$ vs.\ $0.060$--$0.062$). These exploratory contrasts shift the recall-delay trade-off and are not part of the headline results.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating and Quiet-Fault Reference Monitors}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling, a non-causal target-only leaky control, and independent context/target gating under an identical TS-JEPA configuration (seed 42, four streams). Macro Point-F1 ranks uniform weighting ($0.118$) above context gating ($0.077$), with the leaky control at $0.128$ and independent gating weakest ($0.071$); the effect is stream-dependent in sign (MSL \texttt{M-1}: $0.047 \to 0.227$; SMAP \texttt{P-3}: $0.280 \to 0.019$). The gate is an evaluated variant; the deployed pipeline retains uniform input weighting.

Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d) injects six single-channel fault classes into six multivariate streams. The variance gate roughly doubles point recall relative to uniform pooling (macro $0.045$ vs.\ $0.023$) at about $3.6\times$ the nominal FPR ($0.069$ vs.\ $0.019$), and a median-absolute-deviation variant is marginally stronger ($0.060$ recall). Quiet faults (flatline, stuck value, dropout) stay weakly detected under every latent gating regime (point recall $0.02\text{--}0.07$). Two observation-space reference monitors test whether a complementary score can cover this blind spot: a rolling channel-variance floor (\texttt{lowvar}) and a maximum channel $z$-deviation (\texttt{dev\_maxz}). The variance floor reaches point recall $0.18$ ($0.32$ on mid-variance flatlines) and the $z$-deviation monitor $0.09\text{--}0.11$, but at nominal FPR of $0.14\text{--}0.16$, roughly eight times that of the latent detector. A complementary variance monitor recovers part of the quiet-fault gap only at a substantial alarm cost, so quiet-fault coverage remains an open limitation.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a modest, stream-dependent effect (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)): macro buffered Point-F1 improves from $0.088$ to $0.131$ across a five-stream exploratory panel (including two diagnostic streams, SMD and GECCO, outside the 33 primary streams), concentrated on MSL and SMD, with a small SMAP regression. The block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters and shell overlap prevents octave isolation, so it is a modest contributor, not the primary driver.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
To discriminate the operational mechanisms driving tail conditioning and threshold transfer, all 19 non-GHL streams were re-run under the primary held-out protocol (five seeds, identical windows, AdamW budget, and SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$ calibration) across controlled backbone arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, three dilated TCN layers): TCN-Obs-Recon (observation reconstruction), TCN-Obs-Pred (observation forecasting), JEPA-CtxRecon (latent autoencoding of EMA-encoded \emph{context} without foresight), JEPA-NoEMA (latent prediction with hard-copy target), and TS-JEPA. 

\noindent\textbf{Mechanism Discrimination: Foresight vs. Geometry vs. Dynamics:} The controls are consistent with three distinct operational factors:
(1)~\emph{Future temporal forecasting is not uniquely necessary for score conditioning}: JEPA-CtxRecon autoencodes context representations without forecasting future timesteps, yet achieves an FPR reduction of $0.202$ ($0.310 \to 0.108$ relative to TCN-Obs-Pred) and higher event recall ($0.513$ vs.\ $0.473$ for TS-JEPA on this controlled panel; $0.481$ on the primary benchmark). Latent representation compression, rather than future-state extrapolation, conditions scores for tail estimation.
(2)~\emph{Bounded score geometry mechanically suppresses tail kurtosis}: LayerNorm bottlenecks compress excursions regardless of architecture (TCN-ObsPred-LN $\gamma_2 = 0.14$ vs.\ $8.24$ unnormalized; $1.14$ for TS-JEPA).
(3)~\emph{Representation dynamics and conservative subspace transfer}: kurtosis suppression alone cannot prevent threshold transfer breakdown across temporal blocks. TCN-ObsPred-LN suffers held-out nominal FPR of $0.186$ (buffered) and $0.117$ (causal trailing), versus $0.080$ and $0.014$ for TS-JEPA. At conservative budgets, PCA-LinPred-LN and IsoForest collapse to event recalls of $0.665$ and $0.631$ versus $0.774$ for TS-JEPA ($0.768$ ungated). Representation rank diagnostics show $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$ occupy $5.6\text{--}6.1$ and $8.5\text{--}14.0$ of 32 dimensions, so low nominal FPR is consistent with conservative subspace projection and bounded score geometry rather than an exclusive property of predictive dynamics.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$), with buffered (a) and strictly causal (b) attribution reported separately. The principal finding is false-alarm control; the evidence for general detection superiority is inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} Latent predictive scoring via Operator-Entropy JEPA and TS-JEPA substantially suppresses false alarms under static calibration: on 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076\text{--}0.086$ versus $0.300$ for TimesNet and $0.307$ for TranAD (a four-fold reduction buffered, and $12$--$18\times$ causal: $0.014\text{--}0.019$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.243$). Across all 33 streams the paired buffered reduction is significant: OpEntropy JEPA achieves $-0.144$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.241, -0.065]$, $p=0.006$) and $-0.142$ vs.\ TranAD ($p=0.006$); TS-JEPA yields $-0.153$ ($p=0.002$) and $-0.151$ ($p=0.003$). Clustering by dataset ($N=7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$. The advantage is stable under leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) resampling: across the seven folds, the six-dataset macro buffered FPR stays $0.038\text{--}0.070$ (OpEntropy) and $0.038\text{--}0.061$ (TS-JEPA) against $0.084\text{--}0.176$ (TimesNet) and $0.098\text{--}0.183$ (TranAD). The per-dataset panel (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}c) shows the limits: on MSL, TimesNet records lower buffered FPR than OpEntropy ($0.133$ vs.\ $0.191$), and on Genesis, Metro, and SWAN the reconstructive baselines record zero FPR together with zero F1, which reflects inactivity rather than calibration. The controlled backbone panel (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}) examines the mechanism.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not establish superior anomaly detection. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences remain inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$). Between-stream SDs in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c) are as large as the means (for example SMAP $0.415 \pm 0.389$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $0.289 \pm 0.334$ for TimesNet), so per-dataset rankings are not resolved. At the dataset level only SMAP exceeds the constant-alarm reference for the latent models ($0.415$ and $0.401$ vs.\ $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}{=}0.236$); MSL ($0.237$, $0.303$ vs.\ $0.381$), Daphnet, and SWAN fall below it, and their PR-AUC lead over prevalence is the more informative signal (SMAP $0.504$/$0.525$ at $\pi{=}0.150$; MSL $0.434$/$0.422$ at $\pi{=}0.255$, against $0.455$/$0.311$ for TimesNet). Detection superiority is unproven on point-level metrics. The matched operating-point comparison is in Section~\ref{subsec:matched_operating}.







\noindent\textbf{Regimes of Applicability:} Spacecraft telemetry (SMAP, MSL) shows the largest PR-AUC lead over prevalence and over the reconstructors, though F1 margins sit within between-stream SD and the labels carry known defects \cite{wu2023flawed}. Quiet faults and subtle low-frequency drifts are filtered with ambient noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}d). High-frequency process noise and continuous baseline ramps (GHL, Metro) defeat every detector ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ Point-F1), and on high-prevalence streams (Daphnet $\pi{=}14.8\%$, SWAN $\pi{=}27.9\%$) all detectors score near or below a constant alarm.

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data do not support an aggregate gain:
Paired differences against TS-JEPA are $+0.002$ Point-F1 for Operator-Entropy (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$; PR-AUC $+0.002$, $p{=}0.86$; FPR $+0.009$, $p{=}0.75$; leads on 9 of 33 streams), $-0.015$ for Reynolds-Stress ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$; leads on 11 of 33), and $-0.081$ for Potential-Flow ($p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Potential-Flow's higher event-recall area over nominal causal budgets is descriptive; without matched realized-FPR support and multiplicity-adjusted evidence, neither an intrinsically stronger detector nor a purely operating-point explanation is established.

These findings align with the matched-capacity controls and the factorial sweep in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,c): the physical regularizers provide no aggregate detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction, and the entropy term changes operator conditioning without changing representation rank or detection. We therefore treat the regularizer as a theoretically motivated constraint, not a demonstrated source of improvement.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

The strictly causal results are the primary operational evaluation, and buffered results are retrospective. Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b) reports the benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression; on the 14 GHL streams, causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and causal event recall is $46.55\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $58.82\%$ for TimesNet: without a lookahead buffer, latent predictive scoring is an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of detection sensitivity. Rolling (dynamic) SPOT on a five-stream incident cohort suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at a recall cost.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
A causal model is not automatically a low-latency one: with $S{=}64$ a score is emitted $63$ steps after the window opens, which is $10.5$~min on 10-s telemetry and $63$~h on hourly Metro data. Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains OpEntropy JEPA, TS-JEPA, and TimesNet at $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams at three seeds. Shortening the horizon does not erode the false-alarm advantage: at $S{=}1$, which has no emission lag, OpEntropy keeps causal held-out FPR at $0.005$ (TimesNet $0.025$) and event recall $0.729$ (TimesNet $0.895$). Causal FPR for the reconstructor rises steeply with horizon ($0.025 \to 0.213$), whereas the latent predictors stay at $0.003\text{--}0.020$. Event recall is non-monotone for the latent models ($0.729, 0.624, 0.257, 0.219, 0.410$ for OpEntropy) and falls to $0.22\text{--}0.26$ at intermediate horizons, and delays over detected events ($76$--$398$ steps) differ across detected sets and carry no clean trend. Buffered attribution inflates FPR with $S$ ($0.005 \to 0.099$ for OpEntropy) because each score is painted over $S$ steps. The sweep covers five streams, so it shows that short horizons are feasible with the false-alarm advantage intact, not that detection delay improves. Where early response matters on low-rate telemetry, $S{=}1$ to $4$ is the natural setting to evaluate first, at the recall level shown.

\subsection{Detection Quality at Matched Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We fit empirical-quantile thresholds on nominal budgets $q\in[10^{-4},0.9]$ and report event recall, precision, and alarm volume at fixed budgets (Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}b). At every causal budget the latent predictors raise fewer alarms than the reconstructors at comparable or higher precision: at $q^*{=}0.05$, TS-JEPA reaches event recall $0.774$ at $179.8$ alarms per 1{,}000 steps (precision $0.257$) against $0.618$ at $262.3$ ($0.221$) for TimesNet. Buffered at $q^*{=}0.001$ the trade-off reverses on recall ($0.486$ vs.\ $0.583$) while precision is higher ($0.277$ vs.\ $0.194$) and alarm volume lower ($207.9$ vs.\ $259.2$). These are matched calibration budgets, not matched realized FPR (at $q^*{=}0.05$ realized FPR is $0.180$ vs.\ $0.262$). Given multi-hundred-step anomalies (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), alarming at $30\%$ FPR catches $>95\%$ of segments by chance ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$); TimesNet's $58\%$ recall at $30\%$ FPR falls within this random expectation.

Whether latent prediction detects more events at comparable realized alarm rates remains unresolved for lack of common support. Interpolating stream--seed curves over realized FPR $[0.10,0.50]$ without extrapolation, the causal TS-JEPA--TimesNet difference is $+0.061$ (95\% dataset-bootstrap CI $[-0.153,0.329]$; sign-permutation $p{=}0.625$; 20 paired cells across four streams). Nominal-budget orderings do not establish a causal-regime advantage.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out of sample? It does not. At design risk $q{=}10^{-3}$, observed held-out FPR is $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ buffered and $1.0\text{--}1.5\%$ causal for the JEPAs, which is a calibration failure under distribution shift: the latent models overshoot the target by one to two orders of magnitude and are only less wrong than the reconstructors ($\approx 22\%$ buffered, $16\text{--}18\%$ causal). Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under strictly causal trailing and buffered smear inference. The calibrator evaluated is the \emph{static} peaks-over-threshold step of SPOT, a single threshold fit once on the calibration block. Three realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Causal vs.\ buffered smear overshoot:} Under causal trailing, TS-JEPA reaches held-out FPR $0.0101$ (median $0.0002$, IQR $[0.0000, 0.0025]$); buffered inference smears each alarm over $S{=}64$ steps and reaches $0.0526$, below the $S \cdot q = 6.4\%$ smear ceiling. TimesNet and TranAD exceed that ceiling even causally ($0.1795$, $0.1601$), which indicates distributional breakdown.
    \item \textbf{Parity with other rules:} Empirical p99.5, validation-maximum, and split conformal thresholding ($\alpha{=}10^{-3}$) also overshoot because drift violates exchangeability, and the kurtosis-adaptive multiplier raises TS-JEPA buffered FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$.
\end{enumerate}

Empirical tail diagnostics on nine non-GHL streams confirm tail-sample scarcity: median exceedances above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and Grimshaw returns degenerate $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits. 

To test whether drift or scarcity dominates, an exchangeable permutation control uniformly samples calibration and evaluation splits from pooled nominal blocks. Under point exchangeability, causal TS-JEPA FPR drops from $0.0121$ ($12.1\times$ overshoot) to $0.0019$ ($1.9\times$ overshoot); TimesNet drops from $0.0872$ to $0.0037$ (a $96\%$ reduction). Block permutation ($L{=}64$) yields intermediate overshoot ($5.8\times$ for TS-JEPA). This indicates that inter-block drift dominates calibration failure \emph{under the tested permutation protocol}, with autocorrelation secondary; it does not show that drift accounts for the same share of failures in other deployments. Across $N=1080$ dumped cells, KS drift between calibration and test blocks predicts realized FPR (Spearman $\rho = +0.377$, 95\% CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$).

The controlled-drift stress test (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}b) injects nine shift conditions into real held-out residual streams (16 streams, design $q{=}10^{-3}$); runs declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ effective exceedances per cell. Static SPOT already sits at $0.061$ FPR with no injected shift. Rolling calibrators hold risk to $1$--$7\%$ under step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33$--$0.39$ FPR), so adaptive tracking helps for discrete shifts and not for within-block drift.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We formulated Operator-Entropy JEPA for multi-sensor anomaly detection and evaluated false-alarm control, discriminative detection, and operational calibration across 33 telemetry streams under a strict held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{False-Alarm Control, Not Detection Superiority:} OpEntropy JEPA suppresses nominal false alarms under static calibration by four-fold (buffered) and $12$--$18\times$ (strictly causal) against TimesNet and TranAD, a result that holds across leave-one-dataset-out folds. Point-F1, PR-AUC, and common-support event recall differences remain statistically inconclusive, per-dataset rankings fall within between-stream spread, and causal streaming loses event recall on quiet flatlines and subtle drifts, which a complementary variance monitor recovers only at roughly eight times the latent detector's nominal FPR.
    \item \textbf{Regularizers as Constraints, Not Gains:} The tested regularizers provide no aggregate detection benefit over unconstrained TS-JEPA: Operator-Entropy and Reynolds-Stress confirm formal TOST equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 ($p \le 0.0054$; $95\%$ CIs $[-0.014, +0.021]$ and $[-0.042, +0.007]$). The factorial sweep shows that the entropy term keeps the operator well conditioned ($\kappa$ from $1.235$ to $1.034$) without changing representation rank or detection, and the supporting proposition guarantees operator non-degeneracy only.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Failure Under Shift:} At design risk $q{=}10^{-3}$, static extreme-value calibration reaches $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ buffered and $1.0\text{--}1.5\%$ causal held-out FPR for the JEPAs, and $\approx 22\%$ and $16\text{--}18\%$ for the reconstructors, so the design risk is not achieved. Under the exchangeable permutation protocol tested here, inter-block drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of the causal overshoot; this does not transfer automatically to other deployments. Rolling calibrators help under discrete shifts and fail under continuous ramps.
    \item \textbf{Latency Limits:} A score becomes available $S{-}1$ steps after a window opens, which is $10.5$~min at $S{=}64$ on 10-s telemetry and $63$~h on hourly data. At $S{=}1$ the false-alarm advantage persists on a five-stream sweep (causal FPR $0.005$ vs.\ $0.025$ for TimesNet) with event recall $0.729$, but recall is non-monotone at intermediate horizons.
\end{enumerate}
Open items are larger-scale horizon sweeps, matched realized-FPR detection tests with adequate common support, and adaptive thresholding that tracks within-block drift.
