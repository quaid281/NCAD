\begin{abstract}
In fluid mechanics, smooth laminar flow breaks down into turbulence not from one isolated ripple, but when coherent instabilities couple across scales. Real-world telemetry from spacecraft and industrial machines behaves much the same way, yet deep anomaly detectors miss this structure. Waveform reconstructors overreact to harmless sensor jitter and flood operators with false alarms. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) stay quieter by forecasting compact latent summaries, but collapsing all errors into one threshold allows a single noisy coordinate to blind the monitor. Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we introduce Laminar-JEPA-Lite, which brings fluid-dynamics diagnostics directly into latent residual analysis. Trained only on healthy history across nested short-to-long horizons, the model treats nominal latent trajectories as a steady laminar baseline and applies Reynolds decomposition to separate sustained mean-flow drift from transient fluctuations. Each coordinate and drift channel receives its own healthy tripwire, alarming only when a quorum trips together as a coherent disturbance. While anomaly ranking matches scalar JEPAs, this design sets a far more effective label-free alarm threshold. Across 118 untouched streams from nine datasets evaluated without point adjustment, standalone Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent and flags faults 41.4 steps earlier than single-score multi-horizon JEPA, while test false-positive rate (FPR) rises from 8.8 to 17.0 percent. Adding a pre-deployment readiness check for short or drifting streams achieves 59.4 percent ER at 10.0 percent test FPR, far below the 25 percent false-alarm rates of waveform reconstructors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold. When one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise that threshold and mask moderate faults across the other 30 coordinates ($0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also skewed by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed partial differential equation (PDE) loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we borrow fluid-mechanics diagnostics purely as post-hoc residual algebra. In latent space, normal operation behaves like a steady \emph{laminar} baseline, whereas an isolated noisy sensor acts like a local disturbance and a real fault behaves like a coherent multi-scale instability. Across nested horizons of $16$, $32$, and $64$ steps, we apply Reynolds mean-fluctuation decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} to separate sustained drift from brief errors. Each whitened coordinate and mean-drift \emph{dial} then receives its own healthy tripwire, much like a circuit-breaker panel in which each breaker tracks its own branch rather than total load. To keep isolated noise spikes from sounding false alarms, the monitor fires only when a \emph{quorum} of dials trips at the same time, meaning the count of simultaneously active dials exceeds a minimum voting threshold learned directly from healthy history. Because normal correlated bursts set that count threshold automatically, higher variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the rest of the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Without claiming superior intrinsic ranking, the quorum shifts the label-free operating point toward higher fault coverage when scalar calibration under-triggers (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational model): predicts three nested horizons in parallel behind a single layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization}, monitors $128$ position-and-drift dials under one quorum, and fits within $1$ megabyte (MB) (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($224$-dial reference): extends the panel to four horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays); \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points for lower false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our main contribution is a fluid-inspired diagnostic and alarm framework that solves single-threshold blindness in label-free time-series monitoring, without adding brittle physics-informed loss penalties. Each stream fits solely to its own healthy history ($K\in[2,55]$ channels, Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}), and the work rests on three pillars:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics (no physics loss):} We introduce \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, a compact ($<1\,\text{MB}$) multi-horizon latent predictor trained with an unconstrained predictive objective. Treating healthy latent dynamics as a steady laminar baseline, we apply Reynolds mean-fluctuation decomposition strictly as post-hoc residual algebra across nested horizons. Isolating sustained cross-horizon drift from brief errors drives our sharpest live-detection and latency gains without stiff PDE penalties.
    \item \emph{Coordinate circuit-breaker panel with quorum voting:} Rather than collapsing all latent errors into one global score that a few noisy coordinates can hijack, we assign each dial its own healthy tripwire and alarm only when enough dials fire simultaneously to clear a healthy-calibrated quorum count. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes why this stops high-variance coordinates from masking faults elsewhere. Rather than claiming better intrinsic score ranking, we show that the panel succeeds by choosing a stronger label-free alarm operating point when scalar calibration under-triggers.
    \item \emph{Strict multi-domain evaluation and operational readiness:} Evaluated step-by-step without point adjustment across $118$ untouched streams from nine spacecraft, server, and industrial datasets, standalone \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ over single-score multi-horizon JEPA and detects shared faults $41.4$ steps earlier, at higher prospective test FPR ($17.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$). Because calibrating $128$ per-coordinate tripwires requires enough stable history, we pair the panel with a label-free pre-deployment readiness check that switches short or drifting streams to scalar calibration ($59.4\%$ ER at $10.0\%$ test FPR) and a quarantined recalibration policy, while documenting operational limits on slow ramps, single-sensor flatlines, and regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Reconstructive and Forecasting Detectors:} Most deep anomaly detectors reconstruct or forecast raw waveforms and alarm on high squared error, including long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, and Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)-based TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}. Because raw telemetry contains unpredictable jitter and benign baseline shifts, summing reconstruction errors across dozens of channels produces heavy-tailed spikes even during normal operation \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised encoders \cite{zhang2023self} avoid decoding raw signals. Contrastive methods such as temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} learn boundaries from synthetic corruptions that may not match real faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead predict future latent summaries from recent context without decoding, preventing representation collapse through exponential moving average (EMA) target encoders \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Physics-Inspired Dynamics vs.\ Post-Hoc Residual Algebra:} Because physical telemetry follows structured dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}, prior work embeds governing laws directly into training via neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa}. On uncalibrated industrial sensors, however, differential loss penalties create stiff gradients \cite{wang2021understanding}. We therefore keep training purely predictive and borrow Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as post-hoc algebra on frozen residuals.

\noindent\emph{Statistical Process Control and Multiple-Testing Thresholds:} Classical statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse all dimensions into one scalar score. SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} likewise fits a single Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tail \cite{balkema1974residual} to scalar scores despite temporal clustering \cite{ferro2003inference}. Monitoring coordinates separately with Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} or split-conformal Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, and Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) rules \cite{benjamini1995controlling} either floods operators with alarms or hits a discrete $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ floor on short calibration histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Following recent critiques \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}, all evaluations omit point adjustment.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ synchronized channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy prefix followed chronologically by a labeled test segment. Our evaluation separates design from validation across two benchmark collections drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. A $45$-stream development pool ($11$ datasets) yields a $33$-stream primary development benchmark, whereas a $141$-stream evaluation archive ($12$ datasets) yields $118$ untouched evaluation streams across nine datasets once every tuning stream is removed. Below we detail the physical origin, sensor composition, fault structure, and calibration challenges of each dataset before summarizing the cohort boundaries.

\subsection{Dataset Descriptions by Application Domain}
\label{subsec:dataset_domains}

\noindent\emph{1) Spacecraft and space-weather telemetry:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{SMAP and MSL \cite{Hundman2018b}:} Real spacecraft telemetry from NASA's Soil Moisture Active Passive satellite ($K{=}25$) and Mars Science Laboratory rover ($K{=}55$). Each stream pairs one continuous physical telemetry channel with binary one-hot command encodings. NASA divides ground-truth incidents into out-of-range \emph{point} faults and in-range \emph{contextual} faults that violate normal sensor-command dynamics without crossing amplitude bounds. Archive prefixes are short ($2{,}032\text{--}4{,}308$ healthy steps; $N_{\text{cal}}\in[298,376]$ on SMAP and $227\text{--}625$ on MSL), so all $40$ untouched SMAP streams and $12$ of $13$ untouched MSL streams fall below the $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$ screening cutoff, and command-mode switches induce sharp healthy-to-test shifts ($47$ SMAP and $18$ MSL archive streams; $40$ and $13$ untouched, with $52$ and $16$ test events).
    \item \emph{SWAN-SF \cite{angryk2020multivariate}:} Solar photospheric magnetogram telemetry ($K{=}38$ magnetic-field descriptors including total unsigned current helicity, magnetic flux, and Lorentz force proxies; $19{,}672$ healthy and $72{,}000$ test steps; development cohort). Anomalies mark active-region solar flare intervals, creating dense, bursty positive spans ($6{,}615$ labeled segments, anomalous-step fraction $\pi{=}27.9\%$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{2) Wearable biomechanics and human activity:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) \cite{bachlin2010wearable}:} Three body-worn triaxial accelerometers on the ankle, thigh, and trunk of Parkinson's disease patients performing walking trials ($K{=}9$ channels sampled at $64\,\text{Hz}$, so our $S{=}64$ target horizon equals $1\,\text{s}$). Anomalies correspond to clinical freezing-of-gait episodes ($6$ dev streams with $72$ episodes; $22$ archive streams with $15$ untouched spanning $92$ episodes, $\pi{=}11.6\%$). Subject gait transitions introduce non-stationary covariance shifts across moderate healthy prefixes ($1{,}920\text{--}14{,}223$ steps; $N_{\text{cal}}\in[208,2{,}361]$).
    \item \emph{OPPORTUNITY \cite{roggen2010collecting}:} Body-worn inertial measurement units (IMUs) capturing complex daily living activities ($K{=}32$ Euler-angle and body/navigation acceleration channels; $13$ archive streams, $12$ untouched). Healthy prefixes span $3{,}825\text{--}7{,}667$ steps ($N_{\text{cal}}\in[541,1{,}213]$), followed by $17{,}784\text{--}34{,}238$ test steps containing sparse anomalous activity transitions ($12$ untouched events, $\pi{=}2.0\%$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{3) Cloud computing, servers, and transaction/network streams:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{Server Machine Dataset (SMD) \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}:} Production server-cluster telemetry from an internet company tracking CPU load, memory, disk, and network utilization ($K{=}38$ channels; $18$ untouched zero-tuning archive streams). Long nominal prefixes ($23{,}688\text{--}28{,}726$ steps; $N_{\text{cal}}\in[4{,}017,4{,}899]$) precede $4{,}930\text{--}20{,}367$ test steps per stream covering $196$ collective service-degradation events ($\pi{=}6.0\%$).
    \item \emph{Exathlon \cite{jacob2021exathlon}:} Distributed Apache Spark streaming traces collected on a multi-node cluster ($K{=}20$ executor queue latency, Java virtual-machine heap, memory, and filesystem metrics; $17$ untouched zero-tuning archive streams). Anomalies represent injected root-cause disturbances such as bursty input stalls, CPU contention, and memory leaks ($63$ events across $8{,}200\text{--}77{,}519$ test steps, $\pi{=}12.5\%$). Workload and job-phase transitions make Exathlon the strongest test of chronological regime drift ($N_{\text{cal}}\in[230,4{,}091]$).
    \item \emph{Pooled Server Metrics (PSM) \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}:} Internally pooled application-server telemetry from eBay ($K{=}26$ anonymized metrics; $1$ untouched zero-tuning stream). It supplies the longest healthy history in the archive ($132{,}481$ steps; $N_{\text{cal}}{=}23{,}056$) alongside prolonged, high-prevalence test faults ($70{,}273$ test steps, $39$ events, $\pi{=}32.1\%$).
    \item \emph{CreditCard \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}:} Chronologically ordered payment transactions encoded as $K{=}29$ anonymized principal component analysis (PCA) features plus transaction amount ($1$ untouched zero-tuning stream; $56{,}804$ healthy and $170{,}885$ test steps). Fraudulent transactions occur as isolated, ultra-rare point spikes ($229$ events, $\pi{=}0.15\%$) with minimal temporal precursor.
    \item \emph{CICIDS \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Network-intrusion traffic flows ($K{=}72$ packet-length, timing, and flag statistics; $6$ development-pool streams, $25{,}784\text{--}66{,}766$ healthy steps and up to $414{,}844$ test steps). Because $K{=}72$ exceeds our $K{\le}55$ development cap, CICIDS stays outside the $33$-stream primary development suite.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{4) Industrial process control and environmental infrastructure:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Simulated industrial oil-heating control loops monitoring tank levels, temperatures, and actuator valves ($K{=}16$ channels; $14$ development streams, $85{,}137\text{--}120{,}000$ healthy steps, $26$ faults, $\pi{=}1.3\%$). Faults are slow cyber-physical tampering ramps unfolding over thousands of steps with permanent post-fault baseline offsets, directly testing sliding-window blind spots.
    \item \emph{GECCO \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Municipal drinking-water quality monitoring ($K{=}9$ sensors measuring temperature, chlorine, pH, redox potential, conductivity, and turbidity; $1$ archive-heldout stream with $27{,}560$ healthy and $83{,}113$ test steps, $37$ abrupt contamination and sensor faults, $\pi{=}1.5\%$).
    \item \emph{Genesis \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Portable industrial pick-and-place demonstrator recording motor currents, positions, speeds, and force indicators ($K{=}18$ channels; $1$ development stream, $3{,}244$ healthy and $9{,}332$ test steps, $2$ rare mechanical-failure episodes, $\pi{=}0.3\%$).
    \item \emph{Metro I-94, Room-Occupancy, and CalIt2 \cite{candanedo2016accurate, asuncion2007uci, schmidl2022anomaly}:} Low-dimensional infrastructure streams testing small-$K$ operation without the $K{\ge}25$ multiscale front end. Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$: hourly interstate traffic volume and weather; $24{,}072$ healthy steps, $25$ rare events, $\pi{=}0.13\%$) belongs to the primary development benchmark, whereas Room-Occupancy ($K{=}5$: temperature, humidity, light, carbon dioxide, and humidity ratio; $2$ streams) and CalIt2 ($K{=}2$: building entry and exit counts; $1$ stream) form short-history case studies ($940\text{--}1{,}492$ healthy steps).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cohort Splits, Qualification Criteria, and Benchmark Caveats}
\label{subsec:cohort_caveats}

\noindent\emph{Development cohort vs.\ untouched archive:} From the $45$-stream development pool, $33$ streams qualify for the primary development benchmark ($19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL streams across $7$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)) by satisfying $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$, $K{\le}55$, and ${\le}1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps. The broader $141$-stream archive retains every stream across $12$ datasets having at least $100$ calibration windows and one labeled fault ($1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ healthy steps): $47$ SMAP, $22$ Daphnet, $18$ MSL, $18$ SMD, $17$ Exathlon, $13$ OPPORTUNITY, and single streams from GECCO, Genesis, PSM, CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN-SF. Excluding the $23$ tuning streams leaves the $118$-stream untouched archive across nine datasets: $37$ streams from four zero-tuning external datasets (SMD, Exathlon, PSM, CreditCard), $1$ archive-heldout GECCO stream, and $80$ unseen streams from tuned datasets (SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, OPPORTUNITY; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a,b)).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and benchmark caveats:} Each stream fits strictly on its own healthy prefix, transferring only the frozen architecture, damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum across $K{\in}[2,55]$ without sharing weights across assets (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Because SMAP and MSL contain known labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed} and streams from the same facility share hardware, we pair stream-level tests with dataset-clustered inference ($7$ dev, $9$ archive sets), nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) tuning, and $9$-fold dataset-exclusion sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]

\vspace{-6pt}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Stage~1 compresses each raw telemetry window into a bounded $32$-number summary while keeping both slow baselines and fast transients. A length-$320$ window advances one step at a time, splitting at index $t$ into a $256$-step context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and a $64$-step target $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ emitted at causal timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem and $1{\times}1$ channel mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) front end splits features into four smooth frequency shells whose weights sum to one; each shell runs a depthwise causal convolution (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$) and recombines via a softmax router. Features then pass through a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) with dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ \cite{bai2018empirical}, whereas streams with $K < 25$ enter the TCN directly. When evaluating shorter target lengths, the TCN automatically caps dilations to the window length and bypasses temporal pooling steps when too few samples remain.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and an exponential moving average (EMA) target encoder $E_\phi$ map each window to $D=32$ coordinates across $10\text{--}20$ epochs without collapsing \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Both encoders use TCN width $d_{\text{model}}=48$ and EMA momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D controls). Each encoder pools TCN features with three level statistics (last step, max, mean) and three roughness statistics (standard deviation, log mean-square energy, and mean absolute first difference). Compact GELU-LayerNorm heads ($d_{\text{head}}=32$ canonically; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)) project these features, and a final affine-free LayerNorm places embeddings onto a fixed-radius hypersphere. Normalization tames heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$), though dividing by total variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines across channels (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two lightweight additions preserve sharp edges and remove static offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Deep TCN layers smooth out sudden spikes, so both encoders also tap the front end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, blending shallow last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) features into the output via a learned gate bounded at $0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} Subtracting a running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (copied into $E_\phi$) before final LayerNorm removes static training offsets, though post-calibration regime shifts still require live drift monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction and Residual Whitening}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Predicting only a single $64$-step target averages out rapid transients, as in the \emph{TS-JEPA} baseline trained with mean-squared error (MSE) and a log-determinant regularizer \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Instead, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ nested target prefixes of $16$, $32$, and $64$ steps ($\{S/4, S/2, S\}$, clamping to at least one step on short targets) in parallel from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m$ using one shared two-layer GELU residual multi-layer perceptron (MLP), while the $224$-dial reference adds a $48$-step prefix ($M=4$). Weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes equally at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$, the training objective pairs MSE with a simple variance-covariance anti-collapse penalty $\mathcal{R}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M w_m \Bigl(\frac{1}{D}\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \mathcal{R}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\Bigr),
    \label{eq:laminar_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. On each batch of predicted embeddings $\mathbf{Z}$, the anti-collapse term keeps every coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ above $\gamma=0.75$ and penalizes off-diagonal covariance $\Sigma_{dj}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^D \max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d) + \frac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j} \Sigma_{dj}^2 ,
\end{equation}
working cleanly even when batch size $B=16 < D=32$ and raising target effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

Raw prediction errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ across the $M$ horizons have unequal scales and correlate across coordinates. Stage~2 centers and decorrelates them using damped symmetric zero-phase component analysis (ZCA) whitening fitted on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Flattening the $M \times D$ error matrix into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($96$ entries for \emph{Lite}), we estimate its mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and covariance eigendecomposition and apply a damped inverse square root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ with eigenvalue damping $10^{-3}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}^{\text{white}}(t) = \mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}\bigl(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t) - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Damping maps unobserved zero-variance directions smoothly to zero when $N_{\text{fit}} < 96$ ($66$ of $118$ archive streams). Unlike eigenbasis PCA whitening, symmetric ZCA stays aligned with the input axes, keeping correlation $r=0.64\text{--}0.67$ with raw errors. Replacing the joint $96\times 96$ factor with shared per-horizon whitening ($\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$, applying one $32\times 32$ covariance factor independently to each horizon) removes cross-horizon mixing and matches joint ZCA across all $118$ archive streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). To prevent noisy whitening directions from inflating false alarms, any reshaped coordinate $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ whose RMS norm on a disjoint check block $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$ blends smoothly back toward its standardized raw error $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
    w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 3: Reynolds Residual Decomposition and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Applying Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} strictly as post-hoc residual algebra, without physics-informed loss penalties, separates sustained cross-horizon drift from short-lived fluctuations. For every latent coordinate $c$, we split the stabilized multi-horizon error $\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ into its cross-horizon mean flow $\bar{e}_c(t)$ and fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) = \bar{e}_c(t) + F_{m,c}(t), \qquad \bar{e}_c(t) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t).
\end{equation}
Because fluctuations sum to zero across horizons, the cross term cancels and squared error energy partitions orthogonally into mean-drift energy and fluctuation energy: $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)^2 = \bar{e}_c(t)^2 + M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}(t)^2$.

Operational \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($M=3$, $D=32$) forms $128$ nonnegative \emph{dials} $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{128}$: $96$ per-horizon position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ plus $32$ Reynolds mean-drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$. The high-recall $224$-dial reference ($M=4$) also tracks $32$ fluctuation dials $\sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_m F_{m,c}(t)^2}$ and $32$ endpoint-difference dials $|\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t)-\tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, which \emph{Lite} omits because they raise false alarms without helping causal F1 (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
Multivariate residual scoring faces three basic choices across $J$ coordinates $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$. Summing all errors into one scalar score $\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$ allows a few noisy coordinates to inflate the global threshold and hide faults elsewhere. Alarming whenever \emph{any} coordinate exceeds its threshold ($\max_j$) compounds false alarms across $J$ tests. A coordinate quorum instead gives each dial its own healthy quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and alarms only when a joint count of active dials crosses a calibrated quorum $K_q$. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes why this isolates high-variance ``hijacker'' coordinates from blinding the rest of the monitor.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Quorum Scale Equivariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$ be nominal calibration and live residual vectors. Suppose a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k < J$ hijacker coordinates is scaled by $s = \sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta \ge 0$) on both calibration and live data, while all other coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ keep scale $1$:
(i)~under scalar scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile threshold grows as $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2)$, so any fault supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected whenever $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous per-dial thresholds $\tau_j = q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}|)$, every binary trip $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$ is invariant to $\eta \ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate correlation. Thus the calibration trip count $N_{\text{cal}}$, quorum $K_q = \kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$, and live count $N(t)=\sum_j b_j(t)$ stay unchanged, guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ dials in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\sum_{j=1}^J (s_j u_{j,\text{cal}})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2$, quantile monotonicity and positive homogeneity yield~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ leaves the fault below $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity scales each tripwire by $\tau_j^{(\eta)} = s_j \tau_j^{(0)}$; dividing $|s_j u_j(t)| > s_j \tau_j^{(0)}$ by $s_j > 0$ leaves every trip $b_j(t)$ identical on both calibration and live data, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$, $K_q$, and $N(t) \ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration:} Consider $J=32$ standard normal coordinates with $\alpha=0.005$, $\beta=0.015$, and $k=2$ hijackers scaled by $\eta=5$. Scalar calibration pushes $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$, completely masking a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault ($144.4 < 252.4$). The quorum stays invariant at $K_q=3.5$ and detects all $10 > 3.5$ tripped coordinates.

\noindent\emph{Practical scope limits:} In deployment, upstream LayerNorm, whitening, and horizon averaging couple sensors before dials form, so Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} applies to formed dials rather than raw channels. Post-calibration regime shifts also break invariance and require readiness screening and quarantined recalibration (Section~\ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}).

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) calibrates the quorum directly on healthy calibration data $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ using linear quantile interpolation. Because dials share the $M\times 32$ error matrix, trips correlate strongly (count variance is $7.2\times$ independent binomial), so fitting the quorum directly to empirical healthy trip counts captures cross-dial dependence automatically:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial tripwires:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,128\}$ fires when it exceeds its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Live causal vs.\ retrospective buffered timing:} For target window $[t, t+S)$, live \emph{causal attribution} emits its decision strictly at the right edge $\tau = t+S-1$ with zero lookahead. Because a fault starting at $t^*$ enters that right edge immediately, onset latency $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^* \ge 0$ can be well below $63$ steps on sharp faults. Retrospective \emph{buffered attribution} instead spreads each horizon's trips across its target span $[t, t+S_m)$.
    \item \emph{Single-quorum alarm (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Summing active dials yields $N_{128}(\tau) = \sum_{j=1}^{128} b_j(\tau)$, and an alarm fires whenever this count exceeds a $12\%$ margin above the $99.5$th healthy calibration percentile (floored at $3.5$):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) &= \mathbb{I}\bigl(N_{128}(\tau) > K_{\text{Lite}}\bigr), \\
        K_{\text{Lite}} &= \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr).
    \end{aligned}
        \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ 224-Dial Reference}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both archive tiers share the same four-block TCN backbone, $32$D heads, $K \ge 25$ depthwise rFFT shells, and parallel residual MLP predictor (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (128 dials):} Predicts $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$) and monitors $96$ position plus $32$ Reynolds mean-drift dials under the single quorum (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
    \item \emph{High-recall \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reference (224 dials):} Predicts $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$) with $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ auxiliary onset relays (which flag sharp right-edge faults when the full-horizon dial trips alongside endpoint or fluctuation dials at $q_{0.993}$), alarming when total dials ($K_{224}$), position dials ($K_{128}$), or relays ($K_{\text{edge}}$) cross their healthy quorums. Development tables also include its $5$-block sequential predecessor (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite, Readiness Screening, and Quarantine\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active dial count $N_{128}(\tau)$.
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically across $S$-step gaps into $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:laminar_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute stabilized errors (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), Reynolds position-plus-drift dials, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\State Screen frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; if $N_{\text{cal}}{<}500$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}{>}0.05$, or (strict tier) $D_{\text{KS}}{>}0.50$, switch stream to 1D SPOT scalar calibration.
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (causal) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (buffered); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
    \State Monitor sliding $D_{\text{KS}}$; if $D_{\text{KS}}{\ge}0.50$, keep alarming under frozen thresholds while buffering $400$ windows; refit only $(\tau_j, K_{\text{Lite}})$ upon confirmed mode switch and reset $D_{\text{KS}}$ (else discard buffer).
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (combining full-tensor and peak-horizon errors) blend Euclidean norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup and Calibration Protocol}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures use context $C=256$, target horizon $S=64$ ($W=320$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), latent dimension $D=32$, nominal training stride $16$, and evaluation stride $1$. Models are trained with AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) and zero per-dataset tuning. Development cohorts train at batch $16$ for $15\text{--}20$ fixed epochs, while archive cohorts train at batch $16\text{--}64$ for up to $12$ early-stopped epochs with gradient clipping ($1.0$) and cosine annealing.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
To prevent threshold leakage, each stream's healthy history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ splits chronologically into five target-disjoint blocks separated by $64$-step isolation gaps (allowing past-context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ overlap across block cuts unless widened to $W=320$; Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}), freezing all weights and thresholds before evaluation:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Fitting, selection, and stabilization):} Reusing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for early stopping or stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) biases tripwires downward. In the primary archive, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits channel scaling, encoder-predictor weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the early-stopped checkpoint; and $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($160\text{--}2{,}048$ windows) freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is touched. Fixed-epoch development suites use $65\%$ for training, paired with a disjoint $4$-block check ($50\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$) in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Calibration and serial dependence):} The final $35\%$ of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ splits across a $64$-step gap into $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$. Widening all post-fit gaps to a full $320$-step window across $49$ eligible streams shifts causal ER, Point-F1, and precision by at most $0.001$ (all $p\ge 0.66$), while held-out FPR changes by $+0.84$ points ($p=0.11$). Because overlapping sliding windows correlate strongly, a $500$-window calibration block holds a median of only $65$ effectively independent samples, making $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ a practical operating rule rather than a formal tail guarantee (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}).

\paragraph{Statistical vs.\ Practical Significance}
Seeds average within each stream first, with seed standard deviation (SD) $\le 0.008$ on causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER, keeping the stream as the primary unit of analysis. Dataset-clustered inference pairs $95\%$ cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) with exact dataset sign-flip tests ($2{,}000\text{--}4{,}000$ resamples). Stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests add Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} across the four primary contrasts (\emph{Lite} vs.\ 1D and TS-JEPA on F1 and ER). Generalization checks separate zero-tuning datasets from partially tuned sets and combine nested LODO hyperparameter selection with 9-fold exclusion sensitivity (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Cohorts, Baseline Fairness, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Causal and buffered splits align across two cohorts. The \emph{development cohort} spans the $19$-stream residual cache ($6$ sets; Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:matched_alert}) and $33$-stream primary suite ($7$ sets; Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Hyperparameters freeze once on $23$ tuning streams: the $19$ non-GHL streams plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, and \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}. Excluding those $23$ streams leaves the $118$-stream untouched archive across nine datasets (Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

Baselines span three tiers. \emph{Matched-backbone controls} include TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon 1D, and Wu--Keogh cycle-swap, phase-desync, and noise checks against $\max_c|x_c(t)|$ \cite{wu2023flawed}, alongside \emph{system aggregators} that score frozen residuals. \emph{External baselines} cover TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}. We run Linear PCA, TranAD, and TimesNet across all $118$ untouched streams ($3$ seeds, SPOT $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$), and release our code at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\paragraph{Protocol Chronology and Archive Provenance}
To distinguish pre-specified design from archive-informed policy, choices fall into three stages:
(1)~\emph{Frozen before first archive run} from the $23$ tuning streams and dev checks: backbone, $M{=}3$ prefixes, damping $10^{-3}$, shrinkage cutoff $1.10$ in (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$, tripwire $q_{0.985}$, quorum $\kappa{=}1.12$ with floor $3.5$, and live monitor $D_{\text{KS}}{\ge}0.50$.
(2)~\emph{Post-hoc archive diagnostics} (frozen detector unchanged): block permutation, matched budgets, eight-arm PCA/ZCA checks, $W{=}320$ gap checks, and multiplicative floors.
(3)~\emph{Archive-informed readiness policies} added after observing archive tail drift: pre-deployment $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}{\le}5\%$ and $D_{\text{KS}}{\le}0.50$ checks that switch short or drifting streams to 1D SPOT scalar calibration. Holding $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$ fixed, nine-fold nested LODO optimizes ($\tau_{\text{F}},\tau_{\text{K}}$) using historical event labels strictly on each fold's eight training sets, whereas deployment on the held-out ninth set remains label-free using only each stream's healthy prefix (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a,c)).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Stepwise predictions without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} average seeds per stream before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) aggregation. Healthy alarm burden reports pointwise $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and prospective $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ (mean, median, p90, p95) alongside contiguous false-alarm episodes per $1{,}000$ healthy test steps. Step precision, recall, Point-F1 relative to the per-stream all-positive baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}$, and precision-recall (PR) AUC measure accuracy and ranking. Event coverage ER is compared against $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$. We track onset latency $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^* \ge 0$ under three conventions. \emph{Stream-paired} delays compare stream-mean latencies across streams where both detectors trigger at least once, even on different events. \emph{Exact segment-paired} delays restrict strictly to shared fault segments co-detected within a seed, whereas \emph{detection-conditioned} means average across each detector's own triggered streams. Autonomous thresholds (\emph{Auto.}) are contrasted against $q^*=0.05$ and upper-order quantile post-hoc $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$, paired with kurtosis $\gamma_2$ and Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Dial Simplification, Quorum Design, and Whitening}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Before turning to the untouched archive, we ask which parts of the detector earn their place and which can be removed (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations} and \ref{tab:matched_alert}).

\noindent\emph{1) Which parts matter (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b)):} Monitoring coordinates separately drives the causal gain, and Reynolds residual decomposition delivers its sharpest jump with zero physics loss terms. On the $19$-stream cache, splitting the single score into $224$ dials raises causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$). Adding the $32$ Reynolds mean-drift dials on top of sub-horizon position dials jumps causal Point-F1 from $0.063$ to $0.098$ and cuts delay from $110.6$ to $74.8$ steps, whereas adding fluctuation and endpoint dials lowers causal F1. In end-to-end retraining, four TCN blocks match five, and three prefixes match four while running $25.5\%$ faster. Predicting the prefixes one after another doubles causal test FPR, so \emph{Lite} predicts them in parallel.

\noindent\emph{2) Why a quorum (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(a)):} Simpler per-coordinate rules either flood operators with alarms or go blind. Alarming whenever any of $128$ coordinates crosses its threshold fires on $40.6\%\text{--}66.5\%$ of healthy steps. Strict multiplicity corrections (\v{S}id\'{a}k, BH FDR) ask for p-values smaller than a short calibration set can produce, $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$, so they detect almost nothing. At a matched nominal rate, classical multivariate charts (Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, CUSUM) and a conformal minimum p-value still alarm on $21.1\%\text{--}58.6\%$ of healthy steps. Only the dial quorum and Fisher pooling combine useful F1 ($0.098$ and $0.109$) with held-out FPR near $1\%$.

\noindent\emph{3) Whitening choices:} The cheaper whitening options cost nothing under causal scoring. Shared per-horizon whitening, which applies one $32\times 32$ covariance factor to each horizon separately instead of one full $96\times 96$ matrix, matches joint ZCA within its confidence interval (three seeds: $+0.014$ F1, CI $[-0.006,+0.029]$; single seed: $+0.010$, $p=0.76$). PCA whitening also matches ZCA ($0.087$ vs.\ $0.086$ F1, $p=0.60$), provided the shrinkage in (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) runs in the same rotated basis. Mixing a PCA rotation with raw-axis shrinkage lowers causal F1 from $0.091$ to $0.071$. Joint ZCA keeps an edge only under buffered scoring ($0.166$ vs.\ $0.146$ F1, $p=0.005$).

\noindent\emph{4) Sensitivity to settings:} Results change little across reasonable settings. \emph{Lite} beats the 1D baseline in ER at every latent width $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$. Causal F1 stays within $0.080\text{--}0.093$ across damping $\lambda_{\text{damp}}\in[10^{-3},10^{-2}]$, dial quantile $q_{\text{dial}}\in[0.975,0.990]$, and quorum scale $\kappa\in[1.00,1.20]$. Editing predictor weights to suppress noisy coordinates (quenching) leaves F1 unchanged ($0.0901$ vs.\ $0.0904$), so \emph{Lite} omits it.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Single-Sensor Limits}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The panel catches faults that disturb many sensors together but largely misses faults confined to one sensor. Cross-channel inconsistencies, abrupt transients, and in-range phase shifts move many latent coordinates at once and trip the quorum. A single frozen, saturated, or biased channel barely moves the $32$D summary, because the $K-1$ healthy channels dilute it by about $1/K$ and LayerNorm spreads what remains across all coordinates. On \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials, compared with $62.1$ for a harmonic flip.

A separate per-channel guard closes this gap on synthetic faults, raising recall on injected freezing, saturation, shock, ramp, and bias faults to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$. It also raises clean test FPR from $1.5\%$ to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$, so we leave it disabled.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Front-End and Latent vs.\ Observation Tails}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
The frequency-shell front end helps high-channel spacecraft data but not low-channel data. On \texttt{MSL M-1}, it raises F1 from $0.085$ to $0.410$, yet it lowers F1 on the nine-channel \texttt{Daphnet} and \texttt{GECCO} streams. We therefore enable it only when $K \ge 25$, an empirical rule rather than a principled cutoff. Separately, scoring in latent space tames heavy-tailed noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). Observation-space reconstructors show calibration kurtosis near $6.2$ and drift near $D_{\text{KS}}=0.45$. Adding LayerNorm alone brings reconstructor kurtosis down to $0.91$, and latent residuals roughly halve the drift ($D_{\text{KS}}=0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p\le 0.001$).

\subsection{Computational Footprint and Sensor Dropouts}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
\emph{Lite} is small and fast enough for on-board use. The canonical model occupies under $1\,\text{MB}$ ($0.90\text{--}0.97\,\text{MB}$ FP32; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)). Even the larger $64$D-head variant processes a window in $7.1\text{--}10.8\,\text{ms}$ on one CPU thread ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s, over twice Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate) and in $0.16\text{--}0.25\,\text{ms}$ on a batched GPU, with $44\text{--}128\,\text{KB}$ of streaming state. When sensors drop out without warning and are mean-imputed, ER falls only slightly ($54.7\%\text{--}55.9\%$ vs.\ $58.9\%$), while test FPR rises from $1.5\%$ to $3.4\%\text{--}7.9\%$.

\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark, Attribution Regimes, and Horizons}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

\noindent\emph{Buffered vs.\ live scoring (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}):} Scores that look strong in offline review weaken in live streaming. Buffered review can pool evidence across a whole fault, whereas a live detector must decide at each time step, where a few drifting coordinates inflate a single SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Against the $224$-dial reference, \emph{Lite} trades some causal recall for fewer false alarms. It lowers held-out FPR in both modes ($p=0.0025$) but catches fewer events live ($62.2\%$ vs.\ $70.1\%$ ER, $p=0.0029$). Reconstructors such as TimesNet look competitive here only because they alarm on about a quarter of healthy steps. At that rate, random alarms alone would catch $60.0\%$ of events, compared with $28.1\%$ at the panels' $1.3\%$ FPR.

\noindent\emph{Target length (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)):} Short targets suit live alarms, and long targets suit offline review. \subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
Slow faults are a blind spot for any sliding-window predictor. When a fault develops more slowly than the $256$-step context, context and target drift together and their difference stays small. Across $14$ GHL streams, F1 is essentially zero (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). On synthetic ramps, the advantage over chance disappears once a ramp lasts four context windows or longer, and mean delay grows nearly $20$-fold, from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $586.7$ steps on slow drifts.

A separate drift monitor recovers these faults but cannot tell them from benign wander. Adding a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) Mahalanobis monitor, anchored to the training block, raises GHL event recall from $0.8\%$ to $57.7\%$. After each fault, however, GHL baselines wander, and the monitor's test FPR reaches $74.4\%$.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Each alarm can be broken down by which dials fired. Because each dial tracks a learned latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, this \emph{latent alarm decomposition} shows which horizons and drift terms drove an alarm (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On an abrupt \texttt{GECCO} fault, all dial groups surge to $4.8\times$ the quorum while 1D SPOT stays below threshold. On a contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} fault inside the normal amplitude range, the longer horizons and drift dials reach $2.02\times$ the quorum. On a slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramp, the panel stays below quorum while the frozen-reference drift monitor crosses its threshold mid-ramp.

To point operators toward a sensor, we mask one channel at a time on alarming windows, replacing it with its healthy mean, and rank channels by how many dials stop firing. On $14$ dev streams with injected $+2.5\sigma$ shifts on sensor subsets, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen names the right channel first $85.7\%$ of the time ($13.9\%$ by chance) and within the top three $92.9\%$ of the time. Field datasets lack root-cause labels, and latent features mix sensors nonlinearly, so this shows sensitivity to synthetic perturbations rather than causal localization of real failures.

\subsection{Untouched Archive Generalization, External Baselines, and Nested LODO Audit}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

\input{sections/tab_dataset_lodo_audit.tex}

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit} answer four questions about the $118$ untouched streams.

\noindent\emph{1) Lite catches more faults, sooner, at the cost of more false alarms (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} \emph{Lite} detects $64.6\%$ of fault events, compared with $52.5\%$ for the scalar 1D JEPA ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$). It also alarms earlier. On fault segments both detectors catch, \emph{Lite} fires $41.4$ steps sooner, and comparing per-stream mean delays gives a $74.6$-step gain. The price is a higher false-alarm rate on healthy test data, $17.0\%$ versus $8.8\%$. This is a shift in operating point, not a better anomaly score: at matched false-alarm rates the two detectors rank anomalies about equally well (item~3).

Point-F1 is mixed. It rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ overall and on six of nine datasets, but falls on SMAP, OPPORTUNITY, and MSL, so the dataset-level gain is not significant. \emph{Lite} helps most on streams with long healthy histories ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$), where Point-F1 doubles from $0.040$ to $0.085$ ($p=0.047$). Replacing the additive floor in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) with a multiplicative one changes only $0.04\%$ of decisions.

\noindent\emph{2) Lite vs.\ the 224-dial reference:} The larger panel catches more events ($69.4\%$ vs.\ $64.6\%$ ER, $p=0.016$) at similar Point-F1. \emph{Lite} raises fewer false alarms, with mean held-out FPR of $6.4\%$ versus $7.0\%$ and buffered FPR of $13.3\%$ versus $21.0\%$, and it runs $25.5\%$ faster. We therefore deploy \emph{Lite} and keep the $224$-dial panel as a high-recall reference.

\noindent\emph{3) External baselines and ranking parity:} TimesNet and TranAD reach higher raw F1 ($0.175$ and $0.157$) only by alarming on about a quarter of healthy steps ($24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ held-out FPR). Held to $1\%$ test FPR, they catch about $25\%$ of events, versus $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite} and $55.6\%$ for 1D. Against the scalar 1D JEPA, by contrast, \emph{Lite} has no ranking advantage. Their PR-AUC is nearly identical ($0.152$ vs.\ $0.162$). In a separate three-seed matched-budget rerun across healthy calibration rates ($0.5\%\text{--}5\%$) and post-hoc test FPR limits ($1\%, 5\%$), \emph{Lite} stays competitive with Fisher pooling and top-$k$ voting on ER, trails a simple maximum-$z$ rule at post-hoc limits ($0.529$ vs.\ $0.570$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR), and lags 1D on Point-F1 ($0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$ at $1\%$ test FPR). \emph{Lite}'s gains therefore come from where its threshold lands, not from a better score.

\noindent\emph{4) Generalization beyond the tuning data (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} The recall gain holds outside the data used for design. \emph{Lite} raises ER on all nine datasets ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$). On the four datasets never used during tuning ($37$ streams), ER rises by $15.1$ points ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0004$). With only four datasets, however, no dataset-level test can reach $p<0.125$, so this evidence remains suggestive. Nested leave-one-dataset-out tuning, which picks thresholds without seeing the held-out dataset, selects the same setting in all nine folds and keeps the ER gain positive whichever dataset is removed ($+9.7$ to $+17.8$ points).

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Most false alarms come from a few streams whose operating mode changes after calibration. Of the $118$ streams, $104$ keep held-out FPR at or below $5\%$ (median $0.93\%$). The other $14$ involve Spark job changes, gait changes, or command-mode switches.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Mode changes, not too little data:} If these alarms came from a too-small calibration set, shuffling the healthy history would not help. Shuffling healthy blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$, which keeps the sample count but breaks time order, cuts archive held-out FPR by $92.3\%$, from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$. Even on the ten highest-FPR streams across SMAP, Exathlon, and Daphnet ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.44$ to $1.00$, including streams with $N_{\text{cal}} > 1{,}100$), block permutation drops held-out FPR from $11\%\text{--}100\%$ down to $0\%\text{--}3\%$. The alarms therefore reflect operating modes absent from calibration.
    \item \emph{What changes:} In high-FPR streams, the shape of the residual covariance shifts, not its center. Covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger than in stable streams, while effective sample size and latent mean shift are similar.
    \item \emph{Recalibration policy:} The panel tolerates benign amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) but not mean steps or baseline walks ($14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$). Recalibrating automatically whenever drift appears would absorb real faults, cutting recall to $9.1\%$. Instead, once $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, the panel keeps alarming with frozen thresholds and buffers $400$ windows. It refits thresholds only after an operator or control signal confirms a planned mode change.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability, Three-Stage Screening, and Hybrid Routing}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Short, autocorrelated calibration histories cannot guarantee a false-alarm rate. Because neighboring windows overlap, the median stream has only about $65$ effectively independent calibration samples, and $105$ of $118$ streams have too few to estimate a $99.5$th percentile reliably. A Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB) median threshold behaves like the plain quorum, while more conservative bootstrap bounds lose $8\text{--}17$ ER points.

\noindent\emph{1) Screening (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} Short histories, not drift, cause most rejections. Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ keeps $59$ of $118$ streams and rejects every SMAP stream. Screening on held-out FPR keeps $52$, and the drift check keeps $46$. Screening on a short held-out block is optimistic. Accepted streams average $11.0\%$ test FPR after two stages, falling to $6.8\%$ after the drift check ($2.9\%$ median). On these $46$ streams, \emph{Lite} fixes 1D SPOT's under-triggering, raising ER from $32.1\%$ to $49.8\%$ ($p_{\text{str}}\le 0.0004$).

\noindent\emph{2) Full hybrid vs.\ 1D SPOT:} Routing the accepted streams to \emph{Lite} and the rest to 1D SPOT gives a recall-first option, not a universal default. Over all $118$ streams, it raises ER from $52.5\%$ to $59.4\%$ ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0156$) and Point-F1 from $0.076$ to $0.091$, though the F1 gain is not significant at the dataset level ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.070$). Test FPR rises from $8.8\%$ to $10.0\%$, and false-alarm episodes from $8.8$ to $12.9$ per $1{,}000$ steps.

\noindent\emph{3) Choosing by cost (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(c)):} Let $J(w) = (1 - \text{ER}) + w\,\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$, where $w$ sets how much one point of false-alarm rate costs relative to one point of missed events. With routing thresholds picked on eight datasets and applied label-free to the ninth ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ fixed), the hybrid beats 1D SPOT whenever $w<5.92$. Larger weights shrink the accepted set ($40$ streams at $w=5$, ten at $w=6$) until, at $w\ge 20$, every stream falls back to 1D SPOT.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Operational deployment pairs healthy-prefix fitting across $K\in[2,55]$ channels with fold-validated readiness checks (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)). Two-stage screening keeps $52$ streams ($11.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$), and drift screening keeps $46$ ($6.8\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$). Streams that lack enough stable history to calibrate $128$ per-coordinate tripwires, including all $40$ SMAP and $12$ of $13$ MSL below $N_{\text{cal}}=500$, switch to 1D SPOT scalar calibration while alarms remain active during $400$-window quarantine.

The panel succeeds by choosing a stronger label-free operating point under fold-validated readiness checks ($49.8\%$ vs.\ $32.1\%$ ER on $46$ streams) rather than changing intrinsic score ranking. Under constrained cost weighting (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(c)), the adaptive hybrid suits recall-prioritized settings ($w\le 5.92$), whereas strict false-alarm aversion ($w\ge 6\text{--}20$) favors 1D SPOT scalar calibration. Practical deployment spans three tiers: (1)~\emph{standalone panel} on streams passing readiness screening; (2)~\emph{complementary guards} for diluted flatlines, slow ramps, and mode switches; and (3)~\emph{scalar calibration mode} on short or drifting streams.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Evaluations across $33$ development and $118$ untouched archive streams support three main conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Fluid-inspired residual diagnostics without physics loss:} Separating sustained cross-horizon drift from short-horizon errors via Reynolds residual algebra drives our largest causal detection and latency gains, while avoiding the gradient stiffness and domain mismatch of physics-informed loss penalties.
    \item \emph{Preventing single-threshold blindness:} Replacing one scalar threshold with per-coordinate tripwires and a correlation-aware quorum shifts the label-free operating point upward when scalar calibration under-triggers. Across $118$ untouched streams, standalone \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$) and cuts shared-fault delay by $41.4$ steps, while raising prospective test FPR from $8.8\%$ to $17.0\%$ and keeping ranking parity with 1D JEPA. Waveform reconstructors (TimesNet, TranAD) achieve higher raw F1 only near $25\%$ held-out FPR.
    \item \emph{Chronological drift and readiness-gated deployment:} Shuffling healthy blocks breaks chronological order and cuts archive FPR by $92.3\%$, confirming operating-mode drift as the primary source of false alarms. Pairing \emph{Lite} with pre-deployment readiness screening and mode-confirmed quarantine across all $118$ streams achieves $59.4\%$ ER ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0156$) and $0.091$ Point-F1 at $10.0\%$ test FPR versus $52.5\%$ ER and $0.076$ F1 at $8.8\%$ test FPR for 1D SPOT.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Four gaps guide next steps. Perturbation attribution needs field root-cause validation beyond synthetic injections, paired with low-FPR univariate flatline monitors for $1/K$-diluted single-sensor faults. Lightweight online $\mathbf{\Omega}_D$ updates after confirmed mode switches can replace $400$-window buffers, while control-conditioned drift monitors can curb slow-ramp wander.
