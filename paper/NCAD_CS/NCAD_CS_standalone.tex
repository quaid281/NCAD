\begin{abstract}
In fluid mechanics, smooth laminar flow turns turbulent when coherent instabilities couple across scales rather than from one isolated ripple. Spacecraft and industrial telemetry behave similarly, yet deep anomaly detectors miss this structure. Waveform reconstructors overreact to harmless sensor jitter, flooding operators with false alarms. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) remain quiet by forecasting compact latent summaries, but collapsing all errors into a single threshold allows a single noisy coordinate to blind the monitor. Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we introduce Laminar-JEPA-Lite, applying fluid-dynamics diagnostics strictly as post-hoc latent residual algebra. Trained on healthy history across nested horizons, it treats nominal trajectories as a steady laminar baseline and uses Reynolds decomposition to separate sustained mean-flow drift from transient fluctuations. Each coordinate and drift dial carries its own healthy tripwire, alarming only when a quorum trips together. The panel matches scalar JEPAs in anomaly ranking but selects a stronger label-free operating point. Across 118 untouched streams from nine datasets evaluated without point adjustment, standalone Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent, and flags faults 41.4 steps earlier than single-score multi-horizon JEPA. Because per-coordinate calibration requires sufficient stable history, a pre-deployment readiness check routes short or drifting streams back to scalar calibration. This readiness-gated policy achieves 59.4 percent ER at a 10.0 percent test false-positive rate (FPR), compared with 8.8 percent FPR for scalar JEPA and roughly 25 percent FPR held out for waveform reconstructors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while remaining quiet during normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone, since faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams. At that false-alarm rate, even random alarms would catch $60.0\%$ of fault episodes, about as many as TimesNet ($58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 prediction errors into a single scalar score using a single threshold. When one or two noisy coordinates (\emph{hijackers}) inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise that shared threshold and mask moderate faults on the other 30 coordinates, leaving live Point-F1 at only $0.055$. Published benchmarks are also skewed by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed partial differential equation loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2022pinns, rathore2024challenges}, we use fluid-mechanics diagnostics purely as post hoc residual algebra. In latent space, normal operation behaves like a steady \emph{laminar} baseline, whereas an isolated noisy sensor acts like a local disturbance, and a real fault behaves like a multiscale instability. Across nested horizons of $16$, $32$, and $64$ steps, we apply Reynolds mean-fluctuation decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} to separate sustained drift from brief errors. Each whitened coordinate and mean-drift \emph{dial} then receives its own healthy tripwire, much like a circuit-breaker panel in which each breaker tracks its own branch rather than total load. Each dial casts at most one vote, and the monitor fires only when a \emph{quorum} of dials trips simultaneously, with the minimum count learned from normal correlated bursts in the healthy history. A noisy coordinate therefore cannot inflate a global score or sound an alarm on its own. We study two configurations (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPAModel} (operational model): predicts three nested horizons in parallel behind a single layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization}, monitors $128$ position-and-drift dials under one quorum, and fits within $1$ megabyte (MB; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($224$-dial reference): extends the panel to four horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays); \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points for lower false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our central contribution is moving away from single-scalar anomaly thresholding in label-free time-series monitoring by pairing fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics with per-coordinate quorum voting, fitted solely to each stream's own healthy history ($K\in[2,55]$ channels, Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics:} \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} is trained with an unconstrained predictive objective, and Reynolds decomposition of its multi-horizon latent residuals separates sustained cross-horizon drift from brief errors. This separation drives our largest gains in live detection and latency (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).
    \item \emph{Coordinate quorum voting in place of single-scalar thresholding:} Each dial carries its own healthy tripwire, so a noisy coordinate moves only its own threshold, and the alarm requires a healthy-calibrated quorum of simultaneous trips. The gain comes from a stronger label-free operating point when scalar calibration under-triggers, rather than from improved anomaly ranking.
    \item \emph{Strict multi-domain evaluation and operational readiness:} On $118$ untouched streams from nine spacecraft, server, and industrial datasets, standalone \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ over single-score multi-horizon JEPA and detects shared faults $41.4$ steps earlier, at the cost of a higher prospective test FPR ($17.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$). Because $128$ separate tripwires require sufficient stable history for calibration, a label-free readiness check routes short or drifting streams back to scalar calibration ($59.4\%$ ER at $10.0\%$ test FPR overall), and a quarantined recalibration policy handles mode switches. We also document where the panel fails: slow ramps, single-sensor flatlines, and regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Multivariate Time-Series Anomaly Detection and Benchmarks:} Most recent deep detectors reconstruct or forecast raw waveforms and alarm on high reconstruction error. Examples include TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, frequency-period TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, graph-based CST-GL \cite{zheng2024cstgl}, and frequency-patching CATCH \cite{wu2025catch}. Time-series foundation models such as MOMENT \cite{goswami2024moment} and DADA \cite{shentu2025dada} extend reconstruction across domains via zero-shot pretraining. Our earlier autoencoder detectors paired reconstruction with LSTM sequence modeling \cite{akbarian2023aelstm} and with K-means clustering of latent codes for space operations \cite{akbarian2024aekmeans}. Across dozens of noisy channels, however, raw sensor jitter and benign baseline shifts still produce heavy-tailed healthy spikes \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}. Benchmark audits \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, liu2024tsbad, zhou2026mtsbench} also show that point-adjusted scores mask heavy false-alarm burdens. We therefore evaluate every step without point adjustment.

\noindent\emph{Self-Supervised Representation Learning and Temporal JEPAs:} Self-supervised encoders skip raw waveform decoding \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors such as TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} learn from synthetic corruptions that often miss real operational faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). JEPAs \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead forecast future latent summaries directly from recent context. Recent temporal JEPAs \cite{ennadir2025tsjepa} adapt this approach to time series while curbing representation collapse through variance-covariance regularization \cite{bardes2021vicreg, garrido2023rankme}. Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse all coordinates into one scalar threshold. One noisy coordinate can then blind the monitor.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamics Machine Learning vs.\ Residual Diagnostics:} Machine learning in fluid mechanics pairs Reynolds-averaged turbulence modeling with reduced-order representations to separate mean flow, coherent structures, and background fluctuations \cite{vinuesa2022enhancing}. Koopman-based time-series models similarly split time-invariant from time-variant dynamics \cite{liu2023koopa}. Directly imposing physics-informed neural network differential loss penalties during training creates unbalanced convergence rates \cite{wang2022pinns} and ill-conditioned loss landscapes \cite{rathore2024challenges}, and uncalibrated telemetry lacks known governing equations. We omit physics loss penalties during training. Fluid-mechanics diagnostics are strictly post hoc algebra applied to frozen multi-horizon residuals.

\noindent\emph{Conformal Calibration, Multiple Testing, and Drift:} Recent distribution-free methods adapt thresholds under temporal dependence and distribution shift via weighted quantiles \cite{barber2023conformal}, sequential conditional quantiles \cite{xu2023spci}, and online control loops \cite{angelopoulos2023conformalpid}. Conformal p-value tests also control the false discovery rate (FDR) across independent samples \cite{bates2023testing}, while Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) and Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) charts monitor mean shifts. On short, autocorrelated prefixes with $N_{\text{cal}}$ calibration windows, strict per-coordinate multiplicity corrections hit a discrete $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ floor. Unguarded online adaptation can absorb real faults (Sections~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations} and \ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}). Our coordinate quorum, pre-deployment readiness check, and mode-confirmed quarantine mitigate both issues during label-free deployment.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ synchronized channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy prefix followed chronologically by a labeled test segment. Our evaluation separates design from validation across two benchmark collections drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. A $45$-stream development pool ($11$ datasets) yields a $33$-stream primary development benchmark, whereas a $141$-stream evaluation archive ($12$ datasets) yields $118$ untouched evaluation streams across nine datasets once every tuning stream is removed.

\subsection{Dataset Descriptions by Application Domain}
\label{subsec:dataset_domains}

\noindent\emph{1) Spacecraft and space-weather telemetry:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{SMAP and MSL \cite{Hundman2018b}:} Real spacecraft telemetry from NASA's Soil Moisture Active Passive satellite ($K{=}25$) and Mars Science Laboratory rover ($K{=}55$). Each stream pairs one continuous physical telemetry channel with binary one-hot command encodings. NASA divides ground-truth incidents into out-of-range \emph{point} faults and in-range \emph{contextual} faults that violate normal sensor-command dynamics without crossing amplitude bounds. Archive prefixes are short ($2{,}032\text{--}4{,}308$ healthy steps; calibration window count $N_{\text{cal}}\in[298,376]$ on SMAP and $227\text{--}625$ on MSL), so all $40$ untouched SMAP streams and $12$ of $13$ untouched MSL streams fall below the $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$ cutoff. Command-mode switches trigger sharp healthy-to-test shifts. The untouched streams contain $52$ SMAP and $16$ MSL test events.
    \item \emph{SWAN-SF \cite{angryk2020multivariate}:} Solar photospheric magnetogram telemetry ($K{=}38$ magnetic-field descriptors including total unsigned current helicity, magnetic flux, and Lorentz force proxies; $19{,}672$ healthy and $72{,}000$ test steps; development cohort). Anomalies mark active-region solar flare intervals, creating dense, bursty positive spans ($6{,}615$ labeled segments, anomalous-step fraction $\pi{=}27.9\%$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{2) Wearable biomechanics and human activity:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait \cite{bachlin2010wearable}:} Three body-worn triaxial accelerometers on the ankle, thigh, and trunk of Parkinson's disease patients performing walking trials ($K{=}9$ channels sampled at $64\,\text{Hz}$, so our $S{=}64$ target horizon equals $1\,\text{s}$). Anomalies correspond to clinical freezing-of-gait episodes ($6$ dev streams with $72$ episodes; $22$ archive streams with $15$ untouched spanning $92$ episodes, $\pi{=}11.6\%$). Subject gait transitions introduce non-stationary covariance shifts across moderate healthy prefixes ($1{,}920\text{--}14{,}223$ steps; $N_{\text{cal}}\in[208,2{,}361]$).
    \item \emph{OPPORTUNITY \cite{roggen2010collecting}:} Body-worn inertial measurement units capturing complex daily living activities ($K{=}32$ Euler-angle and body/navigation acceleration channels; $13$ archive streams, $12$ untouched). Healthy prefixes span $3{,}825\text{--}7{,}667$ steps ($N_{\text{cal}}\in[541,1{,}213]$), followed by $17{,}784\text{--}34{,}238$ test steps containing sparse anomalous activity transitions ($12$ untouched events, $\pi{=}2.0\%$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{3) Cloud computing, servers, and transaction/network streams:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{Server Machine Dataset (SMD) \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}:} Production server-cluster telemetry from an internet company tracking CPU load, memory, disk, and network utilization ($K{=}38$ channels; $18$ untouched zero-tuning archive streams). Long nominal prefixes ($23{,}688\text{--}28{,}726$ steps; $N_{\text{cal}}\in[4{,}017,4{,}899]$) precede $4{,}930\text{--}20{,}367$ test steps per stream covering $196$ collective service-degradation events ($\pi{=}6.0\%$).
    \item \emph{Exathlon \cite{jacob2021exathlon}:} Distributed Apache Spark streaming traces collected on a multi-node cluster ($K{=}20$ executor queue latency, Java virtual-machine heap, memory, and filesystem metrics; $17$ untouched zero-tuning archive streams). Anomalies represent injected root-cause disturbances such as bursty input stalls, CPU contention, and memory leaks ($63$ events across $8{,}200\text{--}77{,}519$ test steps, $\pi{=}12.5\%$). Workload and job-phase transitions make Exathlon the strongest test of chronological regime drift ($N_{\text{cal}}\in[230,4{,}091]$).
    \item \emph{Pooled Server Metrics (PSM) \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}:} Internally pooled application-server telemetry from eBay ($K{=}26$ anonymized metrics; $1$ untouched zero-tuning stream). It supplies the longest healthy history in the archive ($132{,}481$ steps; $N_{\text{cal}}{=}23{,}056$) alongside prolonged, high-prevalence test faults ($70{,}273$ test steps, $39$ events, $\pi{=}32.1\%$).
    \item \emph{CreditCard \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}:} Chronologically ordered payment transactions encoded as $K{=}29$ anonymized principal component analysis (PCA) features plus transaction amount ($1$ untouched zero-tuning stream; $56{,}804$ healthy and $170{,}885$ test steps). Fraudulent transactions occur as isolated, ultra-rare point spikes ($229$ events, $\pi{=}0.15\%$) with minimal temporal precursor.
    \item \emph{CICIDS \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Network-intrusion traffic flows ($K{=}72$ packet-length, timing, and flag statistics; $6$ development-pool streams, $25{,}784\text{--}66{,}766$ healthy steps and up to $414{,}844$ test steps). Because $K{=}72$ exceeds our $K{\le}55$ development cap, CICIDS stays outside the $33$-stream primary development suite.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{4) Industrial process control and environmental infrastructure:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \emph{Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Simulated industrial oil-heating control loops monitoring tank levels, temperatures, and actuator valves ($K{=}16$ channels; $14$ development streams, $85{,}137\text{--}120{,}000$ healthy steps, $26$ faults, $\pi{=}1.3\%$). Faults are slow cyber-physical tampering ramps that unfold over thousands of steps, with permanent post-fault baseline offsets, directly testing sliding-window blind spots.
    \item \emph{GECCO \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Municipal drinking-water quality monitoring ($K{=}9$ sensors measuring temperature, chlorine, pH, redox potential, conductivity, and turbidity; $1$ archive-heldout stream with $27{,}560$ healthy and $83{,}113$ test steps, $37$ abrupt contamination and sensor faults, $\pi{=}1.5\%$).
    \item \emph{Genesis \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}:} Portable industrial pick-and-place demonstrator recording motor currents, positions, speeds, and force indicators ($K{=}18$ channels; $1$ development stream, $3{,}244$ healthy and $9{,}332$ test steps, $2$ rare mechanical-failure episodes, $\pi{=}0.3\%$).
    \item \emph{Metro I-94, Room-Occupancy, and CalIt2 \cite{candanedo2016accurate, asuncion2007uci, schmidl2022anomaly}:} Low-dimensional infrastructure streams testing small-$K$ operation without the $K{\ge}25$ multiscale front end. Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$: hourly interstate traffic volume and weather; $24{,}072$ healthy steps, $25$ rare events, $\pi{=}0.13\%$) belongs to the primary development benchmark. Room-Occupancy ($K{=}5$: temperature, humidity, light, carbon dioxide, and humidity ratio; $2$ streams) and CalIt2 ($K{=}2$: building entry and exit counts; $1$ stream) form short-history case studies ($940\text{--}1{,}492$ healthy steps).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cohort Splits, Qualification Criteria, and Benchmark Caveats}
\label{subsec:cohort_caveats}

\noindent\emph{Development cohort vs.\ untouched archive:} We design the detector on one set of streams and test it once on a larger set never used for tuning. From the $45$-stream development pool, $33$ streams qualify for the primary development benchmark ($19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL streams across $7$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)) by satisfying $N_{\text{cal}}{\ge}500$, $K{\le}55$, and ${\le}1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps. The broader $141$-stream archive retains every stream across $12$ datasets having at least $100$ calibration windows and one labeled fault ($1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ healthy steps): $47$ SMAP, $22$ Daphnet, $18$ MSL, $18$ SMD, $17$ Exathlon, $13$ OPPORTUNITY, and single streams from GECCO, Genesis, PSM, CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN-SF. Excluding the $23$ tuning streams leaves the $118$-stream untouched archive across nine datasets: $37$ streams from four zero-tuning external datasets (SMD, Exathlon, PSM, CreditCard), $1$ archive-heldout GECCO stream, and $80$ unseen streams from tuned datasets (SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, OPPORTUNITY; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a,b)).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and benchmark caveats:} Each stream fits strictly on its own healthy prefix, transferring only the frozen architecture, damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum across $K{\in}[2,55]$ without sharing weights across assets (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Because SMAP and MSL contain known labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, we add nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) tuning and $9$-fold dataset-exclusion sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}) to the dataset-clustered tests of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}.

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{Streaming pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($M{=}3$, $128$ dials, single quorum). Context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and target $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ windows pass through online and target encoders (Stage~1), multi-horizon prediction and whitening (Stage~2), Reynolds dial expansion (Stage~3), and per-dial tripwires with a quorum alarm at $\tau=t+S-1$ (Stage~4).\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-6pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($T$ time steps across $K$ channels) to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire an alarm only when enough dials trip together to clear a healthy-calibrated minimum count (a \emph{quorum}).

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Stage~1 compresses each raw telemetry window into a bounded $32$-number summary that retains both slow baselines and sudden spikes. A sliding $320$-step window splits at index $t$ into a past context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of length $C=256$ and a target window $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of length $S=64$, emitted causally at step $\tau = t + S - 1$. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), an initial real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) front end splits channels into four smooth frequency shells with per-shell causal convolutions before recombining them; lower-channel streams ($K < 25$) skip this spectral split. Features then pass through a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical} whose dilations scale down automatically when evaluating shorter target horizons.

Following the JEPA design \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ (weights $\theta$) and an exponential moving average (EMA) target encoder $E_\phi$ (weights $\phi$, decay $\beta_{\text{ema}}=0.996$) map each window into $D=32$ latent coordinates without representation collapse \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Each encoder summarizes temporal features using both level statistics (last step, max, mean) and variability statistics (spread, energy, and first differences), projects them through a compact head (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)), and applies a final affine-free LayerNorm onto a fixed-radius sphere. Normalization tames heavy-tailed healthy noise, though dividing by total variance also dilutes a single frozen sensor across all coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two lightweight additions keep sharp transients intact and remove static offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth tapping:} Because deep convolutional layers can smooth out brief spikes, both encoders also tap shallow feature blocks directly and blend their last-step and root-mean-square (RMS) summaries into the output through a learned gate bounded at $0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} Subtracting the healthy training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ before final LayerNorm prevents an off-center bias from compressing trajectories into one patch of the sphere, spreading nominal states across all directions while leaving post-calibration regime shifts to live drift monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction and Residual Whitening}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Predicting only one $64$-step target summary averages out brief faults, as in the single-horizon \emph{TS-JEPA} baseline trained with mean-squared error (MSE) and a log-determinant regularizer \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Instead, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ nested target prefixes of lengths $S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$ in parallel from the context embedding $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ plus a learned horizon embedding $\mathbf{p}_m$, using one shared residual multi-layer perceptron (MLP). This yields predicted summaries $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}$ for the EMA target summaries $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$, and the $224$-dial reference adds a $48$-step prefix ($M=4$). Weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and each shorter prefix equally at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$, training minimizes MSE plus a variance-covariance anti-collapse term $\mathcal{R}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M w_m \Bigl(\frac{1}{D}\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \mathcal{R}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\Bigr),
    \label{eq:laminar_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Across each training batch $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D}$ of $B$ predicted embeddings, $\mathcal{R}_\gamma$ keeps every coordinate's batch standard deviation $\sigma_d$ above $\gamma=0.75$ and penalizes off-diagonal sample covariance $\Sigma_{dj}$ between coordinates $d \neq j$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^D \max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d) + \frac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j} \Sigma_{dj}^2 ,
\end{equation}
avoiding collapse even at small batch size ($B=16 < D=32$) and raising effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

Raw prediction errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ form an elongated, tilted ellipsoid across horizons and coordinates. Thresholding each raw coordinate separately ignores these correlations: it misses coordinated off-axis shifts, and one shared noise mode can trip many correlated dials at once (Fig.~\ref{fig:zca_quorum_geometry}(a)). Stage~2 therefore incorporates the healthy covariance matrix first, reshaping the flattened error vector $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($96$ entries for \emph{Lite}) into an isotropic sphere via damped symmetric zero-phase component analysis (ZCA) whitening fitted on the healthy training block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:zca_quorum_geometry}(b)):
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}^{\text{white}}(t) = \mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}\bigl(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t) - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ is the healthy mean residual vector on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ is the symmetric square root of the damped precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega}$ (mapping each covariance eigenvalue $\lambda$ to $\lambda/(\lambda+10^{-3})^2$) so unobserved zero-variance directions map cleanly to zero on short histories. Unlike eigenbasis PCA whitening, symmetric ZCA stays closely aligned with the original coordinates (correlation $r=0.64\text{--}0.67$). A cheaper per-horizon variant ($\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$, using the $M\times M$ identity $\mathbf{I}_M$ and one shared $32\times 32$ factor $\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) matches joint ZCA across the archive (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Finally, noisy whitening directions are damped on a separate healthy check block $\mathbf{X}_q$. For each horizon $m$ and coordinate $c$, whenever the whitened RMS $\text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c)$ exceeds $1.10$, a weight $w_{m,c}\in(0,1]$ shrinks $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ toward its standardized raw error $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)$ to form the stabilized error $\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
    w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 3: Reynolds Residual Decomposition and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Applying Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} as post-hoc residual algebra separates sustained cross-horizon drift from short-lived fluctuations. The intuition is simple: if the short, medium, and long forecasts all miss in the same direction, the error is persistent, and signals sustained drift; if they disagree, the miss is a brief fluctuation. For every latent coordinate $c$, we split the stabilized multi-horizon error $\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ into its cross-horizon mean flow $\bar{e}_c(t)$ and fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) = \bar{e}_c(t) + F_{m,c}(t), \qquad \bar{e}_c(t) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t).
\end{equation}
Because fluctuations sum to zero across horizons, the cross term cancels and squared error energy partitions orthogonally into mean-drift energy and fluctuation energy: $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)^2 = \bar{e}_c(t)^2 + M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}(t)^2$.

Operational \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} ($M=3$, $D=32$) forms $128$ nonnegative \emph{dials} $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{128}$: $96$ per-horizon position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ plus $32$ Reynolds mean-drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$. The high-recall $224$-dial reference ($M=4$) also tracks $32$ fluctuation dials $\sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_m F_{m,c}(t)^2}$ and $32$ endpoint-difference dials $|\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t)-\tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, which \emph{Lite} omits because they raise false alarms without helping causal F1 (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel and Quorum Voting}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Multivariate residual scoring faces a three-way tradeoff. Collapsing all errors into one scalar norm allows intermittent heavy-tailed coordinates to inflate a global threshold and under-trigger on moderate faults elsewhere. Alarming whenever \emph{any} single dial crosses its threshold ($\max_j$) compounds false alarms across $128$ tests. Binarizing each whitened dial at its own healthy quantile threshold $\tau_j$ caps every coordinate's influence at a single vote, and firing only when enough dials trip together (a \emph{quorum}) absorbs correlated healthy bursts directly from the calibration block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (where trip-count variance is $7.2\times$ higher than under independence). Two practical limits apply. Binarization trades continuous joint sensitivity for bounded per-dial influence, so a subtle coordinated shift that stays below every dial's $98.5$th percentile can pass without tripping a quorum. Post-calibration regime shifts also break fixed thresholds and require readiness screening and Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift quarantine ($D_{\text{KS}}$; Section~\ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}). Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) operates in three steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial tripwires:} Using the binary indicator $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ ($1$ if true, $0$ otherwise), each dial $d_j(t)$ ($j \in \{1,\dots,128\}$) flips on ($b_j(t)=1$) whenever it exceeds its own $98.5$th healthy calibration percentile $q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Live causal vs.\ retrospective buffered timing:} For target window $[t, t+S)$, live \emph{causal attribution} emits its decision strictly at the right edge $\tau = t+S-1$ with zero lookahead. Because a fault starting at $t^*$ enters that right edge immediately, onset latency $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^* \ge 0$ (where $\tau_{\text{alm}}$ is the first alarm step) can be well below $63$ steps on sharp faults. Retrospective \emph{buffered attribution} instead spreads each horizon's trips across its target span $[t, t+S_m)$.
    \item \emph{Quorum alarm (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Counting active dials at step $\tau$ gives $N_{128}(\tau) = \sum_{j=1}^{128} b_j(\tau)$. The binary alarm $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ fires only when $N_{128}(\tau)$ clears the quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}$, set to $12\%$ above the $99.5$th percentile of healthy calibration trip counts $N_{128,\text{cal}}$ (with a minimum floor of $3.5$ active dials so at least four dials must agree):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) &= \mathbb{I}\bigl(N_{128}(\tau) > K_{\text{Lite}}\bigr), \\
        K_{\text{Lite}} &= \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr).
    \end{aligned}
        \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ 224-Dial Reference}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both archive tiers share the same four-block TCN backbone, $32$D heads, $K \ge 25$ depthwise rFFT shells, and parallel residual MLP predictor (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (128 dials):} Predicts $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$) and monitors $96$ position plus $32$ Reynolds mean-drift dials under the single quorum (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
    \item \emph{High-recall \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reference (224 dials):} Predicts $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$) and monitors $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ auxiliary onset relays that flag sharp faults at the window edge. It alarms when any of three healthy quorums is crossed: total dials ($K_{224}$), position dials ($K_{128}$), or relays ($K_{\text{edge}}$). Development tables also include its $5$-block sequential predecessor (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite, Screening, and Quarantine\label{alg:pipeline}}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Healthy history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\,\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active count $N_{128}(\tau)$.
\Statex \textit{1) Offline training and residual whitening:}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ across $S$-step gaps into $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize channels on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$)}
    \State $z_{\text{ctx}} \leftarrow E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, \; $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \leftarrow E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$.
    \State Update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:laminar_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow \beta_{\text{ema}}\phi + (1{-}\beta_{\text{ema}})\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Pick checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\State Freeze shrinkage weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\Statex \textit{2) Quorum calibration and readiness screening:}
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, form Reynolds dials, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\State Screen $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$: if $N_{\text{cal}}{<}500$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}{>}0.05$, or (strict) $D_{\text{KS}}{>}0.50$, route stream to 1D SPOT.
\Statex \textit{3) Live streaming and drift quarantine:}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t{-}C,\,t{+}S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (causal) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (buffered); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
    \If{sliding drift $D_{\text{KS}} \ge 0.50$}
        \State Keep alarming on frozen thresholds; buffer $400$ windows.
        \State Refit $(\tau_j, K_{\text{Lite}})$ only if mode switch is confirmed (else discard buffer); reset $D_{\text{KS}}$.
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (combining full-tensor and peak-horizon errors) collapse each window into one scalar score. That score averages the Euclidean norm and a temperature-$0.5$ softmax-weighted coordinate RMS with equal weight, then applies SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup and Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
Every architecture uses a $256$-step context, a $64$-step target (total window $W=320$, swept down to $S=1$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), a $32$-dimensional latent space, stride-$16$ training, and stride-$1$ live evaluation. Models train with AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) and zero per-dataset hyperparameter tuning, using $15\text{--}20$ fixed epochs on development benchmarks and up to $12$ early-stopped epochs on the archive.

\paragraph{Data Preprocessing}
Each stream retains all $K$ synchronized numeric sensor and command channels in native float32 chronological order, excluding timestamps and labels, after verifying all entries are finite. We apply no resampling, smoothing, detrending, outlier clipping, or synthetic augmentation. Per-channel means $\mu_k$ and standard deviations $\sigma_k$ are computed strictly on the training block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ so no calibration, held-out, or test statistics leak backward. Constant channels ($\sigma_k < 10^{-8}$, such as idle command bits in SMAP and MSL) receive unit scale ($1.0$), and active channels use $\tilde{\sigma}_k = \max(\sigma_k, 0.01)$ before standardizing $(x_{t,k}-\mu_k)/\tilde{\sigma}_k$ across every split. Each block is windowed independently ($W=320$, stride $16$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$, stride $1$ on calibration, screening, and test blocks) so no window crosses a split boundary; missing-value mean imputation (zero in standardized space) is used only in the dropout stress test (Section~\ref{subsec:computational_deployment}).

\paragraph{Five-Block Disjoint Calibration}
To guarantee zero threshold leakage, each stream's healthy history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ splits chronologically into five target-disjoint blocks separated by $64$-step isolation gaps (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}), freezing all weights and thresholds before test evaluation:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep,Modelsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Model fitting and stabilization:} The first block ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$, about $43\%$ of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ in the archive) fits channel scaling, encoder-predictor weights, and whitening $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$. A separate validation slice ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$) chooses the early-stopped checkpoint, and a third slice ($\mathbf{X}_q$) freezes the whitening shrinkage weights (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before calibration data are ever touched.
    \item \emph{Threshold calibration and held-out screening:} The final $35\%$ of $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ splits across a $64$-step gap into threshold calibration ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$) and held-out screening ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$). Widening all post-fit gaps from $64$ steps to a full $320$-step window across $49$ eligible streams shifts causal ER, Point-F1, and precision by at most $0.001$ (all $p \ge 0.66$), confirming negligible context bleed. Because overlapping windows correlate strongly, $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ is a practical operating rule rather than a formal tail guarantee (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Statistical Inference}
Random seeds are averaged within each stream first, with seed standard deviation $\le 0.008$ on causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER, keeping each telemetry stream as the unit of analysis. Because streams from the same facility share hardware, we pair $95\%$ dataset-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) with exact dataset sign-flip tests ($p_{\text{clus}}$), alongside stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests ($p_{\text{str}}$) with Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} across the four primary contrasts.

\subsection{Cohorts, Baselines, and Provenance}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Evaluations separate development from untouched testing across two cohorts. The \emph{development cohort} comprises the $19$-stream residual cache (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:matched_alert}) and the $33$-stream primary benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). All detector hyperparameters are frozen once on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL dev streams plus four archive tuning streams), leaving $118$ untouched archive streams across nine datasets (Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

Baselines span matched-backbone controls and external detectors. \emph{Matched-backbone controls} test single-horizon TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon 1D JEPA, alternative aggregators on frozen residuals, and Wu--Keogh cycle-swap, phase-desync, and noise checks against raw amplitude $\max_c|x_c(t)|$ \cite{wu2023flawed}. \emph{External baselines} cover TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}. We run Linear PCA, TranAD, and TimesNet across all $118$ untouched streams (three seeds, SPOT initial quantile $u=p_{98}$ and target risk $q=10^{-3}$).

\paragraph{Design vs.\ Archive-Informed Policy}
To keep pre-specified design separate from archive-driven policy, choices fall into three tiers:
(1)~\emph{Frozen before first archive run:} the entire \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} architecture, whitening settings, $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ rule, per-dial $q_{0.985}$ tripwire, quorum (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}), and live drift trigger ($D_{\text{KS}} \ge 0.50$).
(2)~\emph{Post-hoc archive diagnostics} (frozen detector unchanged): block permutation, matched-budget comparisons, PCA/ZCA checks, $W=320$ gap checks, and multiplicative floors.
(3)~\emph{Archive-informed readiness screening:} pre-deployment held-out FPR ($\le 5\%$) and drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \le 0.50$) gates that switch short or drifting streams to 1D SPOT scalar calibration. Nine-fold nested LODO validates those gate thresholds on each fold's eight labeled training datasets while deploying strictly label-free on the held-out ninth dataset (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a,c)).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
All predictions are scored step-by-step without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} and averaged across seeds before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) aggregation. Four dimensions summarize operator-facing performance:
(1)~\emph{False-alarm burden:} pointwise held-out $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and prospective $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ (mean, median, p90, p95), plus contiguous false-alarm episodes per $1{,}000$ healthy test steps.
(2)~\emph{Stepwise accuracy and ranking:} precision, recall, Point-F1 (compared against the all-positive baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}$ because F1 ignores true negatives \cite{chicco2020advantages} and penalizes onset-focused event detectors \cite{garg2022evaluation}), and area under the precision-recall curve (PR-AUC).
(3)~\emph{Event coverage and detection speed:} ER, the fraction of labeled fault episodes that receive at least one alarm (contrasted with random alarming $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$ at firing rate $p$), and onset latency $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^* \ge 0$, the steps from fault onset to first alarm, reported both on shared co-detected fault segments (\emph{segment-paired}) and across streams where both methods trigger (\emph{stream-paired}).
(4)~\emph{Calibration diagnostics:} autonomous healthy thresholds (\emph{Auto.}) are contrasted against matched calibration ($q^*=0.05$) and post-hoc test FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$), alongside calibration excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ and KS drift $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Dial Simplification, Quorum Design, and Whitening}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Before turning to the untouched archive, we ask which parts of the detector deserve to remain and which can be removed (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations} and \ref{tab:matched_alert}).

\noindent\emph{1) Which parts matter (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b)):} Monitoring coordinates separately drives the causal gain, and Reynolds residual decomposition delivers the sharpest single jump. On the $19$-stream cache, splitting the single score into $224$ dials raises causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$). Adding the $32$ Reynolds mean-drift dials on top of sub-horizon position dials jumps causal Point-F1 from $0.063$ to $0.098$ and cuts delay from $110.6$ to $74.8$ steps, whereas adding fluctuation and endpoint dials lowers causal F1. In single-factor retraining (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), a fifth TCN block raises causal Point-F1 from $0.073$ to $0.099$ at the same held-out FPR, so the four-block \emph{Lite} gives up some development accuracy for a smaller model. A fourth prefix adds less ($0.086$), three prefixes run $25.5\%$ faster than four, and sequential prefix prediction gives no consistent gain, so \emph{Lite} predicts three prefixes in parallel.

\noindent\emph{2) Why a quorum (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(a)):} Simpler per-coordinate rules either flood operators with alarms or go blind. Alarming whenever any of $128$ coordinates crosses its threshold fires on $40.6\%\text{--}66.5\%$ of healthy steps. Strict multiplicity corrections (\v{S}id\'{a}k, BH FDR) ask for p-values smaller than a short calibration set can produce, $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$, so they detect almost nothing. At a matched nominal rate, classical multivariate charts (Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, CUSUM) and a conformal minimum p-value still alarm on $21.1\%\text{--}58.6\%$ of held-out healthy steps. Only the dial quorum and Fisher pooling achieve useful F1 scores ($0.098$ and $0.109$) while maintaining held-out FPR near $1\%$.

\noindent\emph{3) Whitening choices:} Cheaper whitening costs no measurable causal F1. Applying one shared $32\times 32$ covariance factor independently to each horizon matches full joint ZCA within its $95\%$ CI ($+0.014$ causal F1, $[-0.006, +0.029]$). Standard PCA whitening also matches ZCA ($0.087$ vs.\ $0.086$ F1, $p=0.60$) as long as the shrinkage in (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) runs in the same rotated basis; mixing PCA rotation with raw-axis shrinkage drops causal F1 to $0.071$. Joint ZCA retains an advantage only under retrospective buffered scoring ($0.166$ vs.\ $0.146$ F1, $p=0.005$).

\noindent\emph{4) Sensitivity to settings:} Performance stays steady across broad hyperparameter ranges. \emph{Lite} beats the 1D baseline in ER at every latent dimension from $8$ to $64$. Causal F1 stays between $0.080$ and $0.093$ across all three calibration sweeps. Whitening damping spans $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$, the tripwire percentile $0.975$ to $0.990$, and the quorum safety margin $1.00$ to $1.20$.

\subsection{Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Single-Sensor Limits}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The panel detects faults that affect many sensors simultaneously but largely misses faults confined to a single sensor. Cross-channel inconsistencies, abrupt transients, and in-range phase shifts simultaneously move many latent coordinates and trip the quorum. A single frozen, saturated, or biased channel barely moves the $32$D summary, because the $K-1$ healthy channels dilute it by about $1/K$ and LayerNorm spreads what remains across all coordinates. On \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials, compared with $62.1$ for a harmonic flip.

A separate per-channel guard closes this gap on synthetic faults, raising recall on injected freezing, saturation, shock, ramp, and bias faults to $80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$. It also raises clean test FPR from $1.5\%$ to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$, so we leave it disabled.

\subsection{Multiscale Front-End and Latent vs.\ Observation Tails}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
The frequency-shell front end helps high-channel spacecraft data but not low-channel data. On \texttt{MSL M-1}, it raises F1 from $0.085$ to $0.410$, yet it lowers F1 on the nine-channel \texttt{Daphnet} and \texttt{GECCO} streams. We therefore enable it only when $K \ge 25$, an empirical rule rather than a principled cutoff. Separately, scoring in latent space tames heavy-tailed noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). Observation-space reconstructors show calibration kurtosis near $6.2$ and drift near $D_{\text{KS}}=0.45$. Adding LayerNorm alone brings reconstructor kurtosis down to $0.91$, and latent residuals roughly halve the drift ($D_{\text{KS}}=0.182\text{--}0.214$, $p\le 0.001$).

\subsection{Computational Footprint and Sensor Dropouts}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
\emph{Lite} is compact and keeps pace with live sensor rates on commodity hardware. The canonical model occupies under $1\,\text{MB}$ ($0.90\text{--}0.97\,\text{MB}$ FP32; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)). Even the larger $64$D-head variant needs only $7.1\text{--}10.8\,\text{ms}$ per window on one CPU thread ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s, over twice Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate). On a batched GPU, latency drops to $0.16\text{--}0.25\,\text{ms}$ with $44\text{--}128\,\text{KB}$ of streaming state. When sensors drop out without warning and are mean-imputed, ER falls only slightly ($54.7\%\text{--}55.9\%$ vs.\ $58.9\%$), while test FPR rises from $1.5\%$ to $3.4\%\text{--}7.9\%$.

\noindent\emph{Buffered vs.\ live scoring (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}):} Scores that look strong in offline review weaken in live streaming. \subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
Slow faults are a blind spot for any sliding-window predictor. When a fault develops more slowly than the $256$-step context, context and target drift together, and their difference stays small. Across $14$ GHL streams, F1 is essentially zero (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). On synthetic ramps, the advantage over chance disappears once a ramp lasts four context windows or longer, and mean delay grows nearly $20$-fold, from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $586.7$ steps on slow drifts.

A separate drift monitor recovers these faults but cannot tell them from benign wander. Adding a causal exponentially weighted moving average Mahalanobis monitor, anchored to the training block, raises GHL ER from $0.8\%$ to $57.7\%$. After each fault, however, GHL baselines wander, and the monitor's test FPR reaches $74.4\%$.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Each alarm can be broken down by which dials fired. Because each dial tracks a learned latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, this \emph{latent alarm decomposition} shows which horizons and drift terms drove an alarm (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On an abrupt \texttt{GECCO} fault, all dial groups surge to $4.8\times$ the quorum while 1D SPOT stays below threshold. On a contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} fault inside the normal amplitude range, the longer horizons and drift dials reach $2.02\times$ the quorum. On a slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramp, the panel stays below quorum while the frozen-reference drift monitor crosses its threshold mid-ramp.

To point operators toward a sensor, we mask one channel at a time on alarming windows, replacing it with its healthy mean, and rank channels by how many dials stop firing. On $14$ dev streams with injected $+2.5\sigma$ shifts on sensor subsets, this leave-one-channel-out screen names the right channel first $85.7\%$ of the time ($13.9\%$ by chance) and within the top three $92.9\%$ of the time. Field datasets lack root-cause labels, and latent features mix sensors nonlinearly, so this shows sensitivity to synthetic perturbations rather than causal localization of real failures.

\subsection{Untouched Archive Generalization, External Baselines, and Nested LODO Audit}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit} answer four questions about the $118$ untouched streams.

\noindent\emph{1) Lite catches more faults, sooner, at the cost of more false alarms (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} \emph{Lite} detects $64.6\%$ of fault events, compared with $52.5\%$ for the scalar 1D JEPA ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$). It also alarms earlier. On fault segments, both detectors catch; \emph{Lite} fires $41.4$ steps sooner, and comparing per-stream mean delays yields a $74.6$-step gain. The price is a higher false-alarm rate on healthy test data, $17.0\%$ versus $8.8\%$.

Point-F1 is mixed. It rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ overall and on six of nine datasets, but falls on SMAP, OPPORTUNITY, and MSL, so the dataset-level gain is not significant. \emph{Lite} helps most on streams with long healthy histories ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$), where Point-F1 doubles from $0.040$ to $0.085$ ($p=0.047$). Replacing the additive floor in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) with a multiplicative one changes only $0.04\%$ of decisions.

\noindent\emph{2) Lite vs.\ the 224-dial reference:} The larger panel catches more events ($69.4\%$ vs.\ $64.6\%$ ER, $p=0.016$) at similar Point-F1. \emph{Lite} raises fewer false alarms ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $7.0\%$ mean held-out FPR; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)) and runs $25.5\%$ faster. We therefore deploy \emph{Lite} and keep the $224$-dial panel as a high-recall reference.

\noindent\emph{3) External baselines and ranking parity:} TimesNet and TranAD reach higher raw F1 ($0.175$ and $0.157$) only by alarming on about a quarter of healthy steps ($24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ held-out FPR). Held to $1\%$ test FPR, they catch about $25\%$ of events, versus $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite} and $55.6\%$ for 1D. By contrast, against the scalar 1D JEPA, \emph{Lite} has no ranking advantage, with nearly identical PR-AUC ($0.152$ vs.\ $0.162$). In a matched-budget rerun across calibration rates ($0.5\%\text{--}5\%$) and post-hoc test FPR limits ($1\%, 5\%$), \emph{Lite} matches Fisher pooling and top-$k$ voting on ER. At a $1\%$ post-hoc test FPR limit, however, it trails a simple maximum-$z$ rule on ER ($0.529$ vs.\ $0.570$) and 1D on Point-F1 ($0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$). \emph{Lite}'s gains therefore come from setting a better label-free threshold, not from a superior anomaly score.

\noindent\emph{4) Generalization beyond the tuning data (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} The recall gain holds outside the data used for design. \emph{Lite} raises ER on all nine datasets ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$). On the four datasets never used during tuning ($37$ streams), ER rises by $15.1$ points ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0004$). With only four datasets, however, no dataset-level test can reach $p<0.125$, so this evidence remains suggestive. Nested LODO tuning, which picks thresholds without seeing the held-out dataset, selects the same setting in eight of nine folds and keeps the ER gain positive whichever dataset is removed ($+9.7$ to $+17.8$ points).

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Most false alarms come from a few streams whose operating mode changes after calibration. Of the $118$ streams, $104$ keep held-out FPR at or below $5\%$ (median $0.93\%$). The other $14$ involve Spark job changes, gait changes, or command-mode switches.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Mode changes, not too little data:} If these alarms came from a too-small calibration set, shuffling the healthy history would not help. Shuffling healthy blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ (twice the integrated autocorrelation time $\tau_{\text{int}}$) preserves sample size while breaking time order, cutting archive held-out FPR by $92.3\%$ (from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$). Even on the ten highest-FPR streams across SMAP, Exathlon, and Daphnet ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.44$ to $1.00$, including streams with $N_{\text{cal}} > 1{,}100$), block permutation drops held-out FPR from $11\%\text{--}100\%$ down to $0\%\text{--}3\%$. The alarms, therefore, reflect operating modes that are absent from the calibration.
    \item \emph{What changes:} In high-FPR streams, the shape of the residual covariance shifts, not its center. Covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger than in stable streams, while effective sample size and latent mean shift are similar.
    \item \emph{Recalibration policy:} The panel tolerates benign amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) but not mean steps or baseline walks ($14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$). Recalibrating automatically whenever drift appears would absorb real faults, cutting recall to $9.1\%$. Instead, once $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, the panel keeps alarming with frozen thresholds and buffers $400$ windows. It refits thresholds only after an operator or control signal confirms a planned mode change.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability, Three-Stage Screening, and Hybrid Routing}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Short, autocorrelated calibration histories cannot guarantee a false-alarm rate. Because neighboring windows overlap, the median stream has only about $65$ effectively independent calibration samples, and $105$ of $118$ streams have too few to estimate the $99.5$th percentile reliably. A moving-block bootstrap median threshold behaves like the plain quorum, while more conservative bootstrap bounds lose $8\text{--}17$ ER points.

\noindent\emph{1) Screening (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} Short histories, not drift, cause most rejections. Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59$ of $118$ streams and rejects all SMAP streams. Screening on held-out FPR keeps $52$, and the drift check keeps $46$. Screening on a short held-out block is optimistic. Accepted streams average $11.0\%$ test FPR after two stages, falling to $6.8\%$ after the drift check (median $2.9\%$ FPR). On these $46$ streams, \emph{Lite} fixes 1D SPOT's under-triggering, raising ER from $32.1\%$ to $49.8\%$ ($p_{\text{str}}\le 0.0004$).

\noindent\emph{2) Full hybrid vs.\ 1D SPOT:} Routing the accepted streams to \emph{Lite} and the rest to 1D SPOT gives a recall-first option, not a universal default. Over all $118$ streams, it raises ER from $52.5\%$ to $59.4\%$ ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0156$) and Point-F1 from $0.076$ to $0.091$, though the F1 gain is not significant at the dataset level ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.070$). Test FPR rises from $8.8\%$ to $10.0\%$, and false-alarm episodes from $8.8$ to $12.9$ per $1{,}000$ steps.

\noindent\emph{3) Choosing by cost (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(c)):} Let the operating cost $J(w) = (1 - \text{ER}) + w\,\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$, where $w$ sets how much one point of false-alarm rate costs relative to one point of missed events. With routing thresholds picked on eight datasets and applied label-free to the ninth ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ fixed), the hybrid beats 1D SPOT whenever $w<5.92$. Larger weights shrink the accepted set ($40$ streams at $w=5$, ten at $w=6$) until, at $w\ge 20$, every stream falls back to 1D SPOT.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Because the quorum needs enough stable, healthy history to calibrate, deployment should follow the label-free readiness check of Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}, with the cost weight $w$ in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(c) setting how readily streams are routed to the panel. Practical deployment therefore spans three tiers: (1)~the \emph{standalone panel} on streams passing readiness screening; (2)~\emph{complementary guards} for diluted flatlines, slow ramps, and mode switches; and (3)~\emph{scalar calibration} on short or drifting streams.

\noindent\emph{Where the gain comes from:} A pooled scalar score inherits the healthy tail of its noisiest coordinate, so its threshold rises until that coordinate stops firing, and moderate faults elsewhere stay below the line. Per-dial tripwires and a quorum remove that coupling (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). This is why the panel and the scalar JEPA rank anomalies about equally yet settle at different label-free operating points (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}). Relative to our earlier reconstruction-based detectors \cite{akbarian2023aelstm, akbarian2024aekmeans}, this work shifts scoring from raw-signal reconstruction to latent prediction and focuses on label-free threshold calibration across multiple streams.

\noindent\emph{Limitations:} Five limits bound these results. First, the quorum needs a long, stable, healthy history. Half of the $118$ archive streams have fewer than $500$ calibration windows, and forcing the panel onto short or drifting streams nearly doubles test FPR (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}), which is why the readiness check routes them to scalar calibration. Second, the panel largely misses faults confined to a single sensor because healthy channels dilute a single frozen or biased channel by about $1/K$ (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}); a per-channel guard closes this gap only at a higher false-alarm cost. Third, coordinate attribution is checked against synthetic perturbations rather than field root-cause reports. Fourth, SMAP and MSL carry known labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and GHL streams use three seeds rather than five, so we report dataset-clustered tests and effect sizes alongside stream-level tests. Finally, all evaluations are conducted offline on archived recordings, and the detector has not yet been deployed on a live system.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Evaluations across $33$ development and $118$ untouched archive streams support three main conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Fluid-inspired residual diagnostics:} Treating healthy latent trajectories as a steady laminar baseline, Reynolds residual algebra isolates sustained cross-horizon drift without the stiffness of physics-informed loss penalties. On the $19$-stream cache, adding these drift dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.063$ to $0.098$ and cuts delay from $110.6$ to $74.8$ steps.
    \item \emph{Replacing single-scalar thresholding:} Moving from one scalar threshold to per-coordinate tripwires and a healthy-calibrated quorum shifts the label-free operating point upward when scalar calibration under-triggers. Across $118$ untouched streams, standalone \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$) and cuts shared-fault delay by $41.4$ steps, while raising prospective test FPR from $8.8\%$ to $17.0\%$ and keeping ranking parity with 1D JEPA. Waveform reconstructors (TimesNet, TranAD) achieve higher raw F1 only at held-out FPR near $25\%$.
    \item \emph{Chronological drift and readiness-gated deployment:} Shuffling healthy blocks breaks chronological order, and cuts archive FPR by $92.3\%$, confirming operating-mode drift as the primary source of false alarms. Pairing \emph{Lite} with pre-deployment readiness screening and mode-confirmed quarantine across all $118$ streams achieves $59.4\%$ ER ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0156$) and $0.091$ Point-F1 at $10.0\%$ test FPR versus $52.5\%$ ER and $0.076$ F1 at $8.8\%$ test FPR for 1D SPOT.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Three empirical limits guide follow-on work:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cross-stream calibration:} Half of the archive streams have fewer than $500$ calibration windows (about $65$ independent samples). Pooling healthy covariance and tripwire priors across similar assets via hierarchical shrinkage could let multi-dial voting run on short histories without inflating false alarms.
    \item \emph{Regime-aware adaptation:} Mode switches triple residual covariance shift and require operator confirmation plus a $400$-window buffer. Conditioning whitening on known command modes, or updating a small per-regime covariance bank online after confirmed shifts, could cut downtime without absorbing faults.
    \item \emph{Multi-rate and single-channel guards:} Sliding windows miss ramps slower than four contexts and dilute single-sensor flatlines by $1/K$. Adding a control-conditioned slow-drift tracker and univariate flatline checks under one shared false-alarm budget would close both blind spots.
\end{enumerate}
