\begin{abstract}
Reconstructive anomaly detectors score raw observations, making them sensitive to benign sensor variation. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) forecast latent summaries, but a scalar residual threshold can be distorted by drifting coordinates. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA with an independently calibrated 224-bit circuit-breaker panel. Its 32 coordinates yield four horizon-error magnitudes plus horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference. Optional post-training quenching modifies selected predictor rows and residual-calibration buffers using nominal data. On a separately trained 19-stream development benchmark, the quenched panel achieves buffered point-wise F1 of 0.262 and causal false-alarm rate of 1.6 percent, with 59.6-step detection delay. In a repeat 19-stream ablation, the unquenched panel achieves causal F1 of 0.071 versus 0.013 for scalar scoring; a 160-dial subset reaches 0.098. These runs are not paired. On five selected streams, horizons of one to 64 steps show panel gains over scalar scoring, including zero structural reporting lag. Across 118 untouched held-out streams, the unquenched three-horizon panel raises causal F1 from 0.083 to 0.143 and event recall from 51 to 73 percent. In a matched 162-stream four-horizon comparison, quenching cuts nominal causal false alarms by 8.2 percent relative while slightly lowering causal F1. Threshold calibration depends on quantile and nominal history; slow ramps and single-sensor faults remain limitations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, chemical plants, server farms, and wearable medical devices report health through dozens of continuous sensor channels \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. When a valve sticks, a reaction wheel drifts, or a patient's gait freezes, an automated monitor must detect the fault promptly while learning healthy behavior from normal history alone, because labeled faults are rare. Equally important, the monitor must keep false alarms low during nominal operation so operators can trust its alerts.

\noindent\emph{Why Observation-Space Reconstructors Suffer High False-Alarm Rates:} Deep detectors such as TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} reconstruct the raw sensor signal and alert when reconstruction error is large. Raw telemetry, however, contains unforecastable sensor jitter, thermal drift, and disparate channel scales. Summed across 25 to 55 channels, these misses form a heavy-tailed residual with frequent spikes (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Calibrated on healthy data via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} and evaluated on later healthy data, reconstructors raise false alarms during $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of normal operation; a random alerter firing as often would catch $60.0\%$ of anomaly events, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\noindent\emph{The Bottleneck of Scalar Score Aggregation:} Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoid fitting raw noise by compressing each telemetry window into a 32-dimensional unit-sphere summary and predicting future summaries in latent space, cutting false alarms to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$. Yet existing latent detectors still sum all coordinate errors into one scalar score compared against one threshold. If one or two of the 32 coordinates shift slightly between training and calibration---variance-dominating coordinates we term \emph{hijacker dials}---they dominate the sum and inflate the threshold, so faults on the remaining 30 coordinates go undetected: causal Point-F1 falls to $0.050\text{--}0.052$ and average delay exceeds 240 steps.

\noindent\emph{Four-Stage Architecture (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} We replace scalar score aggregation with a four-stage pipeline, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}). First, a causal dilated encoder maps context and target windows onto a 32-dimensional spherical latent space via layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and forecasts nested target prefixes; training-residual covariance is filtered by a resolvent precision transform. Second, by structural analogy to Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical}, we decompose 32 coordinate error trajectories into four horizon errors, their mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference. Third, these $7\times32=224$ statistics feed independently calibrated circuit-breaker dials and 32 right-edge coincidence relays (\emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} when unquenched). Fourth, optional post-training \emph{weight-space quenching} scales selected predictor rows and adjusts calibration buffers before panel calibration.

\subsection{Contributions}
We separate our evaluation into a 19-stream (and 33-stream) development cohort for ablations and a disjoint 118-stream untouched held-out cohort (within a 141- to 162-stream archive across 15 datasets) providing primary generalization evidence (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Panel, quenching, and held-out cohorts:} On the reproducible 19-stream ablation cache, the unquenched panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ versus scalar scoring ($p=0.0023$), while the 160-dial subset reaches $0.098$. The separately trained 33-stream benchmark yields $0.262$ buffered Point-F1 and $59.6$-step causal delay for quenched \emph{Laminar-JEPA}; these are not paired measurements on the rechecked cache. On 118 untouched held-out streams, the unquenched three-horizon panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.083$ to $0.143$ and event recall from $51.2\%$ to $72.8\%$; four-horizon Laminar-JEPA reaches $62.2\%$ event recall. In a matched four-horizon 162-stream comparison, quenching lowers causal held-out false-positive rate (FPR) by $8.2\%$ relative but slightly lowers causal Point-F1.
    \item \emph{Mechanism and latency limits:} Matched-backbone controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) measure latent bottleneck and calibration-drift effects; scalar spectral reweighting offers no reliable benefit. The rechecked dial ladder in Panel~(a) and a separate residual-mean-buffer recentering study test scoring alternatives without identifying one universally best configuration. An exploratory five-stream sweep over $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ finds $1.9$--$4.3$ times the scalar causal Point-F1 at $S=1,4$; it cannot establish cross-domain latency generalization.
    \item \emph{False-alarm reduction and diagnostic fixed-budget recall:} On the 19 development streams, latent prediction holds autonomous causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($N=7$ dataset $p = 0.016$). Under a non-deployable post-hoc diagnostic control at $f^* = 1\%$ test FPR, the panel catches $58.4\%$ of anomaly events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet and reaches $0.658$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) on NASA contextual faults (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}--\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Calibration sample size, single-channel branch, and null results:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds the horizon-fluctuation concentration ratio $R_{\text{LT}} \in [\frac{1}{M-1}, 1]$ (a structural representation property; scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ reweighting is null, $\Delta\text{F1} = -0.005$). Moving-block bootstrap analyses suggest that longer nominal histories stabilize empirical thresholds, but $N_{\text{eff}}=300$ does not establish reliability for the $99.9$th percentile. Interquartile-range (IQR) and Beta-smoothed calibrators yielded test FPR of $0.47\%\text{--}1.32\%$ in the evaluated short-history settings. Across 17 multivariate streams and six injected single-sensor fault classes, a complementary channel-level branch raises event recall from $61.4\%\text{--}80.7\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}98.2\%$; these controlled injections do not establish performance on naturally occurring sensor faults, and slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timestamps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that trigger frequent false alarms under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: temporal and contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time} and TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximize contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.088$ and $0.097$ stream Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.186$ for latent prediction across 130 streams, Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from a separate target encoder, preventing collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Reynolds-Inspired Residual Decomposition and Circuit-Breaker Panels vs.\ Statistical Process Control:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman and operator-theoretic models \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} lift nonlinear dynamics into learned observable spaces. Rather than imposing partial differential equations on raw sensors, we apply a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon residual decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical}, by analogy, to split multi-horizon latent residuals into per-coordinate horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference. Classical multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} quadratic-form-pool whitened coordinates into a single Mahalanobis scalar, just as deep detectors collapse residuals into a 1D score calibrated via Pickands--Balkema--de Haan extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) fitting in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, where a single coordinate with out-of-sample variance inflation under serial correlation \cite{ferro2003inference} dominates the scalar threshold. Conversely, banks of univariate Shewhart or Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) charts \cite{page1954continuous} monitor raw channels or single statistics under Bonferroni or false-discovery-rate (FDR) multiplicity corrections \cite{benjamini1995controlling}, but lack multi-horizon kinematic perspectives, per-dial drift quarantine guards, and right-edge coincidence relays; soft attention ensemble voting across dials adds no benefit over Boolean quorums (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)). We evaluate all detectors using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm curves \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}, expanded in archive-wide evaluations to 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets and 162 streams across 15 datasets (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}). Thirty-three streams enter the primary protocol (19 non-GHL development streams and 14 GHL streams); 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. The suite spans four domains: \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry} covers six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$ channels), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$, four primary and two short-nominal), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$), where NASA's archive \cite{Hundman2018b} classifies each SMAP/MSL sequence as an out-of-range \emph{point} fault or in-range \emph{contextual} fault (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)). \emph{Wearable kinematics} covers six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals $1\,\text{s}$). \emph{Industrial and infrastructure telemetry} covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution ($K{=}9$, $37$ anomalies), Metro I-94 hourly traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$). Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation} evaluates six single-channel fault classes across all 17 multivariate development streams ($K > 1$).

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$) and labeled test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$. Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by a 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Primary 33-stream inclusion required $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ ($T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$) and $K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); among the 12 reserved streams, five have $T_{\text{nom}} \in [764, 1{,}492]$ ($N_{\text{cal}} \le 101$ across CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), six CICIDS streams have $K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \ge 1.36\times 10^5$, and GECCO ($K{=}9$) enters the archive cohorts. To eliminate subset-selection bias, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates all 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$ and $\ge 1$ test anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, 13 OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN) at three seeds, of which 118 streams were never inspected during development, and evaluates four-horizon Laminar-JEPA across 162 streams spanning 15 datasets (adding 14 GHL, Secure Water Treatment (SWaT), Water Distribution (WADI), and reserve streams; 139 untouched streams).

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within multi-stream archives share hardware platforms. We therefore treat dataset clusters ($N \in \{7, 9, 12, 15\}$) as co-primary statistical units alongside streams, reporting Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) verification (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Windowing, Multi-Depth TCN Hierarchy Tapping, and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to binary alarm decisions. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the stream at index $t$ into a historical \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$) and a future \emph{target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to causal decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). To separate slow drift from high-frequency vibration before cross-channel mixing, multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass) and are split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid normalized into an amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), filtered by per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that penalize raw waveform error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$, we map windows into a compact 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ to approximately radius-$\sqrt D$ layer-normalized vectors; the normalization epsilon prevents exact sphere membership. Target weights $\phi$ track online weights $\theta$ via an exponential moving average (EMA), $\phi_k \leftarrow m \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m) \theta_k$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ in archive cohorts) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Because deep dilated TCN stacks can smooth sharp onset spikes while non-stationary regimes shift the latent mean, Stage~1 introduces two operations inside both encoders:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth functional hierarchy tapping:} To preserve fast transient spikes alongside long-range context, a tap head extracts $L_{\text{tap}}=3$ feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow dyadic stem, mid-depth TCN block, and terminal TCN block. Concatenating the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy of each tap forms $\mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6 d_{\text{model}}}$, which is projected to $\mathbb{R}^D$ and fused with the deep representation $\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}}$ via a bounded gate $g = 0.5\,\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} = (1 - g)\,\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}} + g\,\operatorname{LN}\bigl(W_2\,\phi_{\text{GELU}}(\operatorname{LN}(W_1 \mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}}))\bigr).
        \label{eq:multidepth_tap}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} An online running centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (updated at rate $0.05$ on training batches) is copied synchronously to the EMA target encoder and subtracted from both context and target vectors prior to terminal LayerNorm, $z = \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}})$. This attenuates learned common-mode offsets without guaranteeing invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Nested Causal Multi-Horizon Rollout and Variance-Floor Decorrelation Objective}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a full-window forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a log-determinant volume term $-\frac{\beta}{D}\ln\det(\mathbf{\Sigma} + \epsilon\mathbf{I})$ ($\beta = 0.05$) related to VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. LayerNorm limits each vector's scale but does not bound its temporal tail kurtosis; pooling an $S=64$ window into one vector can average out brief faults.

To resolve how a trajectory evolves across the target window, Stage~2 of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} uses $M=4$ nested prefixes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ with $S_m=\min(S,\max(2,\lfloor mS/M\rfloor))$ for $m<M$ and $S_M=S$ ($\{16,32,48,64\}$ at $S=64$). Each is encoded by $E_\phi$ into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. At $S=1$, all four targets contain the same observation; distinct residuals arise from the predictor rollout, not from distinct target horizons. Starting from $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$, a recurrent sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ using learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the full-horizon anchor $P_\theta$ predicts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ ($m < M$) and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, training minimizes multi-horizon MSE alongside a non-collapse matrix penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MHR}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr).
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ penalizes collapse without requiring $B>D$. Its diagonal entries are $\sqrt{\max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d)}$, where $\sigma_d$ uses the batch-biased variance and $10^{-4}$ stabilization ($\gamma=0.75$). Off-diagonal entries are the sample covariances $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ ($d\ne j$), computed with the $B-1$ divisor. The distinct variance conventions matter for exact correspondence with the objective. At $B=16<D=32$, the measured effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ rises from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}. Archive experiments use three-horizon \emph{Speculative-JEPA} on $141$ streams (the fourth prefix is interpolated, $192$ statistics) and four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} on $162$ streams.

\subsection{Stage 3: Calibration-Stabilized Whitening, Surgical Weight-Space Quenching, and 224-Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Why does collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a 1D scalar score degrade real-time detection? Stacking the $M=4$ prediction errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ forms $\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$ across $MD=128$ coordinates. On training windows, the sample residual covariance has eigendecomposition $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. The deployed resolvent precision is
\[
    \mathbf{\Omega}
    =\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
    \frac{\lambda_k}{(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2}
    \right)\mathbf{U}^{\top}.
\]
This $MD\times MD$ precision matrix is positive semidefinite. Applying $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ to residuals centered by the training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ gives $\mathbf{E}^{\text{white}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$. This attenuates near-null modes rather than imposing unit training variance in every coordinate. Nominal calibration shifts can still inflate selected coordinates and dominate a scalar norm's tail. Stage~3 addresses this through two mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and calibration-stabilized whitening:} A nominal calibration shift can inflate a few residual coordinates. The implemented post-training intervention does not invert a LayerNorm Jacobian or apply an additive tangent-space bias correction. It ranks coordinates using training-to-calibration residual shifts, variance inflation, within-calibration drift, autocorrelation, and nominal trip frequency. For each of the top $k\in\{4,2\}$ coordinates, it scales the selected output row and bias of both predictor multilayer perceptrons and their output LayerNorm gains by $a_c=\operatorname{clip}((1+0.5\max(0,r_c))^{-1},0.35,0.85)$, where $r_c$ is the standardized coordinate instability score. It also moves the corresponding learned horizon embeddings by $0.35$ times the calibration-minus-training mean residual shift and blends each selected residual-mean buffer entry $75\%$ toward its recalculated calibration mean. For the $MD\times MD$ precision buffer, cross-coordinate entries incident to selected coordinates retain $15\%$ of their value; an eigendecomposition projects the result onto the positive-semidefinite cone before per-coordinate calibration-RMS rescaling. These are direct parameter and buffer edits, not an analytic cancellation of predictor bias. The candidate is accepted only if forward and reversed nominal half-split checks lower internal false alarms or near-threshold excess without increasing forward false alarms, while both main quorums retain at least $92\%$ of their original values; otherwise the original model is restored. This nominal-only check does not guarantee preservation of fault responses (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Any coordinate with $\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) > 1.10$ is then blended toward its diagonal-standardized residual $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t) = e_{m,c}(t) / (\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{raw}}(m,c) + 10^{-4})$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} By structural analogy to Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} (applied along the discrete lookahead axis $m \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ of each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$), we decompose $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and zero-mean fluctuation $e'_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$, forming $7D = 224$ non-negative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~\emph{sub-horizon position magnitudes ($4D = 128$)} $d_{\text{pos},m,c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ ($m \in \{1..4\}$);
    (b)~\emph{horizon-mean drift ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{drift},c}(t) = |\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
    (c)~\emph{horizon fluctuation ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sigma'_c(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M e'^2_{m,c}(t)}$; and
    (d)~\emph{endpoint difference ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} describes the dials after stabilization: subtracting their horizon mean separates residual energy into a mean and a zero-mean part. It also bounds endpoint difference and spectral concentration for that same residual matrix, not for the raw or independently transformed horizons; these structural bounds do not guarantee detection accuracy.

\begin{proposition}[Structural Characterization of Multi-Horizon Residual Energy and Spectral Concentration]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) denote the stabilized multi-horizon residual field with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\top \mathbf{1}_M \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Then:
(i)~\emph{Coordinate-wise energy orthogonality and endpoint bound:} For every coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,D\}$, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M d_{\text{pos},m,c}^2(t) = d_{\text{drift},c}^2(t) + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$, and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$.
(ii)~\emph{Spectral concentration bounds for the horizon Gram matrix:} Let $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ have ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ ($\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$). For any $\gamma > -1/2$, the spectral concentration ratio (following Lieb--Thirring power-sum moments \cite{lieb1976inequalities}),
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2}} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr] \quad (\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}),
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant, attaining $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) iff fluctuation energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ modes, and $1$ iff $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ yields a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + e'_{m,c})^2$ with $\sum_{m=1}^M e'_{m,c} = 0$ yields the energy identity, and Cauchy--Schwarz on $e'_{M,c} - e'_{1,c}$ gives $d_{\text{accel},c}^2 \le 2(e'^2_{M,c} + e'^2_{1,c}) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$, proving~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$ forces $\lambda_1 = 0$, so $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$, yielding (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Zero-Collapse 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel, Onset Relays, and Temporal Attribution}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Rather than summing the 224 dials into a scalar norm, Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}) equips each dial with an independent binary circuit breaker calibrated on nominal data. Because all 224 dials derive from the same $4 \times 32$ residual field, their trips are correlated by construction (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort, with joint trip-count variance $7.2\times$ that of independent trials), so alarm quorums are calibrated directly on the empirical distribution of simultaneous nominal trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and nominal hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized by its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled median absolute deviation (MAD) $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. To quarantine any individual coordinate that drifts during calibration, each dial's $98.5$th-percentile tripwire $\tau_j$ is scaled by a two-factor nominal hijack guard $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}},
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}$ is the ratio of second-half to first-half calibration 98th percentiles whenever $>1.35$ ($1$ otherwise), and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$ is $(m_j + s_j)/(1.5\,s_{\text{group},j})$ whenever $>1.25$ ($1$ otherwise, relative to perspective-group median $s_{\text{group},j}$).
    \item \emph{32 fast right-edge onset relays:} To trigger rapidly when an abrupt fault enters the right edge of the $S$-step window while earlier prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still observe nominal data, each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines a strict edge switch $b_{p,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_{p,c}(t) > q_{0.993}(z_{p,c,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_{p,c}^{1.25}\bigr)$ and fires its onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when a terminal-prefix trip ($m=4$) coincides with acceleration, fluctuation, or prefix $m=3$:
    \begin{equation}
        r_c(t) = b_{\text{pos},4,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \wedge \bigl(b_{\text{accel},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{fluc},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{pos},3,c}^{\text{edge}}(t)\bigr).
        \label{eq:onset_relay}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Causal trailing vs.\ buffered attribution and Boolean quorums:} Under \emph{causal trailing attribution}, switches on window $[t,t+S)$ are emitted at its right endpoint $\tau=t+S-1$ ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable, with structural lag $S-1$ and empirical delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, the 224-dial vector routes position votes to $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation votes to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint-difference votes to $[t+S_3,t+S)$; the separate 128-dial counter and onset relays instead average uniformly over the whole $S$-step window. At $S=1$, the buffered endpoint-difference span is empty. Summing active breakers gives $N_{224}(\tau)$, $N_{128}(\tau)$, and $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ (ranking score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)=N_{224}(\tau)+N_{128}(\tau)+2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ for PR-AUC). Binary alarms trigger when any counter exceeds its nominal-calibration quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_{\text{edge}} = \max\bigl(1.25\,q_{0.999}(N_{\text{edge},\text{cal}}),\, 2.5\bigr)$, \emph{224-Bit Breaker} sets $(K_{224}, K_{128}) = \bigl(\max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0),\allowbreak\, \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)\bigr)$ on nominal trip counts, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} tightens them to $\bigl(\max(1.18\,q_{0.9985}, 5.5),\allowbreak\, \max(1.20\,q_{0.9985}, 4.0)\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{1D Scalar Collapse Controls:} To isolate the gain of the 224-bit panel over 1D scalar scoring on identical backbones (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), we compare against a soft coordinate-saliency pooler $g_d(\mathbf{v}) = (1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha\sqrt{d\sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}$ with softmax weights $w_i \propto \exp(|v_i|/(\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}}))$ ($\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2/\sqrt{d} + 10^{-6}$, $\alpha = \tau_g = 0.5$). Single-horizon TS-JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$, and 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA combines full-field and peak-prefix norms via $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))^2 + \max_m g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))^2)}$ (scaled by $1 + \alpha_{\text{LT}}\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t)))$ for $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$), calibrated via extreme-value SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing} ($u = p_{98}, q = 10^{-3}$).

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Two-Stage Nominal Calibration, and Statistical Design}

All architectures operate on a streaming pipeline with context window $C=256$ and target horizon $S=64$ ($W = C + S = 320$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), trained on nominal windows at stride $16$ and evaluated at stride $1$. \paragraph{Two-Stage Nominal Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
Each stream's nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$):
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Training fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$):} Standardization parameters, encoder/predictor weights, running barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and resolvent precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$ are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$; archive checkpoint selection uses unsupervised predictive loss on the nominal calibration portion. The later nominal evaluation block is not used for this selection.
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Circuit-breaker calibration and held-out verification on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Sliding windows of length $W = 320$ inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2) yield $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window timestamps. Splitting at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $[0 : H)$ and \emph{held-out evaluation block} $[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so calibration and evaluation share zero target observations (a full $G=320$ gap check changes causal FPR by $<0.25$ percentage points). On $[0 : H)$, the panel fits $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), per-dial medians $m_j$ and MAD scales $s_j$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), which are frozen and evaluated on $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$ and on the test stream. Throughout the paper we distinguish nominal calibration window count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective independent sample size} $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int}}$ ($\tau_{\text{int}} = 1 + 2\sum_{k\ge 1}\rho_k \approx 3.23$).

\paragraph{Hierarchical Statistical Units, Dataset-Clustered Inference, and Hypothesis Testing}
Because streams within multi-stream archives share hardware and operating environments, we report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Stream-level and dataset-clustered inference:} Seeds are averaged within stream before two-sided paired Wilcoxon tests and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI; $2{,}000$ resamples). For multiplicity reporting, the two focal held-out causal Point-F1 contrasts (panel vs.\ Speculative-Alien-JEPA and vs.\ TS-JEPA) form one family; this family was not preregistered. Benjamini--Hochberg correction \cite{benjamini1995controlling} gives adjusted stream-level $p=3.1\times10^{-13}$ and $6.4\times10^{-11}$, respectively; both dataset sign-flip tests give adjusted $p=0.016$ across nine families. Quoted $p$-values elsewhere are raw and exploratory. Dataset-cluster bootstrap CIs and non-Exathlon held-out subsets ($n=101$) assess sensitivity to shared dataset origin.
(2)~\emph{Retrospective 12-fold LODO calibration-selection audit:} Selecting the calibrator on $D-1$ datasets across the 141-stream archive to maximize causal Point-F1 subject to $\text{FPR} \le 5\%$ selects the 224-bit rule in all $12/12$ folds. This analysis reuses the archive and is not an additional independent validation of the 118-stream comparison.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder, Circuit-Breaker Components, and Weight-Space Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) trace the expansion and component choices on the currently reproducible 19-stream cache. The separately trained study in Panel~(c) is not paired to this cache:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{From scalar collapse to sub-horizon switches:} The full panel raises causal F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ on this cache ($p=0.0023$), but its buffered difference of $+0.033$ has a stream-bootstrap interval spanning zero ($p=0.53$). The 160-dial position-plus-drift rule scores higher causal F1 ($0.098$) than all 224 dials. Scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation remains null in the separate fixed-backbone study.
    \item \emph{Component ablations:} Removing relays changes mean causal delay from $90.1$ to $90.7$ steps; removing prefix routing changes buffered delay from $83.7$ to $115.4$ steps. Without hijack guards, causal F1 rises from $0.071$ to $0.073$ and delay falls to $70.1$ steps on this cache, so these ablations do not establish a universal benefit for the guards, relays, or additional dial groups. None removes the $S-1$ causal reporting lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching components and sensitivity ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{0,\dots,12\}$):} On the development streams, the separate residual-mean-buffer recentering ablation (without predictor-weight edits) raises causal event recall from $56.5\%$ to $64.3\%$ and buffered F1 from $0.224$ to $0.256$, at the cost of higher nominal FPR. Precision tempering alone lowers buffered F1 to $0.135$, while RMS stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) raises causal F1 to $0.099$. Sweeping $k_{\text{rogue}}\in\{1,2,4,8,12\}$ shows that the selected $k\in\{4,2\}$ nominal-split-gated intervention lowers archive-wide causal FPR across 162 streams from $6.71\%$ to $6.17\%$ ($p=0.0011$), while more aggressive edits ($k\in\{8,12\}$) reduce causal PR-AUC from $0.292$ to $0.223$. This ablation does not isolate an additive predictor-bias correction.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Statistical detector controls on the same cached runs:} A position-plus-drift subset reaches causal F1 $0.098$ at nominal held-out FPR $0.006$, versus $0.071$ and $0.008$ for the full panel; its paired F1 interval spans zero. A top-$k$ vote reaches F1 $0.078$ at FPR $0.010$. Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM score F1 $0.271$--$0.282$ but incur held-out FPR $0.304$--$0.594$. These development controls do not establish a matched-alert-rate advantage over statistical process control.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When a single sensor out of $K$ freezes or drifts while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the isolated sensor perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$ before spherical normalization rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. On six injected streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), 1D TS-JEPA catches $19.4\%$ of quiet-fault events, standalone \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} reaches $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$, and a union with a rolling 32-step variance-collapse monitor reaches $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$. To evaluate a broader channel-level remedy across all 17 multivariate development streams ($K \in [5, 55]$, excluding univariate Yahoo), we pair the 224-bit panel with a complementary channel-level branch that monitors three causal per-channel statistics calibrated at $q_{0.999}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ with drift guards $\gamma_c^{\text{cal}}$: (i)~rolling 32-step variance collapse $\max(0, 1 - s_c(t)/\text{med}_{\text{cal}}(s_c))$, (ii)~causal first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) prediction residuals $|x_{t,c} - \hat{\phi}_{1,c} x_{t-1,c} - \hat{\phi}_{0,c}|$, and (iii)~rolling 32-step mean bias $|\bar{x}_{t,c}^{(32)} - \mu_{\text{cal},c}|$. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into the test windows of all 17 streams, adding the variance-collapse monitor lifts event recall from $61.4\%$ to $93.0\%$ on variance collapse, $63.2\%$ to $93.0\%$ on sensor freezing, and $73.7\%$ to $96.5\%$ on sensor saturation (tripling to quintupling causal F1 to $0.376\text{--}0.414$). Adding the full AR(1) and bias branch further lifts event recall on single-channel prediction shocks ($80.7\% \to 98.2\%$, causal F1 $0.139 \to 0.327$), gradual sensor degradation ($68.4\% \to 93.0\%$, F1 $0.080 \to 0.154$), and isolated sensor bias ($70.2\% \to 93.0\%$, F1 $0.095 \to 0.195$). On unmodified test streams, however, monitoring $3K$ channel statistics in parallel raises nominal causal FPR from $1.5\%$ (standalone 224-Bit) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full channel branch); thus, the variance-collapse union provides a practical safeguard for freeze and saturation faults, while full channel-residual monitoring requires cross-channel quorum gating when $K$ is large.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting across five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, and \texttt{GECCO}, three seeds) shows a clear dimensionality threshold: frequency-shell splitting substantially improves detection on high-dimensional spacecraft arrays ($K=55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K=25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), where separating slow thermal drift from high-frequency actuator chatter prevents cross-band masking, but lowers accuracy on low-channel streams ($K=9$ \texttt{Daphnet} and \texttt{GECCO}), paying off only when $K \ge 25$.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 15 model arms under matched budgets and parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five mechanisms governing false-alarm stability:
(1)~\emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations:} Using the identical causal dilated TCN encoder to forecast or reconstruct raw sensor waveforms leaves heavy residual tails ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), severe calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and high causal false-alarm rates ($\approx 25\%$) virtually unchanged.
(2)~\emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space:} Inserting a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck into the observation forecaster compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal false alarms, but predicting raw waveforms still leaves calibration drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$).
(3)~\emph{Learned latent target projection and whitening:} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ spherical space halves calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA, $p \le 0.001$).
(4)~\emph{Nonlinear TCN vs.\ linear PCA and Isolation Forest:} Linear principal-component forecasting maintains low false alarms but lacks the nonlinear capacity to capture fault dynamics, while Isolation Forest misses most anomaly events.
(5)~\emph{Prediction vs.\ autoencoding, frozen targets, and $\ell_2$ scoring:} Latent context autoencoding sacrifices causal accuracy relative to multi-horizon prediction, whereas freezing the target encoder or replacing Mahalanobis whitening with $\ell_2$ scoring still keeps causal false alarms below $2\%$, confirming that false-alarm suppression is an intrinsic geometric property of the normalized latent representation.

\subsection{Development 33-Stream and Untouched Held-Out 118/162-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following the cohort hierarchy in Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the separately trained 33-stream development benchmark under retrospective buffered attribution (Panel~(a)), causal streaming (Panel~(b)), and per-dataset stratification (Panel~(c)). Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} gives the 118-stream design-held-out comparison and the 162-stream matched quenching study. On a separate 130-stream baseline screen, TS-JEPA reaches stream-mean Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$. The observed reduction in nominal false alarms with latent scoring is specific to these protocols; spherical normalization alone does not prove a distributional guarantee.

\noindent\emph{Operational trade-offs:} The separately trained development comparison in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) favors \emph{Laminar-JEPA} for onset delay and buffered Point-F1, whereas the current-cache expansion in Panel~(a) favors 160 over 224 dials for causal F1. The unquenched three-horizon panel leads event recall and Point-F1 in the 118-stream held-out comparison; these arms have different backbones. In the matched four-horizon 162-stream archive, quenching lowers mean causal held-out FPR from $6.71\%$ to $6.17\%$ while causal Point-F1 falls from $0.0869$ to $0.0839$. Selection depends on the desired false-alarm and detection trade-off.

\noindent\emph{Scope boundaries:} Observation-space forecasters and reconstructors have high nominal false-alarm rates in the development comparison. Learned latent targets reduce false alarms under the tested protocol, but this does not show that LayerNorm guarantees light tails on arbitrary data. Both JEPAs and linear autoregressors struggle on the 14 GHL slow-ramp streams: low false-alarm rate without event recall is not detection. Deployment choices must account for sampling cadence, the causal lag $S-1$, stream-specific nominal drift, and the increased false alarms of the optional single-channel fault branch. No universal minimum calibration history follows from these experiments.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} exposes why standard 1D latent detectors break down when moved from retrospective buffered attribution to real-time causal streaming. Buffered attribution averages residuals across all 64 overlapping horizon predictions covering each timestamp, smoothing out transient noise and yielding a moderate right tail ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$). Under causal streaming, each decision relies on a single right-edge step without temporal averaging, nearly doubling tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$). When 128 whitened coordinates are summed into a 1D scalar score, one or two high-variance coordinates push the global SPOT threshold high onto that heavy tail, leaving scalar Reynolds JEPA blind to all but brief anomaly spikes. Replacing the scalar sum with 224 independently calibrated circuit-breaker switches and right-edge onset relays (\ref{eq:onset_relay}) prevents any single drifting coordinate from inflating the thresholds of the remaining 223 dials, sustaining detection across the fault window. Evaluating the analytical event recall of a rate-matched Bernoulli random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, further clarifies the operational stakes: at our panel's $<2\%$ causal false-alarm rate, random guessing catches only $28.1\%$ of events, whereas at the $24.3\%$ false-alarm rate of TimesNet, a random coin flip catches $60.0\%$ of anomaly windows by chance alone, surpassing TimesNet's own causal recall.

\subsection{Multi-Horizon 224-Bit Evaluation Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ and Latency Trade-Offs}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Because a target window of length $S$ emits its causal decision at $\tau=t+S-1$, $S=64$ imposes a 63-step reporting lag. We retrain on five selected streams at $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ to examine the latency trade-off. At $S=1$, all four predictor states are compared with the same one-step target, so this setting has no distinct target-horizon decomposition; at $S=4$, the shortest encoded prefix is two steps. In the buffered $S=1$ setting, the endpoint-difference votes have no routed span. These short-horizon results therefore test the implemented detector, not four distinct horizon views.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a), both panel variants exceed 1D scalar SPOT across the five measured horizons; the nominal false-alarm rate stays below $3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, the measured causal Point-F1 is $1.9$--$4.3$ times the scalar score, with event recall above $75\%$; at $S=4$, detection delay is $36.7$--$44.1$ steps. At $S=32$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches buffered F1 of $0.290$ versus $0.182$ for 1D, while the unquenched panel peaks in causal F1 at $S=64$. Five selected streams cannot establish a deployment rule across sampling rates or datasets.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine why multi-perspective latent circuit breaking shifts the detection frontier upward across operating budgets and fault taxonomies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Dominance across autonomous and diagnostic operating budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Along the causal recall-versus-false-alarm frontier in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), the autonomous operating points of \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit}, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} occupy the upper-left low-false-alarm regime without test-label tuning. When all architectures are evaluated at matched post-hoc test false-alarm budgets ($f^* \in \{1\%, 5\%\}$, dotted vertical guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) as a diagnostic check of tail sensitivity at equal alert rates, the 224-bit panel captures nearly three times as many anomaly events as TimesNet and nearly four times as many as TranAD at a $1\%$ false-alarm budget.
    \item \emph{Relational sensitivity to in-range contextual faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Stratifying benchmark events into out-of-range point spikes versus in-range contextual faults under both NASA's ground-truth spacecraft taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)) and a $5\sigma$ amplitude boundary across all 19 development streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)) reveals the physical source of this gap. Point anomalies that drive raw sensors beyond $5\sigma$ are readily flagged by simple amplitude thresholds and observation-space reconstructors alike. Contextual anomalies, however, occur well inside normal sensor amplitude bounds when cross-channel phase relationships, frequency spectra, or state-transition velocities decouple. Observation-space reconstructors suffer a sharp collapse on these in-range faults, whereas the 224-bit panel monitors horizon drift, fluctuation energy, and velocity shifts across whitened latent coordinates, doubling reconstructor recall on contextual faults while maintaining top performance on point anomalies.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Untouched Held-Out Generalization, Calibration Sample-Size Reliability, and Drift Decomposition}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and our archive-wide diagnostic audits establish three foundational results governing out-of-sample generalization and calibration reliability:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Independent generalization across 118 untouched streams and 162 archive streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}):} On the 118 held-out streams that were never inspected during architectural or calibration design, replacing 1D scalar scoring with the unquenched multi-horizon circuit-breaker panel nearly doubles causal Point-F1 ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$, positive across 8 of 9 dataset families with cluster-level $p = 0.008$), lifts event recall by more than twenty percentage points, and cuts causal onset delay by roughly thirty steps across all six multi-stream held-out datasets (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). On these streams the unquenched panel also exceeds Laminar-JEPA in causal Point-F1 ($0.143$ vs.\ $0.091$), buffered Point-F1 ($0.168$ vs.\ $0.149$), and causal event recall ($0.728$ vs.\ $0.622$). Across the full 162-stream, 15-dataset archive, weight-space quenching in four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reduces held-out causal false-alarm rates ($p = 0.0011$, dataset-clustered $p = 0.027$) and test false-alarm rates ($p < 10^{-4}$), achieving a median held-out causal FPR of $1.03\%$ ($70.4\%$ of streams below $2.0\%$).
    The two focal held-out causal Point-F1 contrasts give stream-mean gains $+0.060$ versus Spec.-Alien-JEPA and $+0.071$ versus TS-JEPA; corresponding 95\% dataset-bootstrap intervals are $[0.045,0.069]$ and $[0.048,0.089]$, respectively. Raw dataset sign-flip $p$-values are $0.008$ and $0.016$ (both adjusted $p=0.016$ for this two-contrast family). In the matched archive comparison, mean event recall decreases from $0.574$ to $0.558$ on the 161 streams with defined event recall; nominal-only quench selection cannot guarantee anomaly preservation.
    \item \emph{Calibration sample size and small-sample remedies:} Overlapping windows reduce effective sample size; $\tau_{\text{int}}\approx3.23$ is a cohort summary, not a constant for each stream or statistic. On the 118 held-out streams, histories below $250$ windows have $19.2\%$ mean causal FPR, versus $1.6\%$ for at least $2{,}500$ windows, but the stream populations and drift differ. At $N_{\text{eff}}=300$, expected effective tail counts are $4.5$ at $q_{0.985}$ (dial tripwires), $1.5$ at $q_{0.995}$ (main quorums), $0.45$ at $q_{0.9985}$ (Strict), and $0.3$ at $q_{0.999}$ (edge quorum). None gives a universal threshold-precision or FPR guarantee. Preliminary per-quantile bootstrap cells cover only two streams and cannot establish a population-level reliability boundary; the short-history IQR and Beta-smoothed results are likewise exploratory.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit via three-stage permutation decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) validation splits proves that excess false alarms are not caused by parametric tail misfit within stationary windows, but by inter-block regime drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), which drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in unconstrained TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in observation-space reconstructors. In our architecture, running-barycenter centering absorbs slow manifold translation to cut the inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$, while per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j$ quarantine individual drifting coordinates before they can trigger a global alarm.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We tested latent circuit-breaker panels on 33 development and 118 untouched held-out streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Circuit-breaker panel, quenching, and operational trade-offs:} Across the current 19-stream cache, the unquenched panel raises causal Point-F1 over 1D collapse ($0.013$ to $0.071$, $p=0.0023$), while a 160-dial subset reaches $0.098$; its buffered F1 gain is not reliable. On $118$ held-out streams, the three-horizon panel improves causal Point-F1 ($p=1.6{\times}10^{-13}$, dataset-level $p=0.008$) but differs from the four-horizon Laminar backbone. In a matched four-horizon comparison across the $162$-stream archive, quenching reduces held-out causal false alarms ($p=0.0011$) while slightly reducing causal Point-F1.
    \item \emph{Mechanisms and horizon scaling:} Matched-backbone ablations measure LayerNorm tail compression and barycenter drift suppression; scalar spectral reweighting has no reliable benefit. Across five selected streams, the $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ horizon sweep yields a $1.9\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 advantage over scalar scoring, including at zero structural reporting lag ($S=1$). This small sweep does not establish a general latency benefit across datasets.
    \item \emph{Applicability boundaries, calibration, and single-channel branch:} The evaluated multichannel streams and nominal histories show a dependence of calibration stability on quantile and sample size. At $N_{\text{eff}}=300$, the expected effective tail count is $1.5$ at $q_{0.995}$ but only $0.3$ at $q_{0.999}$; no universal empirical edge-quorum reliability threshold follows. IQR extrapolation and Beta-smoothed quantiles lowered false alarms in the evaluated short-history settings. A complementary channel-level variance and AR(1) residual branch raises recall above $93\%$ on injected single-sensor faults, at the cost of higher nominal alert rates. Naturally occurring sensor failures and slow process ramps (GHL, Metro) remain unresolved.
\end{enumerate}
