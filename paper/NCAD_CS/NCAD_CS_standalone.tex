\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still sum many prediction errors into one anomaly score, so one or two coordinates that drift between training and calibration can dominate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces the scalar score and its tail-fitted threshold. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their mean, spread, and endpoint difference, borrowed by analogy from Reynolds decomposition), and each statistic receives its own breaker calibrated on healthy data with a drift guard. The breakers are statistically dependent, so alarm quorums are set from joint nominal trip counts. Across 141 multivariate telemetry streams from 12 datasets, the panel raises strictly causal point-wise F1 from 0.067 to 0.077 for scalar JEPAs to 0.139, lifts causal event recall to 72.7 percent, and more than halves detection delay. The panel does not cover every fault type: it misses slow process ramps in a gasoil heating loop and most single-channel sensor freezes, which a simple variance monitor only partly recovers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, chemical plants, server farms, and wearable medical devices all report their health through dozens of sensor channels that never stop streaming \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. Somewhere in that stream a fault begins: a valve sticks, a reaction wheel drifts, a patient's gait freezes. An automated monitor has to notice before people do, and it has to learn what healthy behavior looks like from normal operating history alone, because real faults are rare and seldom labeled in advance. It also has to stay quiet the rest of the time. A monitor that cries wolf every few minutes is soon ignored, and an ignored alarm protects no one.

\noindent\emph{Why Today's Detectors Flood the Console:} Leading deep detectors such as TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} learn to reconstruct the raw sensor signal and raise an alarm wherever the reconstruction misses. Raw telemetry, however, is full of noise that no model can predict: sensor jitter, thermal drift, and channels recorded on very different scales. Summed across 25 to 55 channels, these small misses form an error signal with frequent extreme spikes (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). We calibrate each detector on healthy data with an automated tail rule, Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, and then run it on later healthy data, exactly as a deployed system would. The reconstructors raise false alarms during $22\%$ to $31\%$ of normal operation. At that rate the alarms carry almost no information: a random alerter firing just as often would catch $60.0\%$ of real anomaly events, slightly more than the $58.8\%$ that TimesNet catches.

\noindent\emph{The Hidden Weak Link: One Score, One Threshold:} A more promising idea is to stop predicting the noise. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} compress each window of telemetry into a short summary of 32 numbers and predict the summary of what comes next instead of every raw reading. Because the summary keeps the structure of the signal and discards most of the jitter, false alarms fall by an order of magnitude, to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ of healthy operation. Yet latent detectors keep one habit from their predecessors. At the final step they add all of their prediction errors into a single anomaly score and sound the alarm when that score crosses a threshold. Our analysis shows that this last step is where much of the signal is lost. If one or two of the 32 summary coordinates drift slightly between training and calibration, as routinely happens in long-running systems, they dominate the sum. We call these coordinates \emph{hijackers}. The threshold must rise to tolerate them, and genuine faults on the remaining 30 coordinates no longer clear it. Real-time (causal) Point-F1, a standard measure that balances missed and false detections at every time step, falls to $0.050\text{--}0.052$, and faults go unnoticed for more than 240 steps.

\noindent\emph{Replacing the Threshold with a Circuit-Breaker Panel:} We remove that final step. Instead of one score and one threshold, our model, \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, reports to a panel of 224 separately calibrated circuit breakers. The analogy is the breaker box in a building: a fault on one circuit trips its own switch. To build the panel we borrow an idea from fluid mechanics, by analogy only. Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} splits a turbulent flow into a steady mean current and the fluctuations around it. We apply the same split to each of the 32 prediction errors as it evolves over four nested look-ahead horizons of 16, 32, 48, and 64 steps, which gives seven views of every coordinate: its error at each of the four horizons, its average drift, its fluctuation, and its acceleration at the newest edge of the window. Each of the resulting $7 \times 32 = 224$ dials has its own breaker, set from healthy calibration data. A guard computed only from nominal data damps dials that had already drifted before deployment, so a hijacker mostly trips its own breaker instead of raising one shared threshold for every coordinate. An alarm sounds when enough breakers trip together, or at once when one of 32 fast onset relays sees a coordinate's newest horizon and its acceleration dial trip simultaneously. No single anomaly score is ever formed, and no tail threshold is fitted to one. Upstream, the encoder subtracts a running average of recent summaries to cancel slow level shifts and is trained so that its multi-horizon errors stay decorrelated.

\subsection{Contributions}
We evaluate the approach on 141 telemetry streams from 12 public datasets spanning spacecraft, industrial plants, server clusters, and wearables. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{A breaker panel in place of a thresholded score:} We replace the single anomaly score and its tail-fitted threshold with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel built from Reynolds-decomposed latent errors, nominal-only hijack guards, and 32 fast onset relays (Section~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}). Applied to the same trained network on 19 primary streams, the panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($17$ wins, $0$ losses, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$). Across all 141 streams it raises causal Point-F1 from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.139$ ($114$ wins, $18$ losses, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$), lifts causal event recall to $72.7\%$, and halves detection delay; the causal gain holds on the $118$ streams never used to tune the panel (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}--\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
    \item \emph{Where the gains come from:} Matched-size ablations ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ parameters) trace each step. A layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bottleneck tames extreme error spikes, cutting excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ from $6.10$ to $0.91$; predicting learned summaries instead of raw readings halves calibration drift; and multi-horizon barycenter whitening cuts the share of false alarms caused by regime drift from $71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$. A six-step ladder from one dial to 224, together with leave-one-out tests, isolates each dial family, the onset relays, prefix routing, and the hijack guards (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).
    \item \emph{Fewer false alarms and better recall at a fixed alarm budget:} On the 19 primary streams, latent prediction holds causal false-positive rates (FPRs) at $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$, against $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors. When every detector receives the same $1\%$ false-alarm budget, the panel catches $58.4\%$ of anomaly events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and on five NASA in-range contextual sequences it reaches $0.658$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Limits, theory, and latency:} A sensor that freezes silently barely moves the latent summary, so the panel alone catches only $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ of such events and a rolling variance monitor is needed to reach $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}); slow process ramps remain undetected. Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds a spectral concentration ratio of the horizon fluctuations to $[\frac{1}{M-1}, 1]$, reweighting a scalar score by it gives no measurable benefit, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) remove the 63-step reporting lag (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timestamps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that trigger severe false-alarm flooding under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: temporal and contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time} and TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximize contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent prediction across 11 datasets, Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from a separate target encoder, preventing collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamic Decomposition and Multi-Perspective Circuit Breakers vs.\ Scalar Thresholding:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman and operator-theoretic models \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} lift nonlinear dynamics into learned observable spaces. Rather than imposing partial differential equations on raw sensors, we borrow Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} from turbulence modeling, by analogy, to split multi-horizon latent residuals into a per-coordinate mean, fluctuation, and endpoint acceleration. Existing detectors collapse high-dimensional residuals into a 1D scalar score and extrapolate a threshold via Pickands--Balkema--de Haan extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) fitting in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, where a single drifting coordinate under serial correlation \cite{ferro2003inference} can hijack the scalar threshold. We replace 1D scalar collapse with a 224-bit multi-perspective Boolean circuit-breaker panel and evaluate all detectors using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm curves \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}, expanded to 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets in our archive-wide evaluation. Thirty-three streams enter the primary multi-seed held-out protocol; 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. The suite spans four physical domains: \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry} covers six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$ channels), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$, four primary and two short-nominal), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$), where NASA's ground-truth archive \cite{Hundman2018b} classifies each SMAP/MSL anomaly sequence as either an out-of-range \emph{point} fault or an in-range \emph{contextual} fault (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)). \emph{Wearable kinematics} covers six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} sampled at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ gait-freezing episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals a $1\,\text{s}$ horizon). \emph{Industrial processes and smart infrastructure} covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ testbed valve and heater faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution ($K{=}9$, $37$ hydrological anomalies), Metro I-94 hourly highway traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$). Cohort~3 (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}) separately injects six controlled single-channel faults into six real streams.

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$ across the 33 primary streams) and labeled anomaly test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatlines, matching standardized archive subsets in prior benchmarks \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly}.

Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by a 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Primary 33-stream inclusion required $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ ($T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$) and $K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); among the 12 reserved streams, five have $T_{\text{nom}} \in [764, 1{,}492]$ ($N_{\text{cal}} \le 101$ across CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), six CICIDS streams have $K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \ge 1.36\times 10^5$, and GECCO ($K{=}9$, $1$ held-out event) enters Cohorts 3--4. To eliminate subset-selection bias, Cohort~5 evaluates all 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$ and $\ge 1$ test anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, 13 OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN) at three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}); the 14 GHL streams (reported in Cohort~1) and six CICIDS streams also meet the rule but are excluded by dataset.

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware platforms ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations (SDs), zero-tuning leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)).

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Windowing, Multi-Depth TCN Hierarchy Tapping, and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to binary alarm decisions. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and partitions the stream at index $t$ into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$) and a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). For $K > 1$, a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Channels are decomposed across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit heavy-tailed sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:related}), we map windows into a compact 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ onto the sphere $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, where target weights $\phi$ track online weights $\theta$ via an exponential moving average (EMA), $\phi_k \leftarrow m \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m) \theta_k$ with fixed momentum $m = 0.996$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Because deep dilated TCN blocks smooth out sharp onset spikes while non-stationary streams shift the latent mean, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA introduces two structural operations inside both encoders:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth functional hierarchy tapping:} A tap head extracts $L_{\text{tap}}=3$ intermediate feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow dyadic front-end, the mid-depth TCN block, and the terminal TCN block. For each tap $\ell$, the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy are concatenated into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6 d_{\text{model}}}$, projected into $\mathbb{R}^D$, and fused with the deep representation $\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}}$ via a bounded gate $g = 0.5\,\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} = (1 - g)\,\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}} + g\,\operatorname{LN}\bigl(W_2\,\phi_{\text{GELU}}(\operatorname{LN}(W_1 \mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}}))\bigr).
        \label{eq:multidepth_tap}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} An online running centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ updated at rate $0.05$ on training batches is copied synchronously to the EMA target tap head and subtracted from both context and target fused vectors before terminal LayerNorm projection $z = \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}) \in \sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, canceling common-mode baseline drift between windows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Nested Causal Multi-Horizon Rollout and Covariance Whitening Defect Objective}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-window forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with predictive alignment and VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg} ($\gamma_{\text{var}} = 1.0$). Terminal LayerNorm bounds representations on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ and compresses residual kurtosis relative to observation-space reconstructors, but pooling the entire $S=64$ target window into a single vector averages out short-lived sub-horizon transients.

To resolve sub-window dynamics without sacrificing the 64-step lookahead, Stage~2 of \emph{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} partitions the target horizon $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefixes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ of lengths $S_m = (m/M)S \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ (or $M=3$ prefixes $\{16, 32, 64\}$ in our fast archive-wide variant \emph{Speculative-JEPA}). The EMA target encoder $E_\phi$ encodes each prefix into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. Starting from $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$, a sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ using learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the full-horizon anchor $P_\theta$ forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ ($m < M$) and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, training minimizes multi-horizon mean-squared error (MSE) plus a Frobenius covariance whitening defect:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Sol-Rey}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ measures the whitening defect of centered batch predictions $\mathbf{Z}_c = \mathbf{Z} - \mathbf{1}_B \bar{\mathbf{z}}^\top$ with empirical covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{Z}) = (B-1)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_c^\top \mathbf{Z}_c$ and marginal standard deviations $\sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}) = \sqrt{\Sigma_{dd}(\mathbf{Z}) + \epsilon}$ ($\gamma = 0.75$, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$), defined by $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dd} = \sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}))}$ and $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dj} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Sigma_{dj}(\mathbf{Z})$ ($d \neq j$). Unlike log-determinant barriers that become singular when batch size $B < D$, $\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\|_F^2$ remains smooth for any $B \ge 2$ and raises effective representation rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}.

\subsection{Stage 3: Calibration-Stabilized Whitening and 224-Dial Fluid-Dynamic Reynolds Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Why does collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a single 1D anomaly score degrade point-wise F1? Let $\mathbf{E}(t) = [\mathbf{e}_1(t), \dots, \mathbf{e}_M(t)]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ stack the $M=4$ raw sub-horizon prediction residuals $\mathbf{e}_m(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ across all $MD = 128$ coordinates. Fitting the Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ \cite{ledoit2004well} and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows produces Mahalanobis-whitened residuals $\mathbf{E}^{\text{white}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ with unit training variance per coordinate. On unseen nominal calibration windows, however, one or two whitened directions often experience mild out-of-sample variance growth (\emph{hijacker dials}) where $\mathbf{\Omega}$ amplifies small covariance shifts. When all 128 coordinates are summed or pooled into a 1D scalar score before calibration, those one or two hijacker dials dominate the upper calibration tail and push any scalar threshold to the extreme tip of the distribution, so that only isolated peak timestamps inside a true anomaly cross the threshold.

Stage~3 prevents both precision-matrix amplification and 1D coordinate collapse through two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration-stabilized coordinate whitening:} On nominal calibration windows, we measure the whitened RMS $\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c)$ and raw RMS $\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{raw}}(m,c)$ of each coordinate $(m,c)$. Any coordinate whose whitened calibration RMS exceeds $1.10$ is smoothly blended back toward its calibration-normalized raw residual $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t) = e_{m,c}(t) / (\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{raw}}(m,c) + 10^{-4})$ via the rational stability weight $w_{m,c}$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Coordinate-wise Reynolds decomposition into $7D = 224$ fluid-dynamic dials:} In fluid turbulence, Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} separates a flow field into a mean and zero-mean fluctuations. We borrow this split by analogy only: it is applied along the four-point lookahead axis $m \in \{1,\dots,M\}$, not over time or a spatial field, and amounts to a per-coordinate mean, population standard deviation (denominator $M$), and endpoint difference across horizons. Rather than collapsing across the $D=32$ latent coordinates, we expand the stabilized residual field $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into $7D = 224$ non-negative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
        \item \emph{Sub-horizon flow-position magnitudes ($4D = 128$ dials):} $d_{\text{pos},m,c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ for $m \in \{1,2,3,4\}$, isolating fault magnitude at prefix horizons $\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$.
        \item \emph{Reynolds mean-flow transport / slow-drift dials ($D = 32$ dials):} $d_{\text{drift},c}(t) = |\bar{e}_c(t)| = \bigl|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)\bigr|$, capturing persistent trajectory displacement across all four prefixes.
        \item \emph{Reynolds turbulent-fluctuation / shear dials ($D = 32$ dials):} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sigma'_c(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t))^2}$, capturing inter-horizon shear and oscillatory divergence.
        \item \emph{Convective right-edge acceleration dials ($D = 32$ dials):} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, measuring newly entering fault energy in the terminal sub-block $[48, 64]$ relative to the initial prefix $[0, 16]$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate-Wise Energy Orthogonality, Acceleration Bound, and Spectral Concentration]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) denote the stabilized multi-horizon residual field with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\top \mathbf{1}_M \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Then:
(i)~\emph{Coordinate-wise Reynolds energy orthogonality and acceleration bound:} For every coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,D\}$, mean-square sub-horizon position energy decomposes orthogonally into mean-flow transport and turbulent fluctuation, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M d_{\text{pos},m,c}^2(t) = d_{\text{drift},c}^2(t) + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$, and convective right-edge acceleration satisfies $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$.
(ii)~\emph{Spectral concentration bounds for the horizon Gram matrix:} Let $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ (an $M \times M$ matrix) have ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ (since $\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$). For any $\gamma > -1/2$, the spectral concentration ratio, whose power-sum form follows the Lieb--Thirring moments \cite{lieb1976inequalities},
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2}},
    \label{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant ($R_{\text{LT}}(c\mathbf{E}') = R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$ for $c > 0$) and satisfies for any $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{M - 1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1,
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
attaining $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) iff fluctuation energy is isotropic across $M-1$ modes, and $1$ iff $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$. These are elementary eigenvalue inequalities rather than a new Lieb--Thirring result and imply no physical wave propagation; the implementation adds $10^{-12}$ to the denominator only as a numerical guard near $\mathbf{E}' = \mathbf{0}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each coordinate $c$, expanding $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + e'_{m,c})^2$ with $\sum_{m=1}^M e'_{m,c} = 0$ yields the orthogonal energy identity; Cauchy--Schwarz on $\tilde{e}_{M,c} - \tilde{e}_{1,c} = e'_{M,c} - e'_{1,c}$ gives $d_{\text{accel},c}^2 \le 2(e'^2_{M,c} + e'^2_{1,c}) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$, proving~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{E}'(\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M) = \mathbf{0}$ forces $\lambda_1 = 0$, leaving at most $M-1$ positive eigenvalues $\lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ with $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$, which yields (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Zero-Collapse 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel, Onset Relays, and Temporal Attribution}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
In Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}), each of the 224 dials receives its own binary circuit breaker calibrated strictly on nominal data. The breakers are separately calibrated but not statistically independent: all derive from the same $4 \times 32$ residual field, so position, drift, fluctuation, and acceleration dials of one coordinate are correlated by construction (Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse}(i)), and the quorums below are calibrated empirically on the joint nominal trip counts rather than from an independence assumption:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and nominal hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ (and $j \in \{1,\dots,128\}$ in the 128-bit sub-block panel) is standardized by its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled median absolute deviation (MAD) $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into a non-negative excess score $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. To quarantine any drifting dial individually without desensitizing the remaining dials, each tripwire $\tau_j$ scales its $98.5$th calibration percentile by a two-factor nominal hijack guard $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}},
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}$ equals the ratio of second-half to first-half calibration 98th percentiles whenever that ratio exceeds $1.35$ (and $1$ otherwise), and the cross-dial scale guard $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$, computed from calibration statistics only, equals $(m_j + s_j)/(1.5\,s_{\text{group},j})$ whenever that ratio exceeds $1.25$, where $s_{\text{group},j}$ is the median of $m_j + s_j$ over the dial's perspective group (and $1$ otherwise).
    \item \emph{32 fast right-edge onset relays:} To catch newly entering faults in the terminal sub-block $[48, 64]$ without waiting for earlier prefixes to saturate, each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines a stricter edge switch $b_{p,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_{p,c}(t) > q_{0.993}(z_{p,c,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_{p,c}^{1.25}\bigr)$ across perspectives $p$ and fires its onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when the terminal sub-block ($m=4$) coincides with convective acceleration, turbulent fluctuation, or the third sub-block ($m=3$):
    \begin{equation}
        r_c(t) = b_{\text{pos},4,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \wedge \bigl(b_{\text{accel},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{fluc},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{pos},3,c}^{\text{edge}}(t)\bigr).
        \label{eq:onset_relay}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Causal trailing vs.\ prefix-routed buffered attribution and Boolean quorums:} Under \emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, window switches at index $t$ are emitted solely at the right endpoint $\tau = t + S - 1$, which observes only $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$ ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable, with structural emission lag $S-1 = 63$ steps vs.\ empirical detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, each perspective group votes only over the exact time steps inside $[t, t+S)$ that it physically observes: $[t, t+S_m)$ for sub-block $m \in \{1,2,3,4\}$, $[t, t+S)$ for mean drift and fluctuation, and $[t+S_3, t+S_4)$ for right-edge acceleration. Summing active switches at timestamp $\tau$ yields counts $N_{224}(\tau) = \sum_{j=1}^{224} \bar{b}_j(\tau)$, $N_{128}(\tau) = \sum_{j=1}^{128} \bar{b}_{128,j}(\tau)$, and $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) = \sum_{c=1}^{32} \bar{r}_c(\tau)$, with combined count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = N_{224}(\tau) + N_{128}(\tau) + 2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ used for threshold-free PR-AUC ranking. The 128-bit counter reuses the 128 position dials of the 224-bit panel with the same thresholds: under causal attribution it is a sub-count of $N_{224}$ that up-weights the position dials, and it differs from that sub-count only under buffered attribution, where it votes uniformly over $[t, t+S)$ instead of by prefix. Binary alarms trigger when any counter exceeds its nominal calibration quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_{\text{edge}} = \max\bigl(1.25\,q_{0.999}(N_{\text{edge},\text{cal}}),\, 2.5\bigr)$, \emph{224-Bit Breaker} sets $K_{224} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{224,\text{cal}}),\, 5.0\bigr)$ and $K_{128} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\, 3.5\bigr)$, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} sets $K_{224} = \max\bigl(1.18\,q_{0.9985}(N_{224,\text{cal}}),\, 5.5\bigr)$ and $K_{128} = \max\bigl(1.20\,q_{0.9985}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\, 4.0\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{1D Scalar Collapse Controls (for Ablation Comparison):} To isolate the gains of the 224-bit panel against legacy 1D scalar collapse on the identical trained backbones (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), we evaluate the coordinate saliency gate $g_d(\mathbf{v}) = (1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha\sqrt{d\sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}$ with softmax weights $w_i \propto \exp(|v_i|/(\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}}))$ ($\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2/\sqrt{d} + 10^{-6}$, $\alpha = \tau_g = 0.5$) \label{eq:saliency_gate}. Single-horizon TS-JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$, whereas 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA scores the multi-horizon envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))^2 + \max_m g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))^2)}$ modulated by $\mathcal{S}_{\text{Sol-Rey}}(t) = \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{sol}}\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t)))\bigr)$ ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$, or $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0$ for the unmodulated envelope), calibrated via scalar Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tail fitting in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing} ($u = p_{98}, q = 10^{-3}$).

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Two-Stage Nominal Calibration, and Statistical Design}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures operate on an identical streaming pipeline with context window $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ ($W = C + S = 320$), trained on nominal windows at stride $16$ and evaluated at stride $1$. Context and target windows are encoded into $D=32$ spherical representations by the multiscale frequency front-end and causal dilated TCN, fused with the three-depth functional hierarchy tap head (\ref{eq:multidepth_tap}), and centered by the online running context barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ prior to terminal LayerNorm. Models are trained on nominal sequences using AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size $16$) with zero per-dataset hyperparameter tuning. Budgets are fixed within each comparison but differ by cohort: $20$ epochs at constant learning rate (Cohort~1), $15$ epochs at constant learning rate (Cohort~2 panel ablations), and at most $12$ epochs with cosine annealing to $10^{-5}$, gradient clipping at $1.0$, and early stopping with patience $4$ (Cohort~5).

\paragraph{Two-Stage Nominal Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate autonomous alarm calibration without test-label leakage or train-to-calibration circularity, each stream's nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stage~A (Training fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$):} Channel standardization parameters, encoder/predictor weights, online context barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and the multi-horizon shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ are fit strictly on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$, ensuring zero raw-sample or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$. In Cohort~5, early stopping monitors the unsupervised predictive loss on a strided subsample of the nominal calibration block, so that block influences checkpoint selection (never with labels), while the held-out evaluation block and the test stream play no role in training.
    \item \emph{Stage~B (Circuit-breaker calibration and held-out verification on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2), yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window timestamps after the $(W-1)$-step context warm-up. Splitting at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $[0 : H)$ and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so the last calibration target and first evaluation target share zero target observations. On $[0 : H)$, the panel fits its calibration-stabilized whitening weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), per-dial medians $m_j$ and MAD scales $s_j$, two-factor hijack guards $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$, switch thresholds $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and Boolean quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or scalar $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), which are then frozen and evaluated on $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$ and on the test stream. A full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $[H : H+320)$ across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) confirms that shared passive context changes causal held-out FPR by less than $0.25$ percentage points.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Independent Statistical Units, Multi-Seed Replication, and Hypothesis Testing}
Models are replicated across multiple random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456\}$ for all 224-bit circuit-breaker, ablation, and 141-stream benchmarks; $\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$ for 1D primary baselines). Seeds are averaged within each stream first ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$) so that stream-level tests treat the telemetry stream ($n=19$, $n=33$, or $n=141$) as the independent statistical unit, while domain-level tests treat the dataset family ($N=7$ or $N=12$) as the independent cluster:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and false-discovery-rate control:} We evaluate paired stream differences via the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test without Gaussian assumptions, adjust $p$-values across families using the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure \cite{benjamini1995controlling} ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$), report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples), and cross-check at the dataset-cluster level via exact sign-flip permutation tests and zero-tuning LODO evaluation.
    \item \emph{Perspective-ladder and component-removal ablations:} On the identical trained multi-horizon backbones ($n=19$ streams, $57$ runs), we evaluate both the step-by-step perspective expansion ladder ($1 \to 32 \to 128 \to 160 \to 192 \to 224$ dials) and leave-one-out component ablations removing the 32 right-edge onset relays, prefix-horizon routing, per-dial hijack guards, or discrete Boolean thresholds (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b)); null comparisons are reported as paired effect sizes with $95\%$ bootstrap CIs, without equivalence claims.
    \item \emph{Tail kurtosis and regime-drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} On nominal windows we measure: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, quantifying residual tail heaviness; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, quantifying distributional shift between nominal calibration and held-out evaluation blocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Fairness Audits, Benchmark Cohorts, and Computational Footprint}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Two fairness audits verify that reconstructive baseline performance under the unified $W=320$ pipeline is not an artifact of window length or implementation: (i)~evaluating TranAD ($W=10$) and TimesNet ($W=96$) at their native published window sizes across five streams raises Point-F1 to $0.164$ and $0.218$, but observation residuals remain severely heavy-tailed ($\gamma_2 > 98$ vs.\ $2.30$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA) with high nominal FPR ($28.4\%\text{--}44.1\%$); and (ii)~reconstructors replicate their published point-adjusted F1 on SMD and MSL, while Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) provides a 130-stream, 11-dataset cross-check against NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer.

Experiments span five cohorts: \emph{Cohort~1} ($33$ primary streams: $19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)--(c)); \emph{Cohort~2} ($19$-stream perspective expansion ladder, circuit-breaker component ablation, $15$-arm matched-backbone comparison, and operating-curve suite, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)--(c) and Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)); \emph{Cohort~3} ($6$-stream single-channel quiet-fault injection and 224-bit hybrid variance-collapse monitor suite, Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)); \emph{Cohort~4} ($5$-stream horizon-latency sweep across $S \in \{1,4,16,32,64\}$ and 3-seed 224-bit dyadic front-end ablation, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) and Section~\ref{subsec:dyadic_ablation}); and \emph{Cohort~5} ($141$-stream, $3$-seed archive-wide benchmark across $12$ multivariate datasets comparing 1D JEPAs against the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), plus a separate single-seed screen of external baselines on $130$ streams in $11$ datasets (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)) that shares $91$ streams with Cohort~5 and is never paired with it; its other $39$ streams are the $14$ GHL streams, six CICIDS streams, and $19$ short streams that fail the $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$ rule. The panel's calibration constants in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire})--(\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) were chosen during development by inspecting test metrics on $23$ streams (the 19 non-GHL primary streams plus SMAP T-2, MSL P-10, Daphnet S09R01E2, and OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3), so Cohort~2 results are development-set results; the constants were then frozen, and the $118$ Cohort~5 streams never used in development serve as the held-out test of the panel. At $K=25$ channels, online parameter counts are matched ($272\text{k}$ for TS-JEPA, $312\text{k}$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, $304\text{k}\text{--}307\text{k}$ for TCN observation forecasters, $649\text{k}$ for TimesNet, and $206\text{k}$ for TranAD; the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel adds zero trainable parameters). Runs executed on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (RTX 5090, $0.60\text{--}0.88\,\text{ms}$/window, $188\,\text{MB}$ memory); code is at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Operational Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate detectors across three tiers: (i)~\emph{primary accuracy and false-alarm outcomes} comprising realized held-out nominal FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC (computed from the total active switch count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$ for the 224-bit panel); (ii)~\emph{operating characteristics} evaluating event recall across autonomous circuit-breaker operating points (\emph{224-Bit Strict} and \emph{224-Bit Breaker}), deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)), and post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)); and (iii)~\emph{secondary operational endpoints} reporting unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, event recall (ER, fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder and Circuit-Breaker Component Analysis}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
How do each of the seven fluid-dynamic perspectives and three calibration rules contribute to the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel on the identical trained backbone? Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate the step-by-step perspective expansion ladder and leave-one-out component ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (57 runs):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{From 1D scalar collapse to 32-bit and 128-bit sub-horizon switches (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)):} Collapsing the 128 whitened coordinates into a single 1D score (with or without $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation; $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0.12$ changes causal F1 by $-0.005$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.012, 0.000]$) allows one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the scalar threshold, restricting causal Point-F1 to $0.052$ and delay to $246.6$ steps. Replacing 1D collapse with 32 per-coordinate switches on the terminal horizon ($m=4$) nearly doubles causal Point-F1 to $0.101$, and expanding across all four nested sub-horizons (\emph{128-Bit Sub-Horizons}) lifts causal Point-F1 to $0.129$, buffered Point-F1 from $0.137$ to $0.192$, and buffered PR-AUC from $0.370$ to $0.402$. These two rungs are plain per-coordinate threshold-and-quorum detectors, so they are the simple multi-coordinate baselines against which the Reynolds statistics are measured.
    \item \emph{Adding Reynolds mean-flow drift, turbulent fluctuation, and convective right-edge acceleration:} Appending the 32 Reynolds mean-flow drift dials in \emph{160-Bit} cuts causal detection delay in half from $146.2$ to $69.1$ steps and lifts causal Point-F1 to $0.132$. Adding the 32 Reynolds turbulent-fluctuation dials in \emph{192-Bit} lifts buffered Point-F1 to $0.203$ and buffered ER to $56.7\%$ but slightly lowers causal Point-F1 ($0.132 \to 0.131$) and lengthens causal delay ($69.1 \to 179.0$ steps), so no single rung improves every metric. Finally, adding the 32 convective right-edge acceleration dials and 32 onset relays in \emph{224-Bit Breaker} reaches $0.138$ causal Point-F1 ($17$ wins / $0$ losses over 1D collapse, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), $0.228$ buffered Point-F1 ($p = 0.026$), $0.411$ buffered PR-AUC, $60.7\%$ causal and $63.1\%$ buffered ER, and $71.8$-step buffered delay.
    \item \emph{Leave-one-out component ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Removing the 32 fast right-edge onset relays slows causal detection delay from $105.9$ to $123.7$ steps and lowers causal Point-F1 from $0.138$ to $0.135$. Replacing physical prefix-horizon routing with uniform 64-step window smearing degrades buffered ER from $63.1\%$ to $58.4\%$, buffered Point-F1 from $0.228$ to $0.223$, and buffered delay from $71.8$ to $88.6$ steps. Removing per-dial hijack guards ($\gamma_j = 1$) lowers causal and buffered F1 to $0.137$ and $0.225$, whereas adding a soft attention pooling gate provides no gain over discrete Boolean switches ($0.137$ causal and $0.226$ buffered F1).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Quiet Faults and the 224-Bit Hybrid Variance-Collapse Monitor}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When one sensor out of $K$ freezes at a nominal level while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the frozen channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$, and LayerNorm rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ using the remaining active channels. Pre-weighting input channels by context variance lowers macro Point-F1 ($0.118 \to 0.077$), so input encoding stays uniform. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), 1D TS-JEPA detects only $19.4\%$ of quiet-fault events (flatlines, stuck sensors, and dropouts) with $<3\%$ point recall. Because the 224-bit panel thresholds all 32 latent coordinates separately, standalone \emph{Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Breaker} lifts quiet-fault event recall to $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ ($33.3\%$ on $+5\sigma$ spikes) and increases point recall more than seven-fold to $14.3\%\text{--}25.6\%$. Pairing the 224-bit panel in a logical union with a parameter-free observation-space rolling variance-collapse monitor (tracking how far each channel's 32-step trailing standard deviation drops below its nominal median) lifts event recall to $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ and point recall to $24.3\%\text{--}35.6\%$ across all six single-channel fault classes. The JEPA representation alone is therefore insufficient for isolated sensor freezes, and this evidence rests on six injected streams.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
To isolate the effect of the 4-shell rFFT front-end under the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, we train Soliton-Reynolds JEPA with and without dyadic frequency-shell splitting across five benchmark streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, and \texttt{GECCO water-quality}, three seeds). The dyadic front-end helps on high-channel spacecraft telemetry (buffered Point-F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$ on \texttt{MSL M-1}, $K=55$; causal $0.212 \to 0.321$ on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, $K=25$) but hurts on $K=9$ streams ($0.136$ vs.\ $0.217$ on \texttt{Daphnet}, $0.158$ vs.\ $0.321$ on \texttt{GECCO}), so the plain stem wins on 5-stream macro causal Point-F1 ($0.193$ vs.\ $0.156$). Shell splitting pays off only on high-channel telemetry.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 15 model arms across the 19 non-GHL streams under identical splits, training budgets, and matched parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster vs.\ TCN Obs.\ Autoencoder):} Pairing the same causal dilated TCN encoder with a multilayer perceptron decoder to either forecast the 64-step target window or reconstruct the 320-step window in raw sensor space leaves tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and causal FPR ($\approx 25\%$) nearly identical.
    \item \emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster (LN)):} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck and target-window LayerNorm to the observation forecaster compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but targeting raw sensor waveforms leaves calibration drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and drops causal Point-F1 to $0.084$.
    \item \emph{Learned latent target projection, multi-horizon whitening, and 224-bit circuit breakers (TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit):} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ latent space cuts calibration drift nearly in half ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), while the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel lifts buffered PR-AUC to $0.411$, buffered Point-F1 to $0.228$, and causal event recall to $0.607$ at $1.7\%$ held-out FPR.
    \item \emph{Nonlinear TCN encoding vs.\ linear PCA and tree isolation (PCA Linear Forecaster (LN) and Isolation Forest):} Replacing the nonlinear TCN with 32 principal components and ridge regression bounds causal FPR to $2.1\%$ through LayerNorm, but causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ (and Isolation Forest reaches only $0.149$ causal event recall), confirming the need for learned nonlinear temporal features.
    \item \emph{Future prediction vs.\ context autoencoding, EMA stability, and $\ell_2$ scoring (JEPA Context Autoencoder, JEPA Frozen Target, and TS-JEPA ($\ell_2$ Scoring)):} Context autoencoding in latent space keeps causal FPR low ($1.7\%$) but drops causal Point-F1 from $0.161$ to $0.112$ and PR-AUC from $0.278$ to $0.235$. A frozen, never-updated target encoder or Euclidean $\ell_2$ scoring still holds causal FPR below $2\%$ ($0.9\%$ and $1.8\%$), confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent representation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Primary 33-Stream and Archive-Wide 141-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} evaluates all 33 primary continuous telemetry streams under the two-stage chronological held-out protocol: Panel~(a) reports retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and held-out nominal FPRs. Archive-wide 141-stream results appear in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}; on a 130-stream baseline screen, TS-JEPA achieves $0.186$ stream and $0.160$ macro Point-F1 versus $0.152$ for TranAD, $0.151$ for TimesNet, $0.097$ for DCdetector, $0.088$ for NCAD, and $0.082$ for Anomaly Transformer. Across both attribution regimes, pairing multi-horizon Soliton-Reynolds JEPA with the 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 ($0.228$), buffered PR-AUC ($0.411$), causal PR-AUC ($0.312$), causal event recall ($0.607$), and Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.122$), while keeping held-out false alarms an order of magnitude below observation-space reconstructors ($1.1\%\text{--}1.7\%$ causal and $9.8\%\text{--}13.2\%$ buffered vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD).

\noindent\emph{Reducing Observation-Space False-Alarm Flooding:} On the 19 non-GHL primary streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), predicting spherical latent representations cuts held-out nominal FPR three-fold under buffered attribution to $7.8\%\text{--}9.8\%$ versus $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD ($p \le 0.045$), and by an order of magnitude under strictly causal streaming to $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($p = 0.010$). This separation holds across all $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$) and under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, where JEPAs maintain $0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors. In Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit achieves $0.7\%$ causal and $4.7\%$ buffered FPR on Daphnet with $0.223$ buffered F1 versus $0.145$ for TS-JEPA, leads all methods on MSL at $0.199$ buffered F1 versus $0.161$ for TS-JEPA and $0.158$ for TimesNet, reaches $0.354$ buffered and $0.351$ causal F1 on SMAP at a $38.2$-step delay, and reaches $0.063$ buffered F1 on Genesis where reconstructors score $0.000$.

\noindent\emph{Breaking the 1D Scalar Score-Collapse Bottleneck:} Under legacy 1D scalar scoring, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA trades lower Point-F1 ($0.050$ causal, $0.148$ buffered) for ultra-conservative firing because one or two out-of-sample drifting coordinates (\emph{hijacker dials}) inflate the scalar threshold. Replacing 1D scalar collapse with the 224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel resolves this trade-off on the exact same trained networks (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b), and~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). On the 19 primary streams, \emph{Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Strict} and \emph{224-Bit Breaker} lift buffered PR-AUC from $0.331\text{--}0.370$ to $0.411$, raise buffered Point-F1 to $0.190\text{--}0.228$ ($p = 0.026$), more than double causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.116\text{--}0.138$ ($17$ wins / $0$ losses, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), lift causal event recall to $53.4\%\text{--}60.7\%$ and buffered recall to $63.1\%$, and cut detection delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps causally and from $402.7$ to $71.8$ steps buffered. On GHL ($\pi=0.013$, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently at $0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR with $0.000$ F1, confirming that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\emph{Operational Applicability Regimes:} Performance across fault types and sampling rates defines four operational regimes: (i)~\emph{multi-channel spacecraft, server, and wearable arrays} with $K \ge 9$ across SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, SMD, Exathlon, and OPPORTUNITY, where the 224-bit panel maximizes Point-F1 and event recall at low false-alarm rates; (ii)~\emph{latency-constrained infrastructure} where a 63-step window lag is prohibitive, such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$, requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}); (iii)~\emph{quiet single-channel sensor freezes} absorbed by LayerNorm, where pairing the 224-bit panel with the rolling variance-collapse monitor recovers $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ of events (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}); and (iv)~\emph{continuous non-stationary process ramps} on GHL and Metro ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat static calibration rules.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel is essential for real-time streaming. Without 64-step window averaging, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals exhibit a heavier right tail ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$) than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$). Under legacy 1D scalar scoring, hijacker dials push the scalar threshold onto that tail so that 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA alerts on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.4\%$ of test steps ($4.0\%$ true-positive rate (TPR) at $0.6\%$ FPR), flagging only isolated peak steps inside each anomaly ($0.050\text{--}0.052$ causal F1). By monitoring each fluid-dynamic dial with its own hijack-guarded switch $b_j(t)$ and firing immediately when a terminal sub-block and right-edge convective acceleration relay $r_c(t)$ coincide in (\ref{eq:onset_relay}), \emph{224-Bit Breaker} stays active across the true fault window, reaching $0.138$ causal F1, $60.7\%$ event recall, and $105.9$-step delay at $1.67\%$ FPR. A rate-matched i.i.d.\ random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, would catch $28.1\%$ of events at the panel's nominal rate versus $60.7\%$ actual, whereas at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR it reaches $60.0\%$, above TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Structural Emission Lag, and Fast-Tripwire Regimes}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a structural emission lag of $S-1 = 63$ steps before a causal trailing window decision is emitted at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams at three seeds. At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), latent prediction keeps causal FPR at $0.4\%\text{--}0.6\%$ versus $2.5\%\text{--}3.1\%$ for TimesNet, with higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$) and short delays ($\Delta t = 57.8\text{--}85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases from $16$ to $64$, TimesNet causal FPR climbs from $6.1\%$ to $21.3\%$, whereas latent predictors remain bounded at $\le 2.0\%$. Thus, $S=64$ suits high-rate telemetry ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or subsystem trend monitoring on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP and MSL ($10.5\,\text{min}$), while low-rate streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$) and zero-lag tripwires require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ for latent predictors, we evaluate three operating-point comparisons:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Autonomous circuit-breaker points and nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Without test labels, Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Strict ($53.4\%$ ER at $1.1\%$ FPR) and 224-Bit Breaker ($60.7\%$ ER at $1.7\%$ FPR) dominate the low-FPR frontier; at calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$, Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit reaches $77.4\%$ causal ER versus $61.8\%$ for TimesNet and $60.2\%$ for TranAD.
    \item \emph{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models (vertical dotted guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) proves that low false-alarm rates are not an artifact of low alert frequency. At a $1\%$ test FPR budget ($f^* = 0.01$), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit detects $58.4\%$ of anomaly events ($56.2\%\text{--}57.8\%$ for 1D Soliton-Reynolds and $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA), nearly tripling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and quadrupling TranAD ($14.9\%$), with a similar gap at $f^* = 0.05$ ($72.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$).
    \item \emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range point anomalies ($\pm 5\sigma$ spikes) are flagged even by a marginal $z$-score (\texttt{ZScoreMax}), whereas contextual anomalies stay within normal bounds ($\max|z| \le 5\sigma$) and violate multi-step trajectory structure. In Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a), stratifying the 10 primary SMAP/MSL streams by NASA's taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} shows that observation-space detectors collapse on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC and $30\%\text{--}56\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$). Latent JEPAs lead on these five contextual sequences; the 224-bit panel matches 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA ($0.658$ vs.\ $0.652$ buffered PR-AUC, $75.0\%$ autonomous recall, and identical $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) at a higher autonomous FPR ($1.8\%$ vs.\ $0.6\%$), so this evidence is preliminary. Across all 19 non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit doubles reconstructor recall on contextual faults ($61.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) and leads on point faults ($79.2\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied whether Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures and multi-perspective circuit-breaker panels reduce observation-space false-alarm flooding and 1D scalar score collapse across 33 primary and 141 archive-wide telemetry streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{A breaker panel in place of a scalar threshold:} Expanding $D=32$ whitened coordinates into $224$ per-coordinate horizon statistics with nominal drift guards, prefix-routed attribution, and 32 onset relays keeps one or two drifting coordinates from setting a shared threshold. On $n{=}19$ primary streams, causal Point-F1 rises from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$) and causal delay falls from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps; across all 141 streams ($423$ runs), causal Point-F1 rises from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.139$ ($114/18$ wins, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$) at $72.7\%$ recall and $55.3$-step delay.
    \item \emph{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Component Verification:} Matched-encoder ablations isolate LayerNorm kurtosis compression ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent projections ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA ($\operatorname{RankMe}: 11.6 \to 19.8$, $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$, inter-block drift share $71.3\% \to 18.7\%$). Leave-one-out ablations support the right-edge relays ($-17.8$-step causal delay) and prefix routing ($+4.7\%$ buffered recall); hijack guards add little on these streams ($0.137 \to 0.138$ causal F1), scalar spectral-concentration modulation shows no measurable benefit, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) remove the 63-step emission lag.
    \item \emph{Limits:} At a $1\%$ test-FPR budget the panel catches $58.4\%$ of events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, but gains concentrate on multichannel streams. It misses the slow process ramps of GHL and Metro ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ Point-F1), catches only $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ of single-channel freezes alone ($44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ with a variance monitor, on six injected streams), and its contextual-anomaly evidence rests on five NASA sequences. The breakers are dependent, and their joint trip structure, calibration-constant sensitivity, and online conformal adaptation for non-stationary ramps remain to be characterized.
\end{enumerate}
