\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space. Because sensor telemetry contains unforecastable jitter and scale disparities, observation-space residuals develop heavy tails that trigger severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid reconstructing raw noise by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows. We investigate how latent prediction conditions residual distributions under regime shift. Specifically, we compare a single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) against Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, a multi-horizon architecture that taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context barycenters, whitens multi-horizon prediction errors, and scores centered sub-horizon fluctuations via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio. Across continuous physical telemetry benchmarks calibrated on held-out nominal data with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT), latent scoring suppresses false alarms under both retrospective buffered attribution and strictly causal streaming while improving anomaly ranking and event recall. In addition, replacing scalar score aggregation with a 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel that monitors sub-horizon positions, Reynolds slow-drift transport, turbulent fluctuations, and right-edge convective acceleration with fast onset relays prevents isolated drifting coordinates from inflating the alarm threshold, nearly doubling causal point-wise F1 and cutting detection delay in half across 141 multivariate streams. Controlled factorial ablations also show that layer-normalization bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, learned latent projections and barycenter whitening curb calibration-to-holdout drift, short prediction horizons eliminate emission lag, and hybrid sensor-variance monitors recover quiet single-channel faults.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\emph{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} In deployed monitoring, an automated rule must convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using healthy (nominal) calibration data alone. Standard deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) reconstruct raw sensor readings and flag timestamps with large point-by-point reconstruction error \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains sensor jitter and scale differences across channels, point-level errors produce frequent, clustered spikes and a heavy-tailed residual distribution with high excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$, Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Under Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at a $0.1\%$ target risk ($q = 10^{-3}$), normal background shifts easily cross the threshold, triggering false alarms on $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of healthy timesteps.

\noindent\emph{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} Predicting a compact \emph{summary} of a future time window rather than every noisy sensor sample filters out random jitter before the prediction error is computed. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which compress the preceding 256 timesteps into a 32-dimensional summary vector and forecast the summary of the next $S$ timesteps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for fast tripwires) using healthy training data alone \cite{carmona2021neural}. Alongside a single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA), we introduce \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, which taps multiple depths of its temporal convolutional network \cite{bai2018empirical}, subtracts the running context barycenter to cancel slow level shifts, and whitens prediction errors across $M=4$ nested sub-horizons. Drawing on Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} and Lieb--Thirring spectral bounds \cite{lieb1976inequalities}, it separates multi-horizon errors into mean transport and zero-mean fluctuations, measuring whether fluctuation energy is dispersed like background noise or concentrated into a coherent rank-1 wavepacket. We also resolve a critical failure mode of scalar score aggregation: collapsing 128 whitened residuals into a single 1D score before calibration allows one or two mildly drifting coordinates (\emph{hijacker dials}) to inflate the SPOT threshold and suppress causal Point-F1. Replacing 1D collapse with a \emph{224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel} (monitoring seven fluid-dynamic dials per coordinate with independent Boolean tripwires and 32 fast right-edge onset relays) eliminates hijacker inflation without test-set tuning. We evaluate four questions: (i)~how much latent residuals and Boolean circuit breakers lower realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent targets, multi-horizon whitening, and wavepacket concentration each contribute; (iii)~how detection varies across point spikes, contextual faults, and quiet single-sensor freezes; and (iv)~how shortening $S$ trades off alarm lag ($S-1$) against detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Why latent predictors stabilize alarm thresholds:} Across 12 matched-backbone models ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ online parameters), a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bottleneck compresses residual kurtosis from $6.10$ to $0.91$, while predicting a learned latent summary cuts calibration-to-holdout drift in half ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.376$ to $0.214$ in TS-JEPA) and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$ (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \emph{Fewer false alarms, contextual-anomaly dominance, and quiet-fault recovery:} Across the 19 non-GHL streams, latent scoring cuts held-out FPRs three-fold under buffered attribution ($7.8\%\text{--}9.3\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD) and by over an order of magnitude causally ($0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). Soliton-Reynolds JEPA matches TS-JEPA on buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC $0.331$), catches $56.2\%$ of anomaly events at a $1\%$ false-alarm budget, and leads on NASA contextual anomalies ($0.652$ buffered PR-AUC, $75.0\%$ causal SPOT recall at $0.6\%$ FPR, and $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ recall at $1\%\text{--}5\%$ FPR budgets), while a rolling sensor-variance check recovers quiet single-channel freezes (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), \ref{tab:core_benchmark}, and~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)).
    \item \emph{Multi-horizon wavepacket bounds, 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, and fast response:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that the Reynolds-centered Lieb--Thirring concentration ratio satisfies $\frac{1}{M-1} \le R_{\text{LT}} \le 1$ with exact radial scale invariance. While post-hoc wavepacket multipliers satisfy Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) \cite{schuirmann1987comparison} equivalence, replacing 1D scalar collapse with the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel more than doubles causal Point-F1 ($0.052 \to 0.116\text{--}0.138$ on $n{=}19$ streams, $17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$; $0.067\text{--}0.077 \to 0.125\text{--}0.139$ across $n{=}141$ streams, $114/18$ wins, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$) and halves detection delay. Short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step window lag (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations} and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
    \item \emph{Why automated thresholds fail and when they hold:} Permutation decomposition with bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) shows that slow regime drift causes $71.3\%$ of excess causal false alarms in TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors. Barycenter centering in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA cuts this inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$, while runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles stabilize thresholds under step and variance shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timestamps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that destabilize extreme-value threshold fitting under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: temporal and contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time} and TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximize contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent prediction across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ produced by a separate target encoder. Collapse is prevented via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}. How latent predictive residuals behave under unsupervised extreme-value threshold calibration remains unexplored.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamic Reynolds Decomposition and Spectral Concentration Priors:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman and operator-theoretic models \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} lift nonlinear dynamics into learned observable spaces. Rather than imposing partial differential equations on raw sensors, we draw on Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} from turbulence modeling to separate multi-horizon latent predictions into a mean trajectory and centered sub-horizon fluctuations. We then measure wavepacket localization across prediction horizons via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio \cite{lieb1976inequalities} on the centered fluctuation Gram matrix.

\noindent\emph{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration and Evaluation Rigor:} Grounded in the Pickands--Balkema--de Haan extreme-value theorem \cite{balkema1974residual}, Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to score exceedances above quantile $u$ to extrapolate a threshold at design risk $q$. However, continuous telemetry violates the classical independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption through short-range serial correlation (quantified by the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}) and inter-block regime drift. Because ``point-adjusted'' F1 severely inflates scores \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}, we evaluate all models using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm operating curves.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. Thirty-three streams enter the primary multi-seed held-out protocol; 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. The suite spans four physical domains and separates natural operational or physiological anomalies from testbed or simulated faults. \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry} covers six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$ channels), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$, four primary and two short-nominal), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$), all containing natural operational or solar-flare events; on SMAP and MSL, NASA's ground-truth archive \cite{Hundman2018b} further classifies each anomaly sequence as either an out-of-range \emph{point} fault or an in-range \emph{contextual} fault (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)). \emph{Wearable kinematics} covers six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} sampled at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ natural gait-freezing episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals a $1\,\text{s}$ horizon). \emph{Industrial processes and smart infrastructure} covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ testbed valve and heater faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution ($K{=}9$, $37$ natural hydrological anomalies), Metro I-94 hourly highway traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$, simulated attack flows). Cohort~3 (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}) separately injects six controlled single-channel faults into six real streams.

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$ across the 33 primary streams) and labeled anomaly test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatlines, matching standardized archive subsets in prior benchmarks \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly}.

Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by a 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Primary 33-stream inclusion required $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ ($T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$) and $K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)); among the 12 reserved streams, five have $T_{\text{nom}} \in [764, 1{,}492]$ ($N_{\text{cal}} \le 101$ across CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), six CICIDS streams have $K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \ge 1.36\times 10^5$, and GECCO ($K{=}9$, $1$ held-out event) enters Cohorts 3--4. To eliminate subset-selection bias, Cohort~5 evaluates all 141 qualifying multivariate streams across 12 datasets ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$ and $\ge 1$ test anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, 13 Opportunity, GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), credit-card, Metro, and SWAN) at three seeds alongside the 130-stream baseline screen (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware platforms ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations (SDs), zero-tuning leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)).

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to calibrated binary alarms. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the series at index $t$ into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$) and a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). Each window passes through a 4-shell dyadic real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) front-end and a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit leptokurtic sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:related}), Stage~2 shifts prediction into a 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders extract three multi-depth taps across the shallow, mid, and deep TCN hierarchy and map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into spherical representations $z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}} \in \sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. Target weights $\phi$ are updated as an exponential moving average (EMA) of online weights $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with cosine momentum $m_k \in [0.995, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Stage~3 rolls out latent predictions across $M=4$ nested sub-horizons ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$) under a two-term multi-horizon mean-squared error (MSE) and covariance whitening defect objective (Section~\ref{subsec:triad_formulations}), and Stage~4 applies Mahalanobis residual whitening, Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration modulation, and either coordinate-saliency-gated SPOT calibration or the 224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel under causal trailing ($\tau = t + 63$) and buffered smear mappings (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}).

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures: TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We evaluate two nested latent predictors sharing the same multiscale encoder backbone and coordinate saliency gate: an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) and a multi-horizon fluid-dynamic decomposition model (Soliton-Reynolds JEPA).

\subsubsection{Unconstrained TS-JEPA Baseline}
In TS-JEPA, a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-horizon prediction $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with standard single-horizon predictive alignment and VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg} ($\gamma_{\text{var}} = 1.0$). Terminal LayerNorm \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bounds latent representations on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ and compresses nominal residual kurtosis relative to observation-space reconstructors, but single-horizon encoding pools the entire $S=64$ target window into one terminal TCN state, smoothing out short sub-horizon transients.

\subsubsection{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA (Multi-Horizon Reynolds Decomposition and Lieb--Thirring Concentration)}
\noindent\emph{Physical Motivation and Structural Design:} In fluid turbulence, Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} separates a velocity field into its time-averaged mean flow and zero-mean fluctuations, while dispersive wave theory characterizes localized wavepackets (\emph{solitons}) by spectral concentration of the fluctuation operator \cite{lieb1976inequalities}. Soliton-Reynolds JEPA incorporates both principles through two stages before residual scoring:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth TCN hierarchy taps and synchronized barycenter centering:} To capture sharp onset transients attenuated by deep dilated blocks, a multi-depth tap head extracts $L_{\text{tap}}=3$ feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) across the shallow dyadic front-end, mid-depth block, and terminal TCN block. For each tap, the centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy are concatenated into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6 d_{\text{model}}}$, mapped into $\mathbb{R}^D$, and fused with the deep bottleneck via gate $g = 0.5\,\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$. Subtracting the online running context barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ from both online context and EMA target fused embeddings prior to LayerNorm projection onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ cancels common-mode baseline drift.
    \item \emph{Nested causal multi-horizon rollout and covariance whitening defect regularization:} We partition $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefixes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ ($S_m = (m/M)S \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$), encoded by $E_\phi$ into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. From $z_{\text{ctx}}$, sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m < M$ ($\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$) using horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while $P_\theta$ forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ ($m < M$) and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, training minimizes multi-horizon predictive MSE plus the Frobenius covariance whitening defect:
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Sol-Rey}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ is the whitening defect of centered batch predictions $\mathbf{Z}_c = \mathbf{Z} - \mathbf{1}_B \bar{\mathbf{z}}^\top$ with covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{Z}) = (B-1)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_c^\top \mathbf{Z}_c$ and standard deviations $\sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}) = \sqrt{\Sigma_{dd}(\mathbf{Z}) + \epsilon}$ ($\gamma = 0.75$, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$), defined by $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dd} = \sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}))}$ and $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dj} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Sigma_{dj}(\mathbf{Z})$ ($d \neq j$). Unlike log-determinant barriers that fail when $B = 16 < D = 32$, $\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\|_F^2$ remains smooth for any $B \ge 2$ and raises effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}.

\noindent\emph{Reynolds Fluctuation Gram Matrix and Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration:}
Let $\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ stack the $M$ prediction residuals $\mathbf{e}_m = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}$. Fitting the shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ \cite{ledoit2004well} and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows yields Mahalanobis-whitened residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{e}} = \mathbf{\Omega}(\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{E}) - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$, reshaped into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = [\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_1, \dots, \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Reynolds decomposition across horizons separates $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ into horizon-mean transport $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuations $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. From the fluctuation Gram matrix $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ with ordered eigenvalues $0 \le \lambda_1 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$, the Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio \cite{lieb1976inequalities} of order $\gamma = 1$ is:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2} + \epsilon_{\text{LT}}}, \quad \epsilon_{\text{LT}} = 10^{-12}.
    \label{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration Bounds, Centered Gram Rank Deficiency, and Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathbf{E}' = (\mathbf{I}_M - M^{-1}\mathbf{1}_M\mathbf{1}_M^\top)\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ denote the Reynolds-centered multi-horizon fluctuation matrix for $M \ge 2$ and $\gamma > -1/2$. In the exact limit $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$ for any non-zero fluctuation $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~\emph{Centered Gram nullspace:} Because $\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{1}_M \in \ker(\mathbf{G})$, forcing $\lambda_1(\mathbf{G}) \equiv 0$ and $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{G}) \le \min(M-1, D)$.
(ii)~\emph{Sharp isotropy and soliton bounds:} The concentration ratio satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{M - 1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1,
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
attaining $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) iff fluctuation energy is distributed isotropically across $M-1$ modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$), and $1$ iff $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$ ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0 < \lambda_M$).
(iii)~\emph{Exact radial scale invariance:} For any $c > 0$, $R_{\text{LT}}(c\mathbf{E}') = R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{E}'(\mathbf{E}'^\top\mathbf{1}_M) = \mathbf{0}$, $\lambda_1 = 0$, leaving at most $M-1$ positive eigenvalues $\lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ with $\lambda_M > 0$. Monotonicity of $x \mapsto x^{\gamma+1/2}$ on $x \ge 0$ yields $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$; dividing by the sum gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) with the stated equality conditions. Rescaling $\mathbf{E}' \mapsto c\mathbf{E}'$ scales $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \mapsto c^{2\gamma+1}\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$, which cancels in (\ref{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}) as $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, windows are scored at stride $1$. Let $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denote the coordinate saliency gate in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). TS-JEPA scores $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ via $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$. In 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, we combine the joint $MD$-dimensional whitened gate $g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))$ with the peak sub-horizon gate $\max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))$ into $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, modulated by a one-sided median absolute deviation (MAD)-standardized soliton concentration gain:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(g_{MD}\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t)\bigr)^2 + \max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t)\bigr)^2\Bigr)}, \label{eq:soliton_env} \\
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{Sol-Rey}}(t) &= \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\Bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{sol}}\max\bigl(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t))\bigr)\Bigr), \label{eq:soliton_score}
\end{align}
where $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$ and $z_{\text{LT}}(t) = (R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}'(t)) - m_{\text{LT}}) / (1.4826\,\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}})$ standardizes $R_{\text{LT}}$ against its training median $m_{\text{LT}}$ and MAD $\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}}$ ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0$ recovers $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$).

\noindent\emph{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
Online evaluation enforces three criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
(a)~\emph{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision step $\tau = t + S - 1$, encoders observe only $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$.
(b)~\emph{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ summarizes $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$, score $s_t$ is emitted at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3), creating a structural lag of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL), distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$; shortening to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates emission lag (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
(c)~\emph{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} \emph{Causal trailing attribution} assigns $s_t$ only to $\tau = t + S - 1$, whereas \emph{buffered smear attribution} averages $s_t$ over $[t, t+S-1]$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and Scale Equivariance}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
To amplify sparse departures while mitigating channel drowning, we define $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    g_d(\mathbf{v}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{d \sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}, \\
    w_i &= \frac{\exp\bigl(|v_i| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^d \exp\bigl(|v_j| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}, \quad \sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau_g = 0.5$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$, satisfying approximate degree-1 homogeneity $g_d(c\mathbf{v}) \approx c\,g_d(\mathbf{v})$ for $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 \gg \epsilon$. Input channels use uniform weights (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
For $K > 1$, a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Channels are split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point rFFT grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router, acting as a multiscale front-end rather than an orthogonal wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert scalar scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ from nominal calibration data alone, SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits exceedances $Y = \mathcal{S}(t) - u > 0$ above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) \cite{balkema1974residual} with shape $\xi$ and scale $\sigma$ via Grimshaw maximum-likelihood estimation \cite{grimshaw1993computing} with bounded L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10]$). Inverting the GPD tail at target risk $q = 10^{-3}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = \begin{cases}
    u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right], & |\xi| \ge 10^{-5}, \\
    u - \sigma \ln\left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right), & |\xi| < 10^{-5},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:spot_threshold}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $N_u$ count post-attribution calibration timestamps.

\subsubsection{224-Bit Multi-Perspective Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
When $MD = 128$ whitened sub-horizon residuals $\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ ($m \in \{1,\dots,4\}, c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$) are collapsed into a single 1D score $\mathcal{S}(t)$ before calibration, one or two coordinates experiencing mild out-of-sample variance shift between training and calibration (\emph{hijacker dials}) dominate $u = p_{98}$ and push $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ to the extreme tail tip, firing only on isolated peak steps inside an anomaly. To eliminate scalar score collapse and GPD tail extrapolation without test labels, the \emph{224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel} expands each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,D\}$ into seven fluid-dynamic perspectives ($7D = 224$ dials): (i)~four sub-horizon flow-position magnitudes $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ ($128$ dials); (ii)~Reynolds horizon-mean transport $|\bar{e}_c(t)| = |M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ ($32$ dials); (iii)~Reynolds turbulent-fluctuation amplitude $\sigma'_c(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t))^2}$ ($32$ dials); and (iv)~convective right-edge acceleration $|\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$ across $[48, 64]$ vs.\ $[0, 16]$ ($32$ dials). Nominal-only calibration applies two rules:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration-stabilized whitening and hijack guards:} Any coordinate whose calibration RMS exceeds $1.10$ is blended toward its calibration-normalized unwhitened residual via weight $\lambda_{m,c} = \exp\bigl(-1.5\max(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}(m,c) - 1.10)\bigr)$. Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized by its calibration median and MAD into $z_j(t)$ with binary tripwire $b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}(z_j(t) > \tau_j)$, where $\tau_j = q_{1-\alpha}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{tr}\to\text{cal}}$ ($\alpha = 0.002$) scales the calibration quantile by intra-calibration drift $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}$ and train-to-calibration tail growth $\gamma_j^{\text{tr}\to\text{cal}}$.
    \item \emph{Boolean quorum and fast right-edge onset relays:} Let $N_{\text{trip}}(t) = \sum_{j=1}^{224} b_j(t)$ count tripped switches. \emph{224-Bit Strict} alerts when $N_{\text{trip}}(t) \ge K_{\text{strict}}$ (99.3rd calibration percentile). \emph{224-Bit Breaker} alerts when $N_{\text{trip}}(t) \ge K_{\text{bal}}$ (98.5th percentile) \emph{or} any of $32$ fast right-edge onset relays trips: $b_{M,c}(t) \wedge \bigl(b_{\text{accel},c}(t) \vee b_{\text{fluc},c}(t) \vee b_{M-1,c}(t)\bigr)$. Under buffered attribution, each dial votes only over the prefix steps $[t, t+S_m-1]$ (or $[t+S_{M-1}, t+S_M-1]$ for right-edge acceleration) that it physically observes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures use an identical streaming pipeline with context window $C=256$ and target horizon $S=64$ ($W = C + S = 320$), trained at stride $16$ and evaluated at stride $1$. Context and target representations ($D=32$) are extracted by the multiscale frequency front-end followed by a 6-block causal dilated TCN (64 filters, kernel size $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$). Multi-statistic temporal pooling (terminal, mean, max, standard deviation, Log-Ricci energy, and first-order total variation) projects features through a linear bottleneck before LayerNorm and dropout ($p=0.20$). Models are trained for $20$ epochs on nominal sequences using AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3} \to 10^{-5}$ via cosine annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size $32$), with identical training budgets and zero per-dataset hyperparameter tuning across families.

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$. Standardization parameters and neural weights are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$.

Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2). Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations provide context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}})$. Splitting $v$ at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H)$ (used to fit the SPOT threshold at $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, excluding $S=64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S)$. Because suspect target windows have length $S=64$, the last calibration target and first evaluation target are $100\%$ disjoint. The first $C=256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. A full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320)$ across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) confirms that shared passive context does not bias calibration: stream-macro causal FPR changes by less than $0.25$ percentage points for both JEPAs ($0.33\%$ to $0.36\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $3.02\%$ to $3.24\%$ for TS-JEPA), remaining an order of magnitude below TimesNet and TranAD ($9.7\%\text{--}14.0\%$). All primary tables use fixed standard SPOT thresholds during testing.

\paragraph{Independent Statistical Units, Multi-Seed Replication, and Equivalence Bounds}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are evaluated across five random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ observations each) and ablation controls use three seeds ($\{7, 42, 2024\}$). To prevent pseudoreplication, seeds are averaged within each stream first ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$), stream-level paired tests treat the telemetry stream ($n=33$ primary or $n=19$ non-GHL) as the unit of analysis, and domain-level tests treat the dataset family ($N=7$) as the independent cluster:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and false-discovery-rate adjustment:} The Wilcoxon signed-rank test evaluates whether the median paired difference across streams is zero without Gaussian assumptions. We adjust $p$-values across multiple comparisons via the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure \cite{benjamini1995controlling} ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$), report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples), and cross-check at the dataset-cluster level ($N=7$) via exact sign-flip permutation tests and zero-tuning LODO sensitivity.
    \item \emph{Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for fixed-backbone regularizer equivalence:} We use TOST \cite{schuirmann1987comparison} to test whether the multiplicative soliton and manifold gain in (\ref{eq:soliton_score}) alters aggregate Point-F1 relative to the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ on the identical trained backbone ($n=19$ streams). TOST tests $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$), establishing equivalence when $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}) < 0.05$ across $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.015, \pm 0.020, \pm 0.030\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \emph{Distributional tail and drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} On nominal windows we measure: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, distinguishing heavy-tailed \emph{leptokurtic} ($\gamma_2 > 0$) from light-tailed \emph{platykurtic} ($\gamma_2 < 0$) residuals; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, quantifying distributional shift between calibration ($F_{\text{cal}}$) and held-out nominal ($F_{\text{hold}}$) blocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Fairness Checks, Cohort Structure, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Two fairness audits verify that reconstructive baseline results under the unified $20$-epoch, $W=320$ pipeline are not artifacts of window length or implementation: (i)~evaluating TranAD ($W=10$) and TimesNet ($W=96$) at their native published window sizes across five streams raises Point-F1 to $0.164$ and $0.218$, but observation residuals remain severely heavy-tailed ($\gamma_2 > 98$ vs.\ $3.1$ for TS-JEPA) with high nominal FPR ($28.4\%\text{--}44.1\%$); and (ii)~reconstructors replicate their published point-adjusted F1 on SMD and MSL, while Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) provides a secondary 130-stream, 11-dataset single-seed cross-check against NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer.

Experiments span five cohorts: \emph{Cohort~1} ($33$ primary streams: $19$ non-GHL at $5$ seeds and $14$ GHL at $3$ seeds, Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)--(c) and~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)); \emph{Cohort~2} ($19$-stream, $12$-arm matched-backbone factorial and operating-curve suite at $5$ seeds, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)); \emph{Cohort~3} ($6$-stream single-channel quiet-fault and hybrid-monitor suite at $3$ seeds, Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}); \emph{Cohort~4} ($5$-stream horizon-latency sweep across $S \in \{1,4,16,32,64\}$ and dyadic front-end ablation at $3$ seeds, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)); and \emph{Cohort~5} ($141$-stream, $3$-seed SPOT benchmark across $12$ multivariate datasets with validation early stopping plus the $130$-stream single-seed baseline screen, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). At $K=25$ channels, online parameter counts are matched: $272\text{k}$ for TS-JEPA ($540\text{k}$ with EMA target), $312\text{k}$ ($619\text{k}$ total) for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, $304\text{k}\text{--}307\text{k}$ for TCN observation forecasters, $649\text{k}$ for TimesNet, and $206\text{k}$ for TranAD. Non-finite entries are zero-imputed ($\bm{\sigma} \ge 10^{-2}$), and causal and buffered scores receive separately fitted thresholds $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ on $v_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}). Runs executed on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (RTX 5090, $0.60\text{--}0.88\,\text{ms}$/window, $188\,\text{MB}$ memory); code is at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate models across three tiers. First, \emph{primary calibration and accuracy outcomes} comprise realized held-out nominal FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ relative to design risk $q = 10^{-3}$, unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC. Second, \emph{operating characteristics} evaluate event recall across deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) and across post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). Third, \emph{secondary operational endpoints} report unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Multi-Horizon Whitened Envelope vs.\ Soliton Concentration Modulation}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
To separate the multi-horizon representation from post-hoc scoring multipliers on the identical trained network, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) compare single-horizon TS-JEPA against the unmodulated multi-horizon envelope control ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$, outputting $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ directly) and a sweep across $\alpha_{\text{sol}} \in [0.03, 0.24]$ on all 19 non-GHL streams. Replacing single-horizon TS-JEPA with the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ drives the core improvements in false-alarm control and low-FPR sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c)): causal held-out FPR drops four-fold from $2.4\%$ to $0.6\%$, calibration drift decreases, and causal event recall at a $1\%$ false-alarm budget rises from $0.395$ to $0.578$ without loss of buffered PR-AUC. Across $\alpha_{\text{sol}} \in [0, 0.24]$, adding the post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration gain yields a modest buffered FPR reduction ($8.3\%$ to $7.8\%$ at $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.026$) and nudges causal event recall from $0.439$ to $0.478$, while buffered Point-F1 and PR-AUC satisfy TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)). Auxiliary spectral regularizers during training similarly leave downstream recall and FPR flat; the primary gains come from multi-depth TCN tapping, barycenter centering, and multi-horizon Mahalanobis whitening.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Quiet Faults, Input Gating, and Hybrid Monitoring}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When one sensor out of $K$ freezes at a nominal value while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the frozen channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$, and LayerNorm rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ using the remaining active channels. Pre-weighting input channels by context variance lowers macro Point-F1 across four multivariate streams from $0.118$ to $0.077$, so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), uniform TS-JEPA detects $27.8\%$ of abrupt $+5\sigma$ spikes, but on persistent quiet faults (flatlines, stuck sensors, and zero dropouts) event recall falls to $19.4\%$ and point recall drops below $3\%$. Simple observation-space monitors catch these complementary failures: a maximum channel deviation monitor and a rolling variance-collapse monitor (tracking how far each channel's 32-step trailing standard deviation drops below its nominal median) each recover $25\%\text{--}39\%$ of quiet-fault events at $14\%\text{--}15\%$ FPR. Taking the logical union of TS-JEPA alarms and the rolling variance-collapse monitor lifts event recall to $41.7\%\text{--}50.0\%$ across all six fault classes at $17.0\%$ nominal FPR.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) trains TS-JEPA with the identical 3-layer causal TCN encoder across five streams, comparing the 4-shell Fourier filterbank against a standard linear convolutional stem. Enabling the dyadic front-end raises macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ (led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers validation residual kurtosis from $1.41$ to $1.07$, while macro Point-F1 decreases from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to a more conservative SPOT threshold on SMAP P-3. Frequency-shell splitting thus improves score ranking and tail conditioning rather than uniformly raising thresholded F1.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 12 model arms across all 19 non-GHL streams under identical splits, training budgets, and matched parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster vs.\ TCN Obs.\ Autoencoder):} Pairing the same causal dilated TCN encoder with a multilayer perceptron decoder to either forecast the 64-step target window or reconstruct the 320-step window in raw sensor space leaves tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and causal FPR ($\approx 25\%$) nearly identical.
    \item \emph{LayerNorm bottleneck and saliency gating in observation space (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster (LN)):} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck, target-window LayerNorm, and coordinate saliency gating to the observation forecaster compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but targeting raw sensor waveforms leaves calibration drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and drops causal Point-F1 to $0.084$.
    \item \emph{Learned latent target projection and multi-horizon whitening (TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA):} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ latent space cuts calibration drift nearly in half ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), lowering causal FPR to $2.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ while lifting buffered PR-AUC from $0.257$ to $0.331$.
    \item \emph{Nonlinear TCN encoding vs.\ linear PCA and tree isolation (PCA Linear Forecaster (LN) and Isolation Forest):} Replacing the nonlinear TCN with 32 principal components and ridge regression bounds causal FPR to $2.1\%$ through LayerNorm, but causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ (and Isolation Forest reaches only $0.149$ causal event recall), confirming the need for learned nonlinear temporal features.
    \item \emph{Future prediction vs.\ context autoencoding, EMA stability, and $\ell_2$ scoring (JEPA Context Autoencoder, JEPA Hard-Target Copy, and TS-JEPA ($\ell_2$ Scoring)):} Context autoencoding in latent space keeps causal FPR low ($1.7\%$) but drops causal Point-F1 from $0.161$ to $0.112$ and PR-AUC from $0.278$ to $0.235$. Direct weight copying ($\zeta = 0$) or replacing the saliency gate with a Euclidean norm still holds causal FPR below $2\%$ ($0.9\%$ and $1.8\%$), confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent representation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, while Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieves the lowest held-out FPR across both attribution regimes ($0.6\%$ causal, $7.8\%$ buffered), matches TS-JEPA on buffered PR-AUC ($0.331$), and leads in causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.562$ at $f^* = 0.01$).

\noindent\emph{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring cuts held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three-fold under buffered attribution ($7.8\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $9.3\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $\approx 30\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, $p \le 0.045$). Under strictly causal streaming, the false-alarm reduction widens to ten- to forty-fold: $0.6\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $2.4\%$ for TS-JEPA, compared to $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for the reconstructors ($p = 0.010$). This separation holds across all 33 streams ($0.38\%$ and $1.51\%$ causal FPR vs.\ $14.2\%\text{--}16.1\%$ for reconstructors, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), across all $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$), and under zero-tuning LODO evaluation ($0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR for JEPAs vs.\ $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors). In Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c), Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieves the lowest causal held-out FPR on every multi-stream family ($0.1\%\text{--}0.8\%$ vs.\ $5.6\%\text{--}36.0\%$ for baselines).

\noindent\emph{Multi-Horizon Ranking Gains vs.\ Unadjusted Point-F1 Trade-offs and 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Resolution:} Under buffered attribution on the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA tie for the highest PR-AUC ($0.331$ vs.\ $0.329$ for TimesNet and $0.287$ for TranAD). Under scalar 1D SPOT, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA trades lower buffered Point-F1 than TS-JEPA ($0.148$ vs.\ $0.206$) for tighter false-alarm control, outperforming TranAD ($0.130$ F1) at one-quarter the FPR ($7.8\%$ vs.\ $30.7\%$). Across all 141 qualifying multivariate streams in 12 datasets at three seeds with validation early stopping (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)), 3-horizon Speculative-JEPA and Speculative-Alien-JEPA (adding a coreset and residual null-space bank) surpass single-horizon TS-JEPA both causally ($0.184$ vs.\ $0.165$ PR-AUC, $95\%$ CI $[+0.006, +0.034]$; $0.077$ vs.\ $0.067$ F1) and under buffered attribution ($0.253\text{--}0.256$ vs.\ $0.213$ PR-AUC, $p \le 0.0042$, and $0.567$ vs.\ $0.459$ event recall, $p = 1.6{\times}10^{-4}$). Replacing 1D scalar collapse and SPOT extrapolation with the 224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)) resolves the Point-F1 trade-off across all 141 streams: causally, \emph{Speculative-224Bit-Strict} and \emph{Speculative-224Bit-Breaker} lift PR-AUC to $0.197$ ($p = 0.014$ vs.\ TS-JEPA), nearly double causal Point-F1 from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.125\text{--}0.139$ ($97$ wins vs.\ $36$ losses over TS-JEPA, $p = 8.3{\times}10^{-11}$; $114$ wins vs.\ $18$ losses over 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$), raise causal event recall from $48.0\%\text{--}50.7\%$ to $64.8\%\text{--}72.7\%$, and cut mean detection delay from $115.4\text{--}125.4$ to $55.3\text{--}78.0$ steps. Under buffered attribution across the 141 streams, the 224-bit panel raises PR-AUC to $0.261$ ($p = 0.0031$), lifts Point-F1 to $0.177\text{--}0.180$ ($p \le 5.7{\times}10^{-4}$ vs.\ TS-JEPA and $p \le 2.6{\times}10^{-4}$ vs.\ 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA), raises event recall to $66.0\%\text{--}68.5\%$, and lowers buffered held-out FPR below 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA ($16.5\%$ vs.\ $18.0\%$) while cutting delay to $46.6\text{--}54.0$ steps. On GHL ($\pi=0.013$, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), showing that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\emph{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (combining TS-JEPA with a rolling variance-collapse monitor) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each component of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA modifies performance relative to TS-JEPA. First, \emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and barycenter centering} remove common-mode baseline shift, lowering calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.214$ to $0.179$) and cutting the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR four-fold ($71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Second, \emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and Mahalanobis whitening} ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ at $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$) capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, reducing causal FPR from $2.4\%$ to $0.6\%$ and lifting causal event recall at a $1\%$ FPR budget from $0.395$ to $0.578$. Third, \emph{post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) trims buffered FPR slightly ($8.3\%$ to $7.8\%$, $p = 0.026$) and nudges causal event recall from $0.439$ to $0.478$, but leaves Point-F1 and PR-AUC statistically unchanged on the fixed backbone ($p_{\text{TOST}} < 10^{-4}$), confirming that the multi-horizon whitened representation drives the primary gains.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows how zero-lookahead streaming shifts the operating threshold under 1D SPOT. Without 64-step window averaging, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals have a heavier right tail ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$) than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$) or single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\gamma_2 = 1.33$). When 1D SPOT extrapolates that tail to $q=10^{-3}$ in (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}), one or two out-of-sample drifting coordinates (\emph{hijacker dials}) place $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ high on the causal score distribution. Consequently, 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA alerts on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.4\%$ of test steps ($4.0\%$ true-positive rate (TPR) at $0.6\%$ held-out FPR vs.\ $10.8\%$ for TS-JEPA and $24.2\%$ for TimesNet), flagging only the sharpest peak steps inside each anomaly segment ($0.050$ causal Point-F1 despite $47.8\%$ event recall). Against an analytical rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} Note), random firing at Soliton-Reynolds JEPA's held-out nominal rate ($p = 0.56\%$) catches only $27.2\%$ of events versus $47.8\%$ actual, whereas for TimesNet ($24.3\%$ FPR) random firing alone reaches $60.0\%$, exceeding TimesNet's actual $58.8\%$.

Replacing 1D scalar collapse on the 19 non-GHL streams with the \emph{224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel} (Section~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)) eliminates hijacker-dial threshold inflation: in strictly causal streaming, \emph{224-Bit Strict} more than doubles Soliton-Reynolds JEPA's Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.116$ ($16$ wins / $1$ loss, $p = 3.1{\times}10^{-5}$), lifts causal PR-AUC from $0.291$ to $0.312$, and raises event recall from $39.8\%$ to $53.4\%$ at $1.06\%$ held-out FPR, while \emph{224-Bit Breaker} (adding the 32 right-edge onset relays) reaches $0.138$ causal Point-F1 ($17$ wins / $0$ losses, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), $60.7\%$ event recall, and cuts detection delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps ($2.3\times$ faster) at $1.67\%$ FPR. Under buffered attribution with prefix-horizon routing, \emph{224-Bit Strict} and \emph{224-Bit Breaker} lift buffered PR-AUC from $0.370$ to $0.411$, Point-F1 from $0.137$ to $0.190\text{--}0.228$ ($p = 0.026$), and event recall from $37.7\%$ to $54.5\%\text{--}63.1\%$ while lowering held-out FPR below 1D SPOT ($9.77\%$ vs.\ $10.27\%$) and cutting delay four- to five-fold ($402.7 \to 71.8\text{--}97.3$ steps). Adding a soft temporal and cross-perspective attention pooling gate over the 224 dials yields no further gain over discrete Boolean switches ($0.137$ vs.\ $0.138$ causal F1), because continuous soft weights re-introduce cross-coordinate dilution that independent per-dial circuit breakers prevent by construction.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), TS-JEPA keeps causal FPR at $0.4\%\text{--}0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%\text{--}3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while maintaining higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$) and short detection delays ($\Delta t = 57.8\text{--}85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases from $16$ to $64$, TimesNet causal FPR climbs from $6.1\%$ to $21.3\%$, whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Consequently, $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP, MSL, and GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$). Low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, such as hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) and rapid tripwires instead require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.3\%\text{--}11\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we evaluate three comparisons:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.752$) at lower false-alarm rates than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall).
    \item \emph{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models (vertical dotted guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) proves that the JEPA detectors' low false-alarm rate is not an artifact of low alert frequency. At a $1\%$ test FPR budget ($f^* = 0.01$), Soliton-Reynolds JEPA detects $56.2\%$ of anomaly events ($57.8\%$ for $\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$) and TS-JEPA detects $39.5\%$, more than doubling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and TranAD ($14.9\%$). A similar gap holds at $f^* = 0.05$ ($69.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$).
    \item \emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range point anomalies (abrupt amplitude spikes exceeding $\pm 5\sigma$) can be flagged even by a memoryless marginal $z$-score (\texttt{ZScoreMax}), whereas contextual anomalies stay within normal sensor bounds ($\max|z| \le 5\sigma$) and only violate multi-step trajectory structure. In Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a), stratifying the 10 primary SMAP/MSL streams by NASA's official ground-truth taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} shows that observation-space detectors collapse on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC and $30\%\text{--}56\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$). Soliton-Reynolds JEPA leads every contextual metric, achieving $0.652$ buffered PR-AUC, $75.0\%$ causal SPOT recall at $0.6\%$ FPR, and $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$ (surpassing TS-JEPA by $8\text{--}10$ percentage points). Across all 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)), multi-horizon Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and TS-JEPA nearly double the recall of reconstructors on in-range contextual faults ($\approx 60\%$ vs.\ $30\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) while also leading on point faults ($78.1\%\text{--}78.5\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample across all streams and attribution modes. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $0.38\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $1.51\%$ for TS-JEPA, compared to $14.23\%\text{--}16.11\%$ for TranAD and TimesNet. Replacing parametric SPOT with a non-parametric empirical 99.5th percentile ($p_{99.5}$) yields nearly identical results ($0.9\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR for JEPAs vs.\ $15.4\%\text{--}17.5\%$ for reconstructors), demonstrating that reconstructor false-alarm flooding is not an artifact of GPD tail fitting. Under $S=64$ buffered attribution, however, smearing each window score across 64 consecutive steps dilates every isolated exceedance into a 64-step cluster, inflating buffered FPR to $4.6\%\text{--}5.5\%$ for JEPAs and nearly $20\%$ for reconstructors under both rules. Static SPOT is therefore near-calibrated for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA only in strictly causal mode. Two diagnostics explain why causal thresholds hold on latent residuals:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} To separate inter-block regime drift from serial correlation and GPD tail-fit error, we pool nominal calibration and evaluation scores $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ across all $19$ non-GHL streams and \emph{refit} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) under three splits: chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, removing macro-block drift while preserving intra-block serial correlation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$). Under i.i.d.\ shuffling, every model achieves low false-alarm rates near the $0.1\%$ target ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$). In chronological deployment, inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in single-horizon TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors, whereas in barycenter-centered Soliton-Reynolds JEPA the inter-block drift share drops four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.56\%$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.47\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)).
    \item \emph{GPD tail stability, declustering, and rolling adaptation:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, GPD fits converge with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau \le 3.5\%$). Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in only $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells versus $34.4\%\text{--}54.4\%$ for reconstructors. Applying Ferro--Segers runs declustering ($r=5$) \cite{ferro2003inference} prior to GPD fitting trims stationary causal FPR to $0.20\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $0.44\%$ for TS-JEPA. Under discrete step, variance, and regime shifts across 16 streams, adaptive rolling quantiles hold causal TS-JEPA FPR below $0.40\%$ (vs.\ $0.8\%\text{--}3.0\%$ for TimesNet), whereas continuous monotonic mean ramps defeat trailing rolling windows ($>32\%$ FPR across all models).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated calibration across 33 primary and 141 archive-wide continuous telemetry streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Multi-Horizon Whitening:} Matched-encoder ablations separate score bounding from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis ($\gamma_2$ from $6.10$ to $0.91$), learned latent projections cut calibration drift in half ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.376$ to $0.214$ in TS-JEPA), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA lowers drift further to $0.179$ at $0.6\%$ causal FPR. On the fixed backbone, post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation is statistically equivalent to the unmodulated multi-horizon envelope under TOST ($p_{\text{TOST}} < 10^{-4}$), and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag.
    \item \emph{224-Bit Multi-Perspective Circuit Breakers and Archive-Wide Generalization:} Latent scoring lowers held-out false alarms three- to four-fold under buffered attribution and ten- to forty-fold causally while doubling reconstructor event recall at a $1\%$ test FPR budget ($56.2\%$ vs.\ $21.7\%$ for TimesNet). Replacing 1D scalar score collapse and SPOT extrapolation with a 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel and 32 fast right-edge onset relays eliminates hijacker-dial threshold inflation, more than doubling causal Point-F1 ($0.052 \to 0.138$ on $n{=}19$ streams, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$; $0.067\text{--}0.077 \to 0.139$ across all $141$ streams, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$), lifting causal event recall to $72.7\%$, and cutting detection delay in half.
    \item \emph{Calibration Drift and Adaptive Thresholding:} Static SPOT overshoots $q = 10^{-3}$ under buffered window smearing and by $15\times$ causally for single-horizon TS-JEPA, whereas barycenter-whitened Soliton-Reynolds JEPA reduces causal overshoot to $0.38\%$ stream-macro FPR across 33 streams and cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold ($71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$). Runs-declustered SPOT, per-dial hijack guards, and rolling quantiles narrow the remaining gap across step and variance shifts, motivating regime-conditioned conformal calibration.
\end{enumerate}
