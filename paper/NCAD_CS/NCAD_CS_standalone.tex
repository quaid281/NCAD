\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag faults quickly while staying quiet during healthy operation, yet waveform reconstructors fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries, though single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one shared threshold. We integrate multi-horizon JEPA residuals, symmetric or horizon-preserving coordinate whitening, per-coordinate healthy tripwires, and a correlation-aware quorum into a streaming detector. Each stream fits independently on its own healthy prefix, transferring a frozen architecture and calibration protocol across sensor suites rather than pretrained weights across assets. On 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets, our operational Laminar-JEPA-Lite compromise lifts causal event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. It also cuts end-to-end alarm latency from 133.5 to 69.0 steps (including a 63-step window fill lag), while trading 4.8 recall points against our full reference panel for lower mean and buffered false alarms. Overall Point-F1 (0.076 to 0.086) stays uncertain across datasets. TimesNet and TranAD reach higher raw F1 (0.175 and 0.157) only at 24.6 to 25.6 percent mean healthy false-positive rates versus 6.4 percent mean (0.93 percent median) for Lite. Autonomous deployment pairs empirical two-stage screening (52 of 118 accepted; 66 fallback) with quarantined mode-confirmed recalibration.
\end{abstract}

Anomaly detection, Multivariate time series, Representation learning, Self-supervised learning, Statistical process control.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace scalar scoring with an integrated multi-horizon circuit-breaker system, like a breaker box where each circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across dyadic prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, whitened coordinates and their cross-block mean receive individual healthy tripwires and fire when a correlation-aware quorum trips together. Under matched calibration and operational scaling, wider variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), shifting the label-free operating point toward higher coverage within a $256$-step context window (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational compromise): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $128$ position-plus-drift dials under one quorum, using $119.8\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable parameters and $0.90\text{--}0.97$ megabytes (MB) of FP32 weights with the target encoder.
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224-dial reference): monitors $M=4$ horizons across $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays; \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive causal recall points against this reference for lower mean, median, and buffered false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference, though with a higher 95th-percentile tail.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our primary contribution is the integrated streaming detector and gated deployment protocol rather than the supporting algebraic identities alone. Per-stream prefix fitting tests zero-tune transfer of our frozen architecture and calibration rule across sensor suites rather than cross-asset weight transfer. From a 45-stream development pool, 33 qualifying streams guide design, and 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets evaluate generalization under a disjoint five-block protocol (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Integrated panel and live operating point:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes quorum equivariance under matched multiplicative scaling. Across 118 untouched streams, \emph{Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$) over 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA and cuts end-to-end latency from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps at $6.4\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) spans zero under dataset clustering and doubles on $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ streams ($p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{Whitening structure, drift, and empirical screening:} Horizon-preserving Kronecker whitening ($\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) matches dense whitening across all $118$ streams ($0.101$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1, $p=0.76$), and matched-FPR checks confirm ranking parity with 1D JEPAs. Two-stage empirical screening retains $52/118$ streams ($3.9\%$ median independent $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) and routes $66$ to fallback, while block permutation links $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms to chronological nonstationarity, motivating quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}$ recalibration.
    \item \emph{External baselines and controlled attribution:} Under untouched-archive SPOT, TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1) reach higher raw F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, falling to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR versus $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}. Single-core CPU streaming sustains $93\text{--}141$ windows/s within $128$ kilobytes (KB) of state, and leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) masking reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 synthetic sensor-subset attribution on injected shifts.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds block energy with a null reweighting effect, while serial dependence leaves $105/118$ streams without independent tail guarantees. Synthetic channel guards inflate clean FPR to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$, and GHL slow-ramp recovery incurs $74.4\%$ test FPR from baseline wander.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep detectors typically forecast or reconstruct raw windows and threshold $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) forecasters \cite{Hundman2018b} model spacecraft channels directly, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} pairs adversarial training with self-conditioning attention, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior and series attention, and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} reshapes 1D series along Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolution. Equal channel losses fit jitter, so summing across $K \ge 25$ channels yields heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) spikes under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Representation methods encode windows into compact latents \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive methods such as temporal-contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time}, TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, synthetic-anomaly Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}, and dual-attention DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test faults (Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} predict target embeddings $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ without decoding or negative pairs, preventing collapse via exponential moving average (EMA) targets \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance terms \cite{bardes2021vicreg, zbontar2021barlow, ermolov2021whitening}.

\noindent\emph{Residual Decomposition, Conformal Thresholding, and Statistical Process Control:} Telemetry reflects system dynamics \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian/Lagrangian nets \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Koopman lifts \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} model structured laws, though partial differential equation (PDE) penalties exhibit stiff gradients on uncalibrated sensors \cite{wang2021understanding}. We borrow the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as residual algebra. Multivariate statistical process control (SPC) charts such as Hotelling's $T^2$ \cite{hotelling1947multivariate} and Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) \cite{lowry1992multivariate} collapse coordinates into one scalar, as do 1D deep scores calibrated via extreme-value theory \cite{balkema1974residual} and Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) tails in SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} under serial dependence \cite{ferro2003inference}. Per-coordinate Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) banks \cite{page1954continuous} and split-conformal rules under Bonferroni, \v{S}id\'{a}k, or Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate (FDR) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} hit a discrete $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ floor on short histories (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). All evaluations omit point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}.

\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case is a \emph{stream}: one multi-sensor recording ($K$ channels) comprising an unlabeled healthy history followed by a labeled test segment. Two cohorts play distinct roles. From a 45-stream development pool across 11 datasets, 33 qualifying streams formed the primary development cohort, while 141 archive streams across 12 datasets (118 never inspected during design) provide the primary evaluation.

\noindent\emph{Development pool and qualifying cohort:} Drawn from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench} ($K\in[2,72]$), the 45-stream pool spans three domains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar:} six SMAP ($K{=}25$) and six MSL ($K{=}55$) streams, split by NASA into out-of-range \emph{point} and in-range \emph{contextual} faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, plus SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$).
    \item \emph{Wearables:} six $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes; $S{=}64$ spans $1\,\text{s}$).
    \item \emph{Industrial and infrastructure:} 14 slow-ramp Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ faults), Genesis ($K{=}18$), GECCO ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three streams ($19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL across $7$ datasets) meet $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$, $K\le 55$, and $\le 1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c). The remaining $12$ ($5$ short histories of $764\text{--}1{,}492$ steps, $6$ high-channel CICIDS streams, and GECCO) are held aside or evaluated in the archive.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive holds every qualifying stream across 12 datasets with $\ge 100$ calibration windows and one labeled anomaly: SMAP ($47$), Daphnet ($22$), MSL ($18$), SMD ($18$), Exathlon ($17$), OPPORTUNITY ($13$), plus single-stream GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN ($1{,}920\text{--}132{,}481$ nominal and $2{,}158\text{--}114{,}001$ test steps). Removing the $23$ tuning streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($37 + 1 + 80 = 118$): $37$ streams across $4$ strictly zero-tuning external datasets (SMD, Exathlon, PSM, CreditCard), $1$ archive-heldout GECCO stream excluded from tuning, and $80$ unseen streams from $4$ partially tuned datasets (SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, OPPORTUNITY; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(b), Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{Per-stream fitting and transfer:} Each stream fits independently on its own nominal prefix, requiring a healthy warm-up history rather than pretrained-weight transfer. What transfers zero-tune across heterogeneous sensor counts ($K\in[2,55]$) is the frozen architecture, damping, tripwire percentile, and quorum rule across disjoint calibration blocks (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} Because SMAP and MSL have documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed} and same-facility streams share hardware traits, we combine dataset-clustered inference ($7$ dev, $9$ untouched sets), separate zero-tuning vs.\ tuned cohorts, nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) tuning, and 9-fold exclusion sensitivity (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}).

\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-7pt}
\caption{Four-stage pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational compromise, $M{=}3$, $128$ dials) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (high-recall reference, $M{=}4$, $224$ dials plus $32$ separate onset relays): \textbf{(1)}~sliding window ($C{=}256, S{\le}64$) into online/EMA encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) with $K{\ge}25$ rFFT shells, multi-depth taps, centering, and LayerNorm; \textbf{(2)}~parallel MLP multi-horizon prediction and symmetric ZCA (or horizon-preserving $\mathbf{I}_M{\otimes}\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) whitening; \textbf{(3)}~ZCA-anchored position-plus-drift dial expansion; and \textbf{(4)}~per-dial tripwires and correlation-aware quorum alarm.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}

\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ steps at stride $1$, splitting at $t$ into context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ ($C = 256$) and target $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ ($S = 64$) ending at causal decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ mixing. At $K \ge 25$, features split into four smooth dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) summing to one across frequency, pass through depthwise causal convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombine via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN, dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$) \cite{bai2018empirical}; $K < 25$ streams feed directly into the TCN.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) map each window to latent dimension $D=32$ (ablated across $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Each encoder pools three temporal descriptors (last step, max, and mean in $\mathbb{R}^{144}$) and three statistical descriptors (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, and mean absolute first difference in $\mathbb{R}^{144}$) through $d_{\text{head}}$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads ($d_{\text{head}}=D=32$ in canonical archive and primary dev models; $d_{\text{head}}\in\{64,128\}$ in factor and hardware profiles, Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)). The target encoder $E_\phi$ tracks an exponential moving average (EMA) of $E_\theta$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D controls), preventing representation collapse across $10\text{--}20$ epochs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Terminal affine-free LayerNorm projects embeddings onto the $\sqrt{D}$-sphere to curb heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$). However, rescaling by total feature variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines by $O(1/\sqrt{KD})$ across coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two native mechanisms preserve fast transients and training offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Both encoders read the front-end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, passing concatenated mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{288}$) through a bias-free $288\to d_{\text{head}}\to 32$ GELU-LayerNorm head blended at $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} A running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, synchronized into $E_\phi$) is subtracted before terminal LayerNorm, removing static offsets without guaranteeing invariance to post-calibration regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA} predicts one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the 64-step target window via a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant term ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Because one $64$-step vector averages out brief transients, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ dyadic target prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\} = \{S/4, S/2, S\}$ steps, spanning impulse, intermediate, and sustained timescales), while the diagnostic reference \emph{Laminar-JEPA} adds $S_3=48$ ($M=4$). From $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, one parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP predicts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$ behind a single terminal LayerNorm, weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ with a batch anti-collapse penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Acting at batch size $B=16<D=32$ without log-determinant inversion, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ pushes each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma=0.75$ via diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d)}$ and penalizes off-diagonal sample covariance $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ (divisor $B-1$):
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 ,
\end{equation}
raising target effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 converts signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ ($\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$) into nonnegative dials via \emph{zero-phase component analysis (ZCA)} symmetric square-root whitening. Flattening $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($128$ at $M{=}4$, $96$ at $M{=}3$), we fit its mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = (N_{\text{fit}}-1)^{-1}\sum_t (\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})(\cdot)^\top = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Clamping $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$ gives damped precision $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ and symmetric square root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_k}/(\lambda_k + 10^{-3})\bigr)\mathbf{U}^\top$. Multiplying $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})$ by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ normalizes active modes while damping near-null modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$). Unlike eigenbasis whitening, symmetric ZCA minimizes rotation from the $(m,c)$ axes ($r=0.64\text{--}0.67$ correlation with raw marginals vs.\ $r\approx 0$ under eigenbasis PCA). Because nested prefixes correlate, $29.2\%\text{--}31.3\%$ of $\|\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}\|_F^2$ still couples across blocks ($m\neq m'$), making reshaped $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ \emph{ZCA-anchored coordinates} rather than pure horizon-$m$ errors. Replacing $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ with shared $D{\times}D$ Kronecker ZCA $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$ eliminates cross-horizon mixing, commutes with horizon averaging, and matches dense ZCA across all $118$ archive streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Index-aligned shrinkage on $\mathbf{X}_q$:} Because ZCA aligns with the input axes ($r=0.64\text{--}0.67$), coordinates with $\text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c)>1.10$ on disjoint $\mathbf{X}_q$ shrink toward index-matched marginals $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=(e_{m,c}(t)-\bar{\mu}_{\text{res},m,c})/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$, frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (strictly within horizon $m$ under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} Predictor-row editing (Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$ gave no archive gain (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}) and is omitted.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} Splitting reshaped $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t)$ \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} into cross-block mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ ($r=0.63\text{--}0.70$ with pure horizon drift; exact under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$ yields $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{ZCA-anchored positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{cross-block drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{block fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} states two algebraic properties of any reshaped $M\times D$ matrix (exact horizon energy under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$): mean and fluctuation partition squared energy orthogonally, and $\mathbf{F}(t)$ spans at most $M-1$ modes.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ (so the RMS horizon error equals $\sqrt{|\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)}$, not $|\bar{e}_c(t)| + d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$) and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
In plain terms, multivariate residual scoring faces three design options across $J$ coordinates or dials $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$. Global quadratic aggregation $S_{\text{glob}}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$ sums all coordinates into one threshold, whereas a marginal union $\max_j \mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$ compounds per-coordinate false alarms across $J$ tests. A correlation-aware coordinate quorum instead gives every dial its own quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and sets a joint trip-count threshold $K_q$ on healthy calibration data. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} states a multiplicative scale-equivariance property motivating this choice: when $k\ll J$ ``hijacker'' coordinates share a persistent post-training scale factor across both $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and operational data, global quadratic thresholds grow unboundedly while per-coordinate quantile tripwires stay invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Matched-Scale Quorum Equivariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$ denote nominal calibration and operational residual vectors, and assume a matched positive diagonal scaling $\mathbf{S}_\eta = \operatorname{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_J)$ with $s_j=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta\ge 0$) on a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k=|\mathcal{H}|<J$ coordinates and $s_j=1$ for $j\notin\mathcal{H}$ acts jointly on both calibration and operational data ($\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}=\mathbf{S}_\eta\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbf{S}_\eta\mathbf{u}(t)$):
(i)~under global scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-calibration quantile satisfies $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2\bigr)$, growing unboundedly in $\eta^2$ so any live fault $\mathbf{u}(t^*)$ supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected when $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous tripwires $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}|)$, every operational binary trip $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)})$ is invariant to $\eta$ for all $\eta\ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate dependence, leaving $N_{\text{cal}}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J b_{j,\text{cal}}(t)$, $K_q=\kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$, and $N(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J b_j(t)$ unchanged and guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $\sum_{j=1}^J (u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2$ pointwise, monotonicity and positive homogeneity of quantiles give~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ yields $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}(t^*))=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2\le\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity gives $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=s_j\,\tau_j^{(0)}$ for all $j$; dividing $|s_j u_j(t)|>s_j\tau_j^{(0)}$ by $s_j>0$ yields $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=b_j^{(0)}(t)$ identically on both $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and operational $t$, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$, $K_q$, and $N(t)$, while $m$ active coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ yield $N(t^*)\ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration and scope limits:} For $J=32$ standard normal coordinates at $\alpha=0.005$ and $\beta=0.015$, matched $\eta=5$ scaling on $k=2$ hijackers raises $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$, masking a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault ($144.4 < 252.4$) while $K_q=3.5$ stays invariant and trips $10 > 3.5$. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} covers \emph{matched multiplicative scaling} only: if operational variance shifts \emph{after} $\tau_j$ are frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, or if additive bias, covariance rotation, or mode changes occur, $b_j(t)$ is not invariant, requiring empirical $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ screening, live $D_{\text{KS}}$ monitoring, and quarantined recalibration (Section~\ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}). Detection also requires \emph{multi-dial fault coverage ($m > K_q$)}, so diluted single-sensor flatlines miss quorum.

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) implements this quorum on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Because dials share the $M \times 32$ error matrix, trips correlate strongly (median effective rank $65/224$; count variance $7.2\times$ binomial independence), so quorums are fitted directly to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 separate onset relays:} When a sudden fault enters the target window's right edge while $m \in \{1,2\}$ still see healthy data, coordinate $c$ drives an auxiliary binary relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ (excluded from the $224$-dial vector $\mathbf{d}(t)$) under $q_{0.993} + 10^{-4}$, firing when $m=4$ position trips jointly with endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, window $[t,t+S)$ emits at right edge $\tau=t+S-1$, imposing an $S-1=63$-step full-window reporting lag (sharp right-edge faults can fire earlier at $\Delta t < 63$). All causal delays $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ measure end-to-end online latency including this lag. Under \emph{buffered attribution} (retrospective review only), position trips map to $[t,t+S_m)$, drift/fluctuation to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. Summing the $224$ dials gives $N_{224}(\tau)$ (with $128$-dial position count $N_{128}(\tau)$; ranking scores $N_{224}(\tau)$) and summing the $32$ relays gives $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$, firing when:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with calibration quorums $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$ (linear quantiles, strict inequalities).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both archive tiers share the $32$D-head backbone ($119.8\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable, $115.4\text{k}\text{--}124.9\text{k}$ frozen EMA, $235.1\text{k}\text{--}254.2\text{k}$ total or $0.90\text{--}0.97\,\text{MB}$ FP32 across $K\in[5,55]$; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)) with four TCN blocks ($\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), depthwise rFFT shells at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbb{R}^{32} \to 64 \to 32$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and monitors $96$ position plus $32$ drift dials under $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$.
    \item \emph{High-recall $7D=224$ reference (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$, $+32$ params) with $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}); dev tables also report its $5$-block sequential predecessor ($274.2\text{k}\text{--}276.8\text{k}$ trainable; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA (224+32)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\State Partition $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$ or $S_{1:4}{=}\{16,32,48,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and freeze $K_{\text{Lite}}{=}\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$ (or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum})).
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t){=}\mathbb{I}(d_j(t){>}\tau_j)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (\text{causal}) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (\text{buffered}); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ when active counts exceed quorum.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (RMS of full-tensor and peak-horizon scores, $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$) blend $\ell_2$ norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.

\paragraph{Disjoint Calibration Protocol}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ splits chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}) into a fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and validation suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$, with weights and thresholds frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint fitting, selection, and adaptation):} Reusing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for early stopping or stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) biases thresholds, so the primary 118/141-stream archive uses a disjoint \emph{five-block protocol} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Specifically, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits scaling, weights, $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}$, while $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the checkpoint and $S$-isolated $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$) freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) before touching $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Exploratory fixed-epoch suites allocate $65\%$ to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$, and a disjoint 4-block check ($50\%$ $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$) re-evaluates the 19 development streams in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched calibration and dependence):} Splitting $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ windows at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$ across an $S=64$-step gap ($G=320$ shifts FPR by $<0.25$ points). Under independence, $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ gives $2.5$ expected tail windows above $q_{0.995}$; under serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$ via Sokal's $c=5$, median $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}} = 65$), only $0.325$ independent exceedances remain, making $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ an empirical operating cutoff rather than a statistical tail guarantee (Section~\ref{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}).

\paragraph{Statistical vs.\ Practical Significance}
Seeds average per stream with seed standard deviation (SD) $\le 0.008$ on causal F1 and $\le 1.9\%$ on ER, making the stream the independent unit. We report both \emph{dataset-clustered inference}, using $95\%$ cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) and exact $2^D$ sign-flip tests, and stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests ($2{,}000\text{--}4{,}000$ resamples), applying Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) control \cite{benjamini1995controlling} across the four primary contrasts (\emph{Lite} vs.\ 1D and TS-JEPA on F1 and ER). We also separate zero-tuning unseen datasets from partially tuned cohorts and pair nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) tuning selection with 9-fold untouched-archive exclusion sensitivity (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Cohorts, Baseline Fairness, and Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Causal and buffered runs share identical splits:
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts:} Used during design (33-stream qualifying primary cohort shares $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ for thresholds and blending), these cover the 19-stream dial cache ($6$ sets; Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:matched_alert}) and 33-stream external suite ($19$ non-GHL plus $14$ GHL from the 45-stream pool, $7$ sets; Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Hyperparameters were frozen once on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched archive:} Removing those $23$ streams leaves $118$ untouched streams across $9$ datasets ($37$ strictly zero-tuning external streams, $1$ archive-heldout GECCO stream, and $80$ unseen streams from $4$ tuned sets: $37+1+80=118$; Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit}).

\noindent\emph{Baseline fairness across three tiers:} (i)~\emph{Matched-backbone controls} (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon 1D, and w/o Quench) share identical encoders, epochs, and seeds ($\{42,123,456\}$), tested alongside Wu--Keogh cycle-swap, phase-desync, and Gaussian-noise controls against $\max_c|x_c(t)|$ \cite{wu2023flawed}. (ii)~\emph{System aggregators} score identical frozen residuals at matched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alert rates. (iii)~\emph{External baselines} span TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}, with Linear PCA Forecaster (LayerNorm), TranAD, and TimesNet evaluated across all $118$ untouched streams ($3$ seeds, five-block SPOT). External models use published configs ($206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ params), $W=320$, $64$-step gaps, and SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) on PyTorch 2.4 / RTX 5090 (\url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Stepwise predictions without point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards} average seeds per stream before cohort or dataset-macro ($\text{DS-F1}$) aggregation. Healthy alarm rates $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ report mean, median, p90, and p95. Point-F1 ($0.05\text{--}0.25$ at prevalence $\pi$, compared against the all-positive baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all}}=2\pi/(1+\pi)$) and precision-recall (PR) AUC measure step accuracy and ranking. Event coverage ER is compared against $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p)$, while causal $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ measures end-to-end online latency including the $S-1=63$-step fill lag. Autonomous thresholds (\emph{Auto.}) are contrasted against $q^*=0.05$ and post-hoc $f^*\in\{0.01,0.05\}$, paired with kurtosis $\gamma_2$ and Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift $D_{\text{KS}}$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
To isolate which components drive live detection without conflating cached residuals and retrained backbones, we evaluate five controlled ablation tiers across Table~\ref{tab:ablations}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cached dial ladder vs.\ $128$ vs.\ $160$ dials:} On the fixed $19$-stream sequential cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding $1\to 224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$), and pruning fluctuation/relay dials (160 pos.+drift) reaches $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Unweighted $N_{224}$ leaves binary alarms unchanged and shifts archive PR-AUC by $\le 0.0011$. When retrained end-to-end on \emph{Lite} (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), adding a fourth prefix ($160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) gives no Point-F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$) while raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $0.82\%$ to $1.06\%$ and slowing inference by $25.5\%$. On the $118$-stream matched-rate rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} note), $128$-dial \emph{Lite} likewise matches retrained $160$- and $224$-dial panels ($0.078$ vs.\ $0.073\text{--}0.074$ F1).
    \item \emph{Single-factor isolation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Under $3$-seed four-block retraining, four TCN blocks match five on F1 and ER ($p\ge 0.23$, saving $23.3\text{k}$ params), and $64$D pooling heads lift causal ER over $128$D ($55.9\%\to 63.1\%$). Sequential rollout at $M=4$ drops buffered F1 ($0.239\to 0.195$) and doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%\to 4.0\%$). Expanding $160\to 224$ dials raises causal ER ($63.6\%\to 69.0\%$) and F1 ($0.086\to 0.095$), trading higher $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.06\%\to 1.45\%$ causal; $6.8\%\to 11.9\%$ buffered).
    \item \emph{Horizon-preserving vs.\ dense ZCA whitening:} Evaluating five Stage~3 operators on fixed \emph{Lite} checkpoints across all $19$ dev ($3$ seeds) and $118$ untouched archive streams ($s=42$) shows that cross-horizon mixing is unnecessary. Shared Kronecker ZCA ($\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$, $32{\times}32$) matches dense $96{\times}96$ ZCA on the archive ($0.101$ vs.\ $0.091$ causal F1, $p=0.76$; $64.4\%$ vs.\ $62.9\%$ ER, $p=0.53$), while per-horizon block-diagonal ZCA and pre-whitening dial expansion match causal F1 ($0.095, 0.098$) and lift buffered ER ($+6.2, +5.7$ points, $p\le 0.048$). Non-ZCA eigenbasis whitening breaks (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) alignment ($r=-0.003$ vs.\ $0.637$) and drops F1 ($0.091\to 0.071$ causal, $p=0.033$; $0.159\to 0.146$ buffered, $p=0.016$).
    \item \emph{Quenching omission:} On a single-seed $118$-stream screen, predictor-row edits (Q1--Q4) leave causal F1 flat ($0.0901$ vs.\ $0.0904$) while shifting unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS, so \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
    \item \emph{Dimensionality, damping, and quorum sweeps:} Retraining $D\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ ($3$ seeds) beats Multi-Horizon 1D at every size ($56.1\%\text{--}61.0\%$ vs.\ $42.5\%\text{--}48.9\%$ ER). Narrow $D=8$ underfits buffered F1 ($0.131$ vs.\ $0.216$ at $D=32$); $D\in\{16,32\}$ plateaus with peak PR-AUC at $D=32$ ($0.269$), while $D=64$ dips to $0.068$ causal F1. Performance stays flat across whitening damping $\lambda_{\text{damp}}\in[10^{-3},10^{-2}]$ ($0.080\text{--}0.093$ F1, $\le 0.75\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$). Varying the dial quantile $q_{\text{dial}}\in[0.975,0.990]$ also holds F1 steady at $0.080\text{--}0.090$ because $K_{\text{Lite}}$ adapts automatically from $21.2$ to $13.0$. Varying $\kappa\in[1.00,1.20]$ trades ER ($63.3\%\to 55.1\%$) against $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.21\%\to 0.55\%$); $\kappa=1.12$ holds $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}<1\%$ before delay jumps at $\kappa=1.20$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate rules fail through three distinct statistical mechanisms on the cached 19-stream residuals (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Cross-coordinate false-alarm compounding:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds marginal errors across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Discrete finite-sample calibration floor:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k at $0.5\%$ across 128 coordinates or BH FDR ($q=0.005$) across 224 dials fails even under stationarity because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot fall below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, collapsing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{Out-of-sample tail instability:} Matching each stream's $0.34\%$ nominal rate after aggregation bypasses the discrete floor, yet discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $21.1\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on ties and Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM reach $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$. Stable aggregators are led by the 160-dial panel ($0.098$ F1, $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) and Fisher pooling ($0.109$ F1, $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), ahead of top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.056\text{--}0.072$; archive reruns in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Ablation 2: Sensor-Fault Taxonomy and Synthetic vs.\ Natural Failures}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The latent panel flags \emph{cross-channel consistency violations}, \emph{abrupt multi-sensor transients}, and \emph{in-range phase shifts} because they perturb $>K_{\text{Lite}}$ coordinates simultaneously. Conversely, isolated single-sensor faults (freezing, variance collapse, saturation, quantization, dropouts, or single-channel bias) barely move the 32D summary: $K-1$ healthy channels dilute the input projection by $1/K$ and terminal LayerNorm rescales the remainder across all $32$ coordinates (on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, a flatline trips $12.5$ dials vs.\ $62.1$ for a harmonic flip).

Testing an auxiliary channel screen across all $19$ dev streams ($K\in[5,55]$), we threshold 64-step log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, first-order autoregressive (AR) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$ at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ with a $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD) floor and $24$-of-$40$ persistence. Across $57$ injected $200$-step single-channel events per class, the variance-collapse union lifts recall on collapse, freezing, and saturation ($63.2\%\text{--}82.5\%\to 93.0\%\text{--}94.7\%$), and the full branch lifts recall on shocks, linear ramps, and bias ($59.6\%\text{--}75.4\%\to 80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$). On \emph{unmodified} test streams, however, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ climbs from $1.5\%$ ($\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ and $19.4\%$. Because synthetic injections omit real hardware coupling, we claim no field sensor-fault coverage and keep the channel branch disabled.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End and Observation vs.\ Latent Space}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Retraining with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five exploratory streams ($3$ seeds) motivates our spectral gate: shell splitting lifts high-channel spacecraft F1 ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal $0.212 \to 0.321$) but lowers F1 on $K{=}9$ wearable and water-quality streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}). Because this five-stream comparison is small and confounded with sensor domain, $K \ge 25$ is treated as a pragmatic design heuristic rather than proof that channel count alone drives the difference. Across the 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), raw-observation reconstructors exhibit heavy tails, strong drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$). Adding an observation-space LayerNorm bottleneck cuts kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$), while learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). Because \emph{Lite} and Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) share nearly identical score kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$), the panel's live gain stems from coordinate isolation rather than lighter tails.

\subsection{Computational Complexity and Real-Time Deployment Considerations}
\label{subsec:computational_deployment}
Operational spacecraft and industrial monitors require bounded memory and deterministic full-pipeline latency across channel standardization, $W=320$ ring-buffer updates, encoder-predictor inference, $96\times 96$ whitening, stabilization blending, and 128-dial quorum evaluation. Canonical $32$D-head \emph{Lite} holds $119.8\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable parameters ($0.90\text{--}0.97\,\text{MB}$ FP32 weights with the frozen EMA encoder; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)); to obtain conservative upper-bound timings across $K \in \{5, 9, 25, 38, 55, 72\}$, we benchmark the wider $64$D-head profile ($141.5\text{k}\text{--}151.9\text{k}$ trainable, $278.5\text{k}\text{--}299.3\text{k}$ total weights, or $1.10\text{--}1.18\,\text{MB}$ FP32 including $36.6\,\text{KB}$ whitening buffers) plus $44.2\text{--}128.4\,\text{KB}$ of ring-buffer and tripwire state. Full-pipeline GPU inference (batch $64$, RTX 5090, $33\text{--}91\,\text{MB}$ peak memory) takes $0.162\text{--}0.253\,\text{ms}$/window, adding only $0.018\text{--}0.051\,\text{ms}$ over the neural forward pass ($0.144\text{--}0.201\,\text{ms}$). On a single CPU thread (batch $1$, embedded-processor proxy), full-pipeline streaming takes $7.11\text{--}7.45\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \le 9$ and $9.76\text{--}10.79\,\text{ms}$/window at $K \in [25, 72]$, sustaining $93\text{--}141$ windows/s ($>2\times$ Daphnet's $64\,\text{Hz}$ rate at $K=9$). Enabling $8$ CPU threads trims high-channel latency to $7.28\text{--}10.24\,\text{ms}$/window. Under unannounced sensor dropouts on the 19 development streams (mean-imputing dropped channels without retraining), dropping one or two active sensors reduces causal ER gradually from $58.9\%$ to $55.9\%\text{--}54.7\%$ (Point-F1 $0.093$ to $0.066$) while raising $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $1.5\%$ to $3.4\%\text{--}7.9\%$.

\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark, Attribution Regimes, and Horizons}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} explains why scalar scores degrade when moving from buffered review to live streaming. Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$ and causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $1.2\%$ to $0.7\%$ ($p=0.0025$). In return, \emph{Lite} trades lower causal ER ($62.2\%$ vs.\ $70.1\%$, $p=0.0029$) and causal Point-F1 ($0.142$ vs.\ $0.165$, $p=0.035$) against the $224$-dial reference. A separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.151\text{--}0.152$ for TimesNet and TranAD, and $0.082\text{--}0.097$ for DCdetector, NCAD, and Anomaly Transformer). Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ F1 across $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), because context and target latents shift together and cancel. Across linear ramps ($a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$, duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$), chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$. Across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds), mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $433.9$ on exponential $1-e^{-t/512}$ and $586.7$ on $0.001\sigma$/step bias as $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

Pairing \emph{Lite} with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ lifts real GHL causal ER from $0.8\%$ ($0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Post-fault GHL baseline wander, however, drives uncompensated test FPR to $74.4\%$: sliding prediction misses slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift flags benign wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned ZCA-anchored latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across block indices ($m\in\{1,2,3\}$; exact horizons under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and cross-block drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $m\in\{2,3\}$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking provides \emph{controlled perturbation attribution} across input channels. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under injected $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Because latent features mix sensors nonlinearly and real field datasets lack ground-truth root-cause labels, this validates synthetic perturbation sensitivity rather than causal fault localization on natural failures, and $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines remain sub-quorum.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Chronological distribution shift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration variance vs.\ chronological nonstationarity (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and within-block dependence while removing temporal regime evolution across mean, variance, covariance, and operating modes. Across $n=118$, block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ falls from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ ($\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams), showing that chronological distribution shift explains $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms.
    \item \emph{Associated drift correlates:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) match stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$), whereas stabilized residual-covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), strongly correlating with quorum inflation ($4.8\times$) and $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In $3$-seed fault-free dev stress tests, terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and keeps $45^\circ$ basis rotations moderate ($6.8\%$ FPR). Mean steps, duty-cycle shifts, and baseline walks do not self-settle, raising FPR to $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ alongside $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$. Blindly auto-recalibrating on $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, however, absorbs persistent faults: fitting $400$ post-shift windows during a persistent $+2.5\sigma$ fault cuts post-buffer recall to $9.1\%$. Our conditional policy therefore flags $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ under frozen weights, quarantines candidate windows, and permits threshold updates only after operator or control-state confirmation ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall preserved; $4.8\%$ FPR on confirmed shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability and Two-Stage Deployment Funnel}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite histories lack distribution-free tail guarantees. Serial dependence (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams with $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Resampling finite blocks via Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) tests dependence sensitivity rather than generating unobserved extremes. On an independent $3$-seed audit across $n=118$, \texttt{MBB-Median} matches that audit's unbootstrapped quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR and $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1), aligning with the primary run's $0.93\%$ median and $64.7\%$ p95 (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Conservative upper-confidence-bound (UCB) \texttt{MBB-UCB90} and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds cost $8\text{--}17$ ER points, while thinning raises mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

Autonomous operation relies on an empirical two-stage screening funnel rather than a distribution-free guarantee (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). After disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$ isolation, requiring empirical $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59/118$ ($50.0\%$) streams; below $2{,}500$ steps, disjoint cross-fitting cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$. Screening frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ then accepts $52$ streams ($44.1\%$ of $118$, with $35$ also meeting $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$) and routes $7$ drifting streams ($33.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $48.9\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) to fallback. Because these $52$ streams are conditioned on passing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.9\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; dependence-adjusted $95\%$ Wilson UCB median $5.8\%$, p90 $14.5\%$ at median $N_{\text{eff,hold}}=66.7$), $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ alone overstates future stability. On their subsequent independent healthy test intervals, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises to $11.0\%$ mean and $3.9\%$ median ($53.8\%$ holding $\le 5\%$; $76.9\%$ holding $\le 10\%$) while doubling Point-F1 over 1D ($0.076$ vs.\ $0.034$, $p=3.2{\times}10^{-5}$). Routing all $66$ non-accepted streams to 1D SPOT yields a $118$-stream hybrid ($0.094$ F1, $60.9\%$ ER, $4.6\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $11.3\%$ mean / $1.9\%$ median $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) that still requires live $D_{\text{KS}}$ quarantine monitoring.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Deploying \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} centers on an integrated screening and fallback workflow rather than a distribution-free threshold guarantee. Streams passing $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ and frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($52/118$) run the $128$-dial panel, while the remaining $66$ short or drifting streams route to 1D SPOT fallback. Because conditioning on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ introduces selection optimism and finite-sample uncertainty ($5.8\%$ median dependence-adjusted $95\%$ UCB; $11.0\%$ mean and $3.9\%$ median independent $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$), accepted streams still require live $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ monitoring with a $400$-window quarantine buffer awaiting confirmed mode transitions. On alarming windows, batched LOCO masking ranks channels by controlled perturbation sensitivity ($85.7\%$ Top-1 on injected shifts) as a triage aid rather than verified root-cause localization.

Per-stream healthy-prefix fitting suits telemetry spanning $K=2$ to $55$ channels with incompatible physical units, cadences, and regimes, where zero-shot pretrained weights struggle without fine-tuning. Prefix fitting transfers the frozen architecture, symmetric or horizon-preserving $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$ whitening, and calibration rule across domains at $93\text{--}141$ windows/s on one CPU thread without cross-asset label leakage. Null regularizer results, ranking parity with scalar JEPAs, and higher raw F1 for TimesNet and TranAD at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ confirm that the gain comes from low-false-alarm operating-point placement, with \emph{Lite} trading $4.8$ recall points against $224$-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} for lower mean and buffered false alarms. Complementary guards remain necessary for $1/K$-diluted single-channel flatlines, unconditioned slow ramps, and prefixes below $500$ windows.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Integrating multi-horizon JEPA residuals, coordinate whitening, per-dial healthy tripwires, and fallback routing across $33$ primary development streams (from a $45$-stream pool) and $118$ untouched archive streams ($37+1+80=118$) yields three conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operating-point trade-off and whitening:} Under matched multiplicative scaling, per-dial tripwires prevent high-variance coordinates from inflating a shared threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), and horizon-preserving Kronecker ZCA ($\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) matches dense ZCA across all $118$ streams ($0.101$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1, $p=0.76$). \emph{Lite} lifts causal ER over 1D JEPA from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$) and cuts latency from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps, trading $-4.8$ ER points against $224$B for lower mean and buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, while doubling Point-F1 on $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{External baselines and synthetic attribution:} TimesNet and TranAD reach higher raw archive F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ vs.\ $51.2\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR), while matched-FPR ranking matches 1D JEPAs and LOCO masking provides synthetic perturbation attribution.
    \item \emph{Nonstationarity, screening, and quarantine:} Block permutation links $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms to chronological nonstationarity, strongly associated with covariance shift ($3.1\times$). Empirical screening ($52/118$ accepted, $66$ fallback) pairs with quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ recalibration ($31.9\%$ vs.\ $9.1\%$ fault recall; $4.8\%$ confirmed FPR).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Priorities include validating physical fault localization on field-labeled hardware failures, streaming updates to $\mathbf{\Omega}_D$ without $400$-window buffers, control-conditioned slow-ramp monitoring, and low-FPR univariate flatline guards.
