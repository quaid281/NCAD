\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space. Because sensor telemetry contains unforecastable jitter and scale disparities, observation-space residuals develop heavy tails that trigger severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid reconstructing raw noise by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows. We investigate how latent prediction conditions residual distributions under regime shift. Specifically, we compare a single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) against Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, a multi-horizon architecture that taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context barycenters, whitens multi-horizon prediction errors, and scores centered sub-horizon fluctuations via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio. Across continuous physical telemetry benchmarks calibrated on held-out nominal data with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT), latent scoring suppresses false alarms under both retrospective buffered attribution and strictly causal streaming while improving anomaly ranking and event recall. Controlled factorial ablations isolate four distinct mechanisms: layer-normalization bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, learned latent projections and multi-horizon barycenter whitening curb calibration-to-holdout drift, short prediction horizons eliminate emission lag, and hybrid sensor-variance monitors recover quiet single-channel faults.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\emph{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} In deployed monitoring, an automated rule must convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using healthy (nominal) calibration data alone. Standard deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) train a neural network to reconstruct every raw sensor reading and flag timestamps where the point-by-point reconstruction error is large \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains random sensor jitter and scale differences across channels, those point-level errors produce frequent, clustered spikes, resulting in a heavy-tailed residual distribution with high excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$, Table~\ref{tab:terms}). When paired with an automated thresholding rule such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} set to a $0.1\%$ target false-alarm rate ($q = 10^{-3}$), normal background shifts in unseen data easily cross the threshold, triggering false alarms on $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of healthy timesteps.

\noindent\emph{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} Predicting a compact \emph{summary} of a future time window, rather than reconstructing every noisy sensor sample, filters out random jitter before the prediction error is computed. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which compress the preceding 256 timesteps into a 32-dimensional summary vector and forecast the summary of the next $S$ timesteps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for fast-response tripwires) using healthy training data alone \cite{carmona2021neural}. Alongside a single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) that predicts the entire future window at once, we introduce \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, a multi-horizon predictor that monitors four nested lookahead windows simultaneously. To catch sharp fault onsets without being fooled by slow background drift, \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} combines features from multiple depths of its temporal convolutional network \cite{bai2018empirical}, subtracts the current batch average (barycenter) to cancel slow level shifts, and standardizes (whitens) prediction errors across the four sub-horizons. Drawing on Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} and Lieb--Thirring spectral bounds \cite{lieb1976inequalities}, it then separates those multi-horizon errors into an average shift and sub-horizon fluctuations, measuring whether fluctuation energy is spread evenly across horizons like background noise or concentrated into a coherent wavepacket like an emerging fault. Rather than claiming that static thresholds achieve an exact $0.1\%$ false-alarm rate on non-stationary streams, we show that latent prediction reduces false alarms, curbs distribution drift between calibration and test data, and improves anomaly ranking. We evaluate four questions: (i)~how much latent residuals lower realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ under static, declustered, and rolling thresholds; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent targets, multi-horizon whitening, and wavepacket concentration each contribute to those gains; (iii)~how detection varies across abrupt spikes, gradual drifts, and quiet single-sensor freezes; and (iv)~how shortening the lookahead horizon $S$ trades off alarm lag ($S-1$) against detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Why latent predictors stabilize alarm thresholds:} Across 12 matched-backbone models ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ online parameters), we separate two distinct mechanisms. First, a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bottleneck caps extreme error spikes, compressing tail heaviness (excess kurtosis) from $6.10$ to $0.91$. Second, predicting a learned latent summary instead of raw sensor waveforms cuts distribution drift between calibration and later healthy data in half ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in TS-JEPA), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$ (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \emph{Fewer false alarms, stronger ranking, and quiet-fault recovery:} Across the 19 non-GHL benchmark streams, latent scoring cuts held-out FPRs three-fold under retrospective buffered attribution ($7.8\%\text{--}9.3\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD) and ten- to forty-fold under strictly causal streaming ($0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). Soliton-Reynolds JEPA matches TS-JEPA on buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC $0.331$) while achieving the lowest held-out FPR and catching $56.2\%$ of anomaly events when all detectors are compared at an equalized $1\%$ false-alarm budget. Because normalization can hide a single frozen sensor when other channels remain active, pairing latent prediction with a rolling sensor-variance check recovers quiet single-channel faults (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)).
    \item \emph{Multi-horizon wavepacket bounds, ablation controls, and fast response:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that after subtracting the average error across $M$ sub-horizons, the Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio stays strictly between $\frac{1}{M-1}$ (errors spread evenly across horizons like background noise) and $1$ (errors aligned into a single fault wavepacket), regardless of signal amplitude. Controlled ablations on the same trained network show that multi-horizon whitened errors drive the ranking and drift improvements, whereas the post-hoc wavepacket multiplier has a negligible aggregate effect under Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) \cite{schuirmann1987comparison} equivalence. For time-critical monitoring, short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step window lag while keeping false alarms low (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations} and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
    \item \emph{Why automated thresholds fail and when they hold:} By comparing chronological evaluation against block-shuffled and randomly shuffled healthy data (with bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs)), we measure how much false-alarm inflation comes from slow background drift versus short-term correlation. Slow regime drift between calibration and test periods causes $71.3\%$ of the excess causal false alarms in single-horizon TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in observation-space reconstructors. In contrast, subtracting the batch barycenter in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold ($18.7\%$, $95\%$ CI $[-.002, +.004]$), and runs-declustered SPOT or rolling quantiles further stabilize thresholds under step and variance shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly forecast sensor readings or reconstruct input windows in raw observation space, flagging timestamps where point-level error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on spacecraft channels. Recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods for 2D convolutional modeling. Because point-level losses penalize error across every channel equally, reconstructors fit stochastic sensor jitter and environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), summing raw channel errors accumulates heavy-tailed (\emph{leptokurtic}, $\gamma_2 > 0$) residual spikes that destabilize extreme-value threshold fitting under baseline shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\emph{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} Self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent space \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: temporal and contextual contrasting \cite{eldele2021time} and TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximize contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full-window and context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention without reconstruction. However, contrastive detectors depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent prediction across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)). Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate observation decoding and negative pairs by encoding context $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicting the target embedding $z_{\text{tgt}}$ produced by a separate target encoder. Collapse is prevented via exponential moving average (EMA) target updates \cite{grill2020bootstrap} or variance-covariance penalties such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}. How latent predictive residuals behave under unsupervised extreme-value threshold calibration remains unexplored.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamic Reynolds Decomposition and Spectral Concentration Priors:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects physical dynamical trajectories \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics}, and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce differential conservation laws on physical coordinates, but require known governing equations and suffer from stiff gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}. Koopman and operator-theoretic models \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral, brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} lift nonlinear dynamics into learned observable spaces. Rather than imposing partial differential equations on raw sensors, we draw on Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} from turbulence modeling to separate multi-horizon latent predictions into a mean trajectory and centered sub-horizon fluctuations. We then measure wavepacket localization across prediction horizons via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio \cite{lieb1976inequalities} on the centered fluctuation Gram matrix.

\noindent\emph{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration and Evaluation Rigor:} Grounded in the Pickands--Balkema--de Haan extreme-value theorem \cite{balkema1974residual}, Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to score exceedances above quantile $u$ to extrapolate a threshold at design risk $q$. However, continuous telemetry violates the classical independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption through short-range serial correlation (quantified by the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}) and inter-block regime drift. Because ``point-adjusted'' F1 severely inflates scores \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly}, we evaluate all models using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm operating curves.

\section{Multi-Sensor Internet-of-Things and Remote Sensing Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Our benchmark suite comprises 11 datasets spanning 45 continuous telemetry streams from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. Thirty-three streams enter the primary multi-seed held-out protocol; 12 are reserved with explicit accounting below. The suite spans four physical domains and separates natural operational or physiological anomalies from testbed or simulated faults: \emph{spacecraft and solar telemetry} (six SMAP streams, $K{=}25$, $7$ anomaly events; four primary and two short-nominal MSL rover streams, $K{=}55$, $6$ primary events; and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate}, $K{=}38$, $6{,}615$ flare events, all natural operational faults); and \emph{wearable kinematics} (six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} at $64\,\text{Hz}$, $K{=}9$, $72$ natural gait-freezing episodes, where $S{=}64$ equals a $1\,\text{s}$ horizon). In \emph{industrial processes} and \emph{smart infrastructure}, the suite covers 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$, $26$ testbed valve and heater faults), the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$, $2$ actuator faults), GECCO water chemistry at $1$-minute resolution ($K{=}9$, $37$ natural hydrological anomalies), Metro I-94 hourly highway traffic ($K{=}5$, $25$ natural traffic events), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$, simulated attack flows). Cohort~3 (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}) separately injects six controlled single-channel faults into six real streams.

Each stream provides nominal training sequences ($N_{\text{tr}} \in [2{,}074, 120{,}000]$ across the 33 primary streams) and labeled anomaly test sequences ($N_{\text{test}} \in [2{,}158, 114{,}001]$). Channels are standardized by training statistics with $\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$ against flatlines, matching standardized archive subsets in prior benchmarks \cite{Hundman2018b, schmidl2022anomaly}.

Each nominal sequence is split chronologically into training ($65\%$), nominal calibration ($17.5\%$), and held-out nominal evaluation ($17.5\%$) blocks separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}). Inclusion in the primary multi-seed protocol required two prespecified criteria: (i)~at least $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ calibration windows ($M_{\text{val}} = \lceil 0.35\,T_{\text{nom}}\rceil - 319 \ge 1{,}000$, requiring $T_{\text{nom}} \ge 3{,}769$), and (ii)~tractable stride-1 multi-seed runtime ($K \le 55$, $N_{\text{test}} \le 1.2\times 10^5$). Exactly 33 streams across seven datasets satisfy both criteria (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). Among the 12 reserved streams, five have nominal sequences too short to yield $500$ calibration windows: CalIt2 ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}940, N_{\text{cal}}{=}4$), MSL C-2 ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}764, N_{\text{cal}}{=}0$), MSL D-16 ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}451, N_{\text{cal}}{=}94$), and two Room-Occupancy streams ($T_{\text{nom}}{=}1{,}073$ and $1{,}492$; $N_{\text{cal}}{=}28$ and $101$). Six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72, N_{\text{test}} \in [1.36\times 10^5, 4.15\times 10^5]$) exceed the stride-1 multi-seed runtime envelope, and GECCO water-quality ($K{=}9$, $1$ held-out event) is used in Cohorts 3--4. All 11 datasets and 130 archive streams appear in the secondary single-seed cross-check (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).

Two caveats bound external validity: (i)~SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}; and (ii)~streams within SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, and GHL share hardware platforms ($N{=}7$ dataset clusters). We therefore report Dataset-Macro means, between-stream and within-stream seed standard deviations (SDs), zero-tuning leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) sensitivity, and dataset-clustered permutation tests (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)).

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to calibrated binary alarms. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the series at index $t$ into: (i)~a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$), establishing the nominal regime; and (ii)~a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$), inspected for trajectory departures. Each window passes through a 4-shell dyadic real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) front-end and a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit leptokurtic sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro} and~\ref{sec:related}), Stage~2 shifts prediction into a 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders tap all $L=3$ TCN stages and map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into spherical representations $z_{\text{ctx}}, z_{\text{tgt}} \in \sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. To prevent constant-vector collapse, target weights $\phi$ are updated as an exponential moving average (EMA) of online weights $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with cosine momentum $m_k \in [0.995, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Stage~3 then rolls out latent predictions across $M=4$ nested sub-horizons ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$) under a two-term multi-horizon mean-squared error (MSE) and log-determinant whitening objective (Section~\ref{subsec:triad_formulations}), and Stage~4 applies Mahalanobis residual whitening, Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration modulation, and coordinate-saliency-gated SPOT calibration under causal trailing ($\tau = t + 63$) and buffered smear mappings (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}).

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures: TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We evaluate two nested latent predictors sharing the same multiscale encoder backbone and coordinate saliency gate: an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) and a multi-horizon fluid-dynamic decomposition model (Soliton-Reynolds JEPA).

\subsubsection{Unconstrained TS-JEPA Baseline}
In TS-JEPA, a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-horizon prediction $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with standard single-horizon predictive alignment and VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg} ($\gamma_{\text{var}} = 1.0$). Terminal LayerNorm \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bounds latent representations on the sphere of radius $\sqrt{D}$ ($\|z\|_2 = \sqrt{D}$) and compresses nominal residual kurtosis relative to observation-space reconstructors. However, single-horizon encoding pools the entire $S=64$ target window into one terminal TCN state, smoothing out short-lived sub-horizon transients and leaving cross-horizon residual correlations unwhitened.

\subsubsection{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA (Multi-Horizon Reynolds Decomposition and Lieb--Thirring Concentration)}
\noindent\emph{Physical Motivation and Structural Design:} In fluid turbulence, Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} separates a velocity field into its time-averaged mean flow and zero-mean fluctuations, while dispersive wave theory characterizes localized, shape-preserving wavepackets (\emph{solitons}) by spectral concentration of the fluctuation operator \cite{lieb1976inequalities}. Soliton-Reynolds JEPA brings both principles into latent time-series prediction through two architectural stages before residual scoring:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth TCN functional hierarchy taps and synchronized barycenter centering:} Standard TCN encoders read only the deepest dilated block, where wide receptive fields attenuate sharp onset transients. In Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, a multi-depth tap head extracts intermediate feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=64$) across all $L=3$ causal dilated TCN stages, concatenating the temporal mean and terminal state of each stage with the base encoder output into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{cat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times (2 L d_{\text{model}} + D)}$. A two-layer residual projection maps $\mathbf{h}_{\text{cat}}$ onto the unit sphere $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. To eliminate common-mode baseline drift between context and target windows, the online context batch barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} = \operatorname{sg}\bigl(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B \mathbf{u}_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}\bigr)$ is subtracted from both online context and EMA target spherical embeddings prior to final LayerNorm projection.
    \item \emph{Nested causal multi-horizon rollout and log-determinant whitening:} Rather than predicting only the full $S=64$ window, we partition $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefix windows $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ with lengths $S_m = (m/M)S \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$. The EMA target encoder encodes each prefix into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. From $z_{\text{ctx}}$, a sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out intermediate predictions $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ ($\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$) using learnable horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the primary predictor $P_\theta$ forecasts the terminal horizon $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ for $m < M$ and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, the model is trained with a parsimonious two-term objective combining multi-horizon predictive MSE and log-determinant covariance whitening:
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Sol-Rey}} &= \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 \\
    &\quad + \sum_{m=1}^{M-1} w_m \mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr) \\
    &\quad + \frac{w_M}{2}\Bigl(\mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}\bigr) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}(z_{\text{ctx}})\Bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}(\mathbf{Z}) = -\frac{\beta}{D}\log\det\bigl(\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{Z}) + \epsilon\mathbf{I}_D\bigr)$ ($\beta = 0.05$, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$). By Hadamard's inequality ($\det(\mathbf{\Sigma}) \le \prod_{d=1}^D \Sigma_{dd}$ with equality if and only if $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ is diagonal), this single log-determinant barrier simultaneously prevents coordinate variance collapse and decorrelates latent dimensions at each horizon without separate variance-hinge or Frobenius covariance penalties (achieving effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) = 19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} vs.\ $11.6$ for single-horizon baselines).

\noindent\emph{Reynolds Fluctuation Gram Matrix and Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration:}
Physically, nominal background fluctuations disperse energy across multiple uncorrelated sub-horizons, whereas an emerging fault wavepacket concentrates fluctuation energy into a single dominant rank-1 directional mode across horizons. To formalize this distinction, let $\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ stack the $M$ prediction residuals $\mathbf{e}_m = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}$. We fit the empirical shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ \cite{ledoit2004well} and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows to obtain Mahalanobis-whitened residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{e}} = \mathbf{\Omega}(\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{E}) - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$, reshaped into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = [\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_1, \dots, \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Applying Reynolds decomposition across the $M$ horizons separates $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ into its horizon-mean transport $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuations $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. From the $M \times M$ fluctuation Gram matrix $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ with ordered eigenvalues $0 \le \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$, we define the Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio \cite{lieb1976inequalities} of order $\gamma = 1$:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2} + \epsilon_{\text{LT}}}, \quad \epsilon_{\text{LT}} = 10^{-12}.
    \label{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} establishes the exact algebraic bounds and radial scale invariance of $R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$.

\begin{proposition}[Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration Bounds, Centered Gram Rank Deficiency, and Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathbf{E}' = (\mathbf{I}_M - M^{-1}\mathbf{1}_M\mathbf{1}_M^\top)\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ denote the Reynolds-centered multi-horizon fluctuation matrix for $M \ge 2$ and $\gamma > -1/2$. In the exact limit $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$ for any non-zero fluctuation $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~\emph{Centered Gram nullspace and effective rank $M-1$:} Because $\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$, the constant vector $\mathbf{1}_M$ lies in $\ker(\mathbf{G})$, forcing $\lambda_1(\mathbf{G}) \equiv 0$ identically so that $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{G}) \le \min(M-1, D)$.
(ii)~\emph{Sharp isotropy lower bound and soliton upper bound:} The concentration ratio satisfies the tight two-sided inequality
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{M - 1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1,
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
where the lower bound $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) is attained if and only if fluctuation energy is distributed isotropically across $M-1$ orthogonal sub-horizon modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$), and the upper bound $1$ is attained if and only if $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$ ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0 < \lambda_M$), corresponding to a coherent rank-1 soliton wavepacket.
(iii)~\emph{Exact radial scale invariance:} For any scalar $c > 0$, $R_{\text{LT}}(c\mathbf{E}') = R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$, rendering $R_{\text{LT}}$ invariant to homogeneous amplitude rescaling.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction, $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{E}'(\mathbf{E}'^\top\mathbf{1}_M) = \mathbf{0}$, so $\lambda_1 = 0$ is always an eigenvalue of $\mathbf{G} \succeq \mathbf{0}$, leaving at most $M-1$ positive eigenvalues $\lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ with $\lambda_M > 0$ since $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$. Because $x \mapsto x^{\gamma+1/2}$ is strictly increasing on $x \ge 0$ for $\gamma > -1/2$, $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le \lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$ for all $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$. Summing over the $M-1$ remaining modes gives $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$. Dividing through by $\sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$ yields (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}), with equality on the left iff $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M$ and on the right iff $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0$. Finally, scaling $\mathbf{E}' \mapsto c\mathbf{E}'$ maps $\mathbf{G} \mapsto c^2\mathbf{G}$ and $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \mapsto c^{2\gamma+1}\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$, canceling in numerator and denominator of (\ref{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}) as $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, windows are scored at stride $1$. Let $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denote the dimension-agnostic coordinate saliency gate defined in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). Unconstrained TS-JEPA scores the single-horizon residual $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ via $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$. In Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, we combine the joint $MD$-dimensional whitened residual gate $g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))$ with the peak sub-horizon gate $\max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))$ into the multi-horizon envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, and modulate it by a one-sided median absolute deviation (MAD)-standardized soliton concentration gain:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(g_{MD}\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t)\bigr)^2 + \max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t)\bigr)^2\Bigr)}, \label{eq:soliton_env} \\
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{Sol-Rey}}(t) &= \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\Bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{sol}}\max\bigl(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t))\bigr)\Bigr), \label{eq:soliton_score}
\end{align}
where $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$ and $z_{\text{LT}}(t) = (R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}'(t)) - m_{\text{LT}}) / (1.4826\,\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}})$ standardizes $R_{\text{LT}}$ against its nominal training median $m_{\text{LT}}$ and MAD $\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}}$. Because $\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}))$ is one-sided, nominal windows with below-median spectral concentration ($z_{\text{LT}} \le 0$) receive zero amplification. Setting $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0$ recovers the unmodulated multi-horizon envelope control $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ (Soliton-Reynolds ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$)).

\noindent\emph{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
We formalize online evaluation under three criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, the detector observes only $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$; neither encoder accesses observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \emph{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ summarizes suspect observations $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$ spanning sample indices $[t, t+S-1]$, score $s_t$ is emitted at the trailing edge $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3), creating a structural emission lag of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO, and $63\,\text{h}$ on hourly Metro). This lag is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$ from onset $t^*$ to the first alarm $t_{\text{alarm}} \ge t^*$. Shortening the horizon to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates emission latency for rapid tripwires (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} \emph{Causal trailing attribution} assigns $s_t$ only to $\tau = t + S - 1$ for zero-lookahead streaming. \emph{Retrospective buffered smear attribution} averages $s_t$ across all suspect timestamps $[t, t+S-1]$, introducing fixed-lag smoothing. Both regimes are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and Scale Equivariance}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
To amplify sparse departures in high-dimensional representations while mitigating channel drowning, we define the coordinate saliency gate $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for any vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    g_d(\mathbf{v}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{d \sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}, \\
    w_i &= \frac{\exp\bigl(|v_i| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^d \exp\bigl(|v_j| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}, \quad \sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau_g = 0.5$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. When $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$, $g_d(\mathbf{0}) = 0$. For non-zero $\mathbf{v}$, rescaling $\mathbf{v} \mapsto c\mathbf{v}$ with $c > 0$ yields $\sigma_{c\mathbf{v}} = c\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon = c\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} + (1-c)\epsilon$. In the operational regime where $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 \gg \epsilon$, the softmax weights $w_i$ are scale-invariant up to an $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/\|\mathbf{v}\|_2)$ perturbation, satisfying approximate degree-1 homogeneity $g_d(c\mathbf{v}) \approx c\,g_d(\mathbf{v})$ and recovering exact degree-1 homogeneity in the limit $\epsilon \to 0^+$. Input channels use uniform weights in the deployed pipeline (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
For multivariate streams ($K > 1$), a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Each projected channel is split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point rFFT grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax scale router. Because smooth Fourier shells overlap at band edges ($\sum \Psi_j \equiv 1$ rather than $\sum \Psi_j^2 \equiv 1$), this acts as a multiscale front-end rather than an orthogonal Parseval wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ from nominal calibration data alone, SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits exceedances $Y = \mathcal{S}(t) - u > 0$ above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) \cite{balkema1974residual} with shape $\xi$ and scale $\sigma$ via Grimshaw maximum-likelihood estimation \cite{grimshaw1993computing} with bounded L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10]$). Inverting the GPD tail at target risk $q = 10^{-3}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = \begin{cases}
    u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right], & |\xi| \ge 10^{-5}, \\
    u - \sigma \ln\left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right), & |\xi| < 10^{-5},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:spot_threshold}
\end{equation}
where the lower branch is the continuous exponential-tail limit as $\xi \to 0$, evaluated directly to avoid division by zero. Here $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $N_u$ count post-attribution score timestamps on the calibration block. Primary tables use standard SPOT; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} compares parametric SPOT against non-parametric empirical quantiles, runs-declustered GPD fitting, and rolling thresholds.

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's raw nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$. Standardization parameters and neural weights are fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$ windows alone, guaranteeing zero raw-observation or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$.

Within $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$), sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2). Under causal trailing attribution, the first $W - 1 = 319$ observations provide context warm-up, yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid score timestamps $v[0 : M_{\text{val}})$. Splitting $v$ at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $v_{\text{cal}} = v[0 : H)$ (used to fit the SPOT threshold at $u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $v_{\text{hold}} = v[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, excluding $S=64$ intermediate windows $v[H : H+S)$. Because suspect target windows have length $S=64$, the last calibration target and first evaluation target are $100\%$ disjoint. The first $C=256$ evaluation steps share passive historical context with calibration observations, mirroring online streaming where past nominal history forms context for current predictions. A full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $v[H : H+320)$ across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) confirms that shared passive context does not bias calibration: stream-macro causal FPR shifts only from $0.33\%$ to $0.36\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $3.02\%$ to $3.24\%$ for TS-JEPA, versus $12.15\%$ to $13.97\%$ for TimesNet and $9.05\%$ to $9.73\%$ for TranAD. All primary tables use fixed standard SPOT thresholds during testing.

\paragraph{Independent Statistical Units, Multi-Seed Replication, and Equivalence Bounds}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are evaluated across five random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$); the 14 GHL streams ($>10^6$ observations each) and ablation controls use three seeds ($\{7, 42, 2024\}$). To prevent pseudoreplication, seeds are averaged within each stream first ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$), stream-level paired tests treat the telemetry stream ($n=33$ primary or $n=19$ non-GHL) as the unit of analysis, and domain-level tests treat the dataset family ($N=7$) as the independent cluster:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and false-discovery-rate adjustment:} The Wilcoxon signed-rank test evaluates whether the median paired difference across streams is zero without Gaussian assumptions. We adjust $p$-values across multiple comparisons via the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure \cite{benjamini1995controlling} ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$), report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples), and cross-check at the dataset-cluster level ($N=7$) via exact sign-flip permutation tests and zero-tuning LODO sensitivity.
    \item \emph{Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for fixed-backbone regularizer equivalence:} We use TOST \cite{schuirmann1987comparison} to test whether the multiplicative soliton and manifold gain in (\ref{eq:soliton_score}) alters aggregate Point-F1 relative to the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ on the identical trained backbone ($n=19$ streams). TOST tests $H_{01}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \le -\Delta$ ($p_{\text{lower}}$) and $H_{02}: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \ge +\Delta$ ($p_{\text{upper}}$), establishing equivalence when $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}) < 0.05$ across $\Delta \in \{\pm 0.015, \pm 0.020, \pm 0.030\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \emph{Distributional tail and drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} On nominal windows we measure: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, distinguishing heavy-tailed \emph{leptokurtic} ($\gamma_2 > 0$) from light-tailed \emph{platykurtic} ($\gamma_2 < 0$) residuals; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, quantifying distributional shift between calibration ($F_{\text{cal}}$) and held-out nominal ($F_{\text{hold}}$) blocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate models across three tiers. First, \emph{primary calibration and accuracy outcomes} comprise realized held-out nominal FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ relative to design risk $q = 10^{-3}$, unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC. Second, \emph{operating characteristics} evaluate event recall across deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) and across post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). Third, \emph{secondary operational endpoints} report unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm baseline $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$.

\subsection{Ablation 1: Multi-Horizon Whitened Envelope vs.\ Soliton Concentration Modulation}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
What actually drives the gains of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA over single-horizon TS-JEPA? To separate the core representation from post-hoc scoring multipliers on the exact same trained network, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) compare single-horizon TS-JEPA against the unmodulated multi-horizon envelope control ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$, which outputs $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ directly) and a sweep across $\alpha_{\text{sol}} \in [0.03, 0.24]$ on all 19 non-GHL streams. Replacing single-horizon TS-JEPA with the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ produces a clear shift in false-alarm control and low-FPR sensitivity (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a), left separator): buffered held-out FPR drops from $9.3\%$ to $8.3\%$, strictly causal held-out FPR drops four-fold ($2.4\% \to 0.6\%$), calibration-to-holdout drift falls ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.183$), and causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ rises from $0.395$ to $0.578$ while preserving buffered PR-AUC ($0.330$ vs.\ $0.331$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).

Across the modulation sweep $\alpha_{\text{sol}} \in [0, 0.24]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a)), adding the post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration gain on top of $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ yields a modest false-alarm reduction and a null aggregate F1/PR-AUC response. At the deployed weight $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$, buffered held-out FPR decreases from $8.3\%$ to $7.8\%$ (paired difference $-0.5\%$, $95\%$ CI $[-1.0\%, -0.1\%]$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.026$) and causal event recall rises from $0.439$ to $0.478$, while causal FPR ($0.6\%$ vs.\ $0.6\%$) and KS drift ($0.183$ vs.\ $0.179$) remain nearly identical. Buffered Point-F1 ($0.148$ vs.\ $0.149$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.472$) and PR-AUC ($0.331$ vs.\ $0.330$) satisfy TOST equivalence at both $\Delta = \pm 0.030$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 1.1{\times}10^{-7}$) and $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$), and auxiliary spectral operator regularizers during training similarly leave downstream recall and FPR flat. The primary gains come from multi-depth TCN tapping, barycenter centering, and multi-horizon Mahalanobis whitening rather than post-hoc soliton modulation.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Quiet Faults, Input Gating, and Hybrid Monitoring}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Why do normalized latent encoders miss quiet single-channel faults? When one sensor out of $K$ freezes at a nominal value while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the frozen channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$. LayerNorm then rescales the embedding back onto the $\sqrt{D}$ sphere using the variance of the remaining $K-1$ active channels, masking the single-sensor freeze. Weighting input channels by context variance prior to encoding lowers macro Point-F1 across four multivariate benchmark streams compared to uniform channel weighting ($0.077$ vs.\ $0.118$). The deployed pipeline therefore retains uniform input encoding. Across six fault classes injected into individual channels on six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), uniform TS-JEPA detects $27.8\%$ of abrupt $+5\sigma$ spikes ($30.6\%$ with context-variance gating), but on persistent quiet faults (constant or mid-range flatlines, stuck sensors, and zero dropouts) and low-amplitude steps it reaches only $19.4\%$ event recall and $2.0\%\text{--}3.0\%$ point recall ($4.3\%\text{--}6.8\%$ under variance or MAD input gating). Simple observation-space monitors catch these complementary failures: a maximum channel deviation monitor (tracking the largest standardized deviation from the training median and MAD across channels) reaches $25.0\%\text{--}30.6\%$ event recall at $14.1\%$ FPR, and a rolling variance-collapse monitor (tracking how far each channel's 32-step trailing standard deviation drops below its nominal median) achieves $26.0\%\text{--}38.9\%$ event recall at $14.9\%$ FPR. Taking the logical union of TS-JEPA alarms and the rolling variance-collapse monitor raises point recall to $20.2\%\text{--}31.8\%$ and event recall to $41.7\%\text{--}50.0\%$ across all six fault classes, trading $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR ($1.9\% \to 17.0\%$) for quiet-fault coverage.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) trains TS-JEPA with the identical 3-layer causal TCN encoder across five streams, comparing the 4-shell Fourier filterbank against a standard linear convolutional stem. Enabling the dyadic front-end raises macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers validation residual kurtosis ($1.409 \to 1.069$), while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to conservative threshold tightening on SMAP P-3 ($0.301 \to 0.115$). Frequency-shell splitting thus improves score ranking and tail conditioning rather than uniformly raising thresholded F1.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 12 model arms across all 19 non-GHL streams under identical chronological splits, training budgets, and matched parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster vs.\ TCN Obs.\ Autoencoder):} Pairing the exact same causal dilated TCN encoder with a multilayer perceptron decoder to either forecast the 64-step target window or reconstruct the full 320-step window in raw sensor space leaves residual kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 6.10$ vs.\ $6.24$), calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$ vs.\ $0.445$), and causal FPR ($24.7\%$ vs.\ $24.5\%$) virtually unchanged. Switching from reconstruction to forecasting in observation space does not resolve threshold instability.
    \item \emph{LayerNorm bottleneck and saliency gating in observation space (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster (LN)):} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck, target-window LayerNorm, and the coordinate saliency gate to the observation forecaster ($307\text{k}$ parameters) compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and cuts causal FPR in half ($11.7\%$). Because predictions still target raw sensor waveforms, however, calibration-to-holdout drift remains high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and causal Point-F1 drops to $0.084$.
    \item \emph{Learned latent target projection and multi-horizon whitening (TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA):} Moving the prediction target from raw observations into the learned $D=32$ latent space halves calibration-to-holdout drift relative to the bounded observation forecaster ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), cuts causal FPR to $2.4\%$ and $0.6\%$, and raises buffered PR-AUC from $0.257$ to $0.331$.
    \item \emph{Nonlinear TCN encoding vs.\ linear PCA subspace and tree-based isolation (PCA Linear Forecaster (LN) and Isolation Forest):} Projecting context and target windows onto 32 principal components with a ridge-regression predictor, LayerNorm, and the coordinate saliency gate achieves $2.1\%$ causal FPR via score bounding, but its causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ ($0.104$ buffered; Isolation Forest reaches $0.149$ causal event recall). Learned nonlinear temporal features are therefore required to separate anomalies from nominal background.
    \item \emph{Future prediction vs.\ context autoencoding, EMA target stability, and $\ell_2$ scoring (JEPA Context Autoencoder, JEPA Hard-Target Copy, and TS-JEPA ($\ell_2$ Scoring)):} Training the JEPA predictor to reconstruct the context window's own target embedding keeps drift and false alarms low ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, causal FPR $1.7\%$) but reduces causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.161$) and PR-AUC ($0.235$ vs.\ $0.278$), showing that forecasting future windows improves discriminability. Copying online weights directly to the target encoder each step ($\zeta = 0$) reaches $0.9\%$ causal FPR and $0.235$ buffered Point-F1, and replacing the saliency gate with a plain Euclidean norm yields $1.8\%$ causal FPR and $0.156$ causal Point-F1, confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent architecture rather than the scoring gate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, while Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieves the lowest held-out FPR across both attribution regimes ($0.6\%$ causal, $7.8\%$ buffered), matches TS-JEPA on buffered PR-AUC ($0.331$), and leads in causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.562$ at $f^* = 0.01$).

\noindent\emph{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring cuts held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three-fold under buffered attribution: $9.3\%$ for TS-JEPA and $7.8\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, versus $30.0\%$ for TimesNet ($p = 0.045$) and $30.7\%$ for TranAD ($p = 0.012$). Under strictly causal streaming, the false-alarm reduction widens to ten- to forty-fold: $2.4\%$ for TS-JEPA and $0.6\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, compared to $24.3\%$ for TimesNet ($p = 0.010$) and $22.2\%$ for TranAD ($p = 0.010$). This advantage holds across all 33 streams ($0.38\%$ causal stream-macro FPR for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $1.51\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $16.11\%$ and $14.23\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, leaving one dataset out at a time keeps causal FPR at $0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ ($4.2\%\text{--}7.0\%$ buffered) for JEPAs versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ causal ($8.4\%\text{--}22.1\%$ buffered) for reconstructors. In Panel~(c), Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieves the lowest causal held-out FPR on every multi-stream family ($0.8\%$ Daphnet, $0.1\%$ GHL, $0.4\%$ MSL, $0.6\%$ SMAP vs.\ $5.6\%\text{--}36.0\%$ for baselines).

\noindent\emph{Multi-Horizon Ranking Gains vs.\ Unadjusted Point-F1 Trade-offs:} Under buffered attribution on the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieve the highest threshold-free PR-AUC ($0.331$ and $0.331$, with $0.330$ for $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$, vs.\ $0.329$ for TimesNet and $0.287$ for TranAD). Buffered Point-F1 ($0.148$ and $0.149$, vs.\ $0.206$ for TS-JEPA) and event recall ($0.430$ and $0.462$) exceed TranAD ($0.130$ F1) at one-quarter the false-alarm rate ($7.8\%$ vs.\ $30.7\%$). Across datasets (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), SMAP exceeds the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.267$; PR-AUC $0.576$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and Genesis reaches $0.012$ F1 (vs.\ $0.000$). Conversely, on GHL ($\pi=0.013$), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), illustrating that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\emph{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (combining TS-JEPA with a rolling variance-collapse monitor) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each component of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA modifies performance relative to TS-JEPA. First, \emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and barycenter centering} remove common-mode baseline shift, cutting KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.179$) and reducing the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR four-fold ($71.3\% \to 18.7\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Second, \emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and Mahalanobis whitening} ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$) capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, cutting buffered FPR ($9.3\% \to 8.3\%$) and causal FPR ($2.4\% \to 0.6\%$) while lifting causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ ($0.395 \to 0.578$). Third, \emph{post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) lowers buffered FPR slightly ($8.3\% \to 7.8\%$, $p = 0.026$) and raises causal event recall ($0.439 \to 0.478$), but yields a null aggregate Point-F1 and PR-AUC effect on the fixed backbone ($0.148$ vs.\ $0.149$ F1, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 1.1{\times}10^{-7}$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.472$; $0.331$ vs.\ $0.330$ PR-AUC), confirming that the multi-horizon whitened representation drives the primary gains.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows how zero-lookahead streaming shifts the operating threshold. In causal mode, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA preserves competitive threshold-free ranking (PR-AUC $0.259$ vs.\ $0.278$ for TS-JEPA and $0.248$ for TranAD). Without 64-step window averaging, however, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals have a heavier right tail ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$) than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$) or single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\gamma_2 = 1.33$). When SPOT extrapolates that tail to $q=10^{-3}$ in (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}), it places $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ high on the causal score distribution. Consequently, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA fires quietly on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.4\%$ of test steps (true-positive rate (TPR) $4.0\%$, held-out FPR $0.6\%$), compared to $10.8\%$ for TS-JEPA (TPR $16.6\%$, held-out FPR $2.4\%$) and $24.2\%$ for TimesNet (TPR $34.0\%$, held-out FPR $24.3\%$). Because it flags only the sharpest peak steps inside each anomaly segment rather than staying on throughout the event, its causal Point-F1 drops ($0.050$ vs.\ $0.148$ buffered, and $0.161$ for TS-JEPA) even though it catches $47.8\%$ of anomaly events ($\Delta t = 154.0$).

To check whether $47.8\%$ event recall could arise by chance on long anomaly segments ($111$ events with median length $L = 139$ and mean $348.1$ across the 18 non-SWAN streams), Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} (Note) compares each detector against an analytical rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$. At Soliton-Reynolds JEPA's held-out nominal rate ($p = \text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0056$), random firing catches only $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.272$ of events (vs.\ $0.478$ and $154.0$ actual). At its overall test firing rate ($\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 0.024$), unclustered Bernoulli trials reach $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.491$ because real model alarms cluster inside detected events rather than firing independently. For TimesNet ($p_{\text{hold}} = 0.243$), independent random firing reaches $0.600$ ($0.801$ at $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}}$), exceeding TimesNet's actual $0.588$. Relaxing the static threshold to the empirical $p_{99.5}$ calibration quantile raises Soliton-Reynolds JEPA's causal event recall to $0.609$ at $1.26\%$ held-out FPR ($74.4$-step delay).

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), TS-JEPA holds causal FPR to $0.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ and $3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$ for TimesNet), comparable Point-F1 at $S=1$ ($0.028$ vs.\ $0.025$), and fast detection ($\Delta t = 57.8$ and $85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases across $\{16, 32, 64\}$, TimesNet causal FPR escalates nearly nine-fold ($6.1\% \to 9.8\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Consequently, $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP, MSL, and GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$). Low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, such as hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) and rapid tripwires instead require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.3\%\text{--}11\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we compare models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.752$) at lower false-alarm rates than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall).
    \item \emph{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models, at $f^* = 0.01$ TS-JEPA ($0.395$) and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA ($0.562$ causal, $0.578$ for $\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$) substantially increase event recall compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-FPR regime ($f^* \le 0.05$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample across all streams and attribution modes. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $1.51\%$ for TS-JEPA ($15\times q$) and $0.38\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA ($4\times q$), compared to $16.11\%$ for TimesNet ($161\times q$) and $14.23\%$ for TranAD ($142\times q$). Replacing parametric SPOT with a non-parametric empirical 99.5th percentile ($p_{99.5}$) yields nearly identical results ($0.9\%$ causal FPR for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $2.3\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $15.4\%\text{--}17.5\%$ for reconstructors), demonstrating that reconstructor false-alarm flooding is not an artifact of GPD tail fitting. Under $S=64$ buffered attribution, however, smearing each window score across 64 consecutive steps dilates every isolated exceedance into a 64-step cluster, inflating buffered FPR to $4.6\%\text{--}5.5\%$ ($46\text{--}55\times q$) for JEPAs and $19.6\%\text{--}19.9\%$ ($196\text{--}199\times q$) for reconstructors under both rules. Static SPOT is therefore near-calibrated for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA only in strictly causal mode. Two diagnostics explain why causal thresholds hold on latent residuals:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} To separate inter-block regime drift from serial correlation and GPD tail-fit error, we pool nominal calibration and evaluation scores $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ across all $19$ non-GHL streams and \emph{refit} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) under three splits: chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, which removes macro-block drift while preserving intra-block serial correlation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$). Once temporal structure is removed by i.i.d.\ shuffling, every model achieves low false-alarm rates near the $0.1\%$ target ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} = 0.0020$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and $0.0033\text{--}0.0035$ for TS-JEPA, TimesNet, and TranAD). In chronological deployment, inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in single-horizon TS-JEPA ($0.0229 \to 0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.2103\text{--}0.2302 \to 0.0427\text{--}0.0464$). In barycenter-centered Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, by contrast, the inter-block drift share drops four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.0056$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.0047$, difference $+0.0009$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.002, +0.004]$).
    \item \emph{GPD tail stability, declustering, and rolling adaptation:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$). Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in only $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Applying Ferro--Segers runs declustering ($r=5$) \cite{ferro2003inference} prior to GPD fitting trims stationary causal FPR from $0.25\%$ to $0.20\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA and from $0.72\%$ to $0.44\%$ for TS-JEPA. Under discrete step, variance, and regime shifts across 16 streams, adaptive rolling quantiles hold causal TS-JEPA FPR to $0.06\%\text{--}0.40\%$ (vs.\ $0.8\%\text{--}3.0\%$ for TimesNet), whereas continuous monotonic mean ramps defeat trailing rolling windows ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$ FPR across all models).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a chronological held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Multi-Horizon Whitening:} Matched-encoder ablations decouple score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks bound residual kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in TS-JEPA), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$ with $0.6\%$ causal FPR. On the fixed backbone, post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation satisfies TOST equivalence ($\Delta = \pm 0.020$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$), and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag.
    \item \emph{Relative False-Alarm Control and Hybrid Quiet-Fault Recovery:} Latent scoring lowers held-out false alarms three- to four-fold under buffered attribution and ten- to forty-fold causally. Soliton-Reynolds JEPA matches TS-JEPA on buffered PR-AUC ($0.331$) and leads in causal matched-FPR event recall ($f^* = 0.01$: $0.562$ vs.\ $0.217$ for TimesNet), trading lower causal SPOT Point-F1 ($0.050$ at a $2.4\%$ test alert rate vs.\ $0.161$ at $10.8\%$ for TS-JEPA) for $0.6\%$ held-out FPR, while a hybrid variance monitor recovers quiet single-channel faults ($0.42\text{--}0.50$ event recall).
    \item \emph{Calibration Drift and Adaptive Thresholding:} Static SPOT overshoots $q = 10^{-3}$ by $46\text{--}55\times$ under $S=64$ buffered smearing and $15\times$ causally for TS-JEPA, whereas barycenter-whitened Soliton-Reynolds JEPA reduces causal overshoot to $4\times$ ($0.38\%$ stream-macro FPR across 33 streams) and cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold ($71.3\% \to 18.7\%$). Runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles narrow the remaining gap across step and variance shifts, motivating regime-conditioned conformal calibration.
\end{enumerate}
