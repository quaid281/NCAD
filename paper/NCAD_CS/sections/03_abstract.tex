\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still sum many prediction errors into one anomaly score, so one or two coordinates that drift between training and calibration can dominate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces the scalar score and its tail-fitted threshold. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their mean, spread, and endpoint difference), and each statistic receives its own breaker calibrated on healthy data with a drift guard. Surgical weight-space quenching on healthy calibration windows projects mean drift out of the predictor biases and tempers rogue precision-matrix eigenvalues before scoring. On 19 primary streams, this reaches 0.262 buffered point-wise F1, 1.6 percent causal false-alarm rate, and a 59.6-step detection delay. Across 162 streams spanning 15 datasets, it cuts causal false alarms by 8.0 percent relative (median held-out false-alarm rate 1.0 percent and test false-alarm rate 1.6 percent). On 118 streams never used to tune the panel, a three-horizon variant raises causal point-wise F1 from 0.072 to 0.083 for scalar JEPAs to 0.143 and event recall from 51 to 73 percent. The panel needs roughly a thousand calibration steps and misses slow ramps and most single-channel sensor freezes.
\end{abstract}
