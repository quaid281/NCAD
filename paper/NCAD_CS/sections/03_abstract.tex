\begin{abstract}
Reconstructive anomaly detectors score raw observations, making them sensitive to benign sensor variation. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) forecast latent summaries, but a scalar residual threshold can be distorted by drifting coordinates. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA with an independently calibrated 224-bit circuit-breaker panel. Its 32 coordinates yield four horizon-error magnitudes plus horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference. Optional post-training quenching modifies selected predictor rows and residual-calibration buffers using nominal data. On a separately trained 19-stream development benchmark, the quenched panel achieves buffered point-wise F1 of 0.262 and causal false-alarm rate of 1.6 percent, with 59.6-step detection delay. In a repeat 19-stream ablation, the unquenched panel achieves causal F1 of 0.071 versus 0.013 for scalar scoring; a 160-dial subset reaches 0.098. These runs are not paired. On five selected streams, horizons of one to 64 steps show panel gains over scalar scoring, including zero structural reporting lag. Across 118 untouched held-out streams, the unquenched three-horizon panel raises causal F1 from 0.083 to 0.143 and event recall from 51 to 73 percent. In a matched 162-stream four-horizon comparison, quenching cuts nominal causal false alarms by 8.2 percent relative while slightly lowering causal F1. Threshold calibration depends on quantile and nominal history; slow ramps and single-sensor faults remain limitations.
\end{abstract}
