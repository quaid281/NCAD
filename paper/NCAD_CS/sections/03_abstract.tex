\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We investigate Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) scoring predictive discrepancies in a low-dimensional latent space, alongside an Operator-Entropy variant (OpEntropy JEPA) whose linear Koopman operator incorporates spectral entropy and a shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier, coordinate saliency gating, and a multiscale dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, latent predictive scoring suppresses false alarms three- to four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (7.4\% to 10.1\% nominal false-positive rate versus 30.0\% for TimesNet and 30.7\% for TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.1\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%). This false-alarm reduction does not establish general detection superiority: point F1, precision-recall ranking, and event recall remain comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled mechanism ablations isolate two drivers: low-dimensional LayerNorm bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, while learned latent projections reduce inter-block distribution drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. Factorial sweeps confirm that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank and near-unit condition numbers without improving detection, confirming statistical equivalence to unconstrained TS-JEPA within a 0.05 F1 margin. Static extreme-value calibration overshoots its 0.1\% design risk under deployment shift, with inter-block drift accounting for 84\% to 96\% of causal overshoot under exchangeable permutation. Latent residual conditioning suits supervisory multi-sensor monitoring, whereas quiet flatlines and baseline ramps require complementary variance monitors or adaptive recalibration.
\end{abstract}
