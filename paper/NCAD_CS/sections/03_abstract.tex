\begin{abstract}
Automated threshold calibration frequently breaks down when multi-sensor telemetry score distributions shift between nominal calibration and operational deployment. We investigate false-alarm suppression, score geometry, and threshold transfer under chronological held-out calibration across 33 telemetry streams from seven datasets, with five-seed controls on a 19-stream non-GHL cohort. The primary demonstrated result is that latent predictive scoring substantially suppresses false alarms under static calibration compared with observation-space reconstruction baselines. However, this conservatism does not establish superior anomaly detection: paired differences in point F1 and continuous precision-recall area remain statistically inconclusive, and causal event-recall curves matched on realized false-positive rates cover sparse common support without showing aggregate advantage. Bounded linear and temporal convolutional controls confirm that bounded score geometry compresses residual tails, yet tail lightness alone does not guarantee out-of-sample threshold transfer. Static peaks-over-threshold calibration systematically overshoots nominal design risk under chronological deployment. Under an exchangeable permutation protocol, inter-block distribution drift accounts for 84\% to 96\% of this calibration overshoot, whereas serial autocorrelation and sample scarcity are secondary factors. Three physics-inspired regularizers yield no reliable aggregate detection gain, confirming formal statistical equivalence to unconstrained latent prediction within a practical margin. These findings indicate that latent prediction functions primarily as a conservative scoring filter rather than an intrinsically superior detector, highlighting the need to evaluate detectors at matched realized false-positive rates.
\end{abstract}
