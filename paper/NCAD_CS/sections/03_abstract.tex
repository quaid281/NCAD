\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag faults quickly while staying quiet during healthy operation, yet waveform reconstructors fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries, though single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one shared threshold. We replace scalar scoring with coordinate circuit breakers. Each coordinate's horizon errors and mean drift receive healthy tripwires, and an alarm fires when a healthy-calibrated quorum trips. Each stream fits independently on its own healthy prefix. Rather than transferring pretrained weights across assets, this design transfers a frozen architecture and calibration rule across sensor suites. On 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets, our 128-breaker Laminar-JEPA-Lite detector lifts causal event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA and cuts end-to-end alarm latency from 133.5 to 69.0 steps. Latency includes a 63-step window fill lag, while the Point-F1 shift (0.076 to 0.086) stays uncertain across datasets. Under the same protocol, TimesNet and TranAD incur 24.6 to 25.6 percent mean false-positive rates versus 6.4 percent mean (0.93 percent median) for our panel. Autonomous deployment uses two-stage acceptance (52 of 118 accepted; 66 fallback) with quarantined mode-confirmed recalibration.
\end{abstract}
