\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry is central to operational safety across aerospace systems, industrial processes, cyber-physical infrastructure, and smart environments. While reconstructive architectures such as TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive continuous ranking performance (PR-AUC $0.2585$--$0.2612$), their observation-space residuals destabilize automated streaming threshold calibration, generating over $1.05\times 10^6$ false positives. To address this calibration failure, we evaluate Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) that forecast dynamics in regularized latent representation space. We introduce three physics-informed inductive regularizers grounded in dynamical systems theory, continuum mechanics, and field theory: 
(1) \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, which parameterizes a latent Koopman transition matrix and regularizes dynamic spectral entropy to prevent modal collapse; 
(2) \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, which adapts hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition to align second-order cross-channel fluctuation covariances; and 
(3) \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA}, which parameterizes latent trajectory transitions via an irrotational velocity potential ($\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$). 
To handle heterogeneous sensor arrays, the framework incorporates coordinate saliency gating for active channel isolation, dyadic wavelet shells for multi-scale frequency decomposition, and kurtosis-adapted Extreme Value Theory (EVT) for streaming threshold calibration. Evaluated on a matched benchmark of 45 continuous telemetry channels across 11 datasets, latent prediction suppresses false alarms while capturing true physical anomalies. Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves the highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.2066$, a +43.2\% relative improvement over TimesNet and +28.4\% over TranAD) and is the only architecture to exceed the analytical All-Positive baseline ($0.1893$). In Pooled Micro Point-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA achieves $0.3458$ ($3.2\times$ higher than TranAD and $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet) while reducing false positives by 8.0-fold against TimesNet ($130{,}237$ vs $1{,}047{,}144$) and 3.5-fold against TranAD ($455{,}128$). These results show that latent predictive architectures yield better-conditioned residual distributions that substantially mitigate streaming threshold calibration collapse, with physical inductive priors providing targeted performance gains in specialized dynamical regimes.
\end{abstract}
