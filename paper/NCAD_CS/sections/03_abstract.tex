\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in complex multi-sensor time series is a critical capability across aerospace, cyber-physical systems, and industrial IoT. However, prevailing paradigms suffer from fundamental limitations: reconstructive models (e.g., Autoencoders, Masked Transformers) over-allocate capacity to high-frequency sensor noise rather than underlying system dynamics; contrastive methods rely on hand-crafted synthetic anomaly injections that warp physical state spaces; and heuristic thresholding schemes frequently suffer from zero-F1 detection collapse. To address these challenges, we introduce \textbf{TS-JEPA} (Time-Series Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture), a self-supervised framework that reformulates anomaly detection as non-contrastive predictive modeling in continuous representation space. TS-JEPA trains a dilated causal Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) context encoder to predict future latent representations generated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder via a multi-component VICReg (Invariance, Variance hinge, Covariance decorrelation) objective, entirely avoiding point-level signal reconstruction and artificial negative sampling. To robustly detect subtle and sustained physical deviations without false alarm proliferation, we introduce a \textbf{Covariance-Whitened (Mahalanobis) Discrepancy Metric} that normalizes prediction residuals against the empirical precision tensor of nominal transitions, coupled with \textbf{Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT)} tail calibration for asymptotically grounded threshold estimation. We evaluate TS-JEPA across 26 benchmark channels spanning five diverse physical domains: NASA SMAP spacecraft telemetry, Exathlon distributed cloud streaming logs, room-occupancy environmental IoT, Daphnet biomedical gait dynamics, and OPPORTUNITY high-dimensional wearables. TS-JEPA outperforms baseline contrastive and reconstructive architectures across every domain without exception, achieving a \textbf{+35.6\% grand macro PA-F1 improvement} (0.3786 vs. 0.2793) and pushing individual complex channels above 0.90 F1. Phase-space attractor analyses confirm that TS-JEPA learns stable limit-cycle orbits for nominal physical dynamics while triggering immediate, high-magnitude discrepancy vectors upon anomalous dynamical breakdown.
\end{abstract}
