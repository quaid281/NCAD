\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space. Because sensor telemetry contains unforecastable jitter and scale disparities, observation-space residuals develop heavy tails that trigger severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid reconstructing raw noise by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows. We investigate how latent prediction conditions residual distributions under regime shift. Specifically, we compare a single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA) against Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, a multi-horizon architecture that taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context barycenters, whitens multi-horizon prediction errors, and scores centered sub-horizon fluctuations via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio. Across continuous physical telemetry benchmarks calibrated on held-out nominal data with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT), latent scoring suppresses false alarms under both retrospective buffered attribution and strictly causal streaming while improving anomaly ranking and event recall. In addition, replacing scalar score aggregation with a 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel that monitors sub-horizon positions, Reynolds slow-drift transport, turbulent fluctuations, and right-edge convective acceleration with fast onset relays prevents isolated drifting coordinates from inflating the alarm threshold, nearly doubling causal point-wise F1 and cutting detection delay in half across 141 multivariate streams. Controlled factorial ablations also show that layer-normalization bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, learned latent projections and barycenter whitening curb calibration-to-holdout drift, short prediction horizons eliminate emission lag, and hybrid sensor-variance monitors recover quiet single-channel faults.
\end{abstract}
