\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in complex multivariate time-series is critical for operational safety across aerospace telemetry, cyber-physical infrastructure, industrial process loops, and biomedical monitoring. However, contemporary deep learning baselines suffer from fundamental theoretical pathologies: reconstructive autoencoders (e.g., TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) optimize pointwise residuals in observation space, causing fatal ``noise-drowning'' in high-dimensional telemetry; conversely, heuristic contrastive models risk manifold distortion and dimensional collapse. To transcend raw observation-space reconstruction, we propose a family of Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) centered around three foundational formulations grounded in dynamical systems theory, continuum mechanics, and field theory: 
(1) \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, which constructs an infinite-dimensional Koopman transition operator in latent space and detects anomalies via dynamic spectral entropy generation; 
(2) \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, which adapts Reynolds hydrodynamic decomposition to model cross-channel turbulent momentum transport and inter-sensor shear-stress divergence; and 
(3) \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA}, which constrains normal operational trajectories to conservative, irrotational Helmholtz-Hodge velocity potentials. 
To ensure robustness across heterogeneous sensor scales without altering the self-supervised loss landscape, we integrate internal Littlewood-Paley dyadic wavelet shells for multi-scale frequency decomposition, a parameter-free Coordinate Saliency Gate to prevent dimensional drowning on noisy channels, and Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) for distribution-free tail calibration. 
Evaluated across 2,650 model-channel executions spanning 19 real-world benchmark datasets (comprising NASA spacecraft, MIT-BIH cardiac electrophysiology, and server clusters), our Physics-Informed JEPA Triad comprehensively sweeps the top leaderboard positions, consistently outperforming published deep SOTA baselines. On the core multi-sensor benchmark, Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves a Dataset-Macro Point-F1 of $0.2066$ (+43.2\% over TimesNet and +28.4\% over TranAD) and Channel-Macro Point-F1 of $0.1743$, while Potential-Flow JEPA attains a dominant Pooled Micro Point-F1 of $0.3458$ (more than $3.2\times$ higher than TranAD and $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet) alongside an 8-fold reduction in false alarms ($45.98\%$ precision vs TimesNet's $4.42\%$). These results establish that physics-informed latent representation prediction provides a superior, noise-resilient foundation for multivariate time-series anomaly detection.
\end{abstract}
