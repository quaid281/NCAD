\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still sum many prediction errors into one anomaly score, so one or two coordinates that drift between training and calibration can dominate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces the scalar score and its tail-fitted threshold. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their mean, spread, and endpoint difference, borrowed by analogy from Reynolds decomposition), and each statistic receives its own breaker calibrated on healthy data with a drift guard. The breakers are statistically dependent, so alarm quorums are set from joint nominal trip counts. Across 141 multivariate telemetry streams from 12 datasets, the panel raises strictly causal point-wise F1 from 0.067 to 0.077 for scalar JEPAs to 0.139, lifts causal event recall to 72.7 percent, and more than halves detection delay. The panel does not cover every fault type: it misses slow process ramps in a gasoil heating loop and most single-channel sensor freezes, which a simple variance monitor only partly recovers.
\end{abstract}
