\begin{abstract}
We propose the Operator-Entropy Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (OpEntropy JEPA) for multivariate time-series anomaly detection in high-dimensional sensor telemetry. Deep reconstructive detectors minimize observation-space error, accumulating stochastic sensor noise that distorts residual distributions and causes severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. OpEntropy JEPA addresses this limitation by forecasting future telemetry states in an abstract representation space using a linear Koopman transition operator regularized by scale-free Von Neumann spectral entropy and a logarithmic Bregman energy barrier against subspace collapse. Under chronological held-out extreme-value calibration across 33 telemetry streams from seven physical datasets, OpEntropy JEPA suppresses false alarms by four- to five-fold compared with reconstructive baselines, lowering nominal false-positive rates from 30.0\% (TimesNet) and 30.7\% (TranAD) to 7.6\%--8.6\% (paired reductions exceeding 0.14, p < 0.01). To support continuous streaming, the framework integrates causal coordinate saliency gating and multiscale dyadic frequency shells. We evaluate threshold transfer under retrospective buffered and zero-lookahead causal streaming. An exchangeable permutation protocol establishes that inter-block distribution drift accounts for 84\% to 96\% of static calibration risk overshoot, dominating sample scarcity and serial autocorrelation. Across 19 multi-seed streams, point-level F1 and precision-recall ranking remain statistically comparable to reconstructive baselines, and physical regularizers confirm Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence to unconstrained latent prediction within a 0.05 margin (p < 0.006). OpEntropy JEPA provides a well-conditioned representation space that resolves false-alarm flooding under automated operational calibration.
\end{abstract}
