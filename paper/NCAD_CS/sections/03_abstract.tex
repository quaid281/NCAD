\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We study how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space under distribution shift, alongside an Operator-Entropy variant (OpEntropy JEPA) combining a linear Koopman operator, spectral entropy and shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier terms, coordinate saliency gating, and a multiscale dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, our primary finding is relative false-alarm suppression rather than general detection superiority or exact attainment of the 0.1\% nominal design risk: latent scoring reduces false-positive rates three- to four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (7.4\% to 10.1\% versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to twenty-fold under strictly causal streaming (1.1\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%), while point F1 and precision-recall ranking remain comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled ablations isolate two mechanisms: LayerNorm bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, while learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. On a fixed Koopman backbone, Two One-Sided Tests confirm that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank without altering point F1 within a 0.01 to 0.03 margin, while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to unconstrained TS-JEPA. Static thresholding still overshoots its 0.1\% design target out of sample (1.5\% to 2.0\% stream-macro causal and 5.5\% to 6.4\% buffered across 33 streams, or 15- to 64-fold); a three-stage refitted permutation decomposition estimates that inter-block shift accounts for 57\% to 64\% and serial dependence for 29\% to 35\% of excess causal overshoot.
\end{abstract}
