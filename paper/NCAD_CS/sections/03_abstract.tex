\begin{abstract}
Reconstructive time-series anomaly detectors score raw observations, making them sensitive to benign jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) forecast normalized latent summaries, yet collapsing coordinates into one scalar residual allows drifting coordinates to inflate thresholds. Targeting abrupt and intermediate-onset faults, we study two multi-horizon coordinate circuit-breaker architectures: Laminar-JEPA-Lite, our preferred deployment configuration, pairs a parallel three-horizon single-normalization predictor and channel-gated spectral splitting with 128 position-plus-drift switches and zero weight edits; and Laminar-JEPA, a four-horizon, 224-dial reference variant adding fluctuation, endpoint differences, 32 onset relays, and optional weight-space quenching. Laminar-JEPA-Lite cuts parameters by 35 to 52 percent and accelerates inference by 2.43 times. On the 33-stream development benchmark, Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite hold autonomous causal false-positive rate (FPR) at 1.3 and 1.0 percent versus 22.2 to 24.3 percent for reconstructors, reaching buffered point-wise F1 of 0.244 and 0.242 and seven-dataset macro F1 of 0.133 and 0.171. On the 19-stream cache, 224 dials lift causal F1 from 0.013 to 0.071 over scalar collapse, and 160 dials reach 0.098. Across 118 untouched held-out streams under a five-block protocol separating checkpoint selection and nominal adaptation from threshold calibration, Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal F1 from 0.076 to 0.079, buffered F1 from 0.143 to 0.156, and causal event recall from 52.5 to 63.8 percent across all nine datasets, outperforming system-matched coordinate maximum and top-k banks. Calibration precision depends on autocorrelation-adjusted sample size; causal ramp detection vanishes at four context windows, and single-sensor sensitivity remains an optional trade-off.
\end{abstract}
