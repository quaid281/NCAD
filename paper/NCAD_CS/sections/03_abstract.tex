\begin{abstract}
In fluid mechanics, smooth laminar flow turns turbulent when coherent instabilities couple across scales rather than from one isolated ripple. Spacecraft and industrial telemetry behaves similarly, yet deep anomaly detectors miss this structure. Waveform reconstructors overreact to harmless sensor jitter and flood operators with false alarms. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) stay quieter by forecasting compact latent summaries, but collapsing all errors into one threshold allows a single noisy coordinate to blind the monitor. Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we introduce Laminar-JEPA-Lite, applying fluid-dynamics diagnostics strictly as post-hoc latent residual algebra. Trained on healthy history across nested horizons, it treats nominal trajectories as a steady laminar baseline and uses Reynolds decomposition to separate sustained mean-flow drift from transient fluctuations. Each coordinate and drift dial carries its own healthy tripwire, alarming only when a quorum trips together. The panel matches scalar JEPAs in anomaly ranking, yet chooses a stronger label-free operating point. Across 118 untouched streams from nine datasets evaluated without point adjustment, standalone Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent and flags faults 41.4 steps earlier than single-score multi-horizon JEPA. Because per-coordinate calibration requires sufficient stable history, a pre-deployment readiness check routes short or drifting streams back to scalar calibration. This readiness-gated policy reaches 59.4 percent ER at 10.0 percent test false-positive rate (FPR), compared with 8.8 percent test FPR for scalar JEPA and roughly 25 percent held-out FPR for waveform reconstructors.
\end{abstract}
