\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise far fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs sum every coordinate into one number, letting drifting coordinates inflate the threshold and hide faults elsewhere. We replace that scalar score with per-coordinate circuit breakers: each coordinate's error across future horizons receives its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and an alarm fires when a quorum trips. We study Laminar-JEPA-Lite, a 128-breaker operational architecture, and Laminar-JEPA, a 224-breaker ablation reference. Primary evaluation uses 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets, separating model fitting, checkpoint selection, adaptation, and calibration onto disjoint healthy blocks. Under buffered attribution, Laminar-JEPA-Lite raises event recall from 59.0 to 67.4 percent and Point-F1 from 0.143 to 0.156 over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA while lowering held-out false-positive rates from 19.5 to 14.3 percent. Under live causal reporting, it raises event recall from 52.5 to 63.8 percent across all nine datasets, halves delay, and beats system-matched coordinate aggregators; pooled causal Point-F1 rises modestly from 0.076 to 0.079 (doubling from 0.040 to 0.079 when calibration reaches 500 windows), while causal held-out false-positive rates are right-skewed (0.78 percent median, 7.0 percent mean from short calibration histories). Design-stage streams show 1 versus 22 to 24 percent false-positive rates against reconstructors; slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{abstract}
