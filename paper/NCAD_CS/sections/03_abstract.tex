\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs sum every coordinate into one number, letting drifting coordinates inflate the threshold. We replace that scalar score with per-coordinate circuit breakers: across target horizons, each coordinate's position error and horizon-mean drift receive healthy-fitted tripwires, and an alarm fires when a quorum trips, with the quorum fitted to correlated trip counts on healthy data. The operational detector, Laminar-JEPA-Lite, uses 128 circuit breakers across three prediction horizons. On 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets under a disjoint five-block protocol, it raises live event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA across all nine datasets and nearly halves detection delay (69.0 versus 133.5 steps), trading higher mean held-out false alarms (6.4 versus 4.4 percent; median 0.93 percent). Because healthy regime shifts drive upper-tail false alarms and slow ramps remain undetected, autonomous deployment requires a held-out acceptance check and live drift monitoring.
\end{abstract}
