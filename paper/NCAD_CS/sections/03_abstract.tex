\begin{abstract}
In fluid mechanics, smooth laminar flow turns turbulent not from one isolated ripple, but when coherent instabilities couple across scales. Spacecraft and industrial telemetry behaves similarly, yet deep anomaly detectors miss this structure. Waveform reconstructors overreact to harmless sensor jitter and flood operators with false alarms. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) stay quieter by forecasting compact latent summaries, but collapsing all errors into one threshold allows a single noisy coordinate to blind the monitor. Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we introduce Laminar-JEPA-Lite, applying fluid-dynamics diagnostics strictly as post-hoc latent residual algebra. Trained on healthy history across nested horizons, it treats nominal trajectories as a steady laminar baseline and uses Reynolds decomposition to separate sustained mean-flow drift from transient fluctuations. Each coordinate and drift dial carries its own healthy tripwire, alarming only when a quorum trips together. While anomaly ranking matches scalar JEPAs, this panel chooses a stronger label-free operating point. Across 118 untouched streams from nine datasets evaluated without point adjustment, unscreened Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent and flags faults 41.4 steps earlier than single-score multi-horizon JEPA. Forcing per-coordinate tripwires onto short or drifting streams raises test false-positive rate (FPR) from 8.8 to 17.0 percent, so a pre-deployment readiness check routes those streams back to scalar calibration, curbing test FPR to 10.0 percent at 59.4 percent ER, far below the 25 percent held-out FPR of waveform reconstructors.
\end{abstract}
