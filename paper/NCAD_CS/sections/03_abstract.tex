\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry requires setting operational thresholds automatically via statistical calibration, such as Extreme Value Theory (EVT/SPOT), without ground-truth labels. While reconstructive baselines like TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive ranking (PR-AUC $0.29\text{--}0.34$), heavy-tailed observation residuals cause severe false-alarm flooding, yielding nominal false-positive rates of $24\text{--}30\%$. We investigate whether forecasting in latent representation space via Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) conditions residuals for automated thresholding. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical domains evaluated under held-out calibration (up to five seeds on the multi-seed subset), unconstrained latent prediction (TS-JEPA) suppresses nominal false-positive rates roughly four-fold down to $7.6\%$ (paired difference $-0.153$ against TimesNet, $p=0.002$). However, overall Point-F1 ($0.234$ vs. $0.197$, $p=0.54$) and PR-AUC ($0.323$ vs. $0.329$, $p=0.45$) remain statistically indistinguishable from baselines, while detection latency increases relative to the fastest reconstructive baseline ($\Delta t = 80$ vs. $48$ steps) and causal event recall drops ($10.7\%$ vs. $32.9\%$). Furthermore, evaluating three candidate physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds stress, and conservative potential flow) reveals domain-specific trade-offs rather than universal gains: linear Koopman operators excel on spacecraft telemetry (PR-AUC $0.591$ on SMAP), but curl-free priors fail on cyclic kinematics, and on aggregate, physical regularizers do not outperform unconstrained latent prediction ($p \ge 0.61$). Finally, EVT calibration systematically misses its nominal design risk ($q=10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry, performing comparably to empirical percentiles. Latent prediction thus provides a more conservative detector that suppresses false alarms, but does not improve ranking fidelity or resolve tail calibration drift.
\end{abstract}
