\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry requires setting decision thresholds automatically via statistical calibration methods, such as Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT), without access to ground-truth labels. While deep reconstructive architectures such as TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive continuous ranking performance (PR-AUC $0.29\text{--}0.34$), their observation-space residuals exhibit severe tail heaviness under sensor jitter. When calibrated under automated streaming thresholds, these heavy tails trigger excessive false alarms, with nominal false-positive rates reaching $24\text{--}30\%$. We investigate whether forecasting in abstract representation space via Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) produces prediction residuals that are better conditioned for automated thresholding. Alongside an unconstrained latent predictor (TS-JEPA), we evaluate three candidate physics-inspired regularizers: Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds second-moment fluctuation covariance, and curl-free potential flow gradients. We benchmark these models across 33 matched continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets using a strict held-out calibration protocol (chronological 65/35 train/held-out split with a 64-step isolation gap, multiple seeds, and matched-capacity controls). Our empirical evaluation yields four main findings. First, latent prediction lowers the measured nominal false-positive rate under SPOT from $24\text{--}30\%$ to $5.2\text{--}7.9\%$ (paired difference $-0.154$ against TimesNet, $p=0.008$; $-0.157$ against TranAD, $p=0.001$). Second, overall Point-F1 ($0.254$ for TS-JEPA vs. $0.207$ for TimesNet, $p=0.55$) and PR-AUC ($0.333$ vs. $0.336$, $p=0.30$) are not statistically distinguishable from baselines, and under strictly causal streaming (zero lookahead), JEPA event recall falls from $32.9\%$ to $10.7\%$, indicating a conservative operating trade-off rather than universal dominance. Third, controlled ablations on fixed architectures show that none of the three physical regularizers improves Point-F1 beyond seed noise ($+0.001$ for Operator-Entropy, $0.000$ for Reynolds-Stress, and $+0.006$ for Potential-Flow; $p \ge 0.58$), indicating that measured performance differences arise from shared neural modules rather than physical inductive priors. Fourth, automated EVT calibration systematically fails to reach its nominal design risk ($q=10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry, performing similarly to empirical percentiles, and kurtosis-adaptive heuristics fail to correct this drift. We release our complete multi-seed benchmark code and evaluation logs to support reproducible research in streaming anomaly detection.
\end{abstract}
