\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs sum every coordinate into one number, letting drifting coordinates inflate the threshold. We replace that scalar score with per-coordinate circuit breakers: each coordinate's error across future horizons receives its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and an alarm fires when a quorum trips, with the quorum fitted to the correlated trip counts seen on healthy data. Laminar-JEPA-Lite, a 128-breaker design, is the operational detector; Laminar-JEPA, a 224-breaker variant, is kept as an ablation reference. On 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets, with model fitting, checkpoint selection, adaptation, and calibration on disjoint healthy blocks, Laminar-JEPA-Lite raises live event recall from 52.5 to 63.8 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA on every dataset and nearly halves detection delay. Pointwise F1 rises only modestly overall, from 0.076 to 0.079, but doubles when at least 500 calibration windows are available. These gains come mainly from the label-free operating point; at matched alarm budgets the panel performs on par with simpler coordinate pooling. False alarms have a low median of 0.78 percent but a heavy tail, with a 95th percentile of 50 percent caused mostly by healthy regime shifts, so autonomous use requires adequate calibration, a held-out acceptance check, and drift monitoring. Slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{abstract}
