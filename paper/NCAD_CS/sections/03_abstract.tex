\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still sum many prediction errors into one anomaly score, so one or two coordinates that drift between training and calibration can dominate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces the scalar score and its tail-fitted threshold. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their mean, spread, and endpoint difference), and each statistic receives its own breaker calibrated on healthy data with a drift guard. The breakers are statistically dependent, so alarm quorums are set from joint nominal trip counts. On 118 multivariate telemetry streams never used to tune the panel, a lighter three-horizon variant raises strictly causal point-wise F1 from 0.072 to 0.083 for scalar JEPAs to 0.143 and causal event recall from 51 to 73 percent, at the cost of a higher held-out false-alarm rate (8.3 versus 5.9 percent). The panel needs roughly a thousand calibration steps to hold its false-alarm rate, and it misses slow process ramps and most single-channel sensor freezes.
\end{abstract}
