\begin{abstract}
In fluid mechanics, smooth laminar flow turns turbulent when coherent instabilities couple across scales rather than from one isolated ripple. Spacecraft and industrial telemetry behaves similarly, yet deep anomaly detectors miss this structure. Waveform reconstructors overreact to harmless sensor jitter and flood operators with false alarms. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) stay quieter by forecasting compact latent summaries, but collapsing all errors into one threshold allows a single noisy coordinate to blind the monitor. Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed loss penalties, we introduce Laminar-JEPA-Lite, which applies fluid-dynamics diagnostics strictly as post-hoc latent residual algebra. Trained on healthy history across nested horizons, it treats nominal trajectories as a steady laminar baseline and uses Reynolds decomposition to separate sustained mean-flow drift from transient fluctuations. Each coordinate and drift dial carries its own healthy tripwire, alarming only when a quorum trips together. The panel ranks anomalies comparably to scalar JEPAs, yet chooses a stronger label-free operating point. Across 118 untouched streams from nine datasets evaluated without point adjustment, standalone Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent and flags faults 41.4 steps earlier than single-score multi-horizon JEPA, at a higher test false-positive rate (FPR) of 17.0 versus 8.8 percent. A readiness check, with gates validated by nested leave-one-dataset-out tuning, routes short or drifting streams back to scalar calibration, reaching 59.4 percent ER at 10.0 percent test FPR. At 1 percent test FPR, waveform reconstructors catch about 25 percent of events, versus 51.2 percent for Laminar-JEPA-Lite.
\end{abstract}
