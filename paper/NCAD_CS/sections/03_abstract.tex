\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry requires label-free operational thresholds, typically set via Extreme Value Theory (EVT/SPOT). Reconstructive baselines such as TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive ranking (PR-AUC $0.29\text{--}0.34$), but heavy-tailed observation residuals flood operators with false alarms (nominal FPR $24\text{--}30\%$). We investigate whether forecasting in latent representation space via Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) conditions residuals for automated thresholding. Across 33 telemetry streams from seven physical domains under held-out calibration (five seeds on the 19-stream subset supplying reported means), unconstrained latent prediction (TS-JEPA) cuts nominal false-positive rates roughly four-fold to $7.6\%$ (paired difference $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet over all 33 streams, $p=0.002$). Point-F1 ($0.234$ vs.\ $0.197$, $p=0.54$) and PR-AUC ($0.323$ vs.\ $0.329$, $p=0.45$) remain statistically indistinguishable, while detection latency increases ($\Delta t = 80$ vs.\ $48$ steps) and strictly causal event recall falls from $58.8\%$ to $46.6\%$. Three physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds stress, conservative potential flow) yield domain-specific trade-offs rather than universal gains: Koopman operators excel on spacecraft telemetry (PR-AUC $0.591$ on SMAP), curl-free priors fail on cyclic kinematics, and no regularizer outperforms unconstrained latent prediction in aggregate ($p \ge 0.61$). EVT calibration also systematically misses its design risk ($q=10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry, performing comparably to empirical percentiles. Latent prediction thus yields a more conservative detector that suppresses false alarms, but does not improve ranking fidelity or resolve tail-calibration drift.
\end{abstract}
