\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still collapse prediction errors into a single scalar score, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We pair a multi-horizon JEPA with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces scalar scoring and tail-fitted thresholding. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded via a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon decomposition across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference), each monitored by an independently calibrated breaker with a nominal drift guard. Surgical weight-space quenching on healthy calibration windows removes mean predictor-bias drift and tempers high-variance precision directions before scoring. On 19 development streams, the panel reaches 0.262 buffered point-wise F1, 1.6 percent causal false-alarm rate, and a 59.6-step detection delay; across look-ahead horizons from 1 to 64 steps, it doubles to quadruples scalar causal F1 down to zero reporting lag. On 118 untouched held-out streams providing primary generalization evidence, a three-horizon panel raises causal point-wise F1 from 0.072 to 0.083 for scalar JEPAs to 0.143 and event recall from 51 to 73 percent, while weight-space quenching cuts causal false alarms by 8.0 percent relative across 162 streams. The panel requires roughly 1,000 calibration windows (300 effective independent samples), misses slow ramps, and needs a complementary channel-level branch for isolated single-sensor faults.
\end{abstract}
