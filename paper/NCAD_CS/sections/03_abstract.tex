\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs sum every coordinate into one number, letting drifting coordinates inflate the threshold. We replace that scalar score with per-coordinate circuit breakers: across target horizons, each coordinate's position error and horizon-mean drift receive healthy-fitted tripwires, and an alarm fires when a quorum trips, with the quorum fitted to correlated trip counts on healthy data. Laminar-JEPA-Lite, a streamlined 128-breaker three-horizon design (120k to 129k parameters) with depthwise frequency shells and hook-free multi-depth pooling, is the operational detector, paired with a 224-breaker four-horizon diagnostic variant on the same backbone. On 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets under a disjoint five-block protocol, Laminar-JEPA-Lite raises live event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA across all nine datasets and nearly halves detection delay (69.0 versus 133.5 steps), trading higher mean held-out healthy false alarms (6.4 versus 4.4 percent). Pooled pointwise F1 rises from 0.076 to 0.086 overall and more than doubles from 0.040 to 0.085 when at least 500 calibration windows exist. Held-out false alarms have a 0.93 percent median (88.1 percent of streams stay at or below 5 percent) but a 64.7 percent 95th percentile driven by healthy regime shifts rather than calibration length alone, so deployment requires a held-out acceptance check and live drift monitoring. Slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{abstract}
