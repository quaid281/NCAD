\begin{abstract}
Monitoring spacecraft and industrial machines requires fast fault detection without constant false alarms, yet waveform reconstructors fire often on jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) predict compact learned summaries instead, though single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one threshold. We integrate multi-horizon residuals, coordinate whitening, per-coordinate tripwires, and a correlation-aware quorum into a streaming detector. Coordinate calibration shifts the operating point. Underlying ranking matches scalar JEPAs. Each stream fits only on its own healthy prefix, transferring one frozen architecture across sensor suites. Across 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets, our Laminar-JEPA-Lite compromise lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. However, mean held-out false-positive rate (FPR) rises from 4.4 to 6.4 percent (0.93 percent median) and prospective test FPR rises from 8.8 to 17.0 percent. Stream-paired co-detected onset latency drops by 74.6 steps (41.4 steps on shared segments), while detection-conditioned means fall from 133.5 to 69.0 steps. Overall Point-F1 moves from 0.076 to 0.086, losing on three datasets and trailing the scalar baseline at 1 percent test FPR. TimesNet and TranAD reach higher raw F1 only at 24.6 to 25.6 percent held-out FPR. Operational deployment uses empirical screening (accepting 52 streams at 11.0 percent mean test FPR, or 46 with drift screening at 6.8 percent mean and 2.9 percent median), paired with quarantined recalibration and complementary guards.
\end{abstract}
