\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space. Because sensor telemetry contains unforecastable jitter and scale disparities, observation-space residuals develop heavy tails that trigger severe false-alarm flooding under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid reconstructing raw noise by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet collapsing high-dimensional multi-horizon prediction errors into a single scalar anomaly score introduces a second bottleneck: one or two out-of-sample drifting coordinates can dominate the scalar calibration tail, inflating the alarm threshold and suppressing point-wise F1. We introduce Soliton-Reynolds JEPA paired with a 224-bit multi-perspective fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel that foregoes scalar anomaly score thresholding and parametric tail extrapolation altogether. The architecture taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context barycenters, whitens prediction errors across four nested sub-horizons, and expands each of the 32 latent coordinates into seven fluid-dynamic perspectives: four sub-horizon flow positions, Reynolds mean-flow transport, turbulent fluctuation amplitude, and convective right-edge acceleration, governed by calibration-stabilized whitening, per-dial hijack guards, and 32 fast right-edge onset relays. Across 33 primary and 141 archive-wide multivariate telemetry streams, latent prediction suppresses false alarms relative to observation-space reconstructors while the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel doubles strictly causal point-wise F1, lifts event recall to 72.7 percent, and cuts detection delay in half under both causal streaming and retrospective buffered attribution.
\end{abstract}
