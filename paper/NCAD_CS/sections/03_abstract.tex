\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate telemetry is a critical operational requirement for mission safety across aerospace systems, industrial infrastructure, and smart cyber-physical environments. While modern reconstructive architectures such as TimesNet and TranAD achieve competitive continuous ranking performance (PR-AUC $0.2585$--$0.2612$), their raw observation-space residuals exhibit severe sensitivity to non-stationary sensor jitter. When deployed under automated streaming threshold calibration via Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) without ground-truth labels, reconstructive models suffer severe calibration collapse, flooding telemetry streams with hundreds of thousands of false alarms (TimesNet: $1{,}047{,}144$ false alarms, $0.0647$ Micro-F1; TranAD: $452{,}621$ false alarms, $0.1072$ Micro-F1). We study whether Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), which forecast in abstract representation space, yield latent predictive residuals that are better suited to automated thresholding than raw reconstruction errors. We investigate three physics-inspired inductive regularizers that embed domain principles into the latent manifold: \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, regularizing latent Koopman operators via scale-free spectral entropy and Bregman energy barriers to penalize modal collapse; \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, aligning cross-coordinate fluctuation covariances inspired by hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition; and \textit{Potential-Flow JEPA}, constraining trajectory updates to curl-free scalar potential gradients ($\hat{v} = -\nabla \Phi$). Combined with causal coordinate saliency gating with zero target-leakage shielding, Littlewood-Paley dyadic frequency shells, and kurtosis-adaptive EVT tail calibration, these components are evaluated as one framework. Evaluated across 45 matched telemetry channels from 11 benchmarks under an identical calibration protocol, Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves the highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.2066$, a +43.2\% relative gain over TimesNet and +28.4\% over TranAD) and clears the analytical All-Positive reference baseline ($0.1893$). In Pooled Micro Point-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA attains $0.3458$, reducing false alarms by 8.0-fold against TimesNet ($130{,}237$ vs $1{,}047{,}144$) and 3.5-fold against TranAD ($452{,}621$). These 45-stream numbers come from a single-seed pipeline that fits thresholds on training residuals, and we could not reproduce them under a stricter protocol. That protocol (33 streams, thresholds fit on held-out nominal data, up to three seeds) gives a narrower result. The unconstrained TS-JEPA lowers the measured nominal false-positive rate from about 24--30\% (TimesNet, TranAD) to 5--7\% (paired difference about $-0.15$, $p\leq 0.008$), with Point-F1 and PR-AUC not significantly different from the baselines and strong dependence on the dataset. No model reaches the $10^{-3}$ SPOT design risk. None of the three physical variants improves on TS-JEPA, and switching off the entropy, stress and regime terms changes Point-F1 by no more than seed noise on a seven-stream subset, so we cannot attribute any gain to the physical regularizers. We therefore present latent prediction as a conservative, low-false-alarm operating point and describe the regularizers as physics-inspired inductive biases without demonstrated benefit.
\end{abstract}
