\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize point-wise error in raw observation space, where unforecastable jitter and scale disparities produce heavy-tailed residuals and frequent false alarms under automated calibration. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid this by forecasting compact latent summaries of future windows, yet they still collapse prediction errors into a single scalar score, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the threshold and suppress point-wise F1. We propose Laminar-JEPA, which pairs a multi-horizon JEPA with surgical weight-space quenching and a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel that replaces scalar scoring and tail-fitted thresholding. Each of 32 whitened latent coordinates is expanded via a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon decomposition across four nested horizons into seven statistics (four horizon errors and their horizon mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference), each monitored by an independently calibrated breaker with a nominal drift guard after removing mean predictor-bias drift and tempering high-variance precision directions on healthy calibration windows. On 19 development streams, Laminar-JEPA reaches 0.262 buffered point-wise F1, 66.4 percent buffered and 62.5 percent causal event recall, 1.6 percent causal false-alarm rate, and a 59.6-step detection delay; across look-ahead horizons from 1 to 64 steps, it doubles to quadruples scalar causal F1 down to zero reporting lag. Across 162 streams spanning 15 datasets, weight-space quenching cuts causal false alarms by 8.0 percent relative (median held-out false-alarm rate 1.0 percent and test false-alarm rate 1.6 percent), and on 118 untouched held-out streams a three-horizon panel raises causal point-wise F1 from 0.072 to 0.083 for scalar JEPAs to 0.143 and event recall from 51 to 73 percent. The panel requires roughly 1,000 calibration windows (300 effective independent samples), misses slow ramps, and needs a complementary channel-level branch for isolated single-sensor faults.
\end{abstract}
