\begin{abstract}
Deep reconstructive detectors for multivariate time-series anomaly detection minimize observation-space error, fitting stochastic sensor noise that produces heavy-tailed residuals and severe false-alarm flooding under automated threshold calibration. We investigate how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) condition predictive residuals in a low-dimensional latent space under distribution shift, alongside an Operator-Entropy variant (OpEntropy JEPA) combining a linear Koopman transition operator, spectral entropy and shifted logarithmic Bregman barrier regularizers, coordinate saliency gating, and a multiscale dyadic frequency front-end. Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets under chronological held-out Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold calibration, our primary finding is relative false-alarm suppression rather than universal detection superiority or exact attainment of the 0.1\% nominal design risk. Latent predictive scoring reduces held-out false-positive rates three-fold under retrospective buffered attribution (9.3\% to 10.1\% versus 30.0\% to 30.7\% for TimesNet and TranAD) and eight- to ten-fold under strictly causal streaming (2.4\% to 2.8\% versus 22.2\% to 24.3\%), while point F1 and precision-recall ranking remain comparable to reconstructive baselines. Controlled ablations isolate two distinct mechanisms: layer-normalization bottlenecks compress residual kurtosis, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift relative to normalized observation forecasters. On a fixed Koopman backbone, equivalence testing confirms that spectral entropy and barrier terms preserve full operator rank without altering point F1 within a 0.01 to 0.03 margin, while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens detection delay relative to an unconstrained predictor. Static thresholding still overshoots its 0.1\% design target by 15- to 64-fold out of sample, driven primarily by inter-block distribution shift and serial dependence.
\end{abstract}
