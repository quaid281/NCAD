\begin{abstract}
Monitoring systems such as spacecraft and industrial plants must flag sensor faults quickly while staying quiet during normal operation. Detectors that reconstruct raw signals fire often on harmless jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict compact learned summaries and raise fewer false alarms, yet single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one number so drifting coordinates inflate the shared threshold. We replace that scalar score with coordinate-level circuit breakers. Across target horizons, each coordinate's error magnitude and horizon-mean drift receive healthy-fitted tripwires, and an alarm fires when a correlation-aware quorum trips on healthy-calibrated counts. Rather than improving post-hoc score ranking, this quorum shifts the label-free operating point toward higher live event coverage and faster response. On 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets under a disjoint five-block protocol, our operational detector, Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 breakers across three horizons), raises live event recall from 52.5 to 64.6 percent across all nine datasets over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. It also cuts mean detection delay from 133.5 to 69.0 steps at a higher mean held-out false-positive rate of 6.4 versus 4.4 percent (median 0.93 percent). Because nominal regime shifts drive upper-tail false alarms and slow ramps evade fixed-window prediction, autonomous deployment requires a held-out acceptance check and live drift monitoring.
\end{abstract}
