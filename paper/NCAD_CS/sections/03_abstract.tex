\begin{abstract}
Monitoring spacecraft and industrial machines demands fast fault detection without constant false alarms, yet waveform reconstructors fire often on jitter and drift. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) predict compact learned summaries instead, though single-score JEPAs collapse every coordinate into one threshold. We combine multi-horizon residuals, coordinate whitening, per-coordinate tripwires, and a quorum. Underlying score ranking matches scalar JEPAs. Instead, the principal contribution is automated label-free operating-point selection at deployment across streams fitting only on their healthy prefix, while cross-dataset routing thresholds use historical training labels during policy validation. Across 118 untouched archive streams from nine datasets, unscreened Laminar-JEPA-Lite lifts causal event recall (ER) from 52.5 to 64.6 percent over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA. Exact segment-paired onset latency drops by 41.4 steps across shared fault segments (74.6 steps in stream-level paired delays across commonly triggered streams). However, mean held-out false-positive rate (FPR) rises from 4.4 to 6.4 percent and prospective test FPR rises from 8.8 to 17.0 percent. Overall Point-F1 shifts from 0.076 to 0.086 across datasets and trails 1D at 1 percent test FPR, while TimesNet and TranAD need 24.6 to 25.6 percent held-out FPR for higher F1. Three-stage screening accepts 46 streams at 6.8 percent mean test FPR (2.9 percent median). For recall-prioritized monitoring, routing the rest forms a complete hybrid trading 10.0 vs. 8.8 percent test FPR for 59.4 percent ER and 0.091 Point-F1, plus quarantined recalibration.
\end{abstract}
