\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone. Because controls run three seeds against the five-seed principal runs, the paired control means below are computed on the shared three-seed subset and differ slightly from the five-seed Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) rows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$:} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.241 \pm 0.014$ vs. $0.244 \pm 0.024$; difference $-0.003$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.011, +0.005]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.62$); PR-AUC stays $0.347\text{--}0.348$.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$:} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.203 \pm 0.059$ vs. $0.208 \pm 0.071$ ($+0.005$, $[-0.006, +0.016]$, $p=0.41$).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing shifts Point-F1 to $0.129$ from $0.096$ on the shared three-seed subset ($p_{\text{raw}}=0.048, p_{\text{BH}}=0.24$; $0.089$ at five seeds in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)); both underperform TS-JEPA ($0.234$, $p_{\text{raw}}=0.013, p_{\text{BH}}=0.24$, non-significant under FDR adjustment). 
\end{enumerate}
Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction. Formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) reject non-equivalence ($H_0: |\Delta| \ge 0.05$) and confirm statistical equivalence to TS-JEPA within a tight $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ Point-F1 margin: Operator-Entropy achieves $p = 0.0009$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.014, +0.021]$, also passing $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ with $p=0.0283$) and Reynolds-Stress achieves $p = 0.0054$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.042, +0.007]$). These null results establish practical equivalence rather than an empirical advantage.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Factorial Isolation of the Entropy Term and Energy Barrier}
\label{subsec:factorial}
A single on/off control cannot separate the spectral-entropy term from the energy barrier or show how either scales. Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) crosses the two terms (neither, entropy only, barrier only, both) and sweeps $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00\}$ with the barrier on, on six streams at three seeds with a $D{=}32$ operator. Each arm reports operator rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K})$, condition number $\kappa(\mathbf{K})$, representation rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}})/\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$, the coefficient of variation $\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}$ of the training loss as a stability proxy, and held-out calibration and detection outcomes in both attribution regimes.

Three observations follow. First, the unconstrained operator is already near-isotropic ($\operatorname{RankMe}(\mathbf{K}){=}31.83$ of $32$, $\kappa{=}1.235$) because $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized at the identity; the regularizers tighten it only marginally ($31.97$ and $\kappa{=}1.083$ at the deployed $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0.50$, $\kappa{=}1.034$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}2.00$) and lower $\text{CV}_{\mathcal{L}}$ from $1.013$ to $0.917$. Second, the encoder representation is unaffected: $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ stays within $5.15\text{--}5.34$ and $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ within $9.92\text{--}10.07$ of $32$ in every arm. Operator non-degeneracy, representation diversity, and anomaly separability are therefore separate properties, and only the first moves. Third, no arm or strength improves detection: buffered Point-F1 spans $0.076\text{--}0.094$ and causal Point-F1 $0.022\text{--}0.028$ with no monotone trend in $\lambda_{\text{ent}}$, buffered FPR spans $0.066\text{--}0.104$, and the unconstrained arm has the lowest buffered FPR of all arms ($0.066$) and the second-highest causal event recall ($0.516$, behind $0.544$ at $\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}1.00$). The entropy term is a well-posed constraint that preserves operator conditioning, not a demonstrated source of detection or calibration gain.

\noindent\textbf{Decoupling.} On eight streams (causal trailing), adding covariance coupling ($\alpha_Z{=}0.005$) and the Lyapunov defect ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}{=}0.02$) cut delay from $112.0$ (single-operator OpEntropy) to $30.1$ steps at event recall $0.433$ and FPR $0.021$, against $85.1$, $0.376$, and $0.018$ for unconstrained TS-JEPA, at some cost in Point-F1 ($0.050$ vs.\ $0.060$--$0.062$). These exploratory contrasts shift the recall-delay trade-off and are not part of the headline results.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating and Quiet-Fault Reference Monitors}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling, a non-causal target-only leaky control, and independent context/target gating under an identical TS-JEPA configuration (seed 42, four streams). Macro Point-F1 ranks uniform weighting ($0.118$) above context gating ($0.077$), with the leaky control at $0.128$ and independent gating weakest ($0.071$); the effect is stream-dependent in sign (MSL \texttt{M-1}: $0.047 \to 0.227$; SMAP \texttt{P-3}: $0.280 \to 0.019$). The gate is an evaluated variant; the deployed pipeline retains uniform input weighting.

Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d) injects six single-channel fault classes into six multivariate streams. The variance gate roughly doubles point recall relative to uniform pooling (macro $0.045$ vs.\ $0.023$) at about $3.6\times$ the nominal FPR ($0.069$ vs.\ $0.019$), and a median-absolute-deviation variant is marginally stronger ($0.060$ recall). Quiet faults (flatline, stuck value, dropout) stay weakly detected under every latent gating regime (point recall $0.02\text{--}0.07$). Two observation-space reference monitors test whether a complementary score can cover this blind spot: a rolling channel-variance floor (\texttt{lowvar}) and a maximum channel $z$-deviation (\texttt{dev\_maxz}). The variance floor reaches point recall $0.18$ ($0.32$ on mid-variance flatlines) and the $z$-deviation monitor $0.09\text{--}0.11$, but at nominal FPR of $0.14\text{--}0.16$, roughly eight times that of the latent detector. A complementary variance monitor recovers part of the quiet-fault gap only at a substantial alarm cost, so quiet-fault coverage remains an open limitation.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a modest, stream-dependent effect (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)): macro buffered Point-F1 improves from $0.088$ to $0.131$ across a five-stream exploratory panel (including two diagnostic streams, SMD and GECCO, outside the 33 primary streams), concentrated on MSL and SMD, with a small SMAP regression. The block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters and shell overlap prevents octave isolation, so it is a modest contributor, not the primary driver.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
To discriminate the operational mechanisms driving tail conditioning and threshold transfer, all 19 non-GHL streams were re-run under the primary held-out protocol (five seeds, identical windows, AdamW budget, and SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$ calibration) across controlled backbone arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, three dilated TCN layers): TCN-Obs-Recon (observation reconstruction), TCN-Obs-Pred (observation forecasting), JEPA-CtxRecon (latent autoencoding of EMA-encoded \emph{context} without foresight), JEPA-NoEMA (latent prediction with hard-copy target), and TS-JEPA. 

\noindent\textbf{Mechanism Discrimination: Foresight vs. Geometry vs. Dynamics:} The controls are consistent with three distinct operational factors:
(1)~\emph{Future temporal forecasting is not uniquely necessary for score conditioning}: JEPA-CtxRecon autoencodes context representations without forecasting future timesteps, yet achieves an FPR reduction of $0.202$ ($0.310 \to 0.108$ relative to TCN-Obs-Pred) and higher event recall ($0.513$ vs.\ $0.473$ for TS-JEPA on this controlled panel; $0.481$ on the primary benchmark). Latent representation compression, rather than future-state extrapolation, conditions scores for tail estimation.
(2)~\emph{Bounded score geometry mechanically suppresses tail kurtosis}: LayerNorm bottlenecks compress excursions regardless of architecture (TCN-ObsPred-LN $\gamma_2 = 0.14$ vs.\ $8.24$ unnormalized; $1.14$ for TS-JEPA).
(3)~\emph{Representation dynamics and conservative subspace transfer}: kurtosis suppression alone cannot prevent threshold transfer breakdown across temporal blocks. TCN-ObsPred-LN suffers held-out nominal FPR of $0.186$ (buffered) and $0.117$ (causal trailing), versus $0.080$ and $0.014$ for TS-JEPA. At conservative budgets, PCA-LinPred-LN and IsoForest collapse to event recalls of $0.665$ and $0.631$ versus $0.774$ for TS-JEPA ($0.768$ ungated). Representation rank diagnostics show $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$ occupy $5.6\text{--}6.1$ and $8.5\text{--}14.0$ of 32 dimensions, so low nominal FPR is consistent with conservative subspace projection and bounded score geometry rather than an exclusive property of predictive dynamics.
