\subsection{Ablation 1: Multi-Horizon Whitened Envelope vs.\ Post-Hoc Soliton and Spectral Regularizers}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
What actually drives the gains of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA over single-horizon TS-JEPA? To separate the core representation from post-hoc scoring multipliers on the exact same trained network, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) compares full Soliton-Reynolds JEPA against its unmodulated envelope control ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$, which outputs $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ directly without the soliton or prototype multipliers) across all 19 non-GHL streams. Replacing single-horizon TS-JEPA with the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ raises buffered PR-AUC from $0.331$ to $0.374$, cuts strictly causal held-out FPR six-fold ($2.4\% \to 0.4\%$), lowers calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.162$), and lifts causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ from $0.395$ to $0.697$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).

Adding the post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration gain ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) and prototype manifold gain on top of $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ produces a null aggregate effect. Buffered held-out FPR shifts slightly from $12.2\%$ to $10.8\%$ (paired difference $-1.3\%$, $95\%$ CI $[-3.5\%, +0.9\%]$), while causal FPR ($0.4\%$ vs.\ $0.3\%$) and KS drift ($0.162$ vs.\ $0.163$) do not change. Buffered Point-F1 ($0.188$ vs.\ $0.185$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.327$) and PR-AUC ($0.375$ vs.\ $0.374$) satisfy TOST equivalence at both $\Delta = \pm 0.030$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0021$) and $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.026$). We observe the same result during training when regularizing a linear latent transition matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ with Von Neumann spectral entropy and an Itakura--Saito energy barrier across six streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a)): raising the entropy weight from $0$ to $2.0$ lowers the operator condition number monotonically ($\kappa(\mathbf{K}): 1.437 \to 1.154$), yet target effective rank ($\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})/D \approx 0.36$), causal FPR ($1.7\%$), and buffered event recall ($0.250\text{--}0.266$) remain flat. The gains come from multi-depth TCN tapping, barycenter centering, and multi-horizon Mahalanobis whitening, not from post-hoc soliton modulation or spectral penalties.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Quiet Faults, Input Gating, and Hybrid Monitoring}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
Why do normalized latent encoders miss quiet single-channel faults? When one sensor out of $K$ freezes at a nominal value while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the frozen channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$. LayerNorm then rescales the embedding back onto the $\sqrt{D}$ sphere using the variance of the remaining $K-1$ active channels, masking the single-sensor freeze. Weighting input channels by context variance prior to encoding lowers macro Point-F1 across four multivariate benchmark streams compared to uniform channel weighting ($0.077$ vs.\ $0.118$). The deployed pipeline therefore retains uniform input encoding. Across six fault classes injected into individual channels on six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), uniform TS-JEPA detects $27.8\%$ of abrupt $+5\sigma$ spikes ($30.6\%$ with context-variance gating), but on persistent quiet faults (constant or mid-range flatlines, stuck sensors, and zero dropouts) and low-amplitude steps it reaches only $19.4\%$ event recall and $2.0\%\text{--}3.0\%$ point recall ($4.3\%\text{--}6.8\%$ under variance or MAD input gating). Simple observation-space monitors catch these complementary failures: a maximum channel deviation monitor (tracking the largest standardized deviation from the training median and MAD across channels) reaches $25.0\%\text{--}30.6\%$ event recall at $14.1\%$ FPR, and a rolling variance-collapse monitor (tracking how far each channel's 32-step trailing standard deviation drops below its nominal median) achieves $26.0\%\text{--}38.9\%$ event recall at $14.9\%$ FPR. Taking the logical union of TS-JEPA alarms and the rolling variance-collapse monitor raises point recall to $20.2\%\text{--}31.8\%$ and event recall to $41.7\%\text{--}50.0\%$ across all six fault classes, trading $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR ($1.9\% \to 17.0\%$) for quiet-fault coverage.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) trains TS-JEPA with the identical 3-layer causal TCN encoder across five streams, comparing the 4-shell Fourier filterbank against a standard linear convolutional stem. Enabling the dyadic front-end raises macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ ($+0.050$, led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers validation residual kurtosis ($1.409 \to 1.069$), while macro Point-F1 shifts from $0.118$ to $0.078$ due to conservative threshold tightening on SMAP P-3 ($0.301 \to 0.115$). Frequency-shell splitting thus improves score ranking and tail conditioning rather than uniformly raising thresholded F1.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 12 model arms across all 19 non-GHL streams under identical chronological splits, training budgets, and matched parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster vs.\ TCN Obs.\ Autoencoder):} Pairing the exact same causal dilated TCN encoder with a multilayer perceptron decoder to either forecast the 64-step target window or reconstruct the full 320-step window in raw sensor space leaves residual kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 6.10$ vs.\ $6.24$), calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.463$ vs.\ $0.445$), and causal FPR ($24.7\%$ vs.\ $24.5\%$) virtually unchanged. Switching from reconstruction to forecasting in observation space does not resolve threshold instability.
    \item \textbf{LayerNorm bottleneck and saliency gating in observation space (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster (LN)):} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck, target-window LayerNorm, and the coordinate saliency gate to the observation forecaster ($307\text{k}$ parameters) compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and cuts causal FPR in half ($11.7\%$). Because predictions still target raw sensor waveforms, however, calibration-to-holdout drift remains high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and causal Point-F1 drops to $0.084$.
    \item \textbf{Learned latent target projection and multi-horizon whitening (TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA):} Moving the prediction target from raw observations into the learned $D=32$ latent space halves calibration-to-holdout drift relative to the bounded observation forecaster ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.163$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), cuts causal FPR to $2.4\%$ and $0.3\%$, and raises buffered PR-AUC from $0.257$ to $0.331$ and $0.375$.
    \item \textbf{Nonlinear TCN encoding vs.\ linear PCA subspace and tree-based isolation (PCA Linear Forecaster (LN) and Isolation Forest):} Projecting context and target windows onto 32 principal components with a ridge-regression predictor, LayerNorm, and the coordinate saliency gate achieves $2.1\%$ causal FPR via score bounding, but its causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ ($0.104$ buffered; Isolation Forest reaches $0.149$ causal event recall). Learned nonlinear temporal features are therefore required to separate anomalies from nominal background.
    \item \textbf{Future prediction vs.\ context autoencoding, EMA target stability, and $\ell_2$ scoring (JEPA Context Autoencoder, JEPA Hard-Target Copy, and TS-JEPA ($\ell_2$ Scoring)):} Training the JEPA predictor to reconstruct the context window's own target embedding keeps drift and false alarms low ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.203$, causal FPR $1.7\%$) but reduces causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.161$) and PR-AUC ($0.235$ vs.\ $0.278$), showing that forecasting future windows improves discriminability. Copying online weights directly to the target encoder each step ($\zeta = 0$) reaches $0.9\%$ causal FPR and $0.235$ buffered Point-F1, and replacing the saliency gate with a plain Euclidean norm yields $1.8\%$ causal FPR and $0.156$ causal Point-F1, confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent architecture rather than the scoring gate.
\end{enumerate}
