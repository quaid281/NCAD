\subsection{Ablation 1: Dial Views, Single-Factor \emph{Lite} Isolation, and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
We keep experimental regimes separate: Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert} score one fixed $19$-stream $M=4$ cache; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) retrains each single-factor control ($3$ seeds, disjoint four-block protocol); Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) is a five-seed development run; and Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates the untouched $118$-stream archive:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Cached dial ladder vs.\ why \emph{Lite} uses $128$ rather than $160$ dials:} On the fixed $M=4$ sequential cache (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding from $1$ to $224$ dials lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$; buffered $+0.033$, $p=0.53$). Pruning fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to $160$ position-plus-drift dials reaches $0.098$ causal Point-F1 at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (removing relays gives $0.068$, a single $224$-dial quorum gives $0.063$, uniform smearing raises buffered delay to $115.4$ steps, and unweighted $N_{224}$ shifts archive PR-AUC by at most $0.0011$ with identical alarms). Retrained end-to-end on the parallel \emph{Lite} backbone (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)), however, adding the fourth prefix ($S_3=48$, $160$ vs.\ $128$ dials) yields no significant Point-F1 or ER gain ($p=0.29$, $\Delta\text{ER}=+0.4$ points; median causal F1 $0.026$ vs.\ $0.033$). Instead, it raises $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($0.82\%$ to $1.06\%$ causal; $6.2\%$ to $6.8\%$ buffered), expands $\mathbf{\Omega}$ from $96^2$ to $128^2$, and slows inference by $25.5\%$ ($0.123$ vs.\ $0.098\,\text{ms}$/window). On the $118$-stream archive rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), $128$-dial \emph{Lite} also matches or exceeds retrained $160$- and $224$-dial panels ($0.078$ causal and $0.154$ buffered F1, vs.\ $0.073$/$0.140$ and $0.074$/$0.143$).
    \item \emph{Single-factor encoder, predictor, and panel isolation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Flipping one factor from \emph{Lite} validates each simplification. Reducing \emph{depth} ($4$ vs.\ $5$ blocks) preserves F1 and ER ($p=0.98$ causal, $p=0.23$ buffered; median causal F1 $0.033$ vs.\ $0.031$) while saving $23.3\text{k}$ parameters. Narrowing \emph{pooling} ($64$D vs.\ $128$D heads) preserves F1 ($p\ge 0.45$), lifts ER by $+7.3$ causal and $+9.0$ buffered points ($63.1\%$ vs.\ $55.9\%$ causal), and saves $22.9\text{k}$ parameters. Replacing the parallel \emph{predictor} at matched $M=4$ ($160$ dials) with sequential rollout lowers mean F1 ($0.086$ to $0.077$ causal; $0.239$ to $0.195$ buffered, $p=0.078$), doubles causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($1.9\%$ to $4.0\%$), and adds $10.1$ delay steps and $4.5\text{k}$ parameters. Finally, expanding the \emph{panel} on identical $M=4$ residuals ($224$ vs.\ $160$ dials) lifts causal ER ($63.6\%$ to $69.0\%$, $p=0.0077$) and F1 ($0.086$ to $0.095$, $p=0.012$) only by inflating $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($1.06\%$ to $1.45\%$ causal; $6.8\%$ to $11.9\%$ buffered), and no panel removes the $S-1$ causal lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching step isolation:} Across single-seed $118$-stream \emph{Laminar-JEPA} checkpoints (unquenched causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=6.76\%$, $\text{F1}=0.0901$), Step Q1 ($k=4$) shifts unselected coordinates by $11.1\%$ RMS via 32D LayerNorm coupling ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=7.06\%$), Steps Q2--Q3 cut F1 to $0.0826$ ($6.71\%$), and Step Q4 ($39/118$ accepted) reaches $6.62\%$ ($\text{F1}=0.0904$). Ungated edits hurt $k=2$ dev F1 ($0.141\to 0.049$) and $k\in\{8,12\}$ PR-AUC ($0.292\to 0.223$), so \emph{Lite} omits quenching.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate rules fail through three distinct statistical mechanisms on the cached 19-stream residuals (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Cross-coordinate false-alarm compounding:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or even at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds marginal errors across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Discrete finite-sample calibration floor:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) across 128 coordinates or BH false discovery rate (FDR, $q=0.005$) across 224 dials fails even under stationarity because empirical conformal $p$-values cannot fall below $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, paralyzing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{Out-of-sample distributional instability vs.\ stable aggregators:} Calibrating the aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) bypasses the discrete floor, exposing out-of-sample instability instead: discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ from boundary ties, and recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$ under mild cross-block covariance and mean drift. Among distributionally stable aggregators on this cache, the 160-dial panel reaches Point-F1 $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, above 128-coordinate top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.068$, $0.056$, $0.072$), while Fisher pooling reaches $0.109$ (Stouffer $0.095$) at $1.05\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. Independently trained archive comparisons appear in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
A frozen or flatlined sensor barely moves the latent summary because terminal LayerNorm spreads the small change across all $32$ coordinates. On \texttt{SMAP P-3}, for example, a flatline trips only $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip, and a single faulty sensor is further diluted by the $K-1$ healthy channels. Extending an initial six-stream study, we evaluate a causal channel branch across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$) without per-stream or test-label tuning.

For each channel, we compute three 64-step rolling statistics: log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, training-fit first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$. Each statistic is thresholded at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ on nominal validation windows (with a floor of $6.0$ median absolute deviation (MAD) and a $1.25\times$ half-split drift multiplier) behind a $24$-of-$40$-step persistence gate. We test the branch by injecting three nonoverlapping $200$-step faults per stream into clean test segments of the highest-variance active channel ($57$ events per fault class): variance collapse to the training mean, an alternating $\pm1.35\sigma$ prediction shock, sensor freezing, saturation at $\max(q_{.995},\mu+2.2\sigma)$, a linear $2.5\sigma$ degradation, and a $1.5\sigma$ bias. The variance-collapse union lifts injected-event recall for variance collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%$--$82.5\%$ to $93.0\%$--$94.7\%$, while the full channel branch lifts recall on shocks, gradual degradation, and bias from $59.6\%$--$75.4\%$ to $80.7\%$--$96.5\%$. However, on the \emph{unmodified} test streams, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union, $\text{F1}=0.070$) and $19.4\%$ (full branch, $\text{F1}=0.129$). Synthetic single-channel injections therefore do not establish detection on natural sensor failures, and the full union's $19.4\%$ false-alarm rate makes it unsuitable as a default detector.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, \texttt{GECCO}; three seeds) shows a channel-count threshold: shell splitting improves high-channel spacecraft detection ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), but lowers F1 on both $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), setting the $K \ge 25$ spectral gate in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
The 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) separate contributing factors on the development streams; they are not universal causes. Raw-observation forecasting and autoencoding share heavy tails, large drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$). A $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space cuts kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halves FPR but keeps drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$). Learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). PCA and Isolation Forest keep causal FPR low but reach only $0.017$ and $0.041$ causal Point-F1. The panels' gains do not come from lighter tails alone: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps roughly the same kurtosis as Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$).
