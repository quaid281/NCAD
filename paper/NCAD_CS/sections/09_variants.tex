\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder, Circuit-Breaker Components, and Weight-Space Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) trace how each stage of the multi-perspective expansion and surgical weight-space quenching shapes detection accuracy on the 19-stream development cohort:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{From 1D scalar collapse to sub-horizon coordinate switches (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)):} Summing all 128 whitened coordinates into a single 1D scalar score (with or without $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation, which shows a null effect of $\Delta = -0.005$ causal F1) allows one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the global threshold and delay detection. Introducing 32 per-coordinate switches on the terminal horizon ($m=4$) nearly doubles causal Point-F1, and expanding to all four nested prefixes (\emph{128-Bit Sub-Horizons}) establishes a strong multi-coordinate baseline across both causal and buffered regimes.
    \item \emph{Decomposing horizon drift, fluctuation energy, and endpoint relays:} Adding the 32 horizon-mean drift dials (\emph{160-Bit}) cuts causal detection delay in half by locking onto sustained trajectory offsets, while the 32 fluctuation dials (\emph{192-Bit}) capture oscillatory energy across buffered windows. Completing the panel with the 32 endpoint-velocity dials and right-edge onset relays (\emph{224-Bit Breaker}) bridges the 63-step structural lag, achieving significant gains over 1D collapse in both causal ($p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$) and buffered ($p = 0.026$) Point-F1.
    \item \emph{Leave-one-out panel ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Removing the 32 right-edge onset relays slows causal detection by nearly twenty steps, replacing prefix routing with uniform 64-step smearing degrades buffered event recall, removing calibration hijack guards ($\gamma_j = 1$) weakens drift immunity, and replacing hard binary switches with soft attention weights adds no benefit.
    \item \emph{Surgical weight-space quenching components and sensitivity ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{0,\dots,12\}$):} Disentangling the two quenching operations of Section~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel} across the 19 development streams reveals complementary roles: predictor-bias recentering alone ($\Delta\mathbf{b}_m$) boosts causal event recall ($56.5\% \to 64.3\%$) and buffered F1 ($0.224 \to 0.256$) while cutting causal delay ($90.1 \to 63.8$ steps) at the cost of higher nominal FPR, whereas untargeted precision tempering alone degrades buffered F1 ($0.224 \to 0.135$), and RMS stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) raises causal F1 to $0.099$. Sweeping $k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 12\}$ confirms that tempering only the top $k_{\text{rogue}} \in [1, 4]$ coordinates with internal calibration-split gating (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) preserves event recall ($61.4\%\text{--}64.6\%$) while cutting buffered FPR ($24.2\% \to 19.2\%$) and archive-wide causal FPR across 162 streams ($6.71\% \to 6.17\%$, $p = 0.0011$), whereas over-quenching ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{8, 12\}$) attenuates genuine fault signals (causal PR-AUC $0.292 \to 0.223$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When a single sensor out of $K$ freezes or drifts while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the isolated sensor perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$ before spherical normalization rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. On six injected streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), 1D TS-JEPA catches $19.4\%$ of quiet-fault events, standalone \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} reaches $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$, and a union with a rolling 32-step variance-collapse monitor reaches $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$. To evaluate a broader channel-level remedy across all 17 multivariate development streams ($K \in [5, 55]$, excluding univariate Yahoo), we pair the 224-bit panel with a complementary channel-level branch that monitors three causal per-channel statistics calibrated at $q_{0.999}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ with drift guards $\gamma_c^{\text{cal}}$: (i)~rolling 32-step variance collapse $\max(0, 1 - s_c(t)/\text{med}_{\text{cal}}(s_c))$, (ii)~causal first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) prediction residuals $|x_{t,c} - \hat{\phi}_{1,c} x_{t-1,c} - \hat{\phi}_{0,c}|$, and (iii)~rolling 32-step mean bias $|\bar{x}_{t,c}^{(32)} - \mu_{\text{cal},c}|$. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into the test windows of all 17 streams, adding the variance-collapse monitor lifts event recall from $61.4\%$ to $93.0\%$ on variance collapse, $63.2\%$ to $93.0\%$ on sensor freezing, and $73.7\%$ to $96.5\%$ on sensor saturation (tripling to quintupling causal F1 to $0.376\text{--}0.414$). Adding the full AR(1) and bias branch further lifts event recall on single-channel prediction shocks ($80.7\% \to 98.2\%$, causal F1 $0.139 \to 0.327$), gradual sensor degradation ($68.4\% \to 93.0\%$, F1 $0.080 \to 0.154$), and isolated sensor bias ($70.2\% \to 93.0\%$, F1 $0.095 \to 0.195$). On unmodified test streams, however, monitoring $3K$ channel statistics in parallel raises nominal causal FPR from $1.5\%$ (standalone 224-Bit) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full channel branch); thus, the variance-collapse union provides a practical safeguard for freeze and saturation faults, while full channel-residual monitoring requires cross-channel quorum gating when $K$ is large.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting across five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, and \texttt{GECCO}, three seeds) shows a clear dimensionality threshold: frequency-shell splitting substantially improves detection on high-dimensional spacecraft arrays ($K=55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K=25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), where separating slow thermal drift from high-frequency actuator chatter prevents cross-band masking, but lowers accuracy on low-channel streams ($K=9$ \texttt{Daphnet} and \texttt{GECCO}), paying off only when $K \ge 25$.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 15 model arms under matched budgets and parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five mechanisms governing false-alarm stability:
(1)~\emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations:} Using the identical causal dilated TCN encoder to forecast or reconstruct raw sensor waveforms leaves heavy residual tails ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), severe calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and high causal false-alarm rates ($\approx 25\%$) virtually unchanged.
(2)~\emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space:} Inserting a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck into the observation forecaster compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal false alarms, but predicting raw waveforms still leaves calibration drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$).
(3)~\emph{Learned latent target projection and whitening:} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ spherical space halves calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA, $p \le 0.001$).
(4)~\emph{Nonlinear TCN vs.\ linear PCA and Isolation Forest:} Linear principal-component forecasting maintains low false alarms but lacks the nonlinear capacity to capture fault dynamics, while Isolation Forest misses most anomaly events.
(5)~\emph{Prediction vs.\ autoencoding, frozen targets, and $\ell_2$ scoring:} Latent context autoencoding sacrifices causal accuracy relative to multi-horizon prediction, whereas freezing the target encoder or replacing Mahalanobis whitening with $\ell_2$ scoring still keeps causal false alarms below $2\%$, confirming that false-alarm suppression is an intrinsic geometric property of the normalized latent representation.
