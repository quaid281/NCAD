\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone. Because controls run three seeds against the five-seed principal runs, the paired control means below are computed on the shared three-seed subset and differ slightly from the five-seed Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) rows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$:} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.241 \pm 0.014$ vs. $0.244 \pm 0.024$; difference $-0.003$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.011, +0.005]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.62$); PR-AUC stays $0.347\text{--}0.348$.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$:} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.203 \pm 0.059$ vs. $0.208 \pm 0.071$ ($+0.005$, $[-0.006, +0.016]$, $p=0.41$). Covariance estimator comparisons across nine streams (\texttt{reports/heldout\_reynolds\_covmodes.csv}) demonstrate estimator invariance: instantaneous, temporal-window, and batch-empirical covariances shift Point-F1 by $|\Delta\text{F1}| \le 0.010$ (non-significant).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing \emph{improves} Point-F1 to $0.129$ from $0.096$ ($p=0.048$); both underperform TS-JEPA ($0.234$, $p=0.013$). Numerical field diagnostics (\texttt{reports/potential\_flow\_field\_diagnostics.csv}) confirm that while the unprojected spatial gradient is curl-free (antisymmetric fraction $< 10^{-7}$), spherical tangent projection introduces a $15.0\%\text{--}15.7\%$ antisymmetric curl component ($0.150$ on SMAP \texttt{P-3}, $0.157$ on MSL \texttt{M-1}).
\end{enumerate}
Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating Variant}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling and non-causal controls under an identical TS-JEPA configuration (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}, Tables~\ref{tab:gating_variants}, \ref{tab:fault_recall}). The effect is stream-dependent and macro Point-F1 favors uniform weighting ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$): gating roughly doubles recall on transient faults at $\sim$$3\times$ the nominal FPR, while quiet faults remain weakly detected under every regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$). It is therefore an evaluated variant; the deployed pipeline retains uniform input weighting.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a modest, stream-dependent effect (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)): macro buffered Point-F1 improves from $0.088$ to $0.131$ across five multivariate streams, concentrated on MSL and SMD, with a small SMAP regression. Two caveats: the block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters, and measured shell overlap means octave isolation is not attained. The front-end is a modest contributor, not the primary driver.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Threshold Calibration Rules on Nominal Splits}
\label{subsec:ablation_calibration}
The full calibrator comparison is in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}; two findings bear on the ablations: non-parametric rules match SPOT (validation-max FPR $0.0553$ vs.\ $0.0526$ at equal F1), and the kurtosis-adaptive multiplier degrades calibration ($0.0526 \to 0.1768$ FPR at indistinguishable F1).

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
To discriminate the operational mechanisms driving tail conditioning and threshold transfer, all 19 non-GHL streams were re-run under the primary held-out protocol (five seeds, identical windows, AdamW budget, and SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$ calibration) across controlled backbone arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, three dilated TCN layers): \textbf{TCN-Obs-Recon} (observation reconstruction), \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} (observation forecasting), \textbf{JEPA-CtxRecon} (latent autoencoding of EMA-encoded \emph{context} without foresight), \textbf{JEPA-NoEMA} (latent prediction with hard-copy target), and \textbf{TS-JEPA}. Five further controls probe score geometry and gating directly: \textbf{TCN-ObsPred-LN} (same forecaster, LayerNorm on the 32-dimensional bottleneck and flattened forecast, identical saliency gate), \textbf{PCA-LinPred-LN}, \textbf{Obs-MAD}, \textbf{IsoForest}, \textbf{KNN-Dist} (training-free scorers), and \textbf{TS-JEPA-NoGate} (identical model, ungated $\ell_2$ discrepancy). Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:residual_diagnostics} reports per-arm residual diagnostics; the earlier 15-stream multi-SNR study is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:controlled_backbone}.

\noindent\textbf{Mechanism Discrimination: Foresight vs. Geometry vs. Dynamics:} The controls isolate three distinct operational mechanisms:
(1)~\emph{Future temporal forecasting is not uniquely necessary for score conditioning}: \textbf{JEPA-CtxRecon} autoencodes context representations without forecasting future timesteps, yet achieves an FPR reduction of $0.202$ ($0.310 \to 0.108$ relative to \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred}) and higher event recall ($0.513$ vs.\ $0.473$ for TS-JEPA; Table~\ref{tab:residual_diagnostics}). Latent representation compression, rather than future-state extrapolation, conditions scores for tail estimation.
(2)~\emph{Bounded score geometry mechanically suppresses tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2$)}: projecting through a low-dimensional LayerNorm bottleneck compresses residual excursions regardless of architecture, as \textbf{TCN-ObsPred-LN} suppresses kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.14$ and \textbf{PCA-LinPred-LN} achieves $0.87$ (vs.\ $8.24$ for unnormalized \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} and $1.14$ for \textbf{TS-JEPA}).
(3)~\emph{Learned representation dynamics govern out-of-sample threshold transfer and discriminative recall}: kurtosis suppression alone cannot prevent threshold transfer breakdown across temporal blocks. TCN-ObsPred-LN suffers held-out nominal FPR of $0.186$ (buffered) and $0.117$ (causal trailing), versus $0.080$ and $0.014$ for TS-JEPA. At conservative budgets, PCA-LinPred-LN and \textbf{IsoForest} collapse to event recalls of $0.665$ and $0.631$ versus $0.774$ for TS-JEPA ($0.768$ ungated). Geometric contraction conditions sample kurtosis, but learned representations are associated with lower nominal overshoot without sacrificing downstream recall.

\noindent\textbf{Target EMA and Window Horizons:} Removing the EMA target changes little (JEPA-NoEMA: $-0.033$ Point-F1 vs.\ TS-JEPA, $p{=}0.17$); PR-AUC differences across arms are inconclusive ($p \ge 0.16$). Native-window checks (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:native_window_baselines}) confirm that baseline tail heaviness does not arise from the $W{=}320$ window: at native horizons, TranAD ($W{=}10$) spikes to $\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and TimesNet ($W{=}96$) reaches $\gamma_2 = 98.14$ ($44.09\%$ FPR), while TS-JEPA retains $\gamma_2 = 3.14$.
