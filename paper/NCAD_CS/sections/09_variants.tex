\subsection{Ablation 1: Panel Components and Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) evaluate perspective expansion and panel components on the 19-stream residual cache (not paired to the separately trained run in Panel~(c)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Scientific role of the 224-dial reference vs.\ compact panels:} More dials do not make a better operational detector; we keep 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as a diagnostic reference because scoring all four views on one residual tensor isolates which views aid live causal versus buffered attribution. Against 1D scoring on this cache, 224 dials raise causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ ($p=0.0023$); the buffered gain of $0.033$ is not significant. Pruning the fluctuation, endpoint, and relay dials to 160 position-plus-drift dials helps live reporting: causal Point-F1 rises to $0.098$ and ER to $59.1\%$, delay falls from $90.1$ to $74.8$ steps, and $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ falls from $0.76\%$ to $0.55\%$. Buffered Point-F1 drops from $0.224$ to $0.198$. Retraining all three panels on the 118 untouched archive streams with identical splits (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)) gives a similar picture. Pruning $224\to 160$ dials cuts buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $19.2\%$ to $13.0\%$ ($p=3.7\times 10^{-15}$) at nearly the same causal Point-F1 ($0.073$ vs.\ $0.074$), and the 128-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} matches or exceeds both, with causal and buffered Point-F1 of $0.078$ and $0.154$, at lower cost.
    \item \emph{Relays, PR-AUC score weighting, quorum rules, and hijack guards:} Removing the 32 onset relays lowers causal Point-F1 ($0.071 \to 0.068$), and replacing the three-counter quorum (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) with one 224-dial quorum lowers causal Point-F1 to $0.063$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0055$; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}). Replacing the double-position continuous score $N_{\text{tot}} = N_{224} + N_{128} + 2N_{\text{edge}}$ with unweighted $N_{224}+N_{\text{edge}}$ or $N_{224}$ leaves binary alarms unchanged and changes PR-AUC by at most $0.004$ on this cache and $0.0016$ on the 118 untouched streams (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses unweighted $N_{128}$). Removing hijack guards ($\gamma_j=1$) raises causal Point-F1 to $0.073$ ($70.1$-step delay) on this cache when nominal drift is mild, so the guards protect against drift and add no accuracy. No panel component removes the $S-1$ reporting lag.
    \item \emph{Quenching step isolation and LayerNorm coupling:} We tested each quench step in isolation on single-seed \emph{Laminar-JEPA} checkpoints across all $118$ untouched streams (unquenched: causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 6.76\%$, $\text{F1} = 0.0901$). Damping $k=4$ predictor rows (Step Q1) shifts unselected coordinates by a median $11.1\%$ of nominal RMS through 32D LayerNorm coupling and does not lower $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($7.06\%$). Recentering and decoupling (Steps Q2--Q3) lower it to $6.71\%$ but cut causal F1 to $0.0826$. The split gate (Step Q4, accepted on $39/118$ streams) reaches $6.62\%$ with F1 unchanged ($0.0904$). Because ungated edits hurt $k=2$ causal $\text{F1}$ on $11/19$ development streams ($0.141\to 0.049$) and $k\in\{8,12\}$ cuts PR-AUC ($0.292\to 0.223$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} omits weight editing altogether.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Marginal, multiplicity-controlled, and system-matched detector banks:} Simple per-coordinate alarms either fire constantly without a quorum, almost never fire under multiplicity correction on short calibration sets, or lose their calibration out of sample. We compare three calibration regimes on the same 19-stream whitened residuals without test-label tuning (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}):
(1)~\emph{Per-coordinate marginal union:} Thresholding 128 coordinates individually at $q_{0.985}$ or even at the panel's $0.34\%$ rate without a quorum compounds false alarms across coordinates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 66.5\%$ and $40.6\%$).
(2)~\emph{Family-wise and false discovery rate (FDR) multiplicity control:} Applying \v{S}id\'{a}k ($0.5\%$) across 128 coordinates or BH FDR ($q=0.005$) across 224 dials hits the discrete conformal $p$-value floor $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1} > 3.9\times 10^{-5}$, paralyzing causal recall ($\text{F1} \le 0.001$).
(3)~\emph{System-level matched nominal alarm rate:} Calibrating the aggregated statistic directly to each stream's nominal panel rate ($0.34\%$) shows that discrete conformal $\min_j p_j$ still jumps to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 21.1\%$ out of sample from boundary ties, and recalibrated Mahalanobis conformal, Hotelling $T^2$, MEWMA, and CUSUM inflate to $27.9\%\text{--}58.6\%$. Among stable rules on this cache, the 160-dial panel reaches Point-F1 $0.098$ at $0.55\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, above 128-coordinate top-$k$, $\max_j z_j$, and $\ell_2^2$ conformal ($0.068$, $0.056$, $0.072$). Fisher pooling reaches higher Point-F1 ($0.109$; Stouffer $0.095$) at nearly double the held-out FPR, $1.05\%$. Archive comparisons of these aggregators on frozen \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} residuals appear in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
A frozen or flatlined sensor barely moves the latent summary: terminal LayerNorm spreads the small change across all $32$ coordinates, so on \texttt{SMAP P-3} a flatline trips $12.5$ dials versus $62.1$ for a variance-matched harmonic flip, and a single faulty sensor is further diluted by the $K-1$ healthy channels. Extending an initial six-stream study, we evaluate a causal channel branch across all $19$ multivariate development streams ($K\in[5,55]$) without per-stream or test-label tuning. Per channel, three 64-step rolling statistics---log-variance drop $-\ln(\operatorname{Var}_{64}(x_c)+10^{-4})$, training-fit first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) residual RMS, and mean shift $|\bar{x}_{64,c}-\mu_c|/\sigma_c$---are thresholded at $1.25\,q_{0.999}$ on nominal validation windows (floor $6.0$ MAD, $1.25\times$ half-split drift multiplier) with a $24$-of-$40$-step persistence gate. On the highest-variance active channel we inject three nonoverlapping $200$-step faults per stream into clean test segments ($57$ events per class): variance collapse to the training mean, an alternating $\pm1.35\sigma$ prediction shock, sensor freezing, saturation at $\max(q_{.995},\mu+2.2\sigma)$, a linear $2.5\sigma$ degradation, and a $1.5\sigma$ bias. The variance-collapse union lifts injected-event recall for variance collapse, freezing, and saturation from $63.2\%$--$82.5\%$ to $93.0\%$--$94.7\%$, and the full channel branch lifts recall on shocks, gradual degradation, and bias from $59.6\%$--$75.4\%$ to $80.7\%$--$96.5\%$. However, on the \emph{unmodified} test streams, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $1.5\%$ (standalone panel, $\text{F1}=0.071$) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union, $\text{F1}=0.070$) and $19.4\%$ (full branch, $\text{F1}=0.129$). Synthetic single-channel injections do not establish detection on natural sensor failures, and the full union's $19.4\%$ false-alarm rate makes it unsuitable as a default detector.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Retraining the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting on five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, \texttt{GECCO}; three seeds) shows a channel-count threshold: shell splitting improves high-channel spacecraft detection ($K{=}55$ \texttt{MSL M-1} buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$; $K{=}25$ \texttt{SMAP P-3} causal F1 $0.212 \to 0.321$), but lowers F1 on both $K{=}9$ streams (\texttt{Daphnet}, \texttt{GECCO}), setting the $K \ge 25$ spectral gate in \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
The 16 matched-budget arms of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) separate contributing factors on the development streams; they are not universal causes. Raw-observation forecasting and autoencoding share heavy tails, large drift, and high causal FPR ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$, $D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$, FPR $\approx 25\%$). A $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space cuts kurtosis to $\gamma_2 = 0.91$ and halves FPR but keeps drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$). Learned latent targets and whitening halve drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179\text{--}0.214$, $p \le 0.001$). PCA and Isolation Forest keep causal FPR low but reach only $0.017$ and $0.041$ causal Point-F1. The panels' gains do not come from lighter tails alone: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps roughly the same kurtosis as Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) ($\gamma_2 = 2.33$ vs.\ $2.30$).
