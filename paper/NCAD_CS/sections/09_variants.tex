\subsection{Ablation 1: 1D Scalar Collapse vs.\ 128-Bit and 224-Bit Multi-Perspective Circuit Breakers}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
What unlocks the accuracy and speed of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA on the identical trained backbone? Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) trace the step-by-step progression across all 19 non-GHL streams. First, under 1D scalar scoring, the unmodulated multi-horizon whitened envelope ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$) and the Lieb--Thirring soliton-modulated score ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0.12$) achieve strong false-alarm suppression ($0.6\%\text{--}0.7\%$ causal FPR) and satisfy TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.020$ ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$), but collapsing 128 whitened coordinates into a single 1D score allows isolated drifting coordinates to inflate the scalar threshold, restricting causal Point-F1 to $0.052\text{--}0.055$ and delay to $246.6\text{--}248.0$ steps. Second, replacing 1D collapse with independent per-coordinate binary switches over the $128$ sub-block magnitudes (\emph{128-Bit Sub-Block}) immediately lifts causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.129$, buffered Point-F1 from $0.137$ to $0.192$, and causal event recall from $39.8\%$ to $59.0\%$. Third, expanding the same 32 latent coordinates into $7 \times 32 = 224$ fluid-dynamic dials (adding Reynolds mean-flow drift, turbulent fluctuation, and convective right-edge acceleration) and 32 fast right-edge onset relays in \emph{224-Bit Breaker} raises causal Point-F1 to $0.138$ ($17$ wins / $0$ losses, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), buffered Point-F1 to $0.228$ ($p = 0.026$), buffered PR-AUC from $0.370$ to $0.411$, and event recall to $60.7\%$ causal ($63.1\%$ buffered) while cutting detection delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps causally ($402.7$ to $71.8$ steps buffered). Finally, adding a soft temporal and cross-perspective attention pooling gate over the 224 dials (\emph{224-Bit + Soft Attn}) offers no further gain ($0.137$ causal and $0.226$ buffered F1), confirming that discrete Boolean switches prevent cross-coordinate dilution without requiring continuous attention weights.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Quiet Faults, Input Gating, and Hybrid Monitoring}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When one sensor out of $K$ freezes at a nominal value while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the frozen channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$, and LayerNorm rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ using the remaining active channels. Pre-weighting input channels by context variance lowers macro Point-F1 across four multivariate streams from $0.118$ to $0.077$, so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), uniform TS-JEPA detects $27.8\%$ of abrupt $+5\sigma$ spikes, but on persistent quiet faults (flatlines, stuck sensors, and zero dropouts) event recall falls to $19.4\%$ and point recall drops below $3\%$. Simple observation-space monitors catch these complementary failures: a maximum channel deviation monitor and a rolling variance-collapse monitor (tracking how far each channel's 32-step trailing standard deviation drops below its nominal median) each recover $25\%\text{--}39\%$ of quiet-fault events at $14\%\text{--}15\%$ FPR. Taking the logical union of TS-JEPA alarms and the rolling variance-collapse monitor lifts event recall to $41.7\%\text{--}50.0\%$ across all six fault classes at $17.0\%$ nominal FPR.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) trains TS-JEPA with the identical 3-layer causal TCN encoder across five streams, comparing the 4-shell Fourier filterbank against a standard linear convolutional stem. Enabling the dyadic front-end raises macro PR-AUC from $0.213$ to $0.263$ (led by SMD and GECCO) and lowers validation residual kurtosis from $1.41$ to $1.07$, while macro Point-F1 under 1D thresholding decreases from $0.118$ to $0.078$ on SMAP P-3. Frequency-shell splitting thus improves score ranking and tail conditioning.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the 14 model arms across all 19 non-GHL streams under identical splits, training budgets, and matched parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster vs.\ TCN Obs.\ Autoencoder):} Pairing the same causal dilated TCN encoder with a multilayer perceptron decoder to either forecast the 64-step target window or reconstruct the 320-step window in raw sensor space leaves tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and causal FPR ($\approx 25\%$) nearly identical.
    \item \emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster (LN)):} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck and target-window LayerNorm to the observation forecaster compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but targeting raw sensor waveforms leaves calibration drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and drops causal Point-F1 to $0.084$.
    \item \emph{Learned latent target projection, multi-horizon whitening, and 224-bit circuit breakers (TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit):} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ latent space cuts calibration drift nearly in half ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), while the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel lifts buffered PR-AUC to $0.411$, buffered Point-F1 to $0.228$, and causal event recall to $0.607$ at $1.7\%$ held-out FPR.
    \item \emph{Nonlinear TCN encoding vs.\ linear PCA and tree isolation (PCA Linear Forecaster (LN) and Isolation Forest):} Replacing the nonlinear TCN with 32 principal components and ridge regression bounds causal FPR to $2.1\%$ through LayerNorm, but causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ (and Isolation Forest reaches only $0.149$ causal event recall), confirming the need for learned nonlinear temporal features.
    \item \emph{Future prediction vs.\ context autoencoding, EMA stability, and $\ell_2$ scoring (JEPA Context Autoencoder, JEPA Hard-Target Copy, and TS-JEPA ($\ell_2$ Scoring)):} Context autoencoding in latent space keeps causal FPR low ($1.7\%$) but drops causal Point-F1 from $0.161$ to $0.112$ and PR-AUC from $0.278$ to $0.235$. Direct weight copying ($\zeta = 0$) or Euclidean $\ell_2$ scoring still holds causal FPR below $2\%$ ($0.9\%$ and $1.8\%$), confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent representation.
\end{enumerate}
