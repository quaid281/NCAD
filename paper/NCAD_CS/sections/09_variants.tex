\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder and Circuit-Breaker Component Analysis}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
How do each of the seven fluid-dynamic perspectives and three calibration rules contribute to the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel on the identical trained backbone? Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate the step-by-step perspective expansion ladder and leave-one-out component ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (57 runs):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{From 1D scalar collapse to 32-bit and 128-bit sub-horizon switches (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)):} Collapsing the 128 whitened coordinates into a single 1D score (whether modulated by $R_{\text{LT}}$ at $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0.12$ or unmodulated at $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$, which satisfy TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.020$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$) allows one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the scalar threshold, restricting causal Point-F1 to $0.052$ and delay to $246.6$ steps. Replacing 1D collapse with 32 independent switches on the terminal horizon ($m=4$) nearly doubles causal Point-F1 to $0.101$, and expanding across all four nested sub-horizons (\emph{128-Bit Sub-Horizons}) lifts causal Point-F1 to $0.129$, buffered Point-F1 from $0.137$ to $0.192$, and buffered PR-AUC from $0.370$ to $0.402$.
    \item \emph{Adding Reynolds mean-flow drift, turbulent fluctuation, and convective right-edge acceleration:} Appending the 32 Reynolds mean-flow drift dials in \emph{160-Bit} cuts causal detection delay in half from $146.2$ to $69.1$ steps and lifts causal Point-F1 to $0.132$. Adding the 32 Reynolds turbulent-fluctuation dials in \emph{192-Bit} lifts buffered Point-F1 to $0.203$ and buffered ER to $56.7\%$. Finally, adding the 32 convective right-edge acceleration dials and 32 onset relays in \emph{224-Bit Breaker} reaches $0.138$ causal Point-F1 ($17$ wins / $0$ losses over 1D collapse, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), $0.228$ buffered Point-F1 ($p = 0.026$), $0.411$ buffered PR-AUC, $60.7\%$ causal and $63.1\%$ buffered ER, and $71.8$-step buffered delay.
    \item \emph{Leave-one-out component ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Removing the 32 fast right-edge onset relays slows causal detection delay from $105.9$ to $123.7$ steps and lowers causal Point-F1 from $0.138$ to $0.135$. Replacing physical prefix-horizon routing with uniform 64-step window smearing degrades buffered ER from $63.1\%$ to $58.4\%$, buffered Point-F1 from $0.228$ to $0.223$, and buffered delay from $71.8$ to $88.6$ steps. Removing per-dial hijack guards ($\gamma_j = 1$) lowers causal and buffered F1 to $0.137$ and $0.225$, whereas adding a soft attention pooling gate provides no gain over discrete Boolean switches ($0.137$ causal and $0.226$ buffered F1).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Quiet Faults and the 224-Bit Hybrid Variance-Collapse Monitor}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When one sensor out of $K$ freezes at a nominal level while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the frozen channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$, and LayerNorm rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ using the remaining active channels. Pre-weighting input channels by context variance degrades macro Point-F1 across multivariate streams ($0.118 \to 0.077$), so the deployed pipeline retains uniform input encoding. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into six multivariate streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), 1D TS-JEPA detects only $19.4\%$ of quiet-fault events (flatlines, stuck sensors, and dropouts) with $<3\%$ point recall. Because the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel monitors all 32 latent coordinates independently without 1D score collapse, standalone \emph{Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Breaker} lifts quiet-fault event recall to $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ ($33.3\%$ on $+5\sigma$ spikes) and increases point recall more than seven-fold to $14.3\%\text{--}25.6\%$. Pairing the 224-bit panel in a logical union with a parameter-free observation-space rolling variance-collapse monitor (tracking how far each channel's 32-step trailing standard deviation drops below its nominal median) lifts event recall to $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ and point recall to $24.3\%\text{--}35.6\%$ across all six single-channel fault classes.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
To isolate the effect of the 4-shell rFFT front-end under the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, we train Soliton-Reynolds JEPA with and without dyadic frequency-shell splitting across five benchmark streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, and \texttt{GECCO water-quality}, three seeds). On high-dimensional spacecraft telemetry with multi-frequency actuator oscillations, the dyadic front-end raises Point-F1 and PR-AUC: on \texttt{MSL M-1} ($K=55$), buffered Point-F1 jumps from $0.085$ to $0.410$ and PR-AUC from $0.505$ to $0.757$, and on \texttt{SMAP P-3} ($K=25$), causal Point-F1 rises from $0.212$ to $0.321$ and PR-AUC from $0.367$ to $0.415$. It also cuts mean detection delay across the five streams from $28.6$ to $26.2$ steps causally and from $23.5$ to $17.9$ steps buffered while lowering validation residual kurtosis from $1.41$ to $1.07$. Conversely, on lower-dimensional kinematic and water-quality streams ($K=9$), a plain time-domain convolutional stem achieves higher Point-F1 ($0.217$ vs.\ $0.136$ on \texttt{Daphnet} and $0.321$ vs.\ $0.158$ on \texttt{GECCO}), yielding a 5-stream macro causal Point-F1 of $0.193$ for the standard stem versus $0.156$ for the dyadic stem. Frequency-shell decomposition is thus most beneficial on high-channel telemetry with distinct spectral bands.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing all 15 model arms across the 19 non-GHL streams under identical splits, training budgets, and matched parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five architectural factors:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster vs.\ TCN Obs.\ Autoencoder):} Pairing the same causal dilated TCN encoder with a multilayer perceptron decoder to either forecast the 64-step target window or reconstruct the 320-step window in raw sensor space leaves tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and causal FPR ($\approx 25\%$) nearly identical.
    \item \emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space (TCN Obs.\ Forecaster (LN)):} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck and target-window LayerNorm to the observation forecaster compresses tail kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but targeting raw sensor waveforms leaves calibration drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$) and drops causal Point-F1 to $0.084$.
    \item \emph{Learned latent target projection, multi-horizon whitening, and 224-bit circuit breakers (TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit):} Moving the prediction target into the learned $D=32$ latent space cuts calibration drift nearly in half ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, paired Wilcoxon $p \le 0.001$), while the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel lifts buffered PR-AUC to $0.411$, buffered Point-F1 to $0.228$, and causal event recall to $0.607$ at $1.7\%$ held-out FPR.
    \item \emph{Nonlinear TCN encoding vs.\ linear PCA and tree isolation (PCA Linear Forecaster (LN) and Isolation Forest):} Replacing the nonlinear TCN with 32 principal components and ridge regression bounds causal FPR to $2.1\%$ through LayerNorm, but causal Point-F1 collapses to $0.017$ (and Isolation Forest reaches only $0.149$ causal event recall), confirming the need for learned nonlinear temporal features.
    \item \emph{Future prediction vs.\ context autoencoding, EMA stability, and $\ell_2$ scoring (JEPA Context Autoencoder, JEPA Hard-Target Copy, and TS-JEPA ($\ell_2$ Scoring)):} Context autoencoding in latent space keeps causal FPR low ($1.7\%$) but drops causal Point-F1 from $0.161$ to $0.112$ and PR-AUC from $0.278$ to $0.235$. Direct weight copying ($\zeta = 0$) or Euclidean $\ell_2$ scoring still holds causal FPR below $2\%$ ($0.9\%$ and $1.8\%$), confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent representation.
\end{enumerate}
