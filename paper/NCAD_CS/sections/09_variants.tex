\section{Ablation Studies and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Ablation 1: Physical Regularizer Controls on Fixed Architectures}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
To test whether the physics-inspired regularizers benefit detection, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) reports controlled ablations across all 19 non-GHL streams (principal models at five seeds, controls at three, under the held-out protocol), disabling each physical term on a fixed backbone:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$:} Disabling the spectral regularizer leaves Point-F1 unchanged ($0.241 \pm 0.014$ vs. $0.244 \pm 0.024$; difference $-0.003$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.011, +0.005]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.62$); PR-AUC stays $0.347\text{--}0.348$.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. $\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$:} Disabling the stress loss yields $0.203 \pm 0.059$ vs. $0.208 \pm 0.071$ ($+0.005$, $[-0.006, +0.016]$, $p=0.41$); the null is also insensitive to the covariance estimator (temporal/batch covariances shift F1 by $\le 0.010$ over nine streams).
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. no-regime ($K_r{=}1$):} Disabling regime routing \emph{improves} Point-F1 to $0.129$ from $0.096$ ($p=0.048$); both underperform TS-JEPA ($0.234$, $p=0.013$).
\end{enumerate}
Across the full 19-stream suite, the physical regularizers produce no statistically detectable improvement over unconstrained latent prediction.

\subsection{Ablation 2: Auxiliary Input-Channel Gating Variant}
The auxiliary causal input-channel gate is compared against uniform pooling and non-causal controls under identical TS-JEPA configuration (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}, Tables~\ref{tab:gating_variants}, \ref{tab:fault_recall}). The effect is stream-dependent and macro Point-F1 favors uniform weighting ($0.118$ vs.\ $0.077$): gating roughly doubles recall on transient faults at $\sim$$3\times$ the nominal FPR, while quiet faults remain weakly detected under every regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$). It is therefore an evaluated variant; the deployed pipeline retains uniform input weighting.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End}
Replacing the standard front-end with the Fourier-domain dyadic shells plus per-shell causal convolutions and learned routing yields a modest, stream-dependent effect (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)): macro buffered Point-F1 improves from $0.088$ to $0.131$ across five multivariate streams, concentrated on MSL and SMD, with a small SMAP regression. Two caveats: the block adds $\approx 1.6\times 10^5$ parameters, and measured shell overlap means octave isolation is not attained. The front-end is a modest contributor, not the primary driver.

\subsection{Ablation 4: Threshold Calibration Rules on Nominal Splits}
\label{subsec:ablation_calibration}
The full calibrator comparison is in Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}; two findings bear on the ablations: non-parametric rules match SPOT (validation-max FPR $0.0553$ vs.\ $0.0526$ at equal F1), and the kurtosis-adaptive multiplier degrades calibration ($0.0526 \to 0.1768$ FPR at indistinguishable F1).

\subsection{Controlled Backbone Comparison: Latent Space vs. Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Does predicting in a contracted latent space condition residuals for EVT thresholding, or is the gain attributable to prediction, compression, or the EMA target encoder? To isolate the mechanism, all 19 non-GHL streams were re-run under the primary held-out protocol (five seeds, identical windows, AdamW budget, and SPOT $u{=}0.98/q{=}10^{-3}$ calibration) with five arms sharing the \texttt{HybridTCNEncoder} backbone ($C{=}256, S{=}64$, $d{=}32$, three dilated TCN layers): \textbf{TCN-Obs-Recon} (observation reconstruction), \textbf{TCN-Obs-Pred} (observation forecasting), \textbf{JEPA-CtxRecon} (latent ``prediction'' of the EMA-encoded \emph{context} --- compression without foresight), \textbf{JEPA-NoEMA} (latent prediction with an online hard-copy target), and \textbf{TS-JEPA}. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:residual_diagnostics} reports the per-arm residual diagnostics (tail quantiles, excess kurtosis, extremal index, ACF, effective exceedance counts); the earlier 15-stream multi-SNR study remains in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:controlled_backbone}.

\noindent\textbf{Latent space, not forecasting, conditions the tail:} Observation-space objectives produce virtually identical heavy tails regardless of direction (TCN-Obs-Recon vs.\ TCN-Obs-Pred: $\gamma_2$ $8.31$/$8.24$, held-out FPR $0.303$/$0.310$, extremal index $\theta \approx 0.12$). Latent objectives compress tails $\approx$$8\times$ ($\gamma_2$ $1.0$--$1.6$, $\theta \approx 0.18$) and cut held-out FPR $\approx$$3\times$: TS-JEPA $-0.230$ vs.\ TCN-Obs-Pred (Wilcoxon $p{=}0.031$) and $-0.223$ vs.\ TCN-Obs-Recon ($p{=}0.058$). Forecasting in raw space does not condition residuals because they directly reflect sensor jitter.

\noindent\textbf{Compression vs.\ prediction vs.\ EMA:} JEPA-CtxRecon --- which never predicts --- retains most of the conditioning ($-0.203$ vs.\ TCN-Obs-Pred, $p{=}0.055$; only $-0.028$ below TS-JEPA, $p{=}0.23$), and removing the EMA target changes little (JEPA-NoEMA: $-0.033$ vs.\ TS-JEPA, $p{=}0.17$). The threshold-conditioning gain is therefore attributable primarily to \emph{scoring in the contracted latent space}, with the predictive objective and EMA target as secondary contributors. PR-AUC differences across all five arms are inconclusive ($p \ge 0.16$): the effect is residual conditioning, not improved anomaly--nominal separation.

\noindent\textbf{Native Window Horizons:} Baselines do not suffer merely from the long $W{=}320$ window: at their native horizons tail conditioning \emph{worsens} (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:native_window_baselines}). TranAD at $W{=}10$ spikes to $\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and TimesNet at $W{=}96$ reaches $\gamma_2 = 98.14$ with $44.09\%$ empirical FPR, while TS-JEPA retains $\gamma_2 = 3.14$: raw-space tail corruption is inherent, not a window-length artifact.
