\subsection{Ablation 1: Perspective Expansion Ladder, Circuit-Breaker Components, and Weight-Space Quenching}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}
\label{subsec:factorial}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) evaluate the step-by-step expansion ladder, component removals, and surgical weight-space quenching ablations culminating in \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} on the 19-stream development cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{From 1D scalar collapse to 32-bit and 128-bit sub-horizon switches (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)):} Collapsing the 128 whitened coordinates into a 1D score (with or without $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation; $\alpha_{\text{LT}}=0.12$ changes causal F1 by $-0.005$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.012, 0.000]$) lets one or two drifting coordinates inflate the scalar threshold ($0.052$ causal F1, $246.6$-step delay). Using 32 per-coordinate switches on the terminal horizon ($m=4$) nearly doubles causal F1 to $0.101$, and expanding across all four prefixes (\emph{128-Bit Sub-Horizons}) lifts causal F1 to $0.129$ and buffered F1 from $0.137$ to $0.192$, serving as plain multi-coordinate baselines.
    \item \emph{Adding horizon mean, fluctuation, endpoint change, and surgical quenching:} The 32 drift dials (\emph{160-Bit}) halve causal delay ($146.2 \to 69.1$ steps); the 32 fluctuation dials (\emph{192-Bit}) lift buffered F1 to $0.203$ ($56.7\%$ ER) at a longer causal delay ($179.0$ steps). Completing the 224-bit panel with the 32 endpoint-change dials, onset relays, and surgical weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) reaches $0.262$ buffered Point-F1 ($p = 0.026$ vs.\ 1D collapse), $66.4\%$ buffered ER, $62.5\%$ causal ER, $1.6\%$ causal FPR, and $59.6$-step causal onset delay ($0.100$ causal Point-F1; $17/0$ stream wins over 1D collapse, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$).
    \item \emph{Leave-one-out panel ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)):} Removing weight-space quenching slows causal delay from $59.6$ to $105.9$ steps, lowers causal ER ($62.5\% \to 60.7\%$) and buffered F1 ($0.262 \to 0.228$), and raises causal FPR ($1.6\% \to 1.7\%$). Removing the 32 onset relays slows causal delay further to $123.7$ steps ($60.2\%$ ER). Replacing prefix routing with uniform 64-step smearing degrades buffered ER ($63.1\% \to 58.4\%$) and F1 ($0.228 \to 0.223$). Removing hijack guards ($\gamma_j = 1$) raises causal FPR to $2.1\%$ and lowers buffered F1 to $0.225$, and soft attention adds no gain ($0.226$ buffered F1).
    \item \emph{Surgical weight-space quenching components and sensitivity ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{0,\dots,12\}$):} Disentangling the two quenching operations of Section~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel} across the 19 development streams (seed 42) shows distinct roles: (i)~predictor-bias recentering alone ($\Delta\mathbf{b}_m$) lifts causal ER ($56.5\% \to 64.3\%$), causal F1 ($0.071 \to 0.084$), and buffered F1 ($0.224 \to 0.256$) and cuts causal delay ($90.1 \to 63.8$ steps), but raises test FPR ($1.5\% \to 2.8\%$ causal; $17.4\% \to 23.3\%$ buffered); (ii)~untargeted precision tempering alone degrades buffered F1 ($0.224 \to 0.135$) and PR-AUC ($0.449 \to 0.271$); and (iii)~RMS stabilization (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) alone lifts causal F1 to $0.099$ ($59.9\%$ ER). Sweeping $k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 12\}$ confirms that tempering the top $k_{\text{rogue}} \in [1, 4]$ coordinates with internal calibration-split gating (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) preserves event recall ($61.4\%\text{--}64.6\%$) while cutting buffered FPR ($24.2\% \to 19.2\%$ on $n{=}19$) and archive-wide causal FPR across 162 streams ($6.71\% \to 6.17\%$ held-out, $p = 0.0011$), whereas over-quenching ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{8, 12\}$) lowers causal PR-AUC ($0.292 \to 0.223$) and buffered F1 ($0.215 \to 0.144$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation 2: Single-Channel Faults and Complementary Channel-Level Anomaly Branch}
\label{subsec:gating_ablation}
When one sensor out of $K$ freezes or degrades while the remaining $K-1$ channels fluctuate normally, the single channel perturbs the pooled pre-LayerNorm vector by only $O(1/K)$, and LayerNorm rescales the embedding onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. On six injected streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)), 1D TS-JEPA detects $19.4\%$ of quiet-fault events ($<3\%$ point recall); the standalone 224-bit panel lifts quiet-fault event recall to $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ ($14.3\%\text{--}25.6\%$ point recall), and a union with a rolling 32-step variance-collapse monitor reaches $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$. To test a broader channel-level remedy across all 17 multivariate development streams ($K \in [5, 55]$, excluding univariate Yahoo), we evaluate a complementary channel-level branch alongside the 224-bit panel. For each channel $c \in \{1,\dots,K\}$, the branch tracks three causal statistics calibrated at $q_{0.999}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ with per-channel calibration drift guards $\gamma_c^{\text{cal}}$: (i)~rolling 32-step channel variance collapse $\max(0, 1 - s_c(t)/\text{med}_{\text{cal}}(s_c))$, (ii)~causal first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) channel prediction residuals $|x_{t,c} - \hat{\phi}_{1,c} x_{t-1,c} - \hat{\phi}_{0,c}|$, and (iii)~rolling 32-step channel mean bias $|\bar{x}_{t,c}^{(32)} - \mu_{\text{cal},c}|$. Across six single-channel fault classes injected into the test windows of all 17 multivariate streams, comparing standalone \emph{224-Bit Panel} $\to$ \emph{Hybrid Variance-Collapse Monitor} $\to$ \emph{224-Bit + Full Channel Branch} yields: (1)~variance collapse: ER $61.4\% \to 93.0\% \to 93.0\%$ (causal F1 $0.077 \to 0.376 \to 0.269$); (2)~channel prediction shocks: $80.7\% \to 82.5\% \to 98.2\%$ (F1 $0.139 \to 0.139 \to 0.327$, delay $48.6 \to 35.9$); (3)~sensor freezing: $63.2\% \to 93.0\% \to 93.0\%$ (F1 $0.052 \to 0.378 \to 0.273$); (4)~sensor saturation: $73.7\% \to 96.5\% \to 96.5\%$ (F1 $0.113 \to 0.414 \to 0.368$, delay $50.7 \to 34.3$); (5)~gradual degradation: $68.4\% \to 70.2\% \to 93.0\%$ (F1 $0.080 \to 0.078 \to 0.154$); and (6)~isolated sensor bias: $70.2\% \to 71.9\% \to 93.0\%$ (F1 $0.095 \to 0.094 \to 0.195$). On unmodified test data across the 17 streams, however, monitoring $3K$ channel statistics in parallel raises causal FPR from $1.5\%$ (standalone 224-Bit) to $6.8\%$ (variance-collapse union) and $19.4\%$ (full channel branch); thus, the variance-collapse union offers a practical compromise for freeze/saturation faults ($93.0\%\text{--}96.5\%$ ER at $6.8\%$ FPR), whereas full channel-residual monitoring requires cross-channel quorum gating when $K$ is large.

\subsection{Ablation 3: Multiscale Dyadic-Shell Front-End vs.\ Standard Convolutional Stem}
\label{subsec:dyadic_ablation}
Training the multi-horizon JEPA backbone with and without 4-shell rFFT splitting across five streams (\texttt{SMAP P-3}, \texttt{MSL M-1}, \texttt{SMD machine-1-2}, \texttt{Daphnet S01R01E1}, and \texttt{GECCO}, three seeds) shows that shell splitting helps on high-channel spacecraft arrays (buffered F1 $0.085 \to 0.410$ on \texttt{MSL M-1}, $K=55$; causal $0.212 \to 0.321$ on \texttt{SMAP P-3}, $K=25$) but hurts on $K=9$ streams ($0.136$ vs.\ $0.217$ on \texttt{Daphnet}, $0.158$ vs.\ $0.321$ on \texttt{GECCO}; 5-stream causal F1 $0.156$ vs.\ $0.193$), paying off only when $K \ge 25$.

\subsection{Mechanism-Discrimination Study: Latent Space vs.\ Observation Space}
\label{subsec:controlled_backbone}
Comparing the model arms across the 19 development streams under matched budgets and parameter counts in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) isolates five factors:
(1)~\emph{Forecasting vs.\ reconstructing raw observations:} Using the same causal dilated TCN encoder to forecast or reconstruct in raw sensor space leaves tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 \approx 6.2$), drift ($D_{\text{KS}} \approx 0.45$), and causal FPR ($\approx 25\%$) nearly identical.
(2)~\emph{LayerNorm bottleneck in observation space:} Adding a $D=32$ LayerNorm bottleneck to the observation forecaster compresses kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2 = 0.91$) and halves causal FPR to $11.7\%$, but raw-waveform targets leave drift high ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$, causal F1 $0.084$).
(3)~\emph{Learned latent target projection and whitening:} Moving the target into the learned $D=32$ latent space halves calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ in TS-JEPA and $0.179$ in Laminar-JEPA, $p \le 0.001$).
(4)~\emph{Nonlinear TCN vs.\ linear PCA and Isolation Forest:} 32 principal components keep causal FPR at $2.1\%$ but drop causal F1 to $0.017$, and Isolation Forest reaches $0.149$ causal ER.
(5)~\emph{Prediction vs.\ autoencoding, frozen targets, and $\ell_2$ scoring:} Latent context autoencoding lowers causal F1 from $0.161$ to $0.112$, while a frozen target encoder or $\ell_2$ scoring still holds causal FPR below $2\%$, confirming that false-alarm reduction stems from the normalized latent representation.
