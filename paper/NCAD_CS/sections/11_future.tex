\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We formulated Operator-Entropy JEPA for multi-sensor anomaly detection and evaluated latent score conditioning, discriminative detection, and operational calibration across 33 telemetry streams under a strict held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA and False-Alarm Suppression:} OpEntropy JEPA suppresses nominal false alarms under static calibration by four- to five-fold compared with reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD), resolving severe alarm flooding on complex telemetry (NASA SMAP/MSL). However, this conservatism does not establish universal detection dominance: paired Point-F1 and PR-AUC differences remain statistically comparable, and causal streaming without lookahead incurs an event recall penalty on quiet flatlines and subtle drifts.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation of Physical Regularizers:} The tested regularizers provide no aggregate detection benefit over unconstrained TS-JEPA: Operator-Entropy and Reynolds-Stress confirm formal TOST equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 on the multi-seed suite ($p \le 0.0054$; $95\%$ CIs $[-0.014, +0.021]$ and $[-0.042, +0.007]$), establishing practical equivalence.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Dynamics under Distribution Drift:} Static extreme-value calibration systematically overshoots nominal design risk under chronological deployment ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at design $q{=}10^{-3}$). Under an exchangeable permutation protocol, inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of this calibration overshoot, proving that non-stationary partition shifts dominate sample scarcity and autocorrelation, highlighting the need for adaptive threshold tracking in operational deployments.
\end{enumerate}
