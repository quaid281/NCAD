\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied multi-horizon circuit-breaker panels (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for operational deployment; 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as an ablation reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 exploratory design-stage streams and 118 primary untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event coverage and delay:} Under the five-block archive protocol, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ across all nine datasets ($p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay ($69.0$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps) at higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$), using a unified $120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$-parameter backbone shared with the 224-dial reference. Pooled causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ overall ($p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$) and more than doubles when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$); on shorter histories the scalar baseline has higher Point-F1. Weight quenching gives no reliable benefit and is omitted.
    \item \emph{Operating point:} At matched healthy alarm rates the panel performs on par with simpler coordinate pooling, and at matched test FPR it does not rank faults better than the scalar baseline. Its value lies in a label-free quorum, fitted to correlated trip counts, that settles at higher episode coverage and shorter delay than scalar SPOT.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tail and deployment protocol:} Causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has mean $6.4\%$, median $0.93\%$, $90$th percentile $6.1\%$, and $95$th percentile $64.7\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), with $88.1\%$ of streams at or below $5\%$. Because healthy regime shifts ($D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.5$) rather than calibration length alone drive most false alarms, $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ is necessary but insufficient; autonomous deployment requires passing a held-out $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ acceptance gate and live drift monitoring.
    \item \emph{Limits:} An optional channel branch helps synthetic single-sensor faults only at a higher FPR, and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL at $0.000$ Point-F1) and natural sensor failures remain open.
\end{enumerate}
