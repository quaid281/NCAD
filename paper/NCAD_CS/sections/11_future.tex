\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we conducted an empirical investigation into whether Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) and physics-inspired inductive regularizers resolve the false-alarm vulnerability of multivariate time-series anomaly detection. By evaluating 33 continuous telemetry streams across seven physical sensing domains under a strictly held-out calibration protocol, this study yields three primary conclusions:

First, forward prediction in a contracted latent representation space fundamentally conditions residual distributions. By filtering out localized observation-space sensor jitter, latent prediction reduces test residual excess kurtosis and achieves a statistically significant 4-fold to 5-fold reduction in out-of-sample nominal false-positive rates compared to reconstructive baselines (paired difference $-0.154$ against TimesNet, $p=0.008$; $-0.157$ against TranAD, $p=0.001$). However, continuous ranking (PR-AUC) and calibrated Point-F1 remain statistically indistinguishable between unconstrained latent prediction and baselines ($p \ge 0.30$). Under strictly causal streaming, latent prediction suppresses false alarms to $0.47\%\text{--}4.18\%$, but at the cost of lower point-level F1 and delayed event recall.

Second, empirical hypothesis testing refutes the claim that physics-inspired regularizers provide an anomaly detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. Across all 33 streams, neither Operator-Entropy (spectral rank preservation) nor Reynolds-Stress (second-moment fluctuation alignment) outperforms unconstrained TS-JEPA ($p \ge 0.33$), while Potential-Flow (curl-free gradient dynamics) performs significantly worse ($p=0.020$) due to its inability to represent circulating limit-cycle orbits. In matched-capacity controls holding the neural backbone fixed, disabling each physical loss term changes Point-F1 by $+0.001$ to $+0.006$ ($p \ge 0.58$). While diagnostic experiments confirm that these formulations enforce their intended mathematical properties, real-world telemetry deviations do not obey idealized physical conservation laws, rendering these regularizers ineffective as general anomaly detection priors.

Third, automated threshold calibration via Extreme Value Theory (SPOT) systematically overshoots its theoretical design risk on non-stationary telemetry. At $q=10^{-3}$, empirical nominal false-positive rates settle at $5.2\%\text{--}7.4\%$ for JEPAs and $23.9\%\text{--}24.6\%$ for reconstructive baselines, with 95\% bootstrap intervals excluding $q$ in 57\%--87\% of runs. Parametric EVT tail modeling provides no measurable advantage over simple empirical quantiles, and kurtosis adjustments fail to correct pre-asymptotic tail drift.

Four directions extend this investigation. First, incorporating solenoidal vorticity components into velocity fields via Helmholtz decomposition enables modeling dissipative systems with limit-cycle attractors without sacrificing conservative stability. Second, developing memory-bounded online drift adaptation will allow tracking diurnal thermal cycles and operational setpoint transitions without buffer contamination. Third, implementing accelerated sliding-window inference kernels will enable scaling multi-seed evaluations to million-step telemetry streams. Finally, quantizing JEPA encoders into 8-bit integer formats will support deployment on radiation-hardened satellite microcontrollers and low-power industrial edge hardware.
