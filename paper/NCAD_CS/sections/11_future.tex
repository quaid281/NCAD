\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We formulated Operator-Entropy JEPA for multi-sensor anomaly detection and evaluated false-alarm control, discriminative detection, and operational calibration across 33 telemetry streams under a strict held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{False-Alarm Control and Matched Operating Points:} Latent predictive scoring suppresses nominal false alarms under static calibration three- to four-fold buffered ($7.4\%\text{--}10.1\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$) and $8\text{--}20\times$ causally ($1.1\%\text{--}2.8\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$) against TimesNet and TranAD across leave-one-dataset-out folds. At ultra-low matched realized FPR ($f^* = 0.01$ causal), latent predictors reach event recall $0.395\text{--}0.431$ ($0.395$ for TS-JEPA) versus $0.217$ for TimesNet and $0.149$ for TranAD, while at moderate realized FPR ($f^* \ge 0.10$) event recall differences narrow or favor reconstructors. PR-AUC and Point-F1 remain statistically comparable.
    \item \textbf{Mechanism Isolation and Regularizer Equivalence:} Contrasting 12 controlled backbones decouples score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks mechanically bound kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$) and halve FPR, but latent projection is strictly required to halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift ($0.376 \to 0.209\text{--}0.214$) and suppress false alarms to $0.009\text{--}0.028$. Adding physical regularizers yields formal TOST equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 (Reynolds-Stress $p=0.0008$; spectral regularizer on fixed backbone vs.\ $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$ within $\pm 0.03$ with $p < 0.0001$), functioning as structural constraints rather than empirical performance drivers.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Realities Under Non-Stationarity:} Static extreme-value thresholding consistently overshoots design targets across multi-target sweeps ($q \in [10^{-4}, 10^{-1}]$) because inter-block distribution drift violates exchangeability, accounting for $84\text{--}96\%$ of causal overshoot under exchangeable controls. Rolling thresholding tracks discrete shifts but fails under continuous drift ramps.
    \item \textbf{Latency Limits and Fault Boundaries:} Predictive scoring incurs an $S{-}1$ emission lag ($10.5$~min at $S{=}64$ on 10-s telemetry). While zero-lag monitoring ($S{=}1$) preserves false-alarm control ($0.005$ vs.\ $0.025$), quiet flatlines remain undetected without high-alarm auxiliary variance monitors.
\end{enumerate}
Open items include adaptive thresholding tracking within-block drift and low-latency architectures for quiet fault modes.
