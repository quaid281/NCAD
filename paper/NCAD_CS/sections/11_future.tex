\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures condition anomaly residuals under automated extreme-value calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a chronological held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Mechanism Isolation and Regularizer Equivalence:} Controlled ablations decouple score compression from representation learning: LayerNorm bottlenecks bound residual kurtosis, whereas learned latent projections halve calibration-to-holdout distribution drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.209\text{--}0.214$) and reduce causal false-positive rates to $0.9\%\text{--}2.8\%$. On a fixed Koopman backbone, the spectral regularizer satisfies TOST equivalence ($p_{\text{TOST}} < 0.01$ down to $\Delta = \pm 0.010$), while the OpEntropy transition architecture shortens mean detection delay from $110.3$ to $79.5$ steps ($127.0 \to 58.2$ causally).
    \item \textbf{Relative False-Alarm Control vs.\ Detection Parity:} Latent predictive scoring lowers held-out false alarms three-fold under buffered attribution and eight- to ten-fold under causal streaming, and nearly doubles causal event recall at stringent $1\%$ false-alarm budgets ($f^* = 0.01$), while overall Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain comparable to reconstructive baselines.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Drift and Operational Boundaries:} Static SPOT still overshoots its nominal $0.1\%$ design risk by $15\text{--}64\times$ out of sample, driven primarily by inter-block distribution shift ($56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ of excess risk) and serial dependence ($29.1\%\text{--}35.3\%$). Future work should pair latent predictive residuals with declustered GPD fitting, online conformal updates, and hybrid variance monitors to close the remaining calibration gap and detect quiet single-channel flatlines.
\end{enumerate}
