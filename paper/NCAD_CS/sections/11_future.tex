\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We investigated how Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures and fluid-dynamic multi-perspective circuit-breaker panels resolve both observation-space false-alarm flooding and 1D scalar score collapse across 33 primary and 141 archive-wide continuous telemetry streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{224-Bit Fluid-Dynamic Circuit Breakers Eliminate 1D Score Collapse:} Expanding $D=32$ whitened coordinates into $7 \times 32 = 224$ fluid-dynamic dials across sub-horizon positions, Reynolds mean-flow transport, turbulent fluctuation, and convective right-edge acceleration with hijack guards, prefix-routed attribution, and 32 right-edge onset relays prevents drifting coordinates from inflating scalar thresholds. On $n{=}19$ primary streams, this 6-step expansion lifts buffered Point-F1 to $0.228$ at $0.411$ PR-AUC and $71.8$-step delay, doubles causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), and halves causal delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps; across all 141 streams ($423$ runs), it doubles causal Point-F1 from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.139$ ($114/18$ wins, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$) at $72.7\%$ recall and $55.3$-step delay.
    \item \emph{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Component Verification:} Matched-encoder ablations isolate LayerNorm kurtosis compression ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent projections ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA ($\operatorname{RankMe}: 11.6 \to 19.8$, $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$, inter-block drift share $71.3\% \to 18.7\%$). Leave-one-out ablations confirm the 32 right-edge relays ($-17.8$-step causal delay), prefix routing ($+4.7\%$ buffered recall), and hijack guards, while post-hoc 1D Lieb--Thirring modulation satisfies TOST equivalence ($p_{\text{TOST}} < 10^{-4}$) and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag.
    \item \emph{Contextual-Anomaly Dominance and Hybrid Quiet-Fault Coverage:} Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit nearly triples reconstructor recall at a $1\%$ test-FPR budget ($58.4\%$ vs.\ $21.7\%$ for TimesNet) and leads on NASA contextual anomalies ($0.658$ buffered PR-AUC; $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ matched-FPR recall), while standalone 224-Bit Breaker lifts single-channel freeze point recall 7-fold ($14.3\%\text{--}25.6\%$) and its union with a sensor-variance monitor reaches $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ event recall, motivating online conformal circuit-breaker adaptation for non-stationary ramps.
\end{enumerate}
