\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigated empirically whether JEPA-style latent prediction and physics-inspired regularizers resolve the false-alarm vulnerability of multivariate anomaly detection, evaluating 33 telemetry streams across seven physical domains under a strictly held-out calibration protocol. Three conclusions follow.

First, prediction in a contracted latent space conditions residual distributions: filtering observation-space jitter cuts held-out nominal FPR roughly four-fold versus reconstructive baselines ($-0.150$ vs. TimesNet, $p{=}0.007$; $-0.150$ vs. TranAD, $p{=}0.004$, five-seed subset), while Point-F1 and PR-AUC remain statistically indistinguishable ($p \ge 0.16$). Under strictly causal streaming, latent prediction suppresses false alarms to $1.0\%\text{--}1.9\%$ at the cost of lower F1 and delayed event recall.

Second, hypothesis testing refutes a detection advantage for the physics-inspired regularizers: neither Operator-Entropy nor Reynolds-Stress outperforms TS-JEPA ($p \ge 0.50$) across all 33 streams, and Potential-Flow is significantly worse ($p{=}0.013$), unable to represent circulating orbits. Matched-capacity controls confirm that disabling each physical term shifts Point-F1 by only $+0.001$ to $+0.006$ ($p \ge 0.58$); the formulations enforce their intended mathematics but real telemetry does not obey the idealized laws.

Third, SPOT calibration systematically overshoots its design risk on non-stationary telemetry ($6.5\text{--}7.7\%$ nominal FPR for JEPAs, $26.1\text{--}26.6\%$ for baselines at $q{=}10^{-3}$; CIs exclude $q$ in 64--87\% of runs). Parametric tail modeling offers no advantage over empirical quantiles, and kurtosis adjustment degrades rather than corrects the drift.

\textbf{Scope.} All evaluations are \emph{within-dataset}: models are trained, calibrated, and tested on disjoint chronological partitions of the same stream. We claim nothing about cross-dataset transfer or unseen fault types (cf.\ Section~\ref{subsec:gate_fault_study}). Extensions include solenoidal components via Helmholtz decomposition, memory-bounded online drift adaptation, accelerated streaming inference kernels, and integer quantization for edge deployment.
