\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied multi-horizon circuit-breaker panels (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for operational deployment; 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as an ablation reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 exploratory design-stage streams and 118 primary untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event coverage and delay:} Under the five-block archive protocol, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ on all nine datasets ($p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay, with $35\%\text{--}52\%$ fewer parameters and $2.43\times$ faster inference than \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. Pooled causal Point-F1 rises only modestly ($0.076\to 0.079$) but doubles when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.079$); on shorter histories the scalar baseline is preferable on Point-F1. Weight quenching gives no reliable benefit.
    \item \emph{Operating point:} At matched healthy alarm rates the panel performs on par with simpler coordinate pooling, and at matched test FPR it does not rank faults better than the scalar baseline. Its value lies in a label-free quorum, fitted to correlated trip counts, that settles at a more useful recall and delay than scalar SPOT.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tail and deployment:} The panel's causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has mean $7.0\%$, median $0.78\%$, $90$th percentile $10.6\%$, and $95$th percentile $50.2\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Eight streams with healthy regime shifts carry two thirds of all false alarms for both detectors, so autonomous use requires $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, a held-out acceptance check, and drift monitoring.
    \item \emph{Limits:} An optional channel branch helps synthetic single-sensor faults only at a higher FPR, and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL at $0.000$ Point-F1) and natural sensor failures remain open.
\end{enumerate}
