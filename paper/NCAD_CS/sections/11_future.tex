\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
In practice, deploying \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} relies on gated screening and fallback routing rather than a standalone threshold guarantee. Streams meeting both $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ and frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($52$ of $118$) run the $128$-dial panel, while the remaining $66$ short or drifting streams route automatically to 1D SPOT fallback. Because passing $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ carries selection optimism and finite-sample uncertainty, accepted streams still require live $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ drift checks that hold candidate windows in a $400$-window quarantine buffer until an operator or control signal confirms a benign mode switch. When an alarm fires, batched LOCO masking ranks channels by controlled perturbation sensitivity as a quick triage aid rather than verified root-cause localization.

Fitting each stream on its own healthy prefix suits industrial and spacecraft telemetry spanning $K=2$ to $55$ channels with incompatible physical units, sampling rates, and operating regimes, where zero-shot cross-asset weights struggle without target fine-tuning. Healthy-prefix fitting transfers the frozen architecture, symmetric or horizon-preserving whitening, and calibration rule across domains on a single CPU thread without cross-asset label leakage. Taken together, our null regularizer findings, ranking parity with scalar JEPAs, and higher raw F1 for TimesNet and TranAD at high false-alarm rates show that the panel's benefit comes from low-false-alarm operating-point placement, with $128$-dial \emph{Lite} trading moderate causal recall against the $224$-dial reference for quieter average and buffered operation. Complementary guards remain essential in three regimes: $1/K$-diluted single-channel flatlines, unconditioned slow ramps, and prefixes shorter than $500$ windows.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Combining multi-horizon JEPA residuals, coordinate whitening, per-dial healthy tripwires, and fallback routing across $33$ primary development streams and $118$ untouched archive streams yields three main takeaways:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operating-point shift and horizon-preserving whitening:} Under matched multiplicative scaling, per-dial tripwires keep high-variance coordinates from inflating a shared threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), and strictly horizon-preserving Kronecker ZCA matches dense ZCA across the archive. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall over 1D JEPA from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$) and cuts alarm latency from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps, trading $4.8$ recall points against the $224$-dial reference for lower mean and buffered false alarms while doubling Point-F1 when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{Positioning against external baselines:} Matched-FPR checks confirm ranking parity with scalar JEPAs, whereas TimesNet and TranAD attain higher raw F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ healthy FPR and drop below half of \emph{Lite}'s event recall at $1\%$ test FPR. Post-alarm LOCO masking adds $85.7\%$ Top-1 synthetic sensor-subset sensitivity on injected shifts.
    \item \emph{Nonstationarity, empirical screening, and quarantine:} Shuffling healthy blocks links $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms to chronological nonstationarity, strongly correlated with residual-covariance shift. Operational deployment pairs two-stage screening ($52$ accepted, $66$ routed to 1D fallback) with quarantined recalibration that preserves persistent-fault recall until a mode switch is confirmed.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Key next steps include validating physical fault localization on field-labeled hardware failures, updating $\mathbf{\Omega}_D$ online without $400$-window buffers, conditioning slow-ramp monitors on control states, and adding low-FPR univariate flatline guards.
