\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated a two-tier family of multi-horizon circuit-breaker architectures (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, preferred deployment tier; \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, 224-dial reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 development and 118 untouched held-out streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Preferred distilled vs.\ reference panels:} On the 33-stream benchmark, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reach $0.244$ and $0.242$ buffered Point-F1 and $67.3\%$ and $65.0\%$ causal ER at $1.3\%$ and $1.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} cuts parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$, accelerates inference by $2.43\times$, and leads $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$; 19-stream cache $0.071$ and $0.098$ vs.\ $0.013$ 1D). Across $118$ untouched streams ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.079$, $p=0.014$), buffered Point-F1 ($0.143\to 0.156$, $p=0.022$), and causal ER ($52.5\%\to 63.8\%$, $9/9$ sets, $p=0.0039$), beating system-matched $\max_j z_j$ ($0.062$) and raw top-$k$ ($0.073$), while disjoint quenching trims 4H causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ by only $0.07$ points ($p=0.55$ sign-flip).
    \item \emph{Mechanisms and latency:} Matched-backbone controls confirm LayerNorm tail compression and barycenter drift reduction while scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation is null, and five-stream sweeps ($S\in\{1..64\}$) retain $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 gains down to $S=1$.
    \item \emph{Calibration, single-sensor, and ramp limits:} Sokal autocorrelation ($\tau_{\text{int}}\le 206$) leaves $1.5$ and $0.3$ expected tail points at $N_{\text{eff}}=300$, though two-fold cross-fitting cuts short-stream ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$) causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$. An optional channel branch lifts injected single-sensor ER ($80.7\%\text{--}96.5\%$) only at $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$, and excess causal detection vanishes on slow ramps once $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$); pairing raw-level trend charts and testing natural sensor failures remain future priorities.
\end{enumerate}
