\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied whether Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures and multi-perspective circuit-breaker panels mitigate observation-space false-alarm flooding and 1D scalar score collapse across a 33-stream development cohort and 118 untouched held-out validation streams (within a 162-stream, 15-dataset archive):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Circuit-breaker panel, surgical quenching, and operational trade-offs:} Expanding $D=32$ whitened coordinates via a Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon decomposition into $224$ per-coordinate statistics with surgical weight-space quenching, nominal drift guards, prefix-routed attribution, and 32 right-edge onset relays prevents variance-dominating coordinates from inflating a shared scalar threshold. Unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} maximizes real-time causal point coverage ($p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$ over 1D collapse on the development cohort; $p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$ and dataset-clustered $p = 0.007$ across the $118$ untouched validation streams), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} projects out calibration drift in weight space to deliver the fastest causal onset detection, highest event recall and retrospective buffered Point-F1, and a statistically significant causal false-alarm reduction across all $162$ archive streams ($p = 0.0011$, median held-out FPR $1.03\%$).
    \item \emph{Mechanism isolation and multi-horizon scaling ($S \in \{1,\dots,64\}$):} Matched-backbone ablations isolate spherical LayerNorm kurtosis compression, learned latent target stability, running-barycenter drift suppression (cutting the inter-block regime-drift share of excess false alarms four-fold from $71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$), and predictor-bias recentering versus selective precision tempering ($k_{\text{rogue}} \in [1, 4]$), while Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} characterizes the structural horizon-fluctuation concentration bound (with scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation yielding a null effect). Retraining the 224-bit panel and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ confirms a $1.9\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 advantage and $75\%\text{--}84\%$ event recall down to zero reporting lag ($S=1$) and three-step lag ($S=4$).
    \item \emph{Applicability boundaries, calibration sample-size law, and single-channel branch:} Reliable empirical $q_{0.999}$ calibration requires multichannel arrays ($K \ge 9$) and at least $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ effective independent calibration observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows, bounding bootstrap threshold CIs within $\pm 8\%$ and inter-segment FPR SD below $3\%$), whereas order-statistic-free IQR extrapolation or Beta-smoothed quantiles stabilize calibration when $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$. Pairing the latent panel with a complementary channel-level variance and AR(1) residual branch raises single-sensor fault recall above $93\%$ across six fault classes at the cost of higher nominal alert rates, while slow process ramps (GHL, Metro) and online ramp adaptation remain open directions for future work.
\end{enumerate}
