\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Decomposing multi-horizon latent predictive errors into independently calibrated coordinate tripwires (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} at $128$ dials; \emph{Laminar-JEPA} at $224$ dials plus $32$ separate relays) yields three findings across $33$ design-stage and $118$ untouched archive streams, where per-stream healthy prefix fitting transfers the frozen architecture and calibration rule across sensor suites rather than pretrained weights across assets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Coordinate isolation and live operating point:} Per-dial tripwires prevent drifting coordinates from inflating a shared scalar threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), catching contextual and phase-desync faults missed by 1D SPOT \cite{wu2023flawed}. Across $118$ untouched streams ($9/9$ datasets, $37$ zero-tuning unseen streams, and $9$-fold nested LODO), \emph{Lite} lifts causal ER ($52.5\%\to 64.6\%$, $p=0.0039$) and cuts end-to-end latency ($133.5\to 69.0$ steps) at $93\text{--}141$ windows/s on one CPU thread. Overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) stays cluster-uncertain, while $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ doubles Point-F1 ($0.040\to 0.085$, sign-flip $p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{Operating point vs.\ external baselines:} Matched test-FPR checks show no ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs. Yet under untouched-archive SPOT, TimesNet and TranAD suffer $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and fall to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR (vs.\ $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}), confirming a distinct label-free operating point paired with post-alarm LOCO sensor attribution ($85.7\%$ Top-1).
    \item \emph{Drift, gated acceptance, and quarantined adaptation:} Block permutation links $92.3\%$ of tail false alarms to covariance drift. Autonomous deployment pairs two-stage held-out acceptance ($52/118$ accepted, $66$ fallback) with quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ recalibration requiring confirmed mode switches ($31.9\%$ vs.\ $9.1\%$ fault recall; $4.8\%$ confirmed FPR).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Future Work:} Key directions include low-rank streaming updates to $\mathbf{\Omega}$ and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ without $400$-window buffers, control-conditioned slow-ramp monitoring to cut GHL wander ($74.4\%$ test FPR), and low-FPR univariate guards for $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines.
