\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented a Physics-Inspired Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (PI-JEPA) framework for unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate sensor telemetry. By replacing raw observation-space reconstruction with forward prediction in latent representation space, our approach substantially mitigates the threshold calibration collapse that causes reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) to flood continuous streams with hundreds of thousands of false alarms under ambient sensor noise.

We embedded physical domain principles directly into latent representations through three complementary formulations: Operator-Entropy JEPA (regularizing latent Koopman transition matrices via spectral entropy during unsupervised representation training to prevent modal collapse), Reynolds-Stress JEPA (aligning cross-channel second-moment fluctuation covariance tensors inspired by hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition), and Potential-Flow JEPA (enforcing conservative, irrotational trajectory dynamics via a scalar potential gradient). Combined with Causal Coordinate Saliency Gating, Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells, and Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory tail calibration, these components form the evaluated framework. Across 11 telemetry datasets (45 matched channels) evaluated under identical protocols, Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves the highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.2066$, representing a 43.2\% relative improvement over TimesNet and 28.4\% over TranAD) and clears the analytical all-positive baseline ($0.1893$). In Pooled Micro-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA attains $0.3458$ (over $3.2\times$ higher than TranAD and $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet) while reducing false alarms by 8.0-fold.

A held-out-calibration replication on 33 streams with matched controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}) limits these conclusions. The latent-prediction family lowers the nominal false-positive rate by about a factor of four to five relative to TimesNet and TranAD, with Point-F1 and PR-AUC statistically indistinguishable and a clear dependence on the dataset, and no calibrator reaches its design risk. None of the three physical variants beats the unconstrained TS-JEPA, and removing the entropy, stress or regime terms does not change Point-F1 beyond seed noise on the seven streams where controls were run, so the physical regularizers remain motivation and not demonstrated mechanism. The kurtosis multiplier raised held-out FPR for TS-JEPA and offers no advantage over a 99.5th percentile rule. The 45-stream tables above could not be reproduced under the stricter protocol and the script that generated them is not in the repository. Open work includes repeating the controls on all 33 streams, running GECCO and cicids with a faster inference path, and giving the 12 single-seed GHL streams additional seeds.

Four concrete directions extend this work. First, extending conservative field formulations by incorporating parameter-controlled solenoidal vorticity components will allow modeling dissipative systems with limit-cycle attractors. Second, developing memory-efficient, test-time momentum adaptation will enable tracking orbital thermal cycles and environmental seasonal drift without catastrophic forgetting. Third, expanding empirical evaluations across extensive multi-seed benchmarks will enable rigorous confidence interval reporting and rank-based statistical testing (such as Friedman-Nemenyi tests). Finally, quantizing PI-JEPA encoders into 8-bit integer (INT8) formats will enable deployment on radiation-hardened spacecraft microcontrollers and ultra-low-power wearable edge devices.

\section*{Code and Data Availability}
To ensure reproducibility, all benchmark dataset extraction scripts, preprocessing pipelines, model checkpoints, PyTorch training code, fixed random seed configurations (\texttt{seed = 42}), and evaluation logs for all six evaluated architectures across the 11 telemetry datasets are open-sourced at an anonymized public repository: \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/NCAD_CS-Reproducibility-8F21}. All public datasets (NASA SMAP/MSL, Daphnet, CICIDS-2017, GHL, CalIt2, GECCO, Genesis, Metro Traffic, and SWAN-SF) are accessible via their respective referenced public repositories.
