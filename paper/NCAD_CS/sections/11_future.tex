\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compared score conditioning, detection, and static threshold calibration on 33 telemetry streams from seven datasets under a held-out protocol. Three conclusions follow.

First, bounded observation-space controls reproduce much of the apparent latent-score tail conditioning, while the bounded TCN shows that light tails do not guarantee threshold transfer. Causal event-recall areas over nominal budgets favor JEPA on average, but paired comparisons at matched realized FPR have sparse support and do not establish a detector advantage; buffered results remain separate. Point-F1 and PR-AUC differences are imprecise, not evidence of equivalence.

Second, the tested physics-inspired variants show no reliable aggregate benefit. Neither the unadjusted Potential-Flow F1 contrast nor its favorable nominal-budget curve establishes a mechanism; explanations based on curl-free dynamics or operating points remain conjectural.

Third, static POT thresholds overshoot their design risk under the short chronological calibration protocol ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ for JEPAs, $\approx 22\%$ for reconstructive baselines at $q{=}10^{-3}$). Observational drift diagnostics and scarce tail exceedances do not resolve their relative contributions.

\textbf{Scope.} All evaluations are \emph{within-dataset}: models are trained, calibrated, and tested on disjoint chronological partitions of the same stream. We claim nothing about cross-dataset transfer or unseen fault types (cf.\ Section~\ref{subsec:gate_fault_study}). Extensions include solenoidal components via Helmholtz decomposition, memory-bounded online drift adaptation, accelerated streaming inference kernels, and integer quantization for edge deployment.
