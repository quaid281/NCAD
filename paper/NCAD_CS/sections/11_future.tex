\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We investigated score conditioning, discriminative detection, and static threshold calibration across 33 continuous telemetry streams under a held-out protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{False-Alarm Suppression and Regimes of Usefulness:} Latent predictive scoring suppresses false alarms under static calibration, functioning as an effective conservative filter on high-dimensional structured telemetry (NASA SMAP/MSL). However, this conservatism does not establish superior detection: paired Point-F1 and PR-AUC differences remain inconclusive, causal event recall at matched realized false-positive rates lacks aggregate advantage, and performance degrades on quiet flatlines, continuous ramps, and high-prevalence event streams.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Equivalence of Physical Regularizers:} The tested physical regularizers provide no aggregate detection benefit over unconstrained TS-JEPA: Operator-Entropy and Reynolds-Stress confirm formal TOST equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 on the multi-seed suite ($p \le 0.0054$; $95\%$ CIs $[-0.014, +0.021]$ and $[-0.042, +0.007]$), confirming practical parity rather than an advantage. Explanations invoking curl-free dynamics or operating points remain conjectural.
    \item \textbf{Calibration Failure under Distribution Drift:} Static peaks-over-threshold calibration systematically overshoots nominal design risk under chronological deployment ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at design $q{=}10^{-3}$). Under an exchangeable permutation protocol, inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of this calibration overshoot, providing strong evidence that non-stationary partition shifts dominate sample scarcity and autocorrelation. Deployments require transitioning from static EVT to adaptive threshold tracking.
\end{enumerate}
