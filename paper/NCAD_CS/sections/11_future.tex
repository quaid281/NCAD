\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied whether Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures and multi-perspective circuit-breaker panels reduce observation-space false-alarm flooding and 1D scalar score collapse across 33 primary and 141 archive-wide telemetry streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{A breaker panel and laminar weight-space quenching in place of a scalar threshold:} Expanding $D=32$ whitened coordinates into $224$ per-coordinate horizon statistics with surgical weight-space quenching, nominal drift guards, prefix-routed attribution, and 32 onset relays keeps one or two drifting coordinates from setting a shared threshold. On $n{=}19$ primary streams, causal Point-F1 rises from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$) while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $0.262$ buffered Point-F1 and cuts causal delay to $59.6$ steps; across $162$ streams ($15$ datasets), \emph{Laminar-JEPA} cuts causal held-out FPR by $8.0\%$ relative ($p = 0.0011$, median $1.03\%$), and on the $118$ held-out archive streams the panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.072\text{--}0.083$ to $0.143$ ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$) at $72.8\%$ recall.
    \item \emph{Factorial Mechanism Isolation and Component Verification:} Matched-encoder ablations isolate LayerNorm kurtosis compression ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent projections ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$), and barycenter centering in Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA ($\operatorname{RankMe}: 11.6 \to 19.8$, $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$, inter-block drift share $71.3\% \to 18.7\%$). Leave-one-out ablations support the right-edge relays ($-17.8$-step causal delay) and prefix routing ($+4.7\%$ buffered recall); hijack guards add little on these streams ($0.137 \to 0.138$ causal F1), scalar spectral-concentration modulation shows no measurable benefit, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) remove the 63-step emission lag.
    \item \emph{Limits:} At a $1\%$ test-FPR budget the panel catches $58.4\%$ of events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, mainly on multichannel streams. It misses the slow ramps of GHL and Metro ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ Point-F1), catches $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ of single-channel freezes alone ($44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ with a variance monitor), and needs about $10^3$ calibration steps; disjoint early-stopping validation splits and online ramp adaptation remain future work.
\end{enumerate}
