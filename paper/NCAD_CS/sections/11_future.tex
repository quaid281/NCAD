\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied multi-horizon circuit-breaker panels (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} for operational deployment; 224-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} as an ablation reference) for abrupt and intermediate-onset telemetry faults across 33 exploratory design-stage streams and 118 primary untouched archive streams:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event coverage, buffered accuracy, and operating points:} Under the five-block archive protocol ($n=118$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises buffered ER ($59.0\%\to 67.4\%$) and Point-F1 ($0.143\to 0.156$) at lower held-out FPR ($19.5\%\to 14.3\%$), and raises live causal ER ($52.5\%\to 63.8\%$) across all nine datasets ($p=0.0039$), halves detection delay, and beats system-matched coordinate aggregators at $35\%\text{--}52\%$ lower parameter cost and $2.43\times$ speedup over \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. Pooled causal Point-F1 rises modestly ($0.076\to 0.079$), doubling on adequately calibrated streams ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$: $0.040\to 0.079$, $p=2.7\times 10^{-5}$), while weight quenching yields no reliable benefit.
    \item \emph{Contributing factors and latency:} Design-stage matched-backbone controls indicate that LayerNorm and centering contribute to lighter tails and lower drift under these protocols, and an exploratory five-stream sweep preserves $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ causal Point-F1 gains at $S\in\{1,4\}$.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tails, single-sensor, and ramp limits:} Archive causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has a low median ($0.78\%$; $87.3\%$ of streams $\le 5\%$) but a $7.0\%$ mean driven by short autocorrelated calibration blocks (two-fold cross-fitting cuts short-stream $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$). An optional channel branch helps synthetic single-sensor faults only at elevated FPR, and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL at $0.000$ Point-F1) and natural sensor failures remain open priorities.
\end{enumerate}
