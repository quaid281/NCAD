\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented a Physics-Inspired Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (PI-JEPA) framework for unsupervised anomaly detection in multivariate sensor telemetry. By replacing raw observation-space reconstruction with forward prediction in latent representation space, our approach eliminates the threshold calibration collapse that causes reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) to flood continuous streams with hundreds of thousands of false alarms under ambient sensor noise.

We embedded physical domain principles directly into latent representations through three complementary formulations: Operator-Entropy JEPA (regularizing latent Koopman transition matrices via spectral entropy during training to prevent modal collapse), Reynolds-Stress JEPA (aligning cross-channel second-moment fluctuation covariance tensors inspired by hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition), and Potential-Flow JEPA (enforcing conservative, irrotational trajectory dynamics via a scalar potential gradient). Together with Coordinate Saliency Gating, Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells, and Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory tail calibration, the proposed framework stabilizes streaming threshold calibration. Across 11 telemetry datasets (45 matched channels) evaluated under identical protocols, Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves the highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.2066$, representing a 43.2\% relative improvement over TimesNet and 28.4\% over TranAD) and clears the analytical all-positive baseline ($0.1893$). In Pooled Micro-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA attains $0.3458$ (over $3.2\times$ higher than TranAD and $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet) while reducing false alarms by 8.0-fold.

Several promising avenues remain for future investigation. First, extending conservative field formulations by incorporating parameter-controlled solenoidal vorticity components will allow modeling dissipative systems with limit-cycle attractors. Second, developing memory-efficient, test-time momentum adaptation will enable tracking orbital thermal cycles and environmental seasonal drift without catastrophic forgetting. Third, expanding empirical evaluations across extensive multi-seed benchmarks will enable rigorous confidence interval reporting and rank-based statistical testing (such as Friedman-Nemenyi tests). Finally, quantizing PI-JEPA encoders into 8-bit integer (INT8) formats will enable deployment on radiation-hardened spacecraft microcontrollers and ultra-low-power wearable edge devices.
