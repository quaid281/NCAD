\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, while \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the highest buffered PR-AUC ($0.375$) and causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.701$ at $f^* = 0.01$).

\noindent\textbf{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring lowers held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three-fold under buffered attribution ($9.3\%$ and $10.8\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} vs.\ $30.0\%$ and $30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD; paired difference for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} $-19.2\%$ vs.\ TimesNet, $p = 0.049$, and $-19.9\%$ vs.\ TranAD, $p = 0.007$) and ten- to eighty-fold under causal streaming ($2.4\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.3\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, calibration error $0.023$ and $0.002$, vs.\ $24.3\%$ and $22.2\%$; paired difference $-23.9\%$ vs.\ TimesNet, $p = 0.010$, and $-21.9\%$ vs.\ TranAD, $p = 0.010$). The reduction holds across all 33 streams (causal stream-macro FPR $0.25\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} vs.\ $16.11\%$ and $14.23\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, leaving one dataset out at a time keeps causal FPR at $0.2\%\text{--}2.3\%$ ($3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ buffered) for JEPAs versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ causal ($8.4\%\text{--}22.1\%$ buffered) for reconstructors. In Panel~(c), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the lowest causal held-out FPR on every multi-stream family ($0.2\%$ Daphnet, $0.1\%$ GHL, $0.4\%$ MSL, $0.6\%$ SMAP vs.\ $5.6\%\text{--}36.0\%$ for baselines).

\noindent\textbf{Multi-Horizon Ranking Gains vs.\ Unadjusted Point-F1 Trade-offs:} Under buffered attribution on the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and its $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$ control achieve the highest threshold-free PR-AUC ($0.375$ and $0.374$ vs.\ $0.331$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, $0.329$ for TimesNet, and $0.287$ for TranAD) while buffered Point-F1 ($0.188$ and $0.185$, vs.\ $0.206$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) and event recall ($0.496$ and $0.515$) match TimesNet ($0.197$ F1, $0.583$ recall) and beat TranAD ($0.130$ F1, $0.490$ recall). Per-dataset standard deviations equal the means (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)): SMAP beats the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.370$; PR-AUC $0.576$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and Genesis reaches $0.037$ F1 (vs.\ $0.000$), whereas on GHL ($\pi=0.013$) all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), showing that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (\texttt{TS-JEPA} $\lor$ \texttt{lowvar}) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each component of \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} modifies performance relative to \texttt{TS-JEPA}: (i)~\emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and barycenter centering} remove common-mode baseline shift, cutting KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.163$) and eliminating the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR ($71.3\% \to 0.0\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)); (ii)~\emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and Mahalanobis whitening} ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$) capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, raising buffered PR-AUC ($0.331 \to 0.374$; $0.278 \to 0.288$ causal), cutting causal FPR ($2.4\% \to 0.4\%$), and lifting causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ ($0.395 \to 0.697$); and (iii)~\emph{post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) and manifold gain yield a null aggregate effect on the fixed backbone ($0.188$ vs.\ $0.185$ F1, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0021$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.327$; $0.375$ vs.\ $0.374$ PR-AUC; $0.496$ vs.\ $0.515$ buffered recall; $0.621$ vs.\ $0.604$ buffered and $0.701$ vs.\ $0.697$ causal recall at $f^* = 0.01$), confirming that the multi-horizon whitened representation drives the gains.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} isolates the threshold-placement trade-off of zero-lookahead streaming. Causally, \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} preserves leading ranking (PR-AUC $0.289$ vs.\ $0.278$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.281$ for TimesNet), but unsmoothed $128$-dimensional whitened residuals have a less negative GPD shape ($\text{median }\xi = -0.182$, $\gamma_2 = 2.31$) than $64$-step buffered scores ($\xi = -0.473$, $\gamma_2 = 1.58$) or \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($\xi = -0.307$, $\gamma_2 = 1.33$). Inverting (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}) at $q=10^{-3}$ places $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ high on the causal tail: \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} fires quietly (test alert rate $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.2\%$, true-positive rate (TPR) $5.2\%$, held-out FPR $0.3\%$, test nominal FPR $0.9\%$) versus \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 10.8\%$, TPR $16.6\%$, FPR $2.4\%$) and TimesNet ($\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 24.2\%$, TPR $34.0\%$, FPR $24.3\%$), lowering causal Point-F1 ($0.060$ vs.\ $0.188$ buffered, and $0.161$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) while hitting $50.6\%$ of events ($\Delta t = 166.2$). Because single-hit event recall is lenient on long segments ($111$ events, median $L = 139$, mean $348.1$ across 18 non-SWAN streams), Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} (Note) benchmarks a rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter ($\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$): at $p = \text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0033$, random alerts reach $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.288$ ($\Delta t_{\text{rand}} = 214.6$, vs.\ $0.506$ and $166.2$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}), whereas at $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 0.0220$ unclustered Bernoulli trials reach $0.524$ ($\Delta t_{\text{rand}} = 114.7$), and for TimesNet ($p_{\text{hold}} = 0.243$) random firing reaches $0.600$ ($0.801$ at $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}}$), exceeding TimesNet's $0.588$ due to temporal burstiness. Evaluating \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} at $p_{99.5}$ lifts causal event recall to $0.612$ at $0.76\%$ FPR, and rolling SPOT cuts false alarms by up to $21\times$ on discrete shifts, whereas continuous ramps defeat trailing windows ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)).

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), \texttt{TS-JEPA} holds causal FPR to $0.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ and $3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$ for TimesNet), comparable Point-F1 at $S=1$ ($0.028$ vs.\ $0.025$), and fast detection ($\Delta t = 57.8$ and $85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases across $\{16, 32, 64\}$, TimesNet causal FPR escalates nearly nine-fold ($6.1\% \to 9.8\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Thus $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, e.g., $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$), whereas $S=64$ is unacceptable for low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, e.g., hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) or rapid tripwires, which require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.3\%\text{--}11\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we compare models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), \texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.809$) at lower false-alarm rates than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models, at $f^* = 0.01$ \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.395$) and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($0.701$ causal, $0.621$ buffered) substantially increase event recall compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-FPR regime ($f^* \le 0.05$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample across all streams and attribution modes. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.014$, $15\times q$) and $0.25\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.002$, $3\times q$, vs.\ $142\text{--}161\times$ for TranAD and TimesNet), whereas under $S=64$ buffered attribution each exceedance smears across 64 steps, inflating buffered FPR to $5.54\%$ and $6.32\%$ ($55\text{--}126\times q$ across static rules for JEPAs and $196\text{--}215\times q$ for reconstructors); static SPOT is thus near-calibrated only in causal mode ($1\text{--}7\times q$). Similarly, in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), the pooled buffered stationary SPOT rate is $0.061$ ($61\times q$) because 64-step smearing and serial correlation dilate isolated exceedances even without drift, whereas under causal stationary scoring \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves $0.25\%$ (\texttt{Decl.\ SPOT} $0.20\%$) and \texttt{TS-JEPA} achieves $0.72\%$ (\texttt{Decl.\ SPOT} $0.44\%$, vs.\ $24.4\% \to 18.9\%$ for TimesNet). Under buffered smoothing, declustering reduces $N_u$ exceedances to $N_c \approx 4\text{--}6$ clusters ($0.159$ pooled), whereas adaptive \texttt{Roll.\ emp.} cuts pooled FPR to $1.1\%\text{--}2.4\%$ across step/variance/autocorrelation shifts (causal \texttt{TS-JEPA} $0.40\%$) and fails only on continuous ramps ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$). Three diagnostics explain the static behavior:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability and quantile/length sensitivity:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR unchanged ($2.41\%\text{--}2.42\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) raises causal FPR to $3.72\%$ (\texttt{TS-JEPA}).
    \item \textbf{Platykurtic collapse under total-threshold kurtosis scaling:} Total-threshold scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ reduces causal FPR on leptokurtic \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} cells to $0.11\%$ (matching the $0.1\%$ design risk, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), but $64$-step buffered smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of \texttt{TS-JEPA} cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$). Scaling total $\tau$ pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below $u=p_{98}$ in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating buffered FPR from $6.40\%$ to $16.30\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and from $7.57\%$ to $15.18\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Excess-only scaling $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$ prevents collapse below $u$ ($9.19\%$ buffered, $2.40\%$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)):} Pooling $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ and \emph{refitting} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) on each permuted split across all $19$ non-GHL streams separates chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$) rates. All models achieve low i.i.d.\ stage FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} = 0.0025$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $0.0033\text{--}0.0035$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, TimesNet, and TranAD, $2.5\text{--}3.5\times q$). Inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal overshoot in \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.0229 \to 0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.2103\text{--}0.2302 \to 0.0427\text{--}0.0464$), but vanishes ($0.0\%$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.0033$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.0037$, difference $-0.0004$ $[-0.002, +0.001]$) in \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, leaving only its $0.0015$ i.i.d.\ GPD gap and $0.0012$ serial correlation.
\end{enumerate}
