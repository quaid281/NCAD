\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section provides comprehensive quantitative benchmarking and qualitative dynamical analyses across 2,650 model-channel executions spanning 19 real-world and synthetic datasets. We evaluate the proposed Physics-Informed JEPA Triad against modern state-of-the-art reconstructive and contrastive deep learning baselines.

Key findings from Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} include:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Complete Leaderboard Dominance Across Aggregation Regimes:} The proposed JEPA architectures sweep the top positions across both strict unadjusted Point-F1 and Point-Adjusted F1. In unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} secures Rank 1 ($0.2066$), representing a \textbf{+43.2\% relative improvement} over TimesNet ($0.1443$) and \textbf{+28.4\%} over TranAD ($0.1609$). In Channel-Macro Point-F1, \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} ($0.1743$) and \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} ($0.1532$) capture the top two ranks, outperforming both published SOTA baselines. In Pooled Micro-F1, \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} achieves a dominant \textbf{0.3458}, more than \textbf{3.2$\times$ higher than TranAD} ($0.1072$) and \textbf{5.3$\times$ higher than TimesNet} ($0.0647$).
    \item \textbf{Precision and False Alarm Suppression:} A critical operational limitation of conventional baselines is catastrophic false alarm flooding in continuous industrial telemetry. Reconstructive baselines suffer severe precision collapse under nominal sensor jitter: TimesNet produces $1{,}047{,}144$ false positives across the evaluated channels (precision $4.42\%$), while TranAD accumulates $452{,}621$ false positives (precision $9.64\%$). In contrast, \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} achieves a peak precision of \textbf{45.98\%} with only $130{,}237$ false positives---representing an \textbf{8-fold reduction in false alarms} compared to TimesNet---while detecting $110{,}866$ true positives.
    \item \textbf{Reconstruction Drowning in Observation-Space Baselines:} Reconstructive models (TranAD, TimesNet) exhibit severe performance degradation on noisy, high-dimensional telemetry. TimesNet and TranAD achieve only $0.0647$ and $0.1072$ Micro Point-F1 and $0.2072$ / $0.2537$ Channel PA-F1, because minimizing $\|x - \hat{x}\|_2$ in observation space forces the networks to expend representational capacity fitting high-frequency stochastic sensor noise rather than underlying dynamical transitions. In contrast, by learning predictive mappings in latent space with geometric invariants, all three proposed JEPA models surpass $0.320$ Channel PA-F1, led by \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} ($0.3436$) and \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} ($0.3226$).
    \item \textbf{Physics-Informed Regularization vs. Unconstrained Control:} While unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} achieves solid baseline performance ($0.1838$ Dataset-Macro F1) by virtue of predicting in latent space, the physics-informed inductive biases provide critical structural advantages. \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} improves Dataset-Macro Point-F1 by $+12.4\%$ over vanilla TS-JEPA ($0.2066$ vs $0.1838$) via unitary Koopman spectrum monitoring, while \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} achieves the highest overall Micro-F1 ($0.3458$ vs $0.2860$) and highest Channel PA-F1 ($0.3436$ vs $0.3066$) via conservative potential field constraints.
\end{enumerate}

Each model in the triad exhibits specialized mathematical inductive advantages:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Mechanics)}
By monitoring dynamic spectral entropy within an infinite-dimensional Koopman operator representation, \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} is exceptionally sensitive to disruptions in non-linear cyclic and mixing dynamics. It achieves dominant performance across NASA spacecraft telemetry (\texttt{MSL}: $0.2439$ Point-F1, $0.5401$ PA-F1; \texttt{SMAP}: $0.4157$ Point-F1, $0.5234$ PA-F1), industrial cyber-physical process loops (\texttt{GECCO}: $0.4040$ Point-F1 vs TimesNet's $0.0304$, a \textbf{13.3$\times$ improvement}), and coastal wave mechanics (\texttt{swan}: $0.4349$ Point-F1, $0.6114$ PA-F1 vs TimesNet's $0.1402$).

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Turbulent Transport)}
Grounded in hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition ($\mathbf{u} = \bar{\mathbf{u}} + \mathbf{u}'$), \texttt{reynolds\_stress\_jepa} computes the inter-channel momentum and shear-stress divergence tensor between context and target horizons. This formulation excels in systems characterized by fluid, atmospheric, or multi-sensor convective flow. On the SMAP satellite telemetry dataset, it achieves Rank 1 in Point-F1 (\textbf{0.4205 Point-F1} and \textbf{0.5488 PA-F1}), outperforming TimesNet ($0.2334$ Point-F1) by \textbf{+80.2\% relative}. On SWAN wave dynamics, it achieves a high-precision profile ($65.84\%$ precision, $0.6990$ PA-F1 vs TimesNet's $0.4008$ precision).

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
By constraining nominal dynamics to curl-free, conservative velocity potentials ($\mathbf{v} = \nabla \Phi, \nabla \times \mathbf{v} = 0$), \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} enforces physical trajectory conservation. It achieves the \textbf{highest aggregate Micro Point-F1 (0.3458)} and \textbf{highest Channel PA-F1 (0.3436)} across all evaluated models, coupled with an unmatched precision of \textbf{45.98\%}. On NASA spacecraft subsystems (\texttt{MSL}), it scores \textbf{0.6671 PA-F1} (vs TimesNet's $0.3713$, a \textbf{+79.7\% improvement}), reaching up to \textbf{0.9931 PA-F1} on individual propulsion and drive channels (e.g., Channel D-16).

\subsubsection{Role of Vanilla TS-JEPA (Ablation Control)}
To isolate the contribution of the physical inductive biases from the predictive latent paradigm itself, we evaluate \texttt{ts\_jepa} as an unconstrained control. \texttt{ts\_jepa} achieves $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro Point-F1, $0.1442$ Channel-Macro Point-F1, and $0.3066$ Channel PA-F1. While substantially outperforming reconstructive baselines (+27.4\% in Dataset-Macro F1 over TimesNet), it trails \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} in Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.1838$ vs $0.2066$) and Channel Point-F1 ($0.1442$ vs $0.1743$), and trails \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} in Micro-F1 ($0.2860$ vs $0.3458$) and Channel PA-F1 ($0.3066$ vs $0.3436$). This confirms that while latent representation prediction fundamentally mitigates noise drowning, domain-aligned physical conservation laws provide indispensable inductive structure.

\subsection{Efficacy of Architectural Enhancements}
Ablation of the three internal inductive layers confirms their collective role in stabilizing high-dimensional inference:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gating:} On high-dimensional multi-sensor streams ($K \ge 50$ in SMAP, MSL, SVDB), unweighted latent pooling causes informative anomalous dimensions to drown in ambient channel noise. Saliency gating dynamically prioritizes high-variation informative channels, enabling \texttt{reynolds\_stress\_jepa} and \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} to detect localized subsystem faults on SVDB and SMD without manual channel sub-selection.
    \item \textit{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells:} Multi-scale temporal decomposition within the encoder allows the networks to isolate fast transient spikes from low-frequency baseline drifts. On \texttt{GECCO} (water infrastructure with simultaneous daily consumption cycles and high-frequency valve faults), dyadic filtering doubled \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} performance from $0.1429$ to $0.2904$ Point-F1.
    \item \textit{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory:} Real-world anomaly score tails frequently deviate from standard Gaussianity. By dynamically scaling the EVT threshold via excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$, the calibrator suppresses false alarms during high-entropy volatile bursts while boosting overall PA-Recall to $67\%-71\%$ across the benchmark.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation: Event Metrics and Streaming Adaptation}
While rigorous unadjusted Point-F1 is critical for eliminating the artificial score inflation common in prior literature (such as naive point adjustment), point-level scoring imposes a severe ``boundary lag tax'' on operational telemetry. In physical systems, an anomaly represents a continuous operational incident; if an algorithm detects the failure with a sub-second phase delay or slight boundary misalignment, point-level metrics penalize every misaligned point as a false positive or false negative, severely depressing nominal precision even when $100\%$ of true incidents are detected.

To provide a complete and honest operational evaluation, we evaluate our models under three complementary paradigms: (i) strict unadjusted Point-F1, (ii) incident-level Event-F1 ($P_{\text{event}}, R_{\text{event}}$), and (iii) Tatbul et al. (NeurIPS 2018) Range-Aware F1 ($F1_{\text{range}}$) with existence and overlap rewards. Furthermore, we investigate the role of sharp dense temporal mapping and dynamic rolling tail calibration (Streaming SPOT):

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:operational_incident_metrics}, evaluating anomaly detection through the lens of incident occurrence reveals three major insights:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Operational Incident Reliability:} On Room Occupancy and SMAP, the models achieve $100\%$ Event Recall across all configurations. When window smearing is removed in favor of dense scoring, Event-F1 reaches \textbf{0.9167} with \textbf{84.6\% Event Precision}, confirming that in deployment, true system failures are caught reliably without flooding operators with false alarms.
    \item \textbf{Extreme False Alarm Suppression:} Dynamic localized thresholding (Streaming SPOT) tracks non-stationary baseline drift in telemetry. On Room Occupancy, false alarms drop by \textbf{21-fold} (from 4,705 to 223 points) while maintaining over $63\%$ Event Precision.
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Point-Level Discrepancies:} On challenging channels like SMAP P-3 and CalIt2, apparent low point-level scores ($F1 \approx 0.01$) are driven by boundary temporal offset; range-aware and event-level metrics demonstrate that the physical models detect the core trajectory disruptions early, yielding Range-F1 up to $0.3634$ and Event Precision up to $100\%$.
\end{enumerate}
