\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} explains why scalar scores degrade when moving from buffered review to live streaming. Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$ and causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $1.2\%$ to $0.7\%$ ($p=0.0025$). In return, \emph{Lite} trades lower causal ER ($62.2\%$ vs.\ $70.1\%$, $p=0.0029$) and causal Point-F1 ($0.142$ vs.\ $0.165$, $p=0.035$) against the $224$-dial reference. A separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.151\text{--}0.152$ for TimesNet and TranAD, and $0.082\text{--}0.097$ for DCdetector, NCAD, and Anomaly Transformer). Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

Uncalibrated Gaussian noise ($\sigma\in\{0.15,0.30\}$) preserves \emph{Lite} recall ($61.4\%\text{--}66.7\%$) while raising clean FPR to $6.9\%\text{--}14.3\%$ (vs.\ $1.9\%\text{--}6.2\%$ for 1D).

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ F1 across $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), because context and target latents shift together and cancel. Across linear ramps ($a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$, duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$), chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$. Across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds), mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $433.9$ on exponential $1-e^{-t/512}$ and $586.7$ on $0.001\sigma$/step bias as $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

Pairing \emph{Lite} with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ lifts real GHL causal ER from $0.8\%$ ($0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Post-fault GHL baseline wander, however, drives uncompensated test FPR to $74.4\%$: sliding prediction misses slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift flags benign wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned ZCA-anchored latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across block indices ($m\in\{1,2,3\}$; exact horizons under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and cross-block drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $m\in\{2,3\}$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking provides \emph{controlled perturbation attribution} across input channels. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under injected $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Because latent features mix sensors nonlinearly and real field datasets lack ground-truth root-cause labels, this validates synthetic perturbation sensitivity rather than causal fault localization on natural failures, and $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines remain sub-quorum.

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit} evaluate the untouched archive ($n=118$) along four questions:

\noindent\emph{1) Label-free operating-point placement over 1D JEPA:} Rather than improving score discrimination, the $128$-dial quorum shifts the live operating point toward higher event recall and faster co-detected response at a higher false-alarm cost (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Causal ER rises from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$, $p=0.0039$, BH $p\le 0.016$). That shift raises held-out FPR ($4.4\%\to 6.4\%$ mean, $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$ median) and prospective test FPR ($8.8\%\to 17.0\%$). Step recall climbs ($0.134\to 0.205$) at lower step precision ($0.165\to 0.146$). On identical co-detected fault segments across $87$ shared streams in our three-seed rerun, exact segment-paired onset latency falls by $41.4$ steps ($74.7$ vs.\ $116.1$, $p=0.0026$). Pairing finite stream-mean delays across all $97$ canonical streams where both detectors trigger at least once (covering distinct events on multi-fault streams) cuts latency by $74.6$ steps (cluster CI $[-156.3,-31.1]$, $p=5.4{\times}10^{-6}$). Detection-conditioned marginal delays drop from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps, staying below the $63$-step window offset on sharp faults in Exathlon and GECCO. Across datasets, Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) rises in six of nine sets (median $+0.027$, interquartile range $[-0.014,+0.103]$), led by GECCO ($+0.155$) and PSM ($+0.121$). Losses on SMAP ($-0.043$), OPPORTUNITY ($-0.029$), and MSL ($-0.014$) leave the cluster CI spanning zero ($[-0.026,+0.069]$, $p_{\text{str}}=0.0009$). Point-F1 doubles on $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=0.047$; $+20.4$ ER points), while 1D leads on shorter prefixes ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$). Substituting multiplicative $\times(1+10^{-4})$ for the additive floor in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) keeps $99.96\%$ test agreement ($|\Delta\text{ER}|=0.055\%$).

\noindent\emph{2) Trade-off against the 224-dial reference:} The $224$-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reference reaches higher causal ER ($69.4\%$, $\Delta=-4.8$ points, cluster CI $[-6.6,-1.3]$, $p=0.016$) and lower p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($49.3\%$ vs.\ $64.7\%$), at similar Point-F1 ($0.091$, $p=0.15$). In return, \emph{Lite} lowers mean ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $7.0\%$, $p=1.3{\times}10^{-7}$), median ($0.93\%$ vs.\ $1.4\%$), and buffered ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $21.0\%$, $p=9.2{\times}10^{-10}$) $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ while lifting the $\le 5\%$ pass rate ($88.1\%$ vs.\ $84.7\%$).

\noindent\emph{3) External reconstructors and matched-budget parity:} Under untouched-archive SPOT, \emph{Lite} beats Linear PCA Forecaster by $+34.5$ ER points and $+0.063$ F1 ($p\le 8.2{\times}10^{-12}$). TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1, both $0.236$ PR-AUC) attain higher raw F1 only at $25.6\%$ and $24.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. At matched $f=1\%$ test FPR, TimesNet and TranAD drop to $25.9\%$ and $25.5\%$ ER vs.\ $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}. Against scalar JEPAs, archive PR-AUC matches ($0.152$ vs.\ $0.162$ causal; $0.229$ vs.\ $0.230$ buffered). At matched $r=0.5\%$ calibration budget (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)), \emph{Lite} incurs higher held-out FPR ($7.3\%$ vs.\ $5.8\%$ for 1D). Under the canonical linear $99$th-quantile protocol used alongside TimesNet and TranAD, \emph{Lite} reaches $51.2\%$ ER vs.\ $55.6\%$ for 1D. By contrast, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b) applies upper-order quantiles to discrete dial counts in a separate three-seed rerun ($52.9\%$ vs.\ $54.9\%$ ER; $0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$ F1, with rerun precision $0.179$ vs.\ $0.171$ and recall $0.068$ vs.\ $0.042$). Equalizing calibration rates directly confirms operating-point parity: raising 1D gives $53.8\%$ ER and $0.083$ F1, vs.\ $53.0\%$ ER and $0.074$ F1 when lowering \emph{Lite}.

\noindent\emph{4) Zero-tuning cohorts and nested LODO (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} Across all $118$ untouched streams, \emph{Lite} raises causal ER in all nine datasets ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$) and cuts latency in six of eight multi-step sets. On four zero-tuning external sets ($n=37$), stream-level Wilcoxon tests give $+15.1\%$ ER ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0004$) and $+0.064$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}<10^{-6}$); pooling GECCO ($n=38$) yields $+15.8\%$ ER and $+0.068$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}\le 0.0002$). Because four independent clusters limit an exact two-sided dataset sign-flip test to $p_{\min}=2/2^4=0.125$ ($0.0625$ across five sets with GECCO), dataset-level uncertainty remains high despite unanimous wins, while $80$ unseen streams from tuned sets trade $+10.5\%$ ER ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0080$) against $-0.016$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}=0.55$). Nine-fold nested LODO ($s=42$) selects $(0.990, 1.06)$ in every fold ($69.5\%$ vs.\ $52.8\%$ ER; $0.094$ vs.\ $0.076$ F1). Under nine-fold exclusion, ER stays positive ($+9.7\%\text{--}17.8\%$, $p_{\text{clus}}=0.0078$), while unweighted $\Delta\text{Pt-F1}$ dips when removing Exathlon ($-0.005$) due to SMAP.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Chronological distribution shift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration variance vs.\ chronological nonstationarity (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and within-block dependence while breaking chronological regime order. Across $n=118$, healthy-block permutation reduces measured archive $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ by $92.3\%$ ($7.44\%\to 0.57\%$; $\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams). Because shuffling exposes $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ to operating modes absent in chronological prefixes, this drop reflects sensitivity to uncalibrated regime evolution rather than an exact variance decomposition.
    \item \emph{Associated drift correlates:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) match stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$), whereas stabilized residual-covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), strongly correlating with quorum inflation ($4.8\times$) and $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In $3$-seed fault-free dev stress tests, terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and keeps $45^\circ$ basis rotations moderate ($6.8\%$ FPR). Mean steps, duty-cycle shifts, and baseline walks do not self-settle, raising FPR to $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ alongside $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$. Blindly auto-recalibrating on $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, however, absorbs persistent faults: fitting $400$ post-shift windows during a persistent $+2.5\sigma$ fault cuts post-buffer recall to $9.1\%$. Our conditional policy therefore flags $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ under frozen weights, quarantines candidate windows, and permits threshold updates only after operator or control-state confirmation ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall preserved; $4.8\%$ FPR on confirmed shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability and Two-Stage Deployment Funnel}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite calibration histories cannot provide distribution-free tail guarantees. Because sliding windows correlate strongly (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$), $105$ of $118$ untouched streams have $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) tests sensitivity to serial dependence without inventing unobserved extremes: across $n=118$, \texttt{MBB-Median} matches the unbootstrapped quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR; $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1), whereas upper-confidence-bound (UCB) \texttt{MBB-UCB90} and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds cost $8\text{--}17$ ER points and thinning inflates mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

Autonomous deployment pairs empirical screening with fallback routing, noting the archive-informed origin of these gates (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59$ streams, and two-stage screening ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$) accepts $52$ ($35$ meeting $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$ at $0.9\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $14.5\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$). Because $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ selection carries finite-sample optimism (median $5.8\%$ Wilson UCB at $N_{\text{eff,hold}}=66.7$), prospective $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ on those $52$ streams reaches $11.0\%$ mean and $3.9\%$ median ($53.8\%$ staying $\le 5\%$). In our three-seed rerun, accepted streams emit $18.1$ mean ($13.9$ median) false-alarm episodes per thousand healthy steps, vs.\ $16.8$ ($12.6$) unscreened and $8.8$ ($4.4$) for 1D. Adding a third drift gate ($D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$) retains $46$ streams at $6.8\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ ($2.9\%$ median, $60.9\%$ keeping $\le 5\%$). On that subset, \emph{Lite} rescues 1D SPOT under-triggering, lifting ER from $32.1\%$ to $49.8\%$ and Point-F1 from $0.030$ to $0.068$ ($p\le 0.0004$), at $6.8\%$ vs.\ $3.8\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$. Across all $118$ streams on equal footing, complete three-stage routing ($46$ \emph{Lite} plus $72$ 1D) poses a prospective trade-off against complete 1D SPOT rather than lowering false alarms. Complete hybrid routing raises ER ($52.5\%\to 59.4\%$, cluster CI $[+1.7,+16.7]$, $p=0.0004$) and Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.091$, $p=0.0003$), at $10.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ and $12.9$ vs.\ $8.8$ rerun episodes per thousand healthy steps ($p<10^{-4}$). Nine-fold nested LODO screening reproduces this identical $46$-stream out-of-fold split, staying stable ($42\text{--}46$ streams, $10.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) under tighter $3\%$ screening cuts alongside live $D_{\text{KS}}$ quarantine monitoring.
