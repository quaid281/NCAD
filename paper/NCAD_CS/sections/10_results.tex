\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section provides granular qualitative and quantitative analysis of Flow-JEPA across distinct anomaly regimes.

\subsection{Domain-by-Domain Performance Analysis}
As evidenced in Table~\ref{tab:multidataset_benchmark}, Flow-JEPA achieves consistent performance gains across all evaluated physical and cyber-physical domains:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{NASA SMAP Spacecraft Telemetry (0.6912 PA-F1):} On complex spacecraft bus telemetry, Flow-JEPA elevates macro PA-F1 to $\mathbf{0.6912}$, outperforming TimesNet ($0.4486$) and TranAD ($0.5645$). On thermal channel \texttt{A-7}, Flow-JEPA achieves near-perfect performance with $\mathbf{0.9996}$ PA-F1 (Precision: $0.9992$, Recall: $1.0000$, Oracle: $1.0000$). On actuator channel \texttt{P-3}, PA-F1 reaches $\mathbf{0.9766}$ (Precision: $0.9543$), proving that modeling continuous velocity fields captures multi-branching operational mode shifts that deterministic models fail to resolve.
    \item \textbf{Exathlon Distributed Cloud Streaming (0.5189 PA-F1):} In distributed streaming cluster logs, memory leaks and thread starvation manifest as gradual divergence in CPU/JVM heap ratios. On channel \texttt{10\_4\_1000000\_79}, Flow-JEPA reaches $\mathbf{0.7501}$ PA-F1 (Oracle: $0.8929$), while achieving perfect $\mathbf{1.0000}$ precision, recall, and point-F1 on channel \texttt{1\_2\_100000\_68}.
    \item \textbf{Room-Occupancy Environmental Dynamics (0.4100 PA-F1):} On multi-sensor environmental telemetry ($\text{CO}_2$, temperature, humidity), Flow-JEPA scores $\mathbf{0.4654}$ PA-F1 and $\mathbf{0.4649}$ unadjusted Point-F1 on channel \texttt{1}, effectively isolating human occupancy regime shifts from ambient environmental sensor drift.
    \item \textbf{Daphnet Parkinsonian Gait Accelerometry (0.6833 PA-F1, 0.9050 Oracle Ceiling):} Across all patient channels, Flow-JEPA achieves a grand Oracle ceiling of $\mathbf{0.9050}$. On channel \texttt{S03R02E0}, Flow-JEPA achieves $\mathbf{0.9657}$ PA-F1 (Precision: $0.9336$, Recall: $1.0000$) with a near-perfect $\mathbf{0.9940}$ Oracle ceiling.
    \item \textbf{OPPORTUNITY High-Dimensional Wearables (+180.1\% Lift):} In the 77-sensor wearable activity environment, Flow-JEPA delivers a dramatic leap from $0.0895$ (deterministic JEPA) and $0.0462$ (TranAD) to $\mathbf{0.2507}$ PA-F1 and $\mathbf{0.2067}$ unadjusted Point-F1. On channel \texttt{S1-ADL3}, Flow-JEPA scores $\mathbf{0.6425}$ PA-F1 where all baseline models scored $0.0000$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparative Analysis Against Modern SOTA Baselines}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:multidataset_benchmark}, Flow-JEPA achieves the highest grand macro average across benchmarks:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Flow-JEPA vs. DCdetector (Contrastive Representation Learning):} While DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} demonstrates reasonable performance on periodic datasets like Daphnet ($0.5055$), its performance collapses on high-dimensional noisy sensors (OPPORTUNITY: $0.0000$). Because DCdetector relies on contrastive cross-scale consistency, high-dimensional sensor noise degrades its representation alignment. In contrast, Flow-JEPA's non-contrastive VICReg loss prevents dimensional collapse and achieves robust anomaly scoring ($0.2507$ on OPPORTUNITY).
    \item \textbf{Flow-JEPA vs. Anomaly Transformer \& TimesNet (Reconstructive Deep Models):} Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} optimize signal-level reconstruction in raw waveform space. On NASA SMAP spacecraft telemetry, where sensors exhibit non-linear state transitions, reconstructive networks overfit high-frequency telemetry jitter, leading to inflated false positives (Macro PA-F1 of $0.2478$ and $0.4486$, respectively). Flow-JEPA's latent velocity field ignores high-frequency stochastic noise, capturing the true physical transition dynamics ($0.6912$ PA-F1).
    \item \textbf{Flow-JEPA vs. Baseline NCAD (Synthetic Injections):} Across all domains, Flow-JEPA improves upon the baseline NCAD from $0.2470$ to $\mathbf{0.5108}$ grand macro PA-F1 (\textbf{+106.8\% improvement}), validating that continuous velocity field modeling completely supersedes hand-crafted synthetic anomaly injections.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase-Space Geometric Attractor Dynamics}
The qualitative efficacy of Flow-JEPA is visualized in the 2D and 3D phase-space attractor trajectories of Figure~\ref{fig:phase_space_attractor}.
In nominal operation (Figure~\ref{fig:phase_space_attractor}(c) and (d)), periodic physical gait dynamics map directly onto a closed, smooth limit-cycle manifold. Because the system state evolves along this orbit, the Flow-JEPA velocity field $v_\psi(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}})$ anticipates the target embedding trajectory along the manifold, resulting in near-zero midpoint velocity discrepancy scores $S(t) \approx 0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:phase_space_attractor}(b)).

When pathological gait freezing occurs, the physical state collapses out of the periodic attractor into a degenerate point attractor (red trajectory). The velocity field continues to project nominal limit-cycle progression, generating a large, directional discrepancy vector that breaches the EVT decision threshold with zero detection latency.

\subsection{Resolution of Zero-F1 Detection Collapse}
In previous contrastive and heuristic architectures, channels exhibiting high non-stationary variance (such as Daphnet \texttt{S01R01E1} and SMAP \texttt{P-3}) suffered from zero-F1 threshold collapse. By incorporating the **Deterministic Midpoint Velocity Discrepancy** whitened by the empirical precision tensor $\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}$, prediction errors along unconstrained high-variance axes are normalized, recovering \texttt{P-3} from $0.2819$ to $\mathbf{0.9766}$ PA-F1 with $0.9543$ precision.
