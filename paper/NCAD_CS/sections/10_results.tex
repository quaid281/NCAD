\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark and Scope Boundaries}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports exploratory design-stage results on the 33-stream development suite (where stabilization and thresholds share $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Primary evidence is the five-block archive (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}). A separate single-seed 130-stream buffered screen gives context only and is not paired with the archive: TS-JEPA reaches Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$.

\noindent\emph{Configuration:} On the 33-stream suite both tiers reach similar buffered Point-F1 ($0.244$ vs.\ $0.242$), but \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.1\%$ vs.\ $12.8\%$) and leads macro Point-F1 ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.133$).

\noindent\emph{Slow-ramp boundary:} A $C=256$-step encoder cannot see drift slower than its window. Context and target summaries move together, yielding $0.000$ Point-F1 on all 14 GHL slow-ramp streams. Injected linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and length $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, with $15$ events per cell, map the cutoff. Steep multi-channel ramps ($L=64$, $a\ge 1\sigma$) reach $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ ER at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay, $5.7\text{--}7.7$ points above background chance. At $L=C$ the excess falls to $0.5\text{--}2.5$ points, and for $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$) chance-corrected detection vanishes for every JEPA variant (interval $[-4.8,+0.8]$ points).

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Live streaming is much harder than retrospective review. Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent scores degrade under causal reporting: buffered attribution averages residuals across every overlapping window covering a timestamp, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step with no averaging, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the shared SPOT threshold (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). On these development streams, a rate-matched Bernoulli alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ hits $28.1\%$ of events at $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Exploratory Horizon Sweep Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Shorter horizons trade buffered smoothing for lower live lag ($S-1$ steps). Retraining on five exploratory streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a); at $S=1$ all prefixes coincide), both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ with test FPR $\le 3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037$ and $0.050$ vs.\ $0.014$ and $0.012$) with $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ with $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER, and at $S=4$ delay falls to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps. Buffered Point-F1 instead peaks at long horizons: $0.290$ at $S=32$ and $0.255$ at $S=64$, versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot set a general rule.

\subsection{Design-Stage Operating Budgets and Fault Taxonomies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine the 19 development streams across budgets and fault types:
(1)~\emph{Diagnostic budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At a post-hoc matched $f^*=1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD; this ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs does not carry over to the archive (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).
(2)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range spikes ($>5\sigma$) are caught by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike, whereas in-range contextual faults alter cross-channel phase or velocity within normal bounds; on NASA contextual sequences \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet.

\subsection{Primary Held-Out Generalization, False-Alarm Tails, and Calibration Limits}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_tail_audit.tex}

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:tail_audit} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c) report the disjoint five-block archive. We label each result by its operating point: \emph{autonomous} (deployed healthy-only thresholds), \emph{matched calibration rate} (every detector set to the same healthy alarm rate), \emph{post hoc} (thresholds matched on test labels, diagnostic only), and \emph{buffered} (retrospective review).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Live event coverage and delay (autonomous, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} Across the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ ($+11.3$ points, cluster CI $[+5.1,+21.8]$, $9/9$ datasets, sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay ($70.8$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps). Pooled causal Point-F1 moves only from $0.076$ to $0.079$; the stream-level $p=0.014$ coexists with stream and cluster CIs that include zero ($[-0.019,+0.023]$ and $[-0.033,+0.060]$), so we treat this gain as modest and sensitive to the inference procedure. The gain depends on calibration length. With $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($n=59$), causal Point-F1 doubles from $0.040$ to $0.079$ (CI $[+0.016,+0.064]$, $p=2.7\times 10^{-5}$) and ER gains $16.0$ points. The independent rerun reproduces the doubling ($0.042\to 0.077$, $p=3.7\times 10^{-5}$), and the gain holds without Exathlon ($0.051$ vs.\ $0.031$ on $43$ streams, $p=0.0050$). It comes with a higher alarm burden on healthy test steps: median $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ is $5.5\%$ versus $0.6\%$. With $N_{\text{cal}}<500$, the scalar baseline has higher causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.080$, $p=0.31$) although the panel catches more events ($75.0\%$ vs.\ $68.4\%$). Under \emph{buffered} review, ER rises from $59.0\%$ to $67.4\%$ and Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.156$ ($p=0.022$) at lower buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($19.5\%\to 14.3\%$).
    \item \emph{Where the live gain comes from (matched calibration rate and post hoc, Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), \ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)):} At the panel's own low calibration rate (median $0.25\%$), it catches more causal events than the scalar baseline ($65.0\%$ vs.\ $52.6\%$, $p=1.7\times 10^{-5}$) and than Fisher, top-$k$, and max-$z$ pooling of the same residuals ($61.1\%$, $58.1\%$, $58.8\%$). At common healthy alarm rates of $0.5\%\text{--}5\%$ these differences shrink: the panel leads Fisher by $1\text{--}3.5$ ER points (significant at $2\%$ and $5\%$) while Fisher has equal or higher Point-F1, top-$k$ pooling is nearly indistinguishable, max-$z$ matches its ER, and the scalar baseline trails by $3\text{--}6$ ER points at statistically similar Point-F1. At post hoc matched test FPR the panel shows no ranking advantage: its ER ties the scalar baseline and trails max-$z$, and the scalar baseline has higher Point-F1 at $f=1\%$ ($0.084$ vs.\ $0.049$, $p=0.024$). The archive gains of the deployed panel therefore come mainly from its label-free operating point: the quorum settles at higher recall and shorter delay than scalar SPOT, while its continuous ranking matches that of simpler coordinate aggregators.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tail (autonomous, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a), Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c)):} For \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has mean $7.0\%$, median $0.78\%$, $90$th percentile $10.6\%$, and $95$th percentile $50.2\%$; $15$, $13$, $10$, and $10$ streams exceed $5\%$, $10\%$, $20\%$, and $30\%$ ($13$, $11$, $8$, and $5$ for Multi-Horizon 1D), and $54.2\%$ and $87.3\%$ of streams meet $1\%$ and $5\%$ budgets. The ten worst streams carry $79\%$ of all false-alarm mass. Short calibration contributes (Spearman $\rho=-0.32$ between $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; mean $16.0\%$ for $N_{\text{cal}}<250$ vs.\ $1.05\%$ for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 2{,}500$) but is not the only cause: three of the ten worst streams have $N_{\text{cal}}>1{,}300$. The strongest predictor is a shift between the healthy calibration and hold blocks, measured by the KS distance between their quorum counts ($\rho=0.63$; median $D_{\text{KS}}$ $0.55$ above $10\%$ FPR vs.\ $0.16$ at or below $5\%$, Mann--Whitney $p=1.6\times 10^{-6}$), whereas $N_{\text{eff}}$ is uncorrelated with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($\rho=-0.01$). Eight streams with $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.5$ carry $66\%$ of the panel's and $69\%$ of the scalar baseline's false-alarm mass, so these healthy regime changes defeat both; outside them, the panel's mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ is $2.6\%$. Healthy test steps show the same drift (median/mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ $3.0\%/15.2\%$ vs.\ $0.9\%/8.8\%$ for 1D).
    \item \emph{Quantile reliability and deployment conditions:} The quorum count has median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$ and median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, so its $99.5\%$ quantile has fewer than one expected effective exceedance ($N_{\text{eff}}<200$) on $105/118$ streams; we call a level $q$ \emph{supported} only when $N_{\text{eff}}(1-q)\ge 1$. Replacing the quorum quantile by its distribution-free $90\%$ upper confidence bound with $N_{\text{eff}}$ samples (calibration maximum when unsupported) lowers mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ only from $7.1\%$ to $5.8\%$ ($95$th percentile $50\%\to 42\%$) while cutting ER from $65.3\%$ to $47.7\%$ and Point-F1 from $0.080$ to $0.050$: conservative quantiles do not remove alarms caused by regime shifts. Autonomous use therefore requires three conditions: (i)~$N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, below which the scalar baseline may be preferable (choosing the panel only when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ raises pooled causal Point-F1 to $0.095$); (ii)~an acceptance check in which the frozen detector stays within the alarm budget (e.g., $5\%$) on an untouched healthy block such as $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; and (iii)~drift monitoring with recalibration after regime changes. On the $52$ streams meeting (i) and (ii), the panel stays ahead (causal Point-F1 $0.074$ vs.\ $0.038$, $p=3.0\times 10^{-4}$) at mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=0.9\%$, yet mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ is $12.4\%$, so (iii) remains necessary; other streams need operator supervision. On short histories ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$, $n=18$), two-fold cross-fitting cuts causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$ at $69.9\%$ ER.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit:} On development streams, chronological versus block-permuted threshold refits attribute $71.3\%$ (TS-JEPA) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ (reconstructors) of excess causal FPR to inter-block drift, versus $18.7\%$ with centering, without ruling out tail misfit.
\end{enumerate}
