\subsection{Primary 33-Stream and Archive-Wide 141-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} evaluates all 33 primary continuous telemetry streams under the two-stage chronological held-out protocol: Panel~(a) reports retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and held-out nominal FPRs. Archive-wide 141- and 162-stream results appear in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}; on a 130-stream baseline screen, TS-JEPA achieves $0.186$ stream and $0.160$ macro Point-F1 versus $0.152$ for TranAD, $0.151$ for TimesNet, $0.097$ for DCdetector, $0.088$ for NCAD, and $0.082$ for Anomaly Transformer. Pairing the 224-bit panel with surgical weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 ($0.262$), buffered event recall ($0.664$), causal event recall ($0.625$), fastest causal detection delay ($59.6$ steps, ahead of TimesNet's $62.2$ steps), and highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.139$), while the unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit Breaker} reaches $0.228$ buffered and $0.138$ causal Point-F1 ($0.411$ buffered and $0.312$ causal PR-AUC) at $1.1\%\text{--}1.7\%$ causal FPR versus $22.2\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD. Because quenching mean drift focuses causal trips on fault onset rather than sustained fault plateaus, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} trades lower causal point-wise coverage ($0.100$ vs.\ $0.138$ causal Point-F1) for faster onset detection ($59.6$ vs.\ $105.9$ steps) and higher event recall ($62.5\%$ vs.\ $60.7\%$).

\noindent\emph{Reducing Observation-Space False-Alarm Flooding:} On the 19 non-GHL primary streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), predicting spherical latent representations cuts held-out nominal FPR three-fold under buffered attribution to $7.8\%\text{--}9.8\%$ versus $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD ($p \le 0.045$), and by an order of magnitude under strictly causal streaming to $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($p = 0.010$). This separation holds across all $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$) and under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, where JEPAs maintain $0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors.

\noindent\emph{Replacing the Scalar Score:} Under 1D scoring, Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA fires very conservatively ($0.050$ causal and $0.148$ buffered Point-F1) because one or two drifting coordinates inflate the scalar threshold. On the same trained networks, the panel more than doubles causal Point-F1 to $0.116\text{--}0.138$ ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$) and cuts causal delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). On GHL ($\pi=0.013$), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1): low FPR without recall is not detection.

\noindent\emph{Scope of Applicability:} The evidence supports the panel only under four conditions: (i)~multichannel arrays ($K \ge 9$; SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, SMD, Exathlon, OPPORTUNITY); (ii)~sampling rates at which a 63-step emission lag is acceptable, because the panel was evaluated only at $S = 64$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), which rules out hourly Metro ($63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$); (iii)~calibration blocks of roughly $10^3$ steps or more (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}); and (iv)~faults that move the latent summary. It fails on the slow process ramps of GHL and Metro ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1) and on single-channel freezes absorbed by LayerNorm, where the panel alone catches $25.0\%\text{--}29.2\%$ of events and a rolling variance monitor is needed to reach $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why the panel matters most for real-time streaming. Without 64-step averaging, causal whitened residuals have a heavier right tail than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$ vs.\ $1.25$). Under 1D scoring, hijacker dials push the threshold onto that tail, so 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA alerts on only $2.4\%$ of test steps and flags isolated peaks inside each anomaly ($0.050\text{--}0.052$ causal F1). With per-dial switches and onset relays (\ref{eq:onset_relay}), \emph{224-Bit Breaker} stays active across the fault window ($0.138$ causal F1, $60.7\%$ event recall, $105.9$-step delay, $1.7\%$ FPR). A rate-matched random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, would catch $28.1\%$ of events at the panel's nominal rate, but $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR, above TimesNet's own $58.8\%$.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Structural Emission Lag, and Fast-Tripwire Regimes}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a structural emission lag of $S-1 = 63$ steps before a causal trailing window decision is emitted at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams at three seeds. At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), latent prediction keeps causal FPR at $0.4\%\text{--}0.6\%$ versus $2.5\%\text{--}3.1\%$ for TimesNet, with higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$) and short delays ($\Delta t = 57.8\text{--}85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases from $16$ to $64$, TimesNet causal FPR climbs from $6.1\%$ to $21.3\%$, whereas latent predictors remain bounded at $\le 2.0\%$. Thus, $S=64$ suits high-rate telemetry ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or subsystem trend monitoring on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP and MSL ($10.5\,\text{min}$), while low-rate streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$) and zero-lag tripwires require $S \in \{1, 4\}$. These short-horizon runs use 1D latent scoring: the panel needs four nested prefixes and has not been evaluated below $S = 64$.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ for latent predictors, we evaluate three operating-point comparisons:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Autonomous circuit-breaker points and nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Without test labels, Multi-Horizon Reynolds 224-Bit Strict ($53.4\%$ ER at $1.1\%$ FPR) and 224-Bit Breaker ($60.7\%$ ER at $1.7\%$ FPR) dominate the low-FPR frontier; at calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$, Multi-Horizon Reynolds 224-Bit reaches $77.4\%$ causal ER versus $61.8\%$ for TimesNet and $60.2\%$ for TranAD.
    \item \emph{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} This diagnostic sets each threshold from test labels, so no deployed system could reach these points; it only checks that the low false-alarm rates are not an artifact of low alert frequency (vertical dotted guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)). At a $1\%$ test FPR budget ($f^* = 0.01$), Multi-Horizon Reynolds 224-Bit detects $58.4\%$ of anomaly events ($56.2\%\text{--}57.8\%$ for 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds and $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA), nearly tripling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and quadrupling TranAD ($14.9\%$), with a similar gap at $f^* = 0.05$ ($72.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$).
    \item \emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Point anomalies ($\pm 5\sigma$ spikes) are caught even by a marginal $z$-score (\texttt{ZScoreMax}); contextual anomalies stay in range and break multi-step structure. On the 10 primary SMAP/MSL streams stratified by NASA's taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)), observation-space detectors collapse on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC). Latent JEPAs lead on these five contextual sequences; the 224-bit panel matches 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA ($0.658$ vs.\ $0.652$ buffered PR-AUC, $75.0\%$ autonomous recall, and identical $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) at a higher autonomous FPR ($1.8\%$ vs.\ $0.6\%$), so this evidence is preliminary. Across all 19 non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b), Multi-Horizon Reynolds 224-Bit doubles reconstructor recall on contextual faults ($61.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) and leads on point faults ($79.2\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Archive-Wide Held-Out Analysis, Calibration Length, and Drift Decomposition}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates the panel on the three-horizon Speculative-JEPA ($192$ distinct dials, Section~\ref{subsec:triad_formulations}) across 141 streams in 12 datasets at three seeds; 118 of these streams were never used to tune the panel:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Held-out generalization on 118 streams and Laminar-JEPA across 162 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} On the 118 held-out streams, replacing the scalar score on Speculative-JEPA with the panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.072\text{--}0.083$ to $0.134\text{--}0.143$, lifts causal recall from $48.4\%\text{--}51.2\%$ to $66.2\%\text{--}72.8\%$, and cuts causal delay from $84.7\text{--}107.8$ to $55.8\text{--}77.0$ steps, at a higher causal held-out FPR ($7.3\%\text{--}8.3\%$ vs.\ $5.1\%\text{--}5.9\%$; $141$-stream causal $\Delta\text{F1} = +0.062$, dataset $95\%$ CI $[+0.044, +0.072]$, $p = 0.007$). Across $162$ streams ($15$ datasets, including all $118$ held-out streams), surgical quenching in four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} cuts causal held-out FPR from $6.71\%$ to $6.17\%$ ($\Delta = -0.0055$, $p = 0.0011$, dataset $p = 0.027$; $7.93\% \to 7.26\%$ on $n{=}118$, $p = 0.030$) and test FPR from $14.63\%$ to $13.93\%$ ($p < 10^{-4}$, dataset $p = 0.016$), with median causal held-out FPR $1.03\%$ ($1.13\%$ on $n{=}118$) and test FPR $1.59\%$ ($70.4\%$ of streams $\le 2.0\%$).
    \item \emph{Per-dataset consistency (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} The panel leads causal Point-F1 in all six multi-stream held-out datasets, most on SMAP ($0.194$ vs.\ $\le 0.129$) and SMD ($0.090$ vs.\ $\le 0.042$), but it also has the highest causal FPR in each, most on Exathlon ($23.5\%$ vs.\ $\le 13.7\%$). Under buffered attribution it trails the best 1D JEPA on SMAP, Exathlon, and OPPORTUNITY. On CreditCard, GECCO, and PSM (one stream each) all methods lie within $0.007$ causal Point-F1.
    \item \emph{Calibration length:} Every quantile in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire})--(\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) is estimated from overlapping windows. On the 19 primary streams the effective sample size of the calibration dials is about $0.31$ of the window count (median $152$ of $408$), so $q_{0.999}$ rests on few independent observations. On the 118 held-out streams, mean causal FPR is $19.2\%$ for the 12 streams with $<250$ calibration steps and $1.6\%$ for the 22 with $\ge 2{,}500$. Cutting five long primary streams into short sub-streams gives causal held-out FPR of $18.9\%$, $8.3\%$, $2.6\%$, and $4.1\%$ at $100$, $250$, $500$, and $1{,}000$ calibration windows versus $0.5\%$ on the full block, while the coefficient of variation of $K_{224}$ falls from $0.34$ to $0.10$ (1D SPOT stays $\le 1.2\%$ from $250$ windows on). The per-dial quantiles thus need roughly $10^3$ calibration steps.
    \item \emph{Three-stage permutation drift decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across the 19 primary streams under chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits confirms that inter-block regime drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA ($0.0229$ vs.\ $0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.210\text{--}0.230$ vs.\ $0.043\text{--}0.046$), whereas barycenter centering in Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($0.0056$ vs.\ $0.0047$) while per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j$ quarantine individual drifting coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
