\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ F1 across $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), because context and target latents shift together and cancel. Across linear ramps ($a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$, duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$), chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$. Across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds), mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $433.9$ on exponential $1-e^{-t/512}$ and $586.7$ on $0.001\sigma$/step bias as $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

Pairing \emph{Lite} with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) Mahalanobis drift monitor ($\alpha=0.02$), anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, lifts real GHL causal ER from $0.8\%$ ($0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$. A union hybrid reaches $58.5\%$ ER ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Post-fault GHL baseline wander, however, drives uncompensated test FPR to $74.4\%$: sliding prediction misses slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift flags benign wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned ZCA-anchored latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across block indices ($m\in\{1,2,3\}$; exact horizons under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and cross-block drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $m\in\{2,3\}$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking provides \emph{controlled perturbation attribution} across input channels. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under injected $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Because latent features mix sensors nonlinearly and real field datasets lack ground-truth root-cause labels, this validates synthetic perturbation sensitivity rather than causal fault localization on natural failures, and $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines remain sub-quorum.

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit} evaluate the untouched archive ($n=118$) along four questions:

\noindent\emph{1) Label-free operating-point placement over 1D JEPA:} Rather than improving intrinsic score discrimination, the $128$-dial quorum shifts the live operating point toward broader event coverage at a higher false-alarm cost (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Causal ER increases from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$), backed by dataset sign-flip and BH tests ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$, $p\le 0.016$). In exchange, held-out FPR increases ($4.4\%\to 6.4\%$ mean; $0.39\%\to 0.93\%$ median), and prospective test FPR rises from $8.8\%$ to $17.0\%$. Step recall climbs ($0.134\to 0.205$) while precision drops ($0.165\to 0.146$). Across $87$ shared streams in our three-seed rerun, exact segment-paired onset latency falls by $41.4$ steps ($74.7$ vs.\ $116.1$, $p=0.0026$). Pairing finite stream-mean delays across all $97$ canonical streams where both models trigger cuts latency by $74.6$ steps (cluster CI $[-156.3,-31.1]$, $p=5.4{\times}10^{-6}$). Detection-conditioned means drop from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps, falling below the $63$-step window offset on sharp faults in Exathlon and GECCO.

Across datasets, Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) rises in six of nine sets with median $+0.027$ and interquartile range $[-0.014,+0.103]$. Because SMAP, OPPORTUNITY, and MSL decline (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), the dataset-cluster CI spans zero ($[-0.026,+0.069]$, $p_{\text{str}}=0.0009$). Gains concentrate on $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ streams, doubling Point-F1 ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=0.047$) with $+20.4$ ER points, while 1D leads on shorter prefixes ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$). Replacing the additive floor in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) with scale-homogeneous $\times(1+10^{-4})$ leaves $99.96\%$ of test decisions unchanged ($|\Delta\text{ER}|=0.055\%$).

\noindent\emph{2) Trade-off against the 224-dial reference:} The $224$-dial reference raises causal ER to $69.4\%$ (cluster CI $[+1.3,+6.6]$ above \emph{Lite}, $p=0.016$) and lowers p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($49.3\%$ vs.\ $64.7\%$), at similar Point-F1 ($0.091$, $p=0.15$). In return, \emph{Lite} lowers mean ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $7.0\%$, $p=1.3{\times}10^{-7}$) and median held-out FPR ($0.93\%$ vs.\ $1.4\%$). It also halves buffered FPR ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $21.0\%$, $p=9.2{\times}10^{-10}$) and lifts the $\le 5\%$ pass rate from $84.7\%$ to $88.1\%$.

\noindent\emph{3) External reconstructors and matched-budget parity:} Under untouched-archive SPOT, \emph{Lite} beats Linear PCA Forecaster by $+34.5$ ER points and $+0.063$ F1 ($p\le 8.2{\times}10^{-12}$). TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1, both $0.236$ PR-AUC) reach higher raw F1 only at $25.6\%$ and $24.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$. At $1\%$ test FPR under the canonical linear-quantile protocol, TimesNet ($25.9\%$) and TranAD ($25.5\%$) trail \emph{Lite} ($51.2\%$) and 1D ($55.6\%$). By contrast, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b) applies upper-order quantiles to discrete dial counts in a separate three-seed rerun, yielding $52.9\%$ vs.\ $54.9\%$ ER and $0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$ F1, as 1D achieves higher rerun precision ($0.179$ vs.\ $0.171$) and recall ($0.068$ vs.\ $0.042$). Archive PR-AUC matches across JEPA variants ($0.152$ vs.\ $0.162$ causal; $0.229$ vs.\ $0.230$ buffered). At matched $r=0.5\%$ calibration budget (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)), \emph{Lite} incurs higher held-out FPR ($7.3\%$ vs.\ $5.8\%$). Equalizing calibration rates directly confirms operating-point parity: raising 1D yields $53.8\%$ ER and $0.083$ F1, vs.\ $53.0\%$ ER and $0.074$ F1 when lowering \emph{Lite}.

\noindent\emph{4) Zero-tuning cohorts and nested LODO (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} Across all nine datasets ($n=118$), \emph{Lite} raises causal ER everywhere ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.0039$) and cuts delay in six of eight multi-step sets. On four zero-tuning external sets ($n=37$), stream-level Wilcoxon tests show $+15.1\%$ ER ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0004$) and $+0.064$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}<10^{-6}$). Pooling GECCO ($n=38$) yields $+15.8\%$ ER and $+0.068$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}\le 0.0002$). Because four clusters bound exact two-sided dataset sign-flip tests at $p_{\min}=2/2^4=0.125$ ($0.0625$ across five sets with GECCO), dataset uncertainty remains high despite unanimous wins. Meanwhile, $80$ unseen streams from tuned sets trade $+10.5\%$ ER ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0080$) against $-0.016$ F1 ($p_{\text{str}}=0.55$). Nine-fold nested LODO ($s=42$) selects $(0.990, 1.06)$ in every fold ($69.5\%$ vs.\ $52.8\%$ ER), and exclusion ER stays positive across all folds ($+9.7\%\text{--}17.8\%$, $p_{\text{clus}}=0.0078$), dipping in F1 only without Exathlon ($-0.005$) due to SMAP.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Chronological distribution shift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration variance vs.\ chronological nonstationarity (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling length-$\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ blocks across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and local dependence while breaking chronological order, cutting archive $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ by $92.3\%$ ($7.44\%\to 0.57\%$; $\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams). Because this exposes $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ to future modes, the drop reflects uncalibrated regime evolution rather than pure calibration variance.
    \item \emph{Associated drift correlates:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) differ little from stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$). By contrast, stabilized residual-covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), strongly correlating with $4.8\times$ quorum inflation and $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In $3$-seed fault-free dev stress tests, terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and keeps $45^\circ$ basis rotations moderate ($6.8\%$ FPR). Mean steps, duty-cycle shifts, and baseline walks raise FPR to $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ alongside $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$. Blindly auto-recalibrating when $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ absorbs persistent $+2.5\sigma$ faults ($9.1\%$ post-buffer recall). Operationally, crossing $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ keeps live alarms active under frozen thresholds while accumulating a $400$-window passive buffer. Only a control-state tag or operator clearance confirms nominal transition, refitting $(\tau_j,K_{\text{Lite}})$ under frozen encoders and resetting $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall; $4.8\%$ post-shift FPR; stale buffers expire unchanged).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability, Three-Stage Screening, and Hybrid Routing}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite calibration histories cannot provide distribution-free tail guarantees. With strong autocorrelation (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$), $105$ of $118$ untouched streams have $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Testing serial dependence via a Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB; $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$), \texttt{MBB-Median} matches the baseline quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR; $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1). Conversely, upper-confidence-bound (UCB) \texttt{MBB-UCB90} and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds forfeit $8\text{--}17$ ER points, while thinning inflates mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

\noindent\emph{1) Screening attrition and selection optimism (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} Archive-informed gating narrows eligibility in three stages: requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59$ streams, adding $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ accepts $52$ ($35$ also satisfy $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$), and adding $D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$ leaves $46$. Rejection is dominated by prefix length rather than drift: all $40$ SMAP streams ($\max N_{\text{cal}}=344$) and $12$ of $13$ MSL streams fail $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ outright. By contrast, Exathlon clears length on $16$ of $17$ streams before losing five to $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ and six to $D_{\text{KS}}>0.50$. Because $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ gating incurs selection optimism ($5.8\%$ median Wilson UCB), two-stage prospective $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ averages $11.0\%$ ($3.9\%$ median; $53.8\%$ within $5\%$). Those $52$ streams emit $18.1$ rerun episodes per thousand steps ($16.8$ unscreened), while the $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$ slice averages $14.5\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$. Adding $D_{\text{KS}}\le 0.50$ lowers accepted $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ to $6.8\%$ mean ($2.9\%$ median; $60.9\%$ within $5\%$). On that $46$-stream subset, \emph{Lite} rescues 1D SPOT under-triggering, lifting ER ($32.1\%\to 49.8\%$) and Point-F1 ($0.030\to 0.068$, $p_{\text{str}}\le 0.0004$) with higher test FPR ($6.8\%$ vs.\ $3.8\%$).

\noindent\emph{2) Complete 118-stream trade-off vs.\ 1D SPOT:} Evaluated across all $118$ streams, three-stage routing offers a recall-prioritized option rather than a universal default. Against 1D SPOT, hybrid routing lifts ER ($52.5\%\to 59.4\%$, CI $[+1.7,+16.7]$, $p_{\text{str}}=0.0004$). Treating cluster inference as primary gives exact nine-dataset sign-flip $p_{\text{clus}}=0.0156$ ($p_{\min}=0.0039$). Point-F1 shifts from $0.076$ to $0.091$ ($p_{\text{str}}=0.0003$, $p_{\text{clus}}=0.070$) while mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ rises from $8.8\%$ to $10.0\%$ ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.109$). Rerun episodes climb from $8.8$ to $12.9$ per $1{,}000$ steps ($p_{\text{clus}}=0.031$, $p_{\text{str}}<10^{-4}$), reflecting $49$ accepted rerun streams ($12.2$ episodes, $+39.0\%$, under the canonical $46$-stream mask).

\noindent\emph{3) Cost-weighted LODO routing (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a,c)):} Holding $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ fixed, each fold chooses ($\tau_{\text{F}},\tau_{\text{K}}$) minimizing $J(w) = (1 - \text{ER}) + w\,\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ on eight labeled training sets while deploying label-free on the ninth set. Unrounded break-even slope vs.\ 1D SPOT is $w=5.92$ ($5.75$ rounded). For recall-prioritized weights ($w\le 4$), out-of-fold routing selects $46$ streams stably. Higher false-alarm weights tighten acceptance to $40$ streams at $w=5$, ten at $w=6$, eight for $w\le 15$, and 1D SPOT at $w\ge 20$ (out-of-fold rows need not minimize test $J(w)$ across the table).
