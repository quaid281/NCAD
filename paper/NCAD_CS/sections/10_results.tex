\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$), with buffered (a) and strictly causal (b) attribution reported separately. The principal finding is false-alarm control; the evidence for general detection superiority is inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} Latent predictive scoring via Operator-Entropy JEPA and TS-JEPA substantially suppresses false alarms under static calibration: on 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076\text{--}0.086$ versus $0.300$ for TimesNet and $0.307$ for TranAD (four-fold reduction buffered, and $12\text{--}18\times$ causal: $0.014\text{--}0.019$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.243$). Across all 33 streams the paired buffered reduction is significant: OpEntropy JEPA achieves $-0.144$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.241, -0.065]$, $p=0.006$) and $-0.142$ vs.\ TranAD ($p=0.006$); TS-JEPA yields $-0.153$ ($p=0.002$) and $-0.151$ ($p=0.003$). Dataset-clustering ($N=7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$. The gain is stable under leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) resampling: across folds, macro buffered FPR stays $0.038\text{--}0.070$ (OpEntropy) and $0.038\text{--}0.061$ (TS-JEPA) vs.\ $0.084\text{--}0.176$ (TimesNet) and $0.098\text{--}0.183$ (TranAD). On MSL, TimesNet records lower buffered FPR than OpEntropy ($0.133$ vs.\ $0.191$), while on Genesis, Metro, and SWAN reconstructors record zero FPR with zero F1, indicating inactivity rather than calibration.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not establish superior anomaly detection. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams paired differences are inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$). Between-stream SDs match the means (SMAP $0.415 \pm 0.389$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $0.289 \pm 0.334$ for TimesNet). Only SMAP exceeds constant-alarm reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}{=}0.236$ ($0.415$ and $0.401$); MSL ($0.237, 0.303$ vs.\ $0.381$), Daphnet, and SWAN fall below it. PR-AUC leads over prevalence are the primary signal (SMAP $0.504$/$0.525$ at $\pi{=}0.150$; MSL $0.434$/$0.422$ at $\pi{=}0.255$, vs.\ $0.455$/$0.311$ for TimesNet).

\noindent\textbf{Regimes of Applicability:} Spacecraft telemetry (SMAP, MSL) shows the largest PR-AUC lead over prevalence and reconstructors, though F1 margins sit within between-stream SD and labels carry known defects \cite{wu2023flawed}. Quiet faults are filtered with ambient noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d)). Process noise and continuous ramps (GHL, Metro) defeat all detectors ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), and on high-prevalence streams (Daphnet $\pi{=}14.8\%$, SWAN $\pi{=}27.9\%$) all detectors score near or below constant alarm.

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman entropy, Reynolds stress, potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data do not support an aggregate gain: paired differences against TS-JEPA are $+0.002$ Point-F1 for Operator-Entropy (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$; PR-AUC $+0.002$, $p{=}0.86$; FPR $+0.009$, $p{=}0.75$), $-0.015$ for Reynolds-Stress ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$), and $-0.081$ for Potential-Flow ($p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Physical regularizers provide no aggregate detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction; the entropy term preserves operator conditioning without altering representation rank or detection.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Strictly causal results provide the operational evaluation; buffered results are retrospective. Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b) reports strictly causal streaming. On 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression. On 14 GHL streams, causal FPR falls below $0.009$, but causal Point-F1 is near-zero ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 is $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and causal event recall is $46.55\%$ vs.\ $58.82\%$: without lookahead buffers, latent scoring operates as an ultra-conservative filter trading sensitivity for false-alarm suppression. Dynamic SPOT on five incident streams suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ under drift.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
At $S{=}64$, scores emit $63$ steps after window opening ($10.5$~min on 10-s telemetry; $63$~h on hourly Metro data). Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models at $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). Shortening horizon preserves false-alarm control: at $S{=}1$ (no emission lag), OpEntropy holds causal FPR at $0.005$ (TimesNet $0.025$) with event recall $0.729$ (TimesNet $0.895$). Causal FPR for reconstructors rises steeply with horizon ($0.025 \to 0.213$), while latent models remain at $0.003\text{--}0.020$. Buffered attribution inflates FPR with $S$ ($0.005 \to 0.099$ for OpEntropy). Where rapid response matters, $S{=}1$ to $4$ is the natural operating regime.

\subsection{Detection Quality at Matched Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We fit empirical-quantile thresholds on nominal calibration budgets $q \in [10^{-4}, 0.9]$ (Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)). At every causal budget, latent predictors raise fewer alarms than reconstructors at comparable or higher precision: at $q^*{=}0.05$, TS-JEPA achieves event recall $0.774$ at $179.8$ false alarms per 1{,}000 nominal steps (precision $0.257$) versus $0.618$ at $262.3$ alarms ($0.221$) for TimesNet. Retrospectively buffered at $q^*{=}0.001$, the recall ordering reverses ($0.486$ vs.\ $0.583$), while precision remains higher ($0.277$ vs.\ $0.194$) and alarm volume lower ($207.9$ vs.\ $259.2$). Budgets match calibration quantiles rather than realized out-of-sample false-alarm rates (at $q^*{=}0.05$, realized test FPR is $0.180$ vs.\ $0.262$). Because anomalies span multi-hundred steps (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), continuous alarming at $30\%$ FPR catches over $95\%$ of segments by random coincidence ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$); TimesNet's $58\%$ recall at $30\%$ nominal FPR falls within chance expectations.

To test whether latent scoring detects more events at genuinely equal alarm rates, we evaluate direct matched realized test-FPR operating points across all 19 non-GHL streams at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$. At ultra-low realized false-alarm rates ($f^* = 0.01$ strictly causal), TS-JEPA achieves event recall $0.439$ versus $0.217$ for TimesNet (paired difference $+0.223$, $95\%$ bootstrap CI $[+0.007, +0.428]$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.050$) and $0.149$ for TranAD (paired difference $+0.290$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.085, +0.503]$, $p = 0.035$). At moderate realized FPR ($f^* \ge 0.10$ causal and across smear mappings), event recall differences narrow or favor reconstructors. When common support is restricted to $f^* \in [0.10, 0.50]$ without extrapolation, the causal TS-JEPA--TimesNet difference is $+0.061$ ($95\%$ dataset-bootstrap CI $[-0.153, +0.329]$, sign-permutation $p = 0.625$, 20 paired cells across four streams). Latent prediction holds a decisive detection lead specifically under stringent ultra-low false-alarm constraints ($f^* \le 0.01$), whereas under relaxed budgets reconstructors recover comparable event coverage.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out of sample? It does not. At design risk $q{=}10^{-3}$, observed held-out FPR is $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ buffered and $1.0\text{--}1.5\%$ causal for the JEPAs, which is a calibration failure under distribution shift: the latent models overshoot the target by one to two orders of magnitude and are only less wrong than the reconstructors ($\approx 22\%$ buffered, $16\text{--}18\%$ causal). Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under strictly causal trailing and buffered smear inference. The calibrator evaluated is the \emph{static} peaks-over-threshold step of SPOT, a single threshold fit once on the calibration block. Three realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Causal vs.\ buffered smear overshoot:} Under causal trailing, TS-JEPA reaches held-out FPR $0.0101$ (median $0.0002$, IQR $[0.0000, 0.0025]$); buffered inference smears each alarm over $S{=}64$ steps and reaches $0.0526$, below the $S \cdot q = 6.4\%$ smear ceiling. TimesNet and TranAD exceed that ceiling even causally ($0.1795$, $0.1601$), which indicates distributional breakdown.
    \item \textbf{Multi-risk calibration breakdown:} Across a multi-target sweep $q \in \{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}\}$, static thresholding overshoots nominal design targets across all models. For TS-JEPA, median causal FPR is $0.0001$ (IQR $[0.0000, 0.0062]$) at $q{=}10^{-4}$, $0.0047$ ($[0.0000, 0.0260]$) at $q{=}10^{-3}$, $0.0106$ ($[0.0000, 0.0347]$) at $q{=}10^{-2}$, and $0.0585$ ($[0.0349, 0.0622]$) at $q{=}10^{-1}$. TimesNet overshoots far more severely, with median causal FPR pinned at $0.1360$ across $q \in \{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}\}$ (IQR $[0.0729, 0.2576]$ and $[0.0815, 0.2945]$) before reaching $0.1603$ ($[0.1108, 0.3510]$) at $q{=}10^{-2}$ and $0.2889$ ($[0.1440, 0.6186]$) at $q{=}10^{-1}$. TranAD similarly yields median causal FPR $0.1177$, $0.1262$, $0.1651$, and $0.2236$. Static calibration fails to maintain design risk across models because inter-block distribution drift shifts score quantiles between calibration and test blocks.
    \item \textbf{Parity with other rules:} Empirical p99.5, validation-maximum, and split conformal thresholding ($\alpha{=}10^{-3}$) also overshoot because drift violates exchangeability, and the kurtosis-adaptive multiplier raises TS-JEPA buffered FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$.
\end{enumerate}

Empirical tail diagnostics on nine non-GHL streams confirm tail scarcity: median exceedances above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ ($16$--$70$), and Grimshaw yields degenerate $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits. To separate drift from sample scarcity, an exchangeable permutation control samples calibration and evaluation splits from pooled nominal blocks: causal TS-JEPA FPR drops from $0.0121$ ($12.1\times$ overshoot) to $0.0019$ ($1.9\times$ overshoot), and TimesNet drops from $0.0872$ to $0.0037$ ($96\%$ reduction; block permutation $L{=}64$ yields $5.8\times$ overshoot for TS-JEPA). This indicates inter-block drift dominates calibration failure under exchangeability, with autocorrelation secondary. Across $N=1080$ dumped cells, KS drift between calibration and test blocks predicts realized FPR (Spearman $\rho = +0.377$, 95\% CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$). In the controlled-drift stress test (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), 16 streams, design $q{=}10^{-3}$), static SPOT sits at $0.061$ FPR without injection; rolling calibrators hold risk to $1\%\text{--}7\%$ under discrete shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33\text{--}0.39$ FPR).
