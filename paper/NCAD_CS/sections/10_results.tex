\subsection{Development 33-Stream and Untouched Held-Out 118/162-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following the cohort hierarchy in Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} evaluates the 33-stream development benchmark under retrospective buffered attribution (Panel~(a)), real-time causal streaming (Panel~(b)), and per-dataset stratification (Panel~(c)), while Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} reports our primary independent generalization evidence across the 118 untouched held-out validation streams and the 162-stream archive. Across both populations, as well as the 130-stream external screen where TS-JEPA ($0.186$ stream and $0.160$ macro Point-F1) outpaces TranAD ($0.152$), TimesNet ($0.151$), DCdetector ($0.097$), NCAD ($0.088$), and Anomaly Transformer ($0.082$), a consistent physical picture emerges: projecting telemetry onto a compact spherical manifold stops observation-space false-alarm flooding, and decomposing the resulting residual trajectory into a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel overcomes the sensitivity loss of 1D scalar norm collapse.

\noindent\emph{Operational Trade-Offs and Detector Selection:} Rather than a single universal winner across every metric, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} reveal a structured operational trade-off governed by how each detector handles background residual drift. In the unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit Breaker}, sustained post-onset residual excursions keep multiple coordinate switches tripped throughout an anomaly plateau, maximizing real-time causal Point-F1 ($17/0$ stream wins over 1D scalar collapse, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$, balancing point precision $P = 0.252$ and recall $R = 0.123$ vs.\ $P = 0.285, R = 0.032$ for 1D Reynolds). On the 118 untouched held-out streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), replacing 1D scalar SPOT with the circuit-breaker panel simultaneously lifts causal precision ($P: 0.175 \to 0.241$) and more than doubles recall ($R: 0.081 \to 0.179$). Pairing the 224-bit panel with surgical weight-space quenching in \emph{Laminar-JEPA} shifts the detector into an onset-focused regime: subtracting mean calibration drift in predictor weight space strips away persistent background offsets, concentrating causal trips sharply at the dynamical transition where a fault first erupts ($P = 0.214, R = 0.088$). Consequently, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} achieves the fastest causal detection response, the highest causal and buffered event recall, and the strongest retrospective buffered Point-F1 and Dataset-Macro accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, while trading away continuous causal firing across stationary post-onset plateaus. Meanwhile, raising the panel quorum in \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit Strict} establishes a conservative sub-$1.1\%$ false-alarm floor ($P = 0.254, R = 0.097$). Practitioners can therefore match the configuration directly to their operational priority: \emph{Laminar-JEPA} for earliest fault warning, highest event discovery, or retrospective root-cause attribution; unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} for continuous real-time point coverage; and \emph{224-Bit Strict} when false-alarm budgets are tightly capped.

\noindent\emph{Eliminating Observation-Space False-Alarm Flooding and Defining Scope Boundaries:} Why do observation-space reconstructors and forecasters suffer false-alarm rates exceeding $22\%\text{--}30\%$ across nominal held-out blocks in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} (and $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ vs.\ $0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ for JEPAs under zero-tuning LODO transfer)? Reconstructing raw waveforms forces the scoring function to penalize benign sensor amplitude swings, high-frequency phase jitter, and unmodeled load transitions. Predicting running-barycenter-centered embeddings on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ absorbs these benign variations into the geometry of the latent sphere, cutting held-out false alarms three-fold under buffered attribution ($p \le 0.045$) and by an order of magnitude under causal streaming ($p = 0.010$; dataset-clustered $p = 0.016$). At the same time, Panel~(c) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} highlights an essential physical boundary on the 14 GHL process streams: when faults unfold as ultra-slow thermal or pressure ramps over thousands of steps, both JEPAs and linear autoregressors remain virtually silent. A low false-alarm rate without event recall is not detection. Taken together, our experiments define four clear boundary conditions for deployment: (i)~multichannel sensor arrays ($K \ge 9$) where cross-sensor dynamical coupling is informative; (ii)~prediction horizon $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ matched to the tolerable reporting lag $S-1$ of the sampling cadence (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}); (iii)~calibration histories providing at least $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ effective independent observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows), or IQR/Beta-smoothed thresholds on shorter series (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}); and (iv)~multi-channel trajectory faults, supplemented by the channel-level residual branch when isolated single-sensor freeze or bias faults are of concern (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} exposes why standard 1D latent detectors break down when moved from retrospective buffered attribution to real-time causal streaming. Buffered attribution averages residuals across all 64 overlapping horizon predictions covering each timestamp, smoothing out transient noise and yielding a moderate right tail ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$). Under causal streaming, each decision relies on a single right-edge step without temporal averaging, nearly doubling tail kurtosis ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$). When 128 whitened coordinates are summed into a 1D scalar score, one or two high-variance coordinates push the global SPOT threshold high onto that heavy tail, leaving scalar Reynolds JEPA blind to all but brief anomaly spikes. Replacing the scalar sum with 224 independently calibrated circuit-breaker switches and right-edge onset relays (\ref{eq:onset_relay}) prevents any single drifting coordinate from inflating the thresholds of the remaining 223 dials, sustaining detection across the fault window. Evaluating the analytical event recall of a rate-matched Bernoulli random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, further clarifies the operational stakes: at our panel's $<2\%$ causal false-alarm rate, random guessing catches only $28.1\%$ of events, whereas at the $24.3\%$ false-alarm rate of TimesNet, a random coin flip catches $60.0\%$ of anomaly windows by chance alone, surpassing TimesNet's own causal recall.

\subsection{Multi-Horizon 224-Bit Evaluation Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ and Latency Trade-Offs}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Because a target window of length $S$ emits its causal decision at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}), operating at $S=64$ introduces a structural reporting lag of $63$ steps. A 63-step lag is negligible on kilohertz vibration rigs or $1\text{--}10\,\text{Hz}$ spacecraft telemetry, but translates to over an hour on minute-level industrial historians and over two days on hourly traffic streams. To determine whether the 224-bit circuit-breaker architecture and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} depend on a 64-step window or retain their advantages when structural lag is eliminated, we retrain the full multi-horizon backbone, 224-bit panel, and surgical weight-space quenching across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ (structural lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$ steps, partitioning $S \ge 4$ into four nested prefixes $\lceil mS/4 \rceil$ and $S=1$ into four lag-1 difference views).

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a), the multi-perspective 224-bit panel and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} outperform 1D scalar SPOT across every horizon from zero lag ($S=1$) to $S=64$ while holding nominal false alarms below $3.6\%$ (whereas TimesNet's causal false-alarm rate escalates from $2.5\%$ at $S=1$ to $21.3\%$ at $S=64$). At zero reporting lag ($S=1$) and three-step lag ($S=4$), where 1D scalar scoring suffers severe recall collapse, \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} multiply causal Point-F1 by $1.9\times$ to $4.3\times$, recover over $75\%\text{--}84\%$ of anomaly events, and cut empirical detection delay from nearly a hundred steps to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps at $S=4$. Stepping up to intermediate horizons ($S=16$ and $S=32$) progressively sharpens point-wise precision as multi-step trajectory dynamics become visible, with \emph{Laminar-JEPA} achieving peak retrospective buffered F1 ($0.290$ vs.\ $0.182$ for 1D scalar) at $S=32$ and unquenched \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} reaching peak causal F1 at $S=64$. Horizon $S$ thus acts as a tunable latency-versus-precision dial: operators monitoring minute- or hour-scale processes can deploy $S \in \{1, 4, 16\}$ for near-instantaneous tripwire response, while high-frequency telemetry systems can use $S \in \{32, 64\}$ to capture multi-scale dynamical evolution.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine why multi-perspective latent circuit breaking shifts the detection frontier upward across operating budgets and fault taxonomies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Dominance across autonomous and diagnostic operating budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Along the causal recall-versus-false-alarm frontier in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), the autonomous operating points of \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, \emph{Reynolds 224-Bit}, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} occupy the upper-left low-false-alarm regime without test-label tuning. When all architectures are evaluated at matched post-hoc test false-alarm budgets ($f^* \in \{1\%, 5\%\}$, dotted vertical guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) as a diagnostic check of tail sensitivity at equal alert rates, the 224-bit panel captures nearly three times as many anomaly events as TimesNet and nearly four times as many as TranAD at a $1\%$ false-alarm budget.
    \item \emph{Relational sensitivity to in-range contextual faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Stratifying benchmark events into out-of-range point spikes versus in-range contextual faults under both NASA's ground-truth spacecraft taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)) and a $5\sigma$ amplitude boundary across all 19 development streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)) reveals the physical source of this gap. Point anomalies that drive raw sensors beyond $5\sigma$ are readily flagged by simple amplitude thresholds and observation-space reconstructors alike. Contextual anomalies, however, occur well inside normal sensor amplitude bounds when cross-channel phase relationships, frequency spectra, or state-transition velocities decouple. Observation-space reconstructors suffer a sharp collapse on these in-range faults, whereas the 224-bit panel monitors horizon drift, fluctuation energy, and velocity shifts across whitened latent coordinates, doubling reconstructor recall on contextual faults while maintaining top performance on point anomalies.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Untouched Held-Out Generalization, Calibration Sample-Size Reliability, and Drift Decomposition}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and our archive-wide diagnostic audits establish three foundational results governing out-of-sample generalization and calibration reliability:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Independent generalization across 118 untouched streams and 162 archive streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}):} On the 118 held-out streams that were never inspected during architectural or calibration design, replacing 1D scalar scoring with the multi-horizon circuit-breaker panel nearly doubles causal Point-F1 ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$, positive across 8 of 9 dataset families with cluster-level $p = 0.007$), lifts event recall by more than twenty percentage points, and cuts causal onset delay by roughly thirty steps across all six multi-stream held-out datasets (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Across the full 162-stream, 15-dataset archive, surgical weight-space quenching in four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} consistently reduces both held-out causal false-alarm rates ($p = 0.0011$, dataset-clustered $p = 0.027$) and test false-alarm rates ($p < 10^{-4}$), achieving a median held-out causal FPR of $1.03\%$ ($70.4\%$ of streams below $2.0\%$).
    \item \emph{Statistical reliability law for calibration sample size and small-sample remedies:} Because sliding windows of length $C=256$ overlap at unit stride, integrated autocorrelation ($\tau_{\text{int}} = 3.23$) reduces the effective independent sample size to $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}} \approx 0.31\,N_{\text{cal}}$ (median $N_{\text{eff}} = 152$ on the development cohort). On the 118 untouched streams, short calibration histories ($N_{\text{cal}} < 250$, $N_{\text{eff}} < 78$) suffer a $19.2\%$ mean causal FPR due to extreme quantile estimation variance, compared with $1.6\%$ on streams with $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 2{,}500$ ($N_{\text{eff}} \ge 775$). Moving-block bootstrap audits ($B=200$, block length $64$) across $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100, 250, 500, 1{,}000, 2{,}500, 5{,}000\}$ show that the median 95\% confidence interval half-width on the 224 per-dial thresholds $\tau_j$ contracts smoothly from $\pm 11.0\%$ (coefficient of variation (CV) $6.7\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=100$ to $\pm 7.8\%$ at $N_{\text{eff}}=500$ and $\pm 2.8\%$ (CV $1.6\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=5{,}000$, while inter-segment test-FPR SD across disjoint calibration blocks drops from $12.9\%$ at $N_{\text{cal}}=100$ to $2.8\%$ ($1.7\%$ under Strict quorum) once $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$. We therefore establish $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ independent observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows, guaranteeing expected tail count $N_{\text{eff}}(1 - q_{0.995}) \ge 1.5$, threshold 95\% CI half-width $\le 8\%$, and inter-segment FPR SD $< 3\%$) as the minimum sample size for empirical $q_{0.999}$ calibration. When operational constraints limit calibration to $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$ windows ($N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100, 250\}$), replacing empirical tail order statistics with order-statistic-free IQR extrapolation ($\text{med}_j + 4.8\,\text{IQR}_j$) or Harrell--Davis Beta-smoothed quantiles cuts small-sample test FPR from $2.1\%$ to $0.5\%\text{--}0.9\%$ while improving causal Point-F1 and cutting inter-segment FPR variability to $4.2\%$.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit via three-stage permutation decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) validation splits proves that excess false alarms are not caused by parametric tail misfit within stationary windows, but by inter-block regime drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), which drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in unconstrained TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in observation-space reconstructors. In our architecture, running-barycenter centering absorbs slow manifold translation to cut the inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$, while per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j$ quarantine individual drifting coordinates before they can trigger a global alarm.
\end{enumerate}
