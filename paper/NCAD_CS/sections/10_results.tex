\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$), with buffered (a) and strictly causal (b) attribution reported separately. The principal finding is relative false-alarm suppression; the evidence for general detection superiority is inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{Relative False-Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} Latent predictive scoring via Operator-Entropy JEPA and TS-JEPA improves relative false-alarm control under static calibration: on 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.074\text{--}0.101$ ($95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs: TS-JEPA $0.093$ $[0.048, 0.145]$, OpEntropy $0.101$ $[0.043, 0.180]$) versus $0.300$ $[0.162, 0.446]$ for TimesNet and $0.307$ $[0.177, 0.445]$ for TranAD (three- to four-fold reduction buffered, and $8\text{--}20\times$ causal: $0.011\text{--}0.028$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.243$). Across all 33 streams the paired buffered reduction is significant: OpEntropy JEPA achieves $-0.144$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.241, -0.065]$, $p=0.006$) and $-0.142$ vs.\ TranAD ($p=0.006$); TS-JEPA yields $-0.153$ ($p=0.002$) and $-0.151$ ($p=0.003$). Dataset-clustering ($N=7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$, and leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) folds hold macro buffered FPR to $0.038\text{--}0.070$ (OpEntropy) and $0.038\text{--}0.061$ (TS-JEPA) vs.\ $0.084\text{--}0.183$ (reconstructors). On MSL, TimesNet records lower buffered FPR than OpEntropy ($0.133$ vs.\ $0.177$), while on Genesis, Metro, and SWAN reconstructors record zero FPR with zero F1 due to inactivity.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1, Event Recall, and Ranking Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not establish universal detection superiority. On the 19 non-GHL streams, buffered Point-F1 ($95\%$ CI) and event recall ($95\%$ CI) are $0.206$ $[0.113, 0.319]$ and $0.477$ $[0.331, 0.629]$ for TS-JEPA, and $0.233$ $[0.131, 0.355]$ and $0.550$ $[0.398, 0.700]$ for OpEntropy, versus $0.197$ $[0.098, 0.315]$ and $0.583$ $[0.383, 0.781]$ for TimesNet, and $0.130$ $[0.053, 0.232]$ and $0.490$ $[0.295, 0.679]$ for TranAD. Across all 33 streams paired differences remain inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$). Between-stream SDs match the means (SMAP $0.379 \pm 0.361$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $0.289 \pm 0.334$ for TimesNet). Only SMAP exceeds the constant-alarm reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}{=}0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.396$); MSL, Daphnet, and SWAN fall below it, while PR-AUC leads over prevalence on SMAP ($0.504$/$0.525$ at $\pi{=}0.150$) and MSL ($0.434$/$0.422$ at $\pi{=}0.255$).

\noindent\textbf{Domain Boundaries:} Latent scoring benefits multi-channel spacecraft and kinematic streams with localized transients, whereas quiet flatlines are filtered with ambient noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(d)) and continuous process ramps (GHL, Metro) defeat all tested detectors ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1).

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
What does the Operator-Entropy formulation contribute beyond unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? While isolating the spectral regularizer on the fixed Koopman backbone ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}{=}0$ control) confirms strict TOST equivalence within $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ Point-F1 ($-0.004$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.011, +0.003]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.00074$, Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}), the OpEntropy transition architecture (linear Koopman operator $\mathbf{K}$, detached Lyapunov alignment, and pre-LayerNorm relative radial energy scoring) shortens buffered detection delay from $110.3$ steps ($95\%$ CI $[70.3, 151.9]$) in TS-JEPA to $79.5$ steps ($[47.9, 113.2]$). Across all 33 streams, cross-architecture Point-F1 differences against TS-JEPA satisfy $\text{TOST}(\pm 0.05)$ for OpEntropy ($+0.016$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.009, +0.045]$, two-sided $p{=}0.23$; $p_{\text{lower}} = 8.8\times 10^{-6}, p_{\text{upper}} = 0.0019, p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0019$) and Reynolds-Stress ($-0.008$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.031, +0.016]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0017$), whereas on the 19-stream non-GHL subset the $+0.028$ OpEntropy difference ($90\%$ CI $[-0.018, +0.074]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.11$) is inconclusive. Potential-Flow degrades Point-F1 ($-0.081$, $p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Physical regularizers therefore act as structural conditioning constraints rather than detection drivers.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Strictly causal results provide the operational evaluation; buffered results are retrospective. Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b) reports strictly causal streaming. On 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.024$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.007, 0.046]$; OpEntropy $0.028$ $[0.007, 0.063]$, Reynolds $0.011$ $[0.006, 0.017]$) versus $0.243$ $[0.123, 0.375]$ (TimesNet) and $0.222$ $[0.119, 0.337]$ (TranAD). Causal Point-F1 is $0.161$ $[0.063, 0.287]$ (TS-JEPA) and $0.153$ $[0.070, 0.247]$ (OpEntropy) versus $0.209$ $[0.109, 0.326]$ (TimesNet), with causal event recall at $0.487$ $[0.345, 0.627]$ and $0.517$ $[0.367, 0.661]$ vs.\ $0.588$ $[0.394, 0.783]$ (TimesNet). Buffered detection delay is $79.5$ $[47.9, 113.2]$ steps for OpEntropy vs.\ $110.3$ $[70.3, 151.9]$ for TS-JEPA, $234.4$ $[31.0, 603.1]$ for TranAD, and $48.5$ $[20.0, 85.1]$ for TimesNet (causal delay $112.5\text{--}124.0$ vs.\ $62.2$ steps): without lookahead buffers, latent scoring operates as a conservative filter trading raw recall and onset speed for $8\text{--}20\times$ false-alarm suppression. Dynamic SPOT on five incident streams suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ under drift.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
At $S{=}64$, scores emit $63$ steps after window opening ($10.5$~min on 10-s telemetry; $63$~h on hourly Metro data). Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models at $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). Shortening horizon preserves false-alarm control: at $S{=}1$ (no emission lag), OpEntropy holds causal FPR at $0.005$ (TimesNet $0.025$) with event recall $0.729$ (TimesNet $0.895$). Causal FPR for reconstructors rises steeply with horizon ($0.025 \to 0.213$), while latent models remain at $0.003\text{--}0.020$. Buffered attribution inflates FPR with $S$ ($0.005 \to 0.099$ for OpEntropy). Where rapid response matters, $S{=}1$ to $4$ is the natural operating regime.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We strictly separate two operating evaluations: (i)~\emph{deployable calibration quantiles} ($q^* \in [10^{-4}, 0.9]$, Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(b)), selected using nominal calibration data alone, and (ii)~\emph{post-hoc matched realized test-FPR curves} ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)), which set oracle thresholds on test nominal scores to compare intrinsic receiver operating separation at equalized alarm rates. Under deployable calibration quantile $q^*{=}0.05$ causally, TS-JEPA achieves event recall $0.730$ at $177.3$ false alarms per 1{,}000 nominal steps (precision $0.251$) and OpEntropy reaches $0.752$ at $202.8$ alarms ($0.259$) versus $0.618$ at $262.3$ alarms ($0.221$) for TimesNet; buffered at $q^*{=}0.001$, TS-JEPA attains recall $0.487$ (OpEntropy $0.559$) vs.\ $0.583$ for TimesNet at higher precision ($0.236\text{--}0.242$ vs.\ $0.194$). Because anomalies span hundreds of steps (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), alarming at $30\%$ FPR intersects $>95\%$ of segments by chance ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$).

Under post-hoc matched realized test FPR ($f^* = 0.01$ strictly causal), TS-JEPA achieves event recall $0.395$ (latent models $0.371\text{--}0.431$, reaching $0.431$ for \texttt{JEPA-CtxRecon}) versus $0.217$ for TimesNet and $0.149$ for TranAD. At relaxed realized budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$ causal and across smear mappings), event recall differences narrow or favor reconstructors (over $f^* \in [0.10, 0.50]$, causal TS-JEPA vs.\ TimesNet is $+0.061$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.153, +0.329]$, $p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus improves sensitivity specifically at stringent low false-alarm budgets ($f^* \le 0.01$), while achieving those budgets prospectively without test labels remains bounded by calibration drift.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does static extreme-value thresholding achieve its nominal design risk $q{=}10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample? It does not. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a), where Overshoot $>q$ is the share of cells whose $95\%$ CI lower bound exceeds $q$), standard SPOT achieves cell-weighted (stream-macro) causal FPR of $1.71\%$ ($1.51\%$) for TS-JEPA and $2.12\%$ ($1.95\%$) for OpEntropy, and buffered FPR of $6.40\%$ ($5.54\%$) and $7.25\%$ ($6.38\%$), overshooting the $0.1\%$ target by $15\text{--}73\times$ due to deployment shift, though substantially less than TimesNet ($17.95\%$ causal, $22.17\%$ buffered) and TranAD ($16.01\%$, $22.17\%$). Three diagnostic findings explain this behavior:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Multi-risk breakdown and quantile/length sensitivity:} Across $q \in \{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(c)), static thresholding overshoots nominal risk for all models: TS-JEPA median causal FPR is $0.0001$, $0.0047$, $0.0106$, and $0.0585$ vs.\ $0.1360$, $0.1360$, $0.1603$, and $0.2889$ for TimesNet. On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u{=}p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u{=}28$ (IQR $20\text{--}31$) causally and $21$ ($16\text{--}32$) buffered, with $0\%$ numerical fallback and median bootstrap threshold uncertainty $\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal ($0.8\%\text{--}1.1\%$ buffered). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi{=}-0.90$) in $4.4\%$ of TS-JEPA and OpEntropy cells ($\text{median }\xi{=}-0.27, \sigma{=}0.11$) vs.\ $34.4\%$ (TimesNet) and $54.4\%$ (TranAD); buffered $64$-step smoothing raises boundary hits to $26.3\%\text{--}31.6\%$. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR invariant ($0.0241\text{--}0.0242$ for TS-JEPA; $0.0278$ for OpEntropy), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ vs.\ $100\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) lowers median $N_u$ to $20$ and raises causal FPR from $0.0242$ to $0.0372$ (TS-JEPA) and $0.0278$ to $0.0413$ (OpEntropy).
    \item \textbf{Mechanism of the kurtosis-multiplier failure:} Code verification confirms $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ to $<10^{-15}$. On leptokurtic buffered cells ($\gamma_2 \ge 0$, $68.1\%$ of TS-JEPA cells), $\tau_{\text{eff}} \ge \tau$ lowers buffered FPR from $0.0449$ to $0.0111$ (OpEntropy $0.0634 \to 0.0386$). However, $64$-step smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of buffered cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$) with tight dispersion ($\sigma_{\text{cal}}/\mu_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.02\text{--}0.05$): scaling total $\tau$ toward zero pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below $p_{99.5}$ in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating FPR on $\gamma_2 < 0$ cells from $0.1048$ to $0.4870$ ($0.0640 \to 0.1630$ overall). Scaling strictly the tail excess above $u$, $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$, prevents threshold collapse below $u$ and holds 19-stream buffered FPR to $0.0919$ vs.\ $0.0929$ unadjusted for TS-JEPA ($0.1008$ vs.\ $0.1011$ for OpEntropy; $0.0240$ vs.\ $0.0242$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage permutation decomposition of drift vs.\ temporal dependence:} To separate inter-block shift from autocorrelation, we decompose total causal excess overshoot ($\Delta\text{FPR}_{\text{tot}} = \text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} - q$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$) across chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}}$), contiguous $L{=}64$ block permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$, equalizing block distributions while preserving intra-block dependence), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$). For TS-JEPA ($0.0121 \to 0.0058 \to 0.0019$), inter-block shift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chrono}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{block}}$) accounts for $+0.0063$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.0054, +0.0194]$, $56.6\%$ of excess), serial dependence ($\text{FPR}_{\text{block}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{point}}$) for $+0.0039$ ($[+0.0001, +0.0103]$, $35.3\%$), and i.i.d.\ tail error ($\text{FPR}_{\text{point}} - q$) for $+0.0009$ ($[-0.0006, +0.0027]$, $8.1\%$). For OpEntropy ($0.0185 \to 0.0073 \to 0.0022$), inter-block shift accounts for $64.1\%$ ($+0.0112$ $[-0.0015, +0.0336]$), serial dependence for $29.1\%$ ($+0.0051$ $[-0.0005, +0.0146]$), and tail error for $6.9\%$. For TimesNet ($0.0872 \to 0.0204 \to 0.0037$) and TranAD ($0.0741 \to 0.0067 \to 0.0032$), inter-block shift accounts for $77.5\%$ and $92.3\%$ of excess, and serial dependence for $19.4\%$ and $4.7\%$. Across $N{=}1{,}080$ cells, KS drift correlates with realized FPR ($\rho{=}+0.377$, CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$). In Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), rolling calibrators hold risk to $1\%\text{--}7\%$ under discrete shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33\text{--}0.39$ FPR).
\end{enumerate}
