\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$), with buffered (a) and strictly causal (b) attribution reported separately. The principal finding is false-alarm control; the evidence for general detection superiority is inconclusive.

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} Latent predictive scoring via Operator-Entropy JEPA and TS-JEPA substantially suppresses false alarms under static calibration: on 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076\text{--}0.086$ versus $0.300$ for TimesNet and $0.307$ for TranAD (a four-fold reduction buffered, and $12$--$18\times$ causal: $0.014\text{--}0.019$ vs.\ $0.222\text{--}0.243$). Across all 33 streams the paired buffered reduction is significant: OpEntropy JEPA achieves $-0.144$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.241, -0.065]$, $p=0.006$) and $-0.142$ vs.\ TranAD ($p=0.006$); TS-JEPA yields $-0.153$ ($p=0.002$) and $-0.151$ ($p=0.003$). Clustering by dataset ($N=7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$. The advantage is stable under leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) resampling: across the seven folds, the six-dataset macro buffered FPR stays $0.038\text{--}0.070$ (OpEntropy) and $0.038\text{--}0.061$ (TS-JEPA) against $0.084\text{--}0.176$ (TimesNet) and $0.098\text{--}0.183$ (TranAD). The per-dataset panel (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}c) shows the limits: on MSL, TimesNet records lower buffered FPR than OpEntropy ($0.133$ vs.\ $0.191$), and on Genesis, Metro, and SWAN the reconstructive baselines record zero FPR together with zero F1, which reflects inactivity rather than calibration. The controlled backbone panel (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}) examines the mechanism.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not establish superior anomaly detection. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences remain inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$). Between-stream SDs in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c) are as large as the means (for example SMAP $0.415 \pm 0.389$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $0.289 \pm 0.334$ for TimesNet), so per-dataset rankings are not resolved. At the dataset level only SMAP exceeds the constant-alarm reference for the latent models ($0.415$ and $0.401$ vs.\ $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}{=}0.236$); MSL ($0.237$, $0.303$ vs.\ $0.381$), Daphnet, and SWAN fall below it, and their PR-AUC lead over prevalence is the more informative signal (SMAP $0.504$/$0.525$ at $\pi{=}0.150$; MSL $0.434$/$0.422$ at $\pi{=}0.255$, against $0.455$/$0.311$ for TimesNet). Detection superiority is unproven on point-level metrics. The matched operating-point comparison is in Section~\ref{subsec:matched_operating}.







\noindent\textbf{Regimes of Applicability:} Spacecraft telemetry (SMAP, MSL) shows the largest PR-AUC lead over prevalence and over the reconstructors, though F1 margins sit within between-stream SD and the labels carry known defects \cite{wu2023flawed}. Quiet faults and subtle low-frequency drifts are filtered with ambient noise (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}d). High-frequency process noise and continuous baseline ramps (GHL, Metro) defeat every detector ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ Point-F1), and on high-prevalence streams (Daphnet $\pi{=}14.8\%$, SWAN $\pi{=}27.9\%$) all detectors score near or below a constant alarm.

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data do not support an aggregate gain:
Paired differences against TS-JEPA are $+0.002$ Point-F1 for Operator-Entropy (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$; PR-AUC $+0.002$, $p{=}0.86$; FPR $+0.009$, $p{=}0.75$; leads on 9 of 33 streams), $-0.015$ for Reynolds-Stress ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$; leads on 11 of 33), and $-0.081$ for Potential-Flow ($p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Potential-Flow's higher event-recall area over nominal causal budgets is descriptive; without matched realized-FPR support and multiplicity-adjusted evidence, neither an intrinsically stronger detector nor a purely operating-point explanation is established.

These findings align with the matched-capacity controls and the factorial sweep in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,c): the physical regularizers provide no aggregate detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction, and the entropy term changes operator conditioning without changing representation rank or detection. We therefore treat the regularizer as a theoretically motivated constraint, not a demonstrated source of improvement.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

The strictly causal results are the primary operational evaluation, and buffered results are retrospective. Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(b) reports the benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression; on the 14 GHL streams, causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and causal event recall is $46.55\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $58.82\%$ for TimesNet: without a lookahead buffer, latent predictive scoring is an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of detection sensitivity. Rolling (dynamic) SPOT on a five-stream incident cohort suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at a recall cost.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
A causal model is not automatically a low-latency one: with $S{=}64$ a score is emitted $63$ steps after the window opens, which is $10.5$~min on 10-s telemetry and $63$~h on hourly Metro data. Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains OpEntropy JEPA, TS-JEPA, and TimesNet at $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams at three seeds. Shortening the horizon does not erode the false-alarm advantage: at $S{=}1$, which has no emission lag, OpEntropy keeps causal held-out FPR at $0.005$ (TimesNet $0.025$) and event recall $0.729$ (TimesNet $0.895$). Causal FPR for the reconstructor rises steeply with horizon ($0.025 \to 0.213$), whereas the latent predictors stay at $0.003\text{--}0.020$. Event recall is non-monotone for the latent models ($0.729, 0.624, 0.257, 0.219, 0.410$ for OpEntropy) and falls to $0.22\text{--}0.26$ at intermediate horizons, and delays over detected events ($76$--$398$ steps) differ across detected sets and carry no clean trend. Buffered attribution inflates FPR with $S$ ($0.005 \to 0.099$ for OpEntropy) because each score is painted over $S$ steps. The sweep covers five streams, so it shows that short horizons are feasible with the false-alarm advantage intact, not that detection delay improves. Where early response matters on low-rate telemetry, $S{=}1$ to $4$ is the natural setting to evaluate first, at the recall level shown.

\subsection{Detection Quality at Matched Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We fit empirical-quantile thresholds on nominal budgets $q\in[10^{-4},0.9]$ and report event recall, precision, and alarm volume at fixed budgets (Table~\ref{tab:operating_and_horizon}b). At every causal budget the latent predictors raise fewer alarms than the reconstructors at comparable or higher precision: at $q^*{=}0.05$, TS-JEPA reaches event recall $0.774$ at $179.8$ alarms per 1{,}000 steps (precision $0.257$) against $0.618$ at $262.3$ ($0.221$) for TimesNet. Buffered at $q^*{=}0.001$ the trade-off reverses on recall ($0.486$ vs.\ $0.583$) while precision is higher ($0.277$ vs.\ $0.194$) and alarm volume lower ($207.9$ vs.\ $259.2$). These are matched calibration budgets, not matched realized FPR (at $q^*{=}0.05$ realized FPR is $0.180$ vs.\ $0.262$). Given multi-hundred-step anomalies (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), alarming at $30\%$ FPR catches $>95\%$ of segments by chance ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$); TimesNet's $58\%$ recall at $30\%$ FPR falls within this random expectation.

Whether latent prediction detects more events at comparable realized alarm rates remains unresolved for lack of common support. Interpolating stream--seed curves over realized FPR $[0.10,0.50]$ without extrapolation, the causal TS-JEPA--TimesNet difference is $+0.061$ (95\% dataset-bootstrap CI $[-0.153,0.329]$; sign-permutation $p{=}0.625$; 20 paired cells across four streams). Nominal-budget orderings do not establish a causal-regime advantage.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out of sample? It does not. At design risk $q{=}10^{-3}$, observed held-out FPR is $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ buffered and $1.0\text{--}1.5\%$ causal for the JEPAs, which is a calibration failure under distribution shift: the latent models overshoot the target by one to two orders of magnitude and are only less wrong than the reconstructors ($\approx 22\%$ buffered, $16\text{--}18\%$ causal). Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under strictly causal trailing and buffered smear inference. The calibrator evaluated is the \emph{static} peaks-over-threshold step of SPOT, a single threshold fit once on the calibration block. Three realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Causal vs.\ buffered smear overshoot:} Under causal trailing, TS-JEPA reaches held-out FPR $0.0101$ (median $0.0002$, IQR $[0.0000, 0.0025]$); buffered inference smears each alarm over $S{=}64$ steps and reaches $0.0526$, below the $S \cdot q = 6.4\%$ smear ceiling. TimesNet and TranAD exceed that ceiling even causally ($0.1795$, $0.1601$), which indicates distributional breakdown.
    \item \textbf{Parity with other rules:} Empirical p99.5, validation-maximum, and split conformal thresholding ($\alpha{=}10^{-3}$) also overshoot because drift violates exchangeability, and the kurtosis-adaptive multiplier raises TS-JEPA buffered FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$.
\end{enumerate}

Empirical tail diagnostics on nine non-GHL streams confirm tail-sample scarcity: median exceedances above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and Grimshaw returns degenerate $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits. 

To test whether drift or scarcity dominates, an exchangeable permutation control uniformly samples calibration and evaluation splits from pooled nominal blocks. Under point exchangeability, causal TS-JEPA FPR drops from $0.0121$ ($12.1\times$ overshoot) to $0.0019$ ($1.9\times$ overshoot); TimesNet drops from $0.0872$ to $0.0037$ (a $96\%$ reduction). Block permutation ($L{=}64$) yields intermediate overshoot ($5.8\times$ for TS-JEPA). This indicates that inter-block drift dominates calibration failure \emph{under the tested permutation protocol}, with autocorrelation secondary; it does not show that drift accounts for the same share of failures in other deployments. Across $N=1080$ dumped cells, KS drift between calibration and test blocks predicts realized FPR (Spearman $\rho = +0.377$, 95\% CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$).

The controlled-drift stress test (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}b) injects nine shift conditions into real held-out residual streams (16 streams, design $q{=}10^{-3}$); runs declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ effective exceedances per cell. Static SPOT already sits at $0.061$ FPR with no injected shift. Rolling calibrators hold risk to $1$--$7\%$ under step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33$--$0.39$ FPR), so adaptive tracking helps for discrete shifts and not for within-block drift.
