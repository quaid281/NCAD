\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports quantitative benchmarking and dynamical analyses across 45 matched continuous telemetry channels evaluated across 11 benchmark datasets. We evaluate the proposed Physics-Informed JEPA framework against reconstructive and contrastive baselines.

Figure~\ref{fig:hero_sota_comparison} illustrates the comparative bar chart across strict Point-F1 and Point-Adjusted F1 (PA-F1).

\end{figure*}

Key findings from Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} include:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Macro and Micro Point-F1 Performance:} In unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} secures Rank 1 ($0.2066$), representing a \textbf{+43.2\% relative improvement} over TimesNet ($0.1443$) and \textbf{+28.4\%} over TranAD ($0.1609$). In Channel-Macro Point-F1, \texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA} ($0.1743$) and \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} ($0.1532$) capture the top two ranks. In Pooled Micro-F1, \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} achieves the highest score of \textbf{0.3458}, more than \textbf{3.2$\times$ higher than TranAD} ($0.1072$) and \textbf{5.3$\times$ higher than TimesNet} ($0.0647$).
    \item \textbf{Precision and False Alarm Suppression:} An operational limitation of conventional baselines is false alarm flooding in continuous telemetry. Reconstructive baselines suffer precision degradation under sensor jitter: TimesNet produces $1{,}047{,}144$ false positives across the evaluated channels (precision $4.42\%$), while TranAD accumulates $452{,}621$ false positives (precision $9.64\%$). In contrast, \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA} achieves a precision of \textbf{45.98\%} with $130{,}237$ false positives, representing an 8-fold reduction in false alarms compared to TimesNet, while detecting $110{,}866$ true positives.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) vs. Discrete Calibration (Point-F1):} Comparing threshold-free continuous ranking with calibrated decisions highlights an important empirical contrast. In PR-AUC (Average Precision), TranAD ($0.2612$) and TimesNet ($0.2585$) achieve competitive scores alongside Operator-Entropy JEPA ($0.2541$). This demonstrates that reconstructive baselines retain viable continuous anomaly ranking. However, when automated streaming extreme-value thresholding (SPOT) is applied, observation-space reconstruction errors trigger catastrophic false alarm floods due to background sensor noise. In contrast, latent-space predictive JEPAs produce well-conditioned residual distributions that enable stable threshold calibration.
    \item \textbf{Role of Unconstrained Latent Prediction (TS-JEPA Control):} Unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} achieves $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro F1, $0.2860$ Micro-F1, and the highest Dataset-Macro PA-F1 ($0.3449$). This indicates that predicting in latent representation space accounts for the primary improvement in calibration stability. The physical inductive regularizers provide targeted advantages in specialized regimes (e.g., Operator-Entropy in cyclic and spectral domains, Potential-Flow in conservative subsystems).
    \item \textbf{Comparison to Analytical All-Positive Reference:} In Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, the analytical All-Positive baseline achieves a Dataset-Macro F1 of $0.1893$ and a Channel-Macro F1 of $0.1942$. On Dataset-Macro, Operator-Entropy JEPA ($0.2066$) is the sole model to surpass this trivial reference limit, while on Channel-Macro, all deep models fall below the constant alarm baseline due to severe channel-level class imbalance.
\end{enumerate}

Each model in the triad exhibits specialized mathematical inductive advantages:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Mechanics)}
By monitoring dynamic spectral entropy within a latent Koopman transition representation, \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} is sensitive to disruptions in non-linear cyclic and mixing dynamics. It achieves high performance across NASA spacecraft telemetry (\texttt{MSL}: $0.478$ PR-AUC; \texttt{SMAP}: $0.467$ PR-AUC), industrial process loops (\texttt{GECCO}: $0.351$ PR-AUC vs Potential-Flow's $0.015$), and space weather solar flare dynamics (\texttt{swan}: $0.436$ PR-AUC).

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Turbulent Transport)}
Grounded in hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition ($z = \bar{z} + z'$), \texttt{reynolds\_stress\_jepa} aligns cross-channel fluctuation covariance between context and target horizons. This formulation is designed for systems characterized by coupled flows. On the SMAP satellite telemetry dataset, it achieves Rank 1 in PR-AUC (\textbf{0.512} PR-AUC), outperforming TimesNet ($0.479$) and TranAD ($0.345$). On SWAN solar telemetry, it achieves \textbf{0.522} PR-AUC and strong precision.

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
By constraining nominal dynamics to curl-free potential gradients ($\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$), \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} enforces conservative trajectory tracking. It achieves the \textbf{highest aggregate Micro Point-F1 (0.3458)} and \textbf{highest Channel PA-F1 (0.3436)} across evaluated models, with a precision of \textbf{45.98\%}. On NASA spacecraft subsystems (\texttt{MSL}), it scores the top PR-AUC of \textbf{0.551} (vs TimesNet's $0.380$ and TranAD's $0.332$).

\subsubsection{Role of Vanilla TS-JEPA (Ablation Control)}
To isolate the contribution of the physical inductive biases from the predictive latent paradigm itself, we evaluate \texttt{ts\_jepa} as an unconstrained control. \texttt{ts\_jepa} achieves $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro Point-F1, $0.1442$ Channel-Macro Point-F1, and $0.3066$ Channel PA-F1. While substantially outperforming reconstructive baselines (+27.4\% in Dataset-Macro F1 over TimesNet), it trails \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} in Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.1838$ vs $0.2066$) and Channel Point-F1 ($0.1442$ vs $0.1743$), and trails \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} in Micro-F1 ($0.2860$ vs $0.3458$) and Channel PA-F1 ($0.3066$ vs $0.3436$). This indicates that while latent representation prediction mitigates noise drowning, domain-aligned physical conservation laws provide beneficial inductive structure.

\subsection{Impact of Architectural Enhancements}
Ablation of the three internal inductive layers confirms their collective role in stabilizing high-dimensional inference:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gating:} On high-dimensional multi-sensor streams ($K \ge 50$ in NASA SMAP and MSL telemetry), unweighted latent pooling causes informative anomalous dimensions to drown in ambient channel noise. Saliency gating dynamically prioritizes high-variation informative channels, enabling \texttt{reynolds\_stress\_jepa} and \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} to detect localized subsystem faults across complex sensor arrays without manual channel sub-selection.
    \item \textit{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells:} Multi-scale temporal decomposition within the encoder allows the networks to isolate fast transient spikes from low-frequency baseline drifts. On \texttt{GECCO} (water infrastructure with simultaneous daily consumption cycles and high-frequency valve faults), dyadic filtering doubled \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} performance from $0.1429$ to $0.2904$ Point-F1.
    \item \textit{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory:} Real-world anomaly score tails frequently deviate from standard Gaussianity. By dynamically scaling the EVT threshold via excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$, the calibrator suppresses false alarms during high-entropy volatile bursts while boosting overall PA-Recall to $67\%-71\%$ across the benchmark.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation: Event Metrics and Streaming Adaptation}
While unadjusted Point-F1 prevents score inflation from naive point adjustment, point-level scoring can penalize slight temporal boundary offsets in operational telemetry. In physical systems, an anomaly represents a continuous operational incident; if an algorithm detects the failure with a sub-second phase delay or slight boundary misalignment, point-level metrics penalize every misaligned point as a false positive or false negative, depressing nominal precision even when $100\%$ of true incidents are detected.

To evaluate operational incident detection, we analyze model performance under three complementary criteria: (i) strict unadjusted Point-F1, (ii) incident-level Event-F1 ($P_{\text{event}}, R_{\text{event}}$), and (iii) Tatbul et al. (NeurIPS 2018) Range-Aware F1 ($F1_{\text{range}}$) with existence and overlap rewards. In addition, we investigate the role of sharp dense temporal mapping and dynamic rolling tail calibration (Streaming SPOT):

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:operational_incident_metrics}, evaluating anomaly detection at the incident level indicates:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Operational Incident Reliability:} On Room Occupancy and SMAP, the models achieve $100\%$ Event Recall across all configurations. When window smearing is removed in favor of dense scoring, Event-F1 reaches \textbf{0.9167} with \textbf{84.6\% Event Precision}, confirming that in deployment, true system failures are detected reliably without excessive false alarms.
    \item \textbf{Streaming False Alarm Suppression:} Dynamic localized thresholding (Streaming SPOT) tracks non-stationary baseline drift in telemetry. On Room Occupancy, false alarms drop by \textbf{21-fold} (from 4,705 to 223 points) while maintaining over $63\%$ Event Precision.
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Point-Level Discrepancies:} On challenging channels like SMAP P-3 and CalIt2, apparent low point-level scores ($F1 \approx 0.01$) are driven by boundary temporal offset; range-aware and event-level metrics demonstrate that the physical models detect the core trajectory disruptions early, yielding Range-F1 up to $0.3634$ and Event Precision up to $100\%$.
\end{enumerate}
