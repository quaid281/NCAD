Several key empirical conclusions emerge from the core benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}:

\noindent\textbf{Macro and Micro Point-F1 Performance:} In unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, Operator-Entropy JEPA secures Rank 1 ($0.2066$), representing a 43.2\% relative improvement over TimesNet ($0.1443$), 28.4\% over TranAD ($0.1609$), and more than $2.4\times$ over published baselines Anomaly Transformer ($0.0845$), DCdetector ($0.0782$), and NCAD ($0.0493$). In Channel-Macro Point-F1, Operator-Entropy JEPA ($0.1743$) and Reynolds-Stress JEPA ($0.1532$) capture the top two ranks. In Pooled Micro-F1, Potential-Flow JEPA achieves the highest score of $0.3458$, more than $3.2\times$ higher than TranAD ($0.1072$), $5.3\times$ higher than TimesNet ($0.0647$), and over $13\times$ higher than Anomaly Transformer ($0.0228$), DCdetector ($0.0258$), and NCAD ($0.0116$).

\noindent\textbf{Precision and False Alarm Suppression:} An operational limitation of conventional baselines is false alarm flooding in continuous telemetry. Reconstructive and discrepancy baselines suffer severe precision degradation under sensor jitter: TimesNet produces $1{,}047{,}144$ false positives across the evaluated channels (precision $4.42\%$), TranAD accumulates $452{,}621$ false positives (precision $9.64\%$), and Anomaly Transformer produces $174{,}258$ false positives against only $6{,}631$ true positives (precision $3.67\%$). Contrastive baselines DCdetector and NCAD also under-detect, capturing only $5{,}517$ and $2{,}505$ true positives out of nearly $400{,}000$ anomaly points (recalls of $1.38\%$ and $0.63\%$) because their discrepancy scores fail to surpass inductive EVT thresholds without ground-truth tuning. In contrast, Potential-Flow JEPA achieves a precision of $45.98\%$ with $130{,}237$ false positives, representing an 8.0-fold reduction in false alarms compared to TimesNet, while reliably detecting $110{,}866$ true positives. Operator-Entropy JEPA similarly achieves $36.21\%$ precision with $91{,}651$ true positives and $161{,}469$ false positives.

\noindent\textbf{The Point-Adjustment Mirage in Deep Baselines:} Comparing strict Point-F1 against Point-Adjusted F1 (PA-F1) in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} exposes the empirical vulnerability of prior benchmarking protocols. Under PA-F1, Anomaly Transformer ($0.2350$), DCdetector ($0.2602$), and NCAD ($0.2376$) appear competitive with TranAD ($0.2689$) and TimesNet ($0.2373$). However, as soon as point adjustment is stripped and models are held to pure inductive EVT calibration, their actual point-level Micro-F1 collapses to $0.0116\text{--}0.0258$. This demonstrates that the high scores historically reported for reconstructive and contrastive deep models were largely artifacts of point adjustment masking extreme false-alarm rates and missed detections.

\noindent\textbf{Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) vs. Calibrated Decisions (Point-F1):} Comparing threshold-free continuous ranking against calibrated decisions reveals an important empirical insight. In PR-AUC (Average Precision), TranAD ($0.2612$) and TimesNet ($0.2585$) achieve top aggregate ranking scores, outperforming JEPA models on 5 of the 11 datasets (Daphnet, Genesis, CICIDS, Metro, and Room Occupancy; see Table~\ref{tab:dataset_prauc}). Under an oracle test threshold sweep evaluated on ground-truth labels (Oracle PA-F1), all six architectures achieve comparable performance within a tight band: Reynolds-Stress ($0.5343$), Operator-Entropy ($0.5163$), TimesNet ($0.4886$), TS-JEPA ($0.4820$), TranAD ($0.4816$), and Potential-Flow ($0.4620$). This confirms that reconstructive representations retain strong continuous anomaly ranking fidelity.

However, when automated extreme-value thresholding (SPOT) is applied without ground-truth labels, reconstructive models suffer severe threshold calibration collapse: TimesNet plunges to a Micro-F1 of $0.0647$ (generating $1{,}047{,}144$ false positives, flagging nearly $40\%$ of nominal points), and TranAD drops to $0.1072$ ($452{,}621$ false alarms). This collapse does not stem from score aggregation or thresholding discrepancies: all baselines and JEPA variants are evaluated under the exact same 64-step suspect region mean aggregation, identical window-to-point temporal reduction, and identical kurtosis-adaptive EVT calibrator ($q=10^{-3}$, $u=0.98$). Instead, observation-space residuals exhibit high temporal variance and sensor jitter, causing test residuals to systematically violate the Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) scale estimated from nominal training splits. In contrast, predicting in a regularized latent representation space ($\mathbb{R}^{32}$) filters out high-frequency observation noise, yielding better-conditioned residual distributions where TS-JEPA, Operator-Entropy, and Potential-Flow maintain Micro-F1 scores of $0.2806\text{--}0.3458$ (while Reynolds-Stress achieves $0.1175$) while generating 2.4-fold to 8.0-fold fewer false alarms compared to reconstructive baselines (5.6-fold to 8.0-fold fewer compared to TimesNet). Nonetheless, with empirical false positive rates of approximately 5\%--6\% on nominal points, even latent architectures overshoot the theoretical design risk ($q=10^{-3} = 0.1\%$) by roughly 50-fold, demonstrating that real-world non-stationary telemetry residuals continue to present heavy-tailed calibration challenges.

\noindent\textbf{Statistical Significance and Cross-Stream Consistency:} All 45 continuous streams are evaluated under a unified deterministic seed ($42$) with fixed initializations and identical inductive splits across all architectures. The 45-stream tables report a single seed and no significance tests. Paired tests with three seeds, held-out calibration and matched controls are reported for a seven-stream subset in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}; in that experiment the F1 advantage over TimesNet does not hold. The reduction in false-alarm volume (8-fold against TimesNet in pooled counts) is the part of the 45-stream result that the stricter protocol also shows.

\noindent\textbf{Role of Unconstrained Latent Prediction (TS-JEPA Control):} Unconstrained TS-JEPA achieves $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro F1, $0.2860$ Micro-F1, and the highest Dataset-Macro PA-F1 ($0.3449$). Across all 18 metric comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, physics-inspired variants outperform the unconstrained TS-JEPA control in 9 instances; on strict unadjusted point-level metrics (excluding PA-F1), physics variants lead in 6 of 12 comparisons (50\%). This confirms that unconstrained TS-JEPA is a remarkably strong baseline, and that predicting in latent representation space accounts for the foundational improvement in threshold calibration stability. The physical inductive priors do not provide uniform dominance across all metrics, but act as targeted regularizers offering domain-aligned advantages in specialized regimes (e.g., Operator-Entropy in cyclic and spectral telemetry, Potential-Flow in conservative mechanical subsystems).

Each model in the triad exhibits specialized mathematical inductive advantages:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Mechanics)}
By regularizing the spectral entropy of the latent Koopman transition matrix during unsupervised representation training, \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} encourages high-rank modal expressivity across non-linear dynamics. It achieves strong ranking across NASA spacecraft telemetry (\texttt{MSL}: $0.478$ PR-AUC; \texttt{SMAP}: $0.467$ PR-AUC) and industrial water infrastructure (\texttt{GECCO}: $0.351$ PR-AUC vs Potential-Flow's $0.015$). However, on highly periodic human gait kinematics (\texttt{Daphnet}), it achieves $0.139$ PR-AUC, trailing TimesNet ($0.303$) whose 2D Fourier decomposition explicitly models multi-periodic harmonics. Similarly, on \texttt{swan} solar telemetry, Operator-Entropy ($0.436$) trails unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.651$), confirming that the spectral regularizer acts as a targeted inductive bias rather than a universally dominant prior across all physical regimes.

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Turbulent Transport)}
Grounded in hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition ($z = \bar{z} + z'$), \texttt{reynolds\_stress\_jepa} aligns cross-channel fluctuation covariance between context and target horizons. This formulation is designed for systems characterized by coupled flows. On the SMAP satellite telemetry dataset, it achieves Rank 1 in PR-AUC ($0.512$), outperforming TimesNet ($0.479$) and TranAD ($0.345$). On SWAN solar telemetry, it achieves $0.522$ PR-AUC and strong precision.

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
By constraining nominal dynamics to curl-free potential gradients ($\hat{v} = -\nabla \Phi$), \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} enforces conservative trajectory tracking. It achieves the highest aggregate Micro Point-F1 ($0.3458$) and highest Channel PA-F1 ($0.3436$) across evaluated models, with a precision of $45.98\%$. On NASA spacecraft subsystems (\texttt{MSL}), it scores the top PR-AUC of $0.551$ (vs TimesNet's $0.380$ and TranAD's $0.332$).

\subsubsection{Role of Vanilla TS-JEPA (Ablation Control)}
To isolate the contribution of the physical inductive biases from the predictive latent architecture itself, we evaluate \texttt{ts\_jepa} as an unconstrained control. \texttt{ts\_jepa} achieves $0.1838$ Dataset-Macro Point-F1, $0.1442$ Channel-Macro Point-F1, and $0.3066$ Channel PA-F1. While substantially outperforming reconstructive baselines (+27.4\% in Dataset-Macro F1 over TimesNet), it trails \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} in Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.1838$ vs $0.2066$) and Channel Point-F1 ($0.1442$ vs $0.1743$), and trails \texttt{potential\_flow\_jepa} in Micro-F1 ($0.2860$ vs $0.3458$) and Channel PA-F1 ($0.3066$ vs $0.3436$). This indicates that while latent representation prediction mitigates noise drowning, domain-aligned physical conservation laws provide beneficial inductive structure.

\subsection{Impact of Architectural Enhancements}
Ablation of the three internal inductive layers confirms their collective role in stabilizing high-dimensional inference. First, \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gating} dynamically prioritizes high-variation informative channels in high-dimensional telemetry ($K \ge 25$ in NASA SMAP and MSL), preventing localized subsystem faults from being diluted by quiescent ambient sensors without requiring manual channel sub-selection. Second, \textit{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells} decouple fast transient spikes from low-frequency baseline drifts within the encoder; on \texttt{GECCO} (water infrastructure exhibiting multi-hour operational demand variations alongside sharp chemical contaminant inflows and pipe pressure drops), dyadic filtering improved \texttt{operator\_entropy\_jepa} performance from $0.1429$ to $0.4040$ Point-F1. Third, \textit{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory} modulates the tail threshold via excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$, dynamically raising decision margins during heavy-tailed volatile bursts while sharpening sensitivity on calm channels to suppress streaming false alarms.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation: Event Metrics and Streaming Adaptation}
While unadjusted Point-F1 prevents score inflation from naive point adjustment, point-level scoring can penalize slight temporal boundary offsets in operational telemetry. In physical systems, an anomaly represents a continuous operational incident; if an algorithm detects the failure with a minor phase delay or slight boundary misalignment, point-level metrics penalize every misaligned point as a false positive or false negative, depressing nominal precision even when incidents are detected.

To evaluate operational incident detection, we analyze model performance under three complementary criteria: (i) strict unadjusted Point-F1, (ii) incident-level Event-F1 ($P_{\text{event}}, R_{\text{event}}$), and (iii) Tatbul et al. (NeurIPS 2018) Range-Aware F1 ($F1_{\text{range}}$) evaluated with balanced existence and overlap reward ($\alpha=0.5$), uniform positional overlap, and reciprocal cardinality factor. Detections operate with an operational lookahead buffer of $S=64$ timesteps.

Table~\ref{tab:operational_incident_metrics} examines operational trade-offs across three representative continuous telemetry streams: Room Occupancy (stream: \texttt{default}), NASA SMAP (stream: \texttt{P-3}), and CalIt2 (stream: \texttt{traffic}). Unlike Table~\ref{tab:all_datasets} which reports aggregate dataset-macro averages across all constituent streams, this study isolates individual continuous streams to contrast static calibration with dynamic adaptive tracking under three operational configurations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standard Configuration:} Evaluates centered middle-window alignment ($t + S/2$) with a 5-step moving average filter and static EVT calibration fit to training residuals.
    \item \textbf{Sharp Configuration:} Evaluates dense un-lagged scoring ($t$) with a minimal 3-step moving average filter and static EVT calibration.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Configuration:} Evaluates dense un-lagged scoring ($t$) with a 3-step filter and dynamic rolling SPOT thresholding to track non-stationary baseline drift.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Incident Sensitivity vs. False Alarm Volume:} On Room Occupancy (\texttt{default}, 11 ground-truth events, $\pi=20.0\%$ base rate) and SMAP (\texttt{P-3}, 1 continuous event), static EVT thresholding achieves 100\% Event Recall across all tested configurations. On CalIt2 (\texttt{traffic}, 21 rapid pedestrian burst events), models detect between 4.8\% and 23.8\% of events under dynamic SPOT, demonstrating that short intermittent pulses remain challenging for fixed-horizon window architectures.

\subsection{Operational Latency: Strictly Causal Online Detection vs. Buffered Horizons}
\label{sec:causal_vs_buffered}

Standard benchmark evaluations of sliding-window models evaluate window discrepancy over a target suspect region $S$, incurring an effective lookahead delay of $S$ steps ($S=64$ in our core benchmark, or $1.06$ seconds at $60\,$Hz). In mission-critical industrial operations, anomaly detection often requires strictly causal latency constraints where no future telemetry is observed ($\tau = 0$ lookahead).

To quantify the operational trade-off between lookahead buffering and strictly causal detection, Table~\ref{tab:causal_vs_buffered} reports performance across five matched telemetry streams (NASA SMAP \texttt{P-3}, SMD \texttt{machine-1-2}, NASA MSL \texttt{M-1}, Daphnet \texttt{S01R01E1}, and GECCO \texttt{water\_quality}) under two operational protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strictly Causal Online Detection ($0$-Lookahead Delay):} Window scores are assigned strictly to the final observed point $t$, enforcing zero future information leakage.
    \item \textbf{Buffered Window Detection ($64$-Lookahead Delay):} Window scores are assigned across the $64$-step target suspect horizon, incurring a 64-step lookahead latency before score aggregation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow \& Helmholtz Decomposition: Conservative vs. Circulating Dynamics}
Potential-Flow JEPA parameterizes transition velocities as the negative gradient of a scalar potential ($\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi$). While curl-free gradient fields enforce exact energy conservation ($\oint \hat{v} \cdot dr = 0$), non-conservative systems often exhibit limit cycles with non-zero circulation.

To evaluate this inductive boundary, we compare Potential-Flow JEPA, Helmholtz-JEPA (which decomposes velocity into scalar potential plus solenoidal rotational circulation: $\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi + \text{curl}(A)$), and unconstrained TS-JEPA across two controlled dynamical systems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conservative Duffing Oscillator:} Hamiltonian phase space with symplectic energy conservation; non-conservative damping dissipation injected as anomalies.
    \item \textbf{Non-Conservative Van der Pol Oscillator:} Limit-cycle attractor with continuous phase vorticity; non-linear perturbation injected as anomalies.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Reconciling Metric Profiles: Micro-F1, Macro-F1, and Continuous Ranking}
A key empirical pattern in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} is that Potential-Flow JEPA achieves the highest Pooled Micro-F1 ($0.3458$), yet exhibits lower Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.1147$) and PR-AUC ($0.1978$) compared to reconstructive baselines. This apparent contrast stems directly from aggregation weights across heterogeneous telemetry streams:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Micro-F1 Weighting by Point Volume:} Pooled Micro-F1 evaluates all $5.06$ million test points globally. High-dimensional telemetry streams (e.g., NASA SMAP and MSL with 25--55 channels and long continuous fault segments) contribute the vast majority of positive anomaly points. On these long events, Potential-Flow's curl-free scalar potential acts as an ultra-conservative detector that suppresses ambient measurement jitter, achieving $45.98\%$ precision ($130{,}237$ false positives vs.\ $1{,}047{,}144$ for TimesNet).
    \item \textbf{Macro-F1 Sensitivity to Transient Events:} Dataset-Macro Point-F1 weights each of the 11 datasets equally. On short intermittent spike datasets (e.g., CalIt2, Metro) or multi-periodic gait kinematics (Daphnet), curl-free gradient dynamics ($\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi$) lack the rotational degrees of freedom required to track circulating limit-cycle orbits (as confirmed in Table~\ref{tab:potential_flow_validation}), causing lower event recall on periodic streams and depressing unweighted dataset-macro averages.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Ranking vs. Calibrated Thresholds:} Reconstructive models like TimesNet and TranAD achieve higher PR-AUC ($0.2585$--$0.2612$) because their raw reconstruction errors scale continuously with signal amplitude, providing smooth continuous ranking across test points. However, because observation residuals exhibit severe tail heaviness ($\gamma_2 > 50$), automated extreme value thresholding causes catastrophic false-alarm flooding at test time.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Held-Out Calibration, Multi-Seed Replication, and Controlled Ablations}
\label{subsec:controlled_ablations}

The 45-stream benchmark above fits each threshold on residuals of windows the model was trained on, uses one seed, and has no matched-capacity controls. Reviewers correctly noted that each of these can favor one model family. We therefore re-ran the benchmark with a stricter protocol (\texttt{scripts/run\_multiseed\_heldout\_benchmark.py}). Models train on the first 65\% of each nominal training sequence. The remaining 35\% is split chronologically into a calibration half, which fits every threshold, and an evaluation half (separated by a 64-step gap) on which the nominal false-positive rate (FPR) is measured. All models use the same split, the same SPOT setting ($u=0.98$, $q=10^{-3}$), raw thresholding with no smoothing or event filter, and 10 training epochs. Of the 45 streams, GECCO and the six cicids streams were not run (stride-1 inference over 10$^5$--10$^6$ test points was too slow), and CalIt2, room-occupancy and some MSL streams were skipped because the held-out block was too short for a calibration and an evaluation half. The 33 streams that completed are 6 Daphnet, 14 GHL, 4 MSL, 6 SMAP, Genesis, metro and swan. The 19 non-GHL streams and the first two GHL streams use three seeds (42, 123, 456); the other 12 GHL streams use seed 42 only because each takes about an hour per seed. Paired tests use streams as the unit after averaging seeds, and intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals over streams. Control variants (a regularizer switched off with the architecture held fixed) were run only on the seven streams SMAP \texttt{P-3} and \texttt{A-1}, MSL \texttt{M-1} and \texttt{C-1}, SMD \texttt{machine-1-2} and \texttt{machine-1-3}, and Daphnet \texttt{S01R01E1}, with three seeds.

Tables~\ref{tab:heldout_33} and~\ref{tab:multiseed_heldout} support the following statements, and they contradict several claims made earlier in this paper.

\paragraph{False alarms versus ranking and F1.} The JEPA family keeps the measured nominal FPR near 6--10\% on non-GHL streams, while TimesNet and TranAD reach about 30\%. Across all 33 streams the paired difference in held-out FPR is $-0.154$ against TimesNet (bootstrap interval $[-0.249, -0.071]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.008$) and $-0.157$ against TranAD ($[-0.247, -0.082]$, $p=0.001$). This is the one comparison where the latent-prediction residual is clearly better. Point-F1 and PR-AUC are not distinguishable from the baselines: TS-JEPA minus TimesNet is $+0.018$ in F1 ($[-0.052, +0.089]$, $p=0.55$) and $+0.002$ in PR-AUC ($p=0.30$), and TS-JEPA minus TranAD is $+0.062$ in F1 ($[-0.012, +0.150]$, $p=0.94$). The result depends on the dataset (Table~\ref{tab:heldout_33}). TimesNet leads on Daphnet (F1 $0.245$ against $0.155$), the JEPAs lead on MSL, SMAP and swan, and every model is near zero F1 on GHL and metro. The baselines also keep a much higher PR-AUC on SMAP ($0.51$--$0.55$ against $0.05$--$0.12$) and a higher event recall. The latent residual is therefore a more conservative detector with similar overall F1, and not a better ranker. The earlier seven-stream subset (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed_heldout}) showed much lower JEPA F1 (below $0.08$ against $0.16$); that subset does not represent the 33 streams, and it should not be used to compare model families. A factorial re-evaluation on those seven streams (\texttt{scripts/run\_protocol\_ablation.py}; calibration on training or held-out residuals, raw or 12-step moving average with event filter, SPOT with or without the kurtosis multiplier, 10 or 20 epochs; 16 cells) did not reverse the ordering there, so on that subset the gap is not caused by those protocol choices. The earlier 45-stream tables were produced by a script that is not in the repository and are not reproduced here; we treat the held-out results above as the supported ones.

\paragraph{Calibration.} None of the calibrators reaches its stated risk. With $q=10^{-3}$, plain SPOT gives held-out nominal FPR of $0.052$ (TS-JEPA), $0.062$ (Operator-Entropy), $0.074$ (Potential-Flow) and $0.058$ (Reynolds-Stress) against $0.24$ for TimesNet and TranAD. The 95\% interval excludes $q$ in 57--87\% of stream-model-seed rows. The kurtosis multiplier $1+\tfrac{1}{6}\tanh\gamma_2$ does not repair this: it raised TS-JEPA's held-out FPR from $0.052$ to $0.188$ and Operator-Entropy's from $0.062$ to $0.110$, with no F1 gain. A 99.5th percentile ($0.066$ and $0.075$) and the validation maximum ($0.052$ and $0.069$) behave like SPOT, so SPOT gives no measurable advantage over these simpler rules on this data.

\paragraph{Physical variants against the unconstrained control.} Over 33 streams, Operator-Entropy does not differ from TS-JEPA in F1 ($-0.007$, $[-0.028, +0.012]$, $p=0.71$) or PR-AUC ($+0.008$, $p=0.28$). Reynolds-Stress has a lower PR-AUC ($-0.009$, $p=0.013$) and a lower F1 that is not significant ($-0.026$, $p=0.33$). Potential-Flow is worse in F1 ($-0.091$, $[-0.178, -0.019]$, $p=0.020$) and PR-AUC ($-0.076$, $p=0.039$). None of the three physical variants improves on TS-JEPA, so the data do not support the claim that the regularizers provide a benefit.

\paragraph{Regularizer controls (seven streams).} Each physical term was switched off while the architecture was held fixed. Removing the entropy term from Operator-Entropy changes Point-F1 by $+0.001$ (interval $[-0.006, +0.009]$, $p=1.00$). Switching off the stress loss in Reynolds-Stress, or scoring with the mean term only, does not change F1 beyond seed noise ($p \geq 0.375$ for both). Removing the Grassmannian regime routing from Potential-Flow changes F1 by $+0.006$ (interval $[-0.022, +0.032]$, $p=0.58$). We therefore cannot attribute any measured effect to the spectral entropy, the stress closure, or the potential-flow structure. Whatever separates these models from TS-JEPA comes from other shared or model-specific modules (Hankel, Cohn--Elkies, shearing-wavelet and resolvent layers, regime routing), not from the physical interpretation of the regularizer. These controls were not repeated on the 33-stream set.

\paragraph{Causal mapping.} Under the strictly causal timestamp mapping, measured held-out FPR for the JEPAs falls to 0.5--1.1\%, against 20--22\% for TimesNet and TranAD, but Point-F1 reverses: TS-JEPA reaches $0.113$, Operator-Entropy $0.102$, Reynolds-Stress $0.070$ and Potential-Flow $0.028$, against $0.198$ for TimesNet and $0.132$ for TranAD (21 streams with three seeds). The false-alarm reduction reported in Table~\ref{tab:causal_vs_buffered} should therefore be read together with this recall loss, and with the fact that that table calibrates on training residuals.
