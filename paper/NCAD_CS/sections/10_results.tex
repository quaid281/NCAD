\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark and Scope Boundaries}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports exploratory design-stage results on the 33-stream development suite (where stabilization and thresholds share $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Primary evidence is the five-block archive (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}). A separate single-seed 130-stream buffered screen gives context only and is not paired with the archive: TS-JEPA reaches Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$.

\noindent\emph{Configuration:} On the 33-stream suite \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $0.253$ buffered Point-F1 (vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}), more than halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($4.7\%$ vs.\ $11.5\%$), and leads macro Point-F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$).

\noindent\emph{Slow-ramp boundary:} A $C=256$-step encoder cannot see drift slower than its window: context and target summaries move together, yielding $0.000$ Point-F1 on all 14 GHL industrial streams and restricting our scope strictly to abrupt and intermediate-onset faults rather than general industrial coverage. Injected linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and length $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ ($15$ events per cell) map this cutoff: steep ramps ($L=64$, $a\ge 1\sigma$) reach $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ ER at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay ($5.7\text{--}7.7$ points above chance), at $L=C$ the excess falls to $0.5\text{--}2.5$ points, and for $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$) chance-corrected detection vanishes ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points).

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Live streaming is much harder than retrospective review. Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent scores degrade under causal reporting: buffered attribution averages residuals across every overlapping window covering a timestamp, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step with no averaging, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the shared SPOT threshold (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). On these development streams, a rate-matched Bernoulli alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ hits $28.1\%$ of events at $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Exploratory Horizon Sweep Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Shorter horizons trade buffered smoothing for lower live lag ($S-1$ steps). Retraining on five exploratory streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a); at $S=1$ all prefixes coincide), both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ with test FPR $\le 3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037$ and $0.050$ vs.\ $0.014$ and $0.012$) with $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ with $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER, and at $S=4$ delay falls to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps. Buffered Point-F1 instead peaks at long horizons: $0.290$ at $S=32$ and $0.255$ at $S=64$, versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot set a general rule.

\subsection{Design-Stage Operating Budgets and Fault Taxonomies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) examines the 19 development streams across budgets and fault types:
(1)~\emph{Diagnostic budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At a post-hoc matched $f^*=1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD; this ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs does not carry over to the archive (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).
(2)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point faults:} Out-of-range spikes ($>5\sigma$) are caught by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike, whereas in-range contextual faults alter cross-channel phase or velocity within normal bounds; on NASA contextual sequences \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet.

\subsection{Primary Held-Out Generalization, False-Alarm Tails, and Calibration Limits}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_tail_audit.tex}

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and \ref{tab:tail_audit} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c) report the disjoint five-block archive. We label each result by its operating point: \emph{autonomous} (deployed healthy-only thresholds), \emph{matched calibration rate} (every detector set to the same healthy alarm rate), \emph{post hoc} (thresholds matched on test labels, diagnostic only), and \emph{buffered} (retrospective review).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Live event coverage and delay vs.\ false-alarm trade-off (autonomous, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} Across the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$, $9/9$ datasets, sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and nearly halves detection delay ($69.0$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $69.4\%$ ER at $54.7$-step delay, trading higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$). Pooled causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ (Wilcoxon $p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$; cluster CI $[-0.026,+0.069]$) and more than doubles from $0.040$ to $0.085$ when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($n=59$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$; $+20.4$ ER points). When $N_{\text{cal}}<500$, the scalar baseline has higher causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$, $p=0.81$) although the panel catches more events ($72.1\%$ vs.\ $68.4\%$). Under \emph{buffered} review, \emph{Lite} raises ER from $59.0\%$ to $67.7\%$ and Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.158$ ($p=0.0098$) at lower buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($19.5\%\to 13.3\%$, $p=2.8\times 10^{-4}$).
    \item \emph{Where the live gain comes from (matched calibration rate and post hoc, Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), \ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)):} On the independent operating-point control rerun (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), distinct from the primary Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a) run), at the panel's low $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ rate (median $0.25\%$) it catches more causal events than the scalar baseline ($65.0\%$ vs.\ $52.6\%$, $p=1.7\times 10^{-5}$) and than Fisher, top-$k$, and max-$z$ pooling ($61.1\%$, $58.1\%$, $58.8\%$). At common healthy alarm rates ($0.5\%\text{--}5\%$) these gaps narrow—Fisher matches or exceeds Point-F1, top-$k$ and max-$z$ match ER, and 1D trails by $3\text{--}6$ ER points—while at post hoc matched test FPR the panel shows no ranking advantage (1D has higher Point-F1 at $f=1\%$, $0.084$ vs.\ $0.049$, $p=0.024$). Thus the panel's value comes from its label-free quorum operating point, not a superior ranking function.
    \item \emph{False-alarm tail and nominal-regime stability (autonomous, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a), Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c)):} Causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ has mean $6.4\%$, median $0.93\%$, $90$th percentile $6.1\%$, and an acute $95$th percentile of $64.7\%$; $14$, $10$, $8$, and $8$ streams exceed $5\%$, $10\%$, $20\%$, and $30\%$ ($13$, $11$, $8$, and $5$ for 1D), while $56.8\%$ and $88.1\%$ meet $1\%$ and $5\%$ budgets. Calibration length alone does not explain this upper tail ($\rho=-0.11$ with $N_{\text{cal}}$, yet five of the ten worst streams exceed $N_{\text{cal}}>700$, and $N_{\text{eff}}$ has $\rho=-0.01$). Instead, operational reliability depends on \emph{nominal-regime stability}: inter-block quorum-count shift $D_{\text{KS}}$ correlates strongly with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($\rho=0.63$; median $0.55$ above $10\%$ FPR vs.\ $0.16$ at $\le 5\%$, $p=1.6\times 10^{-6}$), and regime-shifting streams ($D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.5$) generate the bulk of false alarms across both the panel and 1D baseline (test healthy steps show $3.2\%/17.0\%$ median/mean vs.\ $0.9\%/8.8\%$ for 1D).
    \item \emph{Threshold computability, tail reliability, and formal deployment protocol:} We distinguish (a)~\emph{threshold computability} (mechanically evaluating $q_{0.995}$ on finite $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), (b)~\emph{tail-estimation reliability} ($N_{\text{eff}}(1-q)\ge 1$, unmet on $105/118$ streams because median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$ and $N_{\text{eff}}=65$ give only $0.325$ expected exceedances), and (c)~\emph{empirical held-out acceptance} on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$. On the control rerun, replacing $q_{0.995}$ with its distribution-free $90\%$ upper confidence bound lowers mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ only from $7.1\%$ to $5.8\%$ ($\text{p95}$ $50\%\to 42\%$) while cutting ER ($65.3\%\to 47.7\%$), confirming that conservative quantiles cannot cure regime shifts and that $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ is necessary (routing $N_{\text{cal}}<500$ streams to 1D lifts pooled causal Point-F1 to $0.095$) but insufficient. Autonomous deployment therefore follows a mandatory five-step protocol: \emph{(1)}~fit backbone, whitening, and stabilization on disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; \emph{(2)}~compute tripwires and $K_{\text{Lite}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$); \emph{(3)}~measure frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; \emph{(4)}~\emph{reject autonomous operation} (routing to operator-supervised review) whenever $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>\alpha_{\text{acc}}$ (e.g., $5\%$); and \emph{(5)}~monitor live quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ and recalibrate upon regime shifts. Across the $50$ archive streams passing steps (1)--(4) at $\alpha_{\text{acc}}=5\%$, \emph{Lite} leads 1D on causal Point-F1 ($0.076$ vs.\ $0.039$, $p=3.5\times 10^{-4}$) at mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=0.7\%$, while post-hold shifts still raise mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ to $12.1\%$, making step~(5) essential (on $T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$, $n=15$, two-fold cross-fitting cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$ at $65.9\%$ ER).
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit:} On development streams, chronological versus block-permuted threshold refits attribute $71.3\%$ (TS-JEPA) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ (reconstructors) of excess causal FPR to inter-block drift, versus $18.7\%$ with centering, without ruling out tail misfit.
\end{enumerate}
