\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark and Scope Boundaries}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports exploratory design-stage results on the 33-stream development suite (where stabilization and thresholds share $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$). Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} provides our primary generalization and calibration evidence under the disjoint five-block protocol across 118 untouched streams and all 141 archive streams. On a separate 130-stream buffered screen, TS-JEPA reaches stream-mean Point-F1 $0.186$ versus TranAD $0.152$, TimesNet $0.151$, DCdetector $0.097$, NCAD $0.088$, and Anomaly Transformer $0.082$. Spherical normalization alone gives no distributional guarantee; lower false alarms with latent scoring hold specifically under these protocols.

\noindent\emph{Design-stage configuration guidance:} Both tiers reach nearly identical unweighted buffered Point-F1 ($0.244$ vs.\ $0.242$) and causal ER ($67.3\%$ vs.\ $65.0\%$) on the 33-stream suite. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, however, halves buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.1\%$ vs.\ $12.8\%$) and leads $7$-dataset macro Point-F1 ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.133$). We deploy \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} operationally and keep \emph{Laminar-JEPA} for ablation.

\noindent\emph{Slow-ramp boundary:} A $C=256$-step encoder cannot see drift slower than its window. Context and target summaries move together, yielding $0.000$ Point-F1 on all 14 GHL slow-ramp streams. Injecting linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and length $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$ ($15$ events per cell) maps the cutoff: steep multi-channel ramps ($L=64$, $a\ge 1\sigma$) reach $86.7\%\text{--}88.7\%$ ER at $46\text{--}54$-step median delay ($5.7\text{--}7.7$ points above background chance), $L=C$ falls to $0.5\text{--}2.5$ points above chance, and for $L\ge 1024$ ($4C$) chance-corrected detection vanishes ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points) across every JEPA variant.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Live streaming is much harder than retrospective review. Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why 1D latent scores degrade under causal reporting: buffered attribution averages residuals across every overlapping window covering a timestamp, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step with no averaging, allowing one or two drifting coordinates to inflate the shared SPOT threshold (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). On these development streams, a rate-matched Bernoulli alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ hits $28.1\%$ of events at $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR, versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$.

\subsection{Exploratory Horizon Sweep Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Shorter horizons trade buffered smoothing for lower live lag. Because a target window of length $S$ reports causally at $\tau=t+S-1$ ($63$-step lag at $S=64$), we retrain on five exploratory streams across $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a); at $S=1$ all prefixes coincide, and at $S=4$ the shortest prefix spans two steps), where both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ with test FPR $\le 3.6\%$. At $S=1$ and $S=4$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $2.6\times$ and $4.3\times$ scalar causal Point-F1 ($0.037,0.050$ vs.\ $0.014,0.012$; $77\%\text{--}84\%$ ER) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $2.1\times$ and $3.1\times$ ($0.030,0.036$; $64\%\text{--}82\%$ ER), cutting delay at $S=4$ to $36.7\text{--}44.1$ steps, whereas buffered Point-F1 peaks at $0.290$ ($S=32$) and $0.255$ ($S=64$) versus $0.182$ for 1D SPOT. Five selected streams cannot set a general rule.

\subsection{Design-Stage Operating Budgets and Fault Taxonomies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b) and Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual} examine the 19 development streams across budgets and fault types:
(1)~\emph{Diagnostic budgets (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At a post-hoc matched $f^*=1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catch $61.2\%$ ($63.1\%$ unquenched) and $56.0\%$ of events versus $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA, $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, and $14.9\%$ for TranAD.
(2)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point faults (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range spikes ($>5\sigma$) are caught by reconstructors and amplitude rules alike, whereas in-range contextual faults alter cross-channel phase or velocity within normal bounds; on NASA contextual sequences \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ autonomous ER at $1.3\%$ and $2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet.

\subsection{Primary Held-Out Generalization, False-Alarm Tails, and Calibration Limits}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c) establish three primary findings under the disjoint five-block protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Event-level gains, operating-point reconciliation, and calibration-length split (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}):} Across all 118 untouched streams (three seeds; on all 141 archive streams \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} reaches $.076/.156$ Point-F1 and $.636/.658$ ER vs.\ $.075/.146$ and $.512/.573$ for Multi-Horizon 1D), buffered and causal modes differ in how gains arise. Under \emph{buffered} attribution, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises ER from $59.0\%$ to $67.4\%$ ($+8.4$ points, cluster CI $[+2.3\%,+16.6\%]$) and Point-F1 from $0.143$ to $0.156$ ($p=0.022$) while \emph{lowering} buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($19.5\%\to 14.3\%$, median $8.7\%\to 4.8\%$, $p=0.0067$), and keeps a $+11.0$-point ER advantage at a post-hoc matched $5\%$ test FPR ($53.3\%$ vs.\ $42.2\%$, $p=1.2\times 10^{-5}$, $7/9$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Under live \emph{causal} streaming, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises autonomous ER from $52.5\%$ to $63.8\%$ ($+11.3$ points, cluster CI $[+5.1\%,+21.8\%]$, $9/9$ datasets, sign-flip $p=0.0039$) and halves detection delay ($70.8$ vs.\ $133.5$ steps; $66$ vs.\ $136$ in the rerun, $p=4.8\times 10^{-4}$), but pooled causal Point-F1 improves only modestly ($0.076\to 0.079$, $\Delta=+0.003$, stream CI $[-0.019,+0.023]$, cluster CI $[-0.033,+0.060]$, $70/43$ wins, $p=0.014$) alongside higher mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($4.4\%\to 7.0\%$). Matching $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rates (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} still catches $+12.4$ points more causal events than rate-matched 1D ($65.0\%$ vs.\ $52.6\%$, $p=1.7\times 10^{-5}$), whereas at post-hoc equalized test FPR ($f^*\in\{1\%,5\%\}$) causal ER matches 1D ($72.6\%$ vs.\ $75.2\%$ at $5\%$, $p=0.20$). Splitting at the development-suite threshold $N_{\text{cal}}=500$ explains the modest pooled Point-F1: on adequately calibrated streams ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, $n=59$), \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} doubles causal Point-F1 over 1D ($0.079$ vs.\ $0.040$, $\Delta=+0.039$, CI $[+0.016,+0.064]$, $p=2.7\times 10^{-5}$; $+16.0$ ER points) and still wins without Exathlon ($n=43$: $0.051$ vs.\ $0.031$, $p=0.0050$, mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}=1.56\%$), whereas on short histories ($N_{\text{cal}}<500$, $n=59$) 1D leads causal Point-F1 ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.080$, $p=0.31$; explaining why pooled non-Exathlon $n=101$ favors 1D on causal F1, $0.073$ vs.\ $0.060$, while \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered F1 $0.148$ vs.\ $0.139$ and causal ER $58.8\%$ vs.\ $50.6\%$). Disjoint quenching barely changes causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.90\%\to 6.83\%$, $p=0.55$) while costing $3.5$ ER points.
    \item \emph{Held-out false-alarm tail distribution, operational budgets, and autocorrelation (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a), Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(c)):} Unlike the $\approx 1\%$ development results, archive causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ is strongly right-skewed across $n=118$ streams. Mean, median, $90$th, and $95$th percentiles are $(4.2\%,0.38\%,7.0\%,20.9\%)$ for TS-JEPA, $(4.4\%,0.39\%,6.8\%,23.7\%)$ for Multi-Horizon 1D, $(6.8\%,1.26\%,7.6\%,38.3\%)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, and $(7.0\%,0.78\%,10.6\%,50.2\%)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}. Stream counts exceeding $(1\%,2\%,5\%,10\%)$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ are $(29,21,14,10)$ for TS-JEPA, $(23,16,13,11)$ for Multi-Horizon 1D, $(67,41,16,12)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, and $(54,33,15,13)$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}; thus $54.2\%$ of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} streams meet a $\le 1\%$ budget ($66.1\%$ when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$) and $87.3\%$ meet a $\le 5\%$ budget ($88.1\%$ when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$). Calibration size correlates inversely with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ (Spearman $\rho=-0.32$, $p=5.2\times 10^{-4}$): $N_{\text{cal}}<250$ ($n=12$) averages $16.0\%$ causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ versus $1.05\%$ (median $0.48\%$) for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 2{,}500$ ($n=22$). Autocorrelation drives this finite-sample variance: on the 19 development streams, median Sokal $\tau_{\text{int}}$ is $2.51$ ($z_j$), $1.88$ ($N_{\text{edge}}$), $5.58$ ($N_{224}$, max $206.0$), and $6.75$ (1D), leaving median $N_{\text{eff}}$ of $(154.6,67.1,195.9)$ at median $N_{\text{cal}}=408$ and only $(2.32,0.34,0.20)$ expected tail exceedances ($12.7\%\text{--}25.0\%$ block-bootstrap CI half-widths). On short histories ($T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$, $n=18$), two-fold cross-fitting cuts causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $15.4\%$ to $3.8\%$ at $69.9\%$ ER.
    \item \emph{Regime drift vs.\ tail misfit:} Comparing chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits links excess FPR primarily to inter-block drift without ruling out tail misfit ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}-\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$ accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors vs.\ $18.7\%$ with centering). Within-checkpoint traces ($n{=}6$, $K\in\{9,25,55\}$) show tap fusion and centering lift anomaly divergence from $0.06\text{--}0.16$ to $0.70\text{--}0.94$ and cut mean coordinate $D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.140$ to $0.096$.
\end{enumerate}
