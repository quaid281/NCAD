\subsection{Primary 33-Stream and Archive-Wide 141-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} evaluates all 33 primary continuous telemetry streams and the 141-stream archive-wide benchmark under the two-stage chronological held-out protocol: Panel~(a) reports retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and held-out nominal FPRs, and Panel~(d) the 130-stream baseline screen and 141-stream, 12-dataset multi-seed benchmark. Across both attribution regimes, pairing multi-horizon Soliton-Reynolds JEPA with the 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 ($0.228$), buffered PR-AUC ($0.411$), causal PR-AUC ($0.312$), causal event recall ($0.607$), and Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.122$), while keeping held-out false alarms an order of magnitude below observation-space reconstructors ($1.1\%\text{--}1.7\%$ causal and $9.8\%\text{--}13.2\%$ buffered vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD).

\noindent\emph{Eliminating Observation-Space False-Alarm Flooding:} On the 19 non-GHL primary streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), predicting spherical latent representations cuts held-out nominal FPR three-fold under buffered attribution ($7.8\%\text{--}9.8\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds 1D and 224-Bit Strict vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, $p \le 0.045$) and by an order of magnitude under strictly causal streaming ($0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds variants and $2.4\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors, $p = 0.010$). This separation holds across all $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$) and under zero-tuning LODO evaluation ($0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR for JEPAs vs.\ $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors). In Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit achieves $0.7\%$ causal and $4.7\%$ buffered FPR on Daphnet ($0.223$ buffered F1 vs.\ $0.145$ for TS-JEPA), leads all methods on MSL ($0.199$ buffered F1 vs.\ $0.161$ for TS-JEPA and $0.158$ for TimesNet), reaches $0.354$ buffered and $0.351$ causal F1 on SMAP at a $38.2$-step delay, and reaches $0.063$ buffered F1 on Genesis (where reconstructors score $0.000$).

\noindent\emph{Breaking the 1D Scalar Score-Collapse Bottleneck:} Under legacy 1D scalar scoring, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA trades lower Point-F1 ($0.050$ causal, $0.148$ buffered) for ultra-conservative firing because one or two out-of-sample drifting coordinates (\emph{hijacker dials}) inflate the scalar threshold. Replacing 1D scalar collapse with the 224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel resolves this trade-off on the exact same trained networks (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b),(d), and~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). On the 19 primary streams, \emph{Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Strict} and \emph{224-Bit Breaker} lift buffered PR-AUC from $0.331\text{--}0.370$ to $0.411$, raise buffered Point-F1 to $0.190\text{--}0.228$ ($p = 0.026$), more than double causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.116\text{--}0.138$ ($17$ wins / $0$ losses, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), lift causal event recall to $53.4\%\text{--}60.7\%$ ($63.1\%$ buffered), and cut detection delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps causally ($402.7$ to $71.8$ steps buffered). On GHL ($\pi=0.013$, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), confirming that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\emph{Operational Applicability Regimes:} Performance across fault types and sampling rates defines four operational regimes: (i)~\emph{multi-channel spacecraft, server, and wearable arrays} ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, SMD, Exathlon, OPPORTUNITY), where the 224-bit panel maximizes Point-F1 and event recall at low false-alarm rates; (ii)~\emph{latency-constrained infrastructure} where a 63-step window lag is prohibitive (hourly Metro, $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}); (iii)~\emph{quiet single-channel sensor freezes} (flatlines and dropouts absorbed by LayerNorm), where pairing the 224-bit panel with the rolling variance-collapse monitor recovers $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ of events (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}); and (iv)~\emph{continuous non-stationary process ramps} (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat static calibration rules.

\subsection{Synthesis of Pipeline Stages: Barycenter Tapping, Multi-Horizon Whitening, and 224-Bit Perspectives}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)--(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) trace how the four pipeline stages from Section~\ref{sec:proposed_method} drive detection performance:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stage~1 (Multi-depth TCN tapping and synchronized barycenter centering):} Subtracting the online context centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ removes common-mode level shifts before spherical projection, lowering calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.214$ in TS-JEPA to $0.179$ in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \emph{Stage~2 (Nested multi-horizon rollout and whitening defect regularization):} Rolling out predictions across $M=4$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$) under the Frobenius whitening defect objective (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) raises $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ from $11.6$ to $19.8$ and lifts causal event recall at $f^*=0.01$ from $0.395$ to $0.584$.
    \item \emph{Stages~3--4 (Calibration-stabilized whitening, 224-dial Reynolds expansion, and onset relays):} While post-hoc 1D Lieb--Thirring modulation ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) leaves 1D Point-F1 unchanged under TOST ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)), expanding the 32 whitened coordinates into $7 \times 32 = 224$ hijack-guarded switches and 32 right-edge onset relays eliminates cross-coordinate dilution, doubling causal Point-F1 and halving detection delay.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel is essential for real-time streaming. Without 64-step window averaging, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals exhibit a heavier right tail ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$) than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$). Under legacy 1D scalar scoring, hijacker dials push the scalar threshold onto that tail so that 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA alerts on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.4\%$ of test steps ($4.0\%$ true-positive rate (TPR) at $0.6\%$ FPR), flagging only isolated peak steps inside each anomaly ($0.050\text{--}0.052$ causal F1). By monitoring each fluid-dynamic dial with its own hijack-guarded switch $b_j(t)$ and firing immediately when a terminal sub-block and right-edge convective acceleration relay $r_c(t)$ coincide in (\ref{eq:onset_relay}), \emph{224-Bit Breaker} stays active across the true fault window ($0.138$ causal F1, $60.7\%$ event recall, and $105.9$-step delay at $1.67\%$ FPR). Against an analytical rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} Note), random firing at 224-Bit Breaker's held-out nominal rate catches only $28.1\%$ of events versus $60.7\%$ actual, whereas for TimesNet ($24.3\%$ FPR) random firing alone reaches $60.0\%$, exceeding TimesNet's actual $58.8\%$.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Structural Emission Lag, and Fast-Tripwire Regimes}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a structural emission lag of $S-1 = 63$ steps before a causal trailing window decision is emitted at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), latent prediction keeps causal FPR at $0.4\%\text{--}0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%\text{--}3.1\%$ for TimesNet) with higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$) and short delays ($\Delta t = 57.8\text{--}85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases from $16$ to $64$, TimesNet causal FPR climbs from $6.1\%$ to $21.3\%$, whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Thus, $S=64$ suits high-rate telemetry ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, e.g., $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL, $10.5\,\text{min}$), while low-rate streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$) and zero-lag tripwires require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ for latent predictors, we evaluate three operating-point comparisons:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Autonomous circuit-breaker points and nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Without test labels, Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Strict ($53.4\%$ ER at $1.1\%$ FPR) and 224-Bit Breaker ($60.7\%$ ER at $1.7\%$ FPR) dominate the low-FPR frontier; at calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$, Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit reaches $77.4\%$ causal ER (vs.\ $61.8\%$ for TimesNet and $60.2\%$ for TranAD).
    \item \emph{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models (vertical dotted guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) proves that low false-alarm rates are not an artifact of low alert frequency. At a $1\%$ test FPR budget ($f^* = 0.01$), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit detects $58.4\%$ of anomaly events ($56.2\%\text{--}57.8\%$ for 1D Soliton-Reynolds and $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA), nearly tripling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and quadrupling TranAD ($14.9\%$), with a similar gap at $f^* = 0.05$ ($72.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$).
    \item \emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range point anomalies ($\pm 5\sigma$ spikes) are flagged even by a marginal $z$-score (\texttt{ZScoreMax}), whereas contextual anomalies stay within normal bounds ($\max|z| \le 5\sigma$) and violate multi-step trajectory structure. In Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a), stratifying the 10 primary SMAP/MSL streams by NASA's taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} shows that observation-space detectors collapse on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC; $30\%\text{--}56\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$). Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit leads every contextual metric ($0.658$ buffered PR-AUC, $75.0\%$ autonomous recall, and $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) and reaches $94.4\%$ recall at $f^* = 0.05$ on NASA point anomalies. Across all 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit doubles reconstructor recall on contextual faults ($61.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) and leads on point faults ($79.2\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel across all 141 qualifying multivariate streams in 12 datasets at three seeds ($423$ runs per model):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Archive-wide gains over 1D single- and multi-horizon JEPAs (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} Moving from single-horizon TS-JEPA to 3-horizon Speculative-JEPA and Speculative-Alien-JEPA under 1D scoring first lifts PR-AUC ($0.165 \to 0.184$ causally; $0.213 \to 0.253\text{--}0.256$ buffered, $p \le 0.0042$). Pairing the multi-horizon predictor with the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel (\emph{Speculative-224Bit-Strict} and \emph{Speculative-224Bit-Breaker}) then lifts causal PR-AUC to $0.197$ ($p = 0.014$ vs.\ TS-JEPA), nearly doubles causal Point-F1 from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.125\text{--}0.139$ ($97$ wins / $36$ losses over TS-JEPA, $p = 8.3{\times}10^{-11}$; $114$ wins / $18$ losses over 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$), raises causal event recall from $48.0\%\text{--}50.7\%$ to $64.8\%\text{--}72.7\%$, and halves causal detection delay from $115.4\text{--}125.4$ to $55.3\text{--}78.0$ steps. Under buffered attribution, prefix-routed voting lifts PR-AUC to $0.261$ ($p = 0.0031$), raises Point-F1 to $0.177\text{--}0.180$ ($p \le 2.6{\times}10^{-4}$), lifts event recall to $66.0\%\text{--}68.5\%$, and lowers held-out FPR below 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA ($16.5\%$ vs.\ $18.0\%$) at $46.6\text{--}54.0$-step delay.
    \item \emph{Consistency across all dataset families (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} Across every multi-stream family, \emph{Speculative-224Bit-Breaker} leads causal Point-F1 and event recall: SMAP ($n=47$: $0.202$ vs.\ $0.130$ for 1D Spec.-Alien and $0.080$ for TS-JEPA at $85.7\%$ ER), Daphnet ($n=22$: $0.097$ causal and $0.155$ buffered F1 at $86.3\%$ ER), MSL ($n=18$: $0.278$ buffered and $0.131$ causal F1 vs.\ $0.170\text{--}0.183$ buffered), SMD ($n=18$: $0.090$ causal and $0.125$ buffered F1 vs.\ $0.028\text{--}0.042$ causal), and Exathlon ($n=17$) and OPPORTUNITY ($n=13$, both $82.1\%$ causal ER vs.\ $47.2\%\text{--}48.7\%$ for TS-JEPA). On the six single-stream datasets, GECCO reaches $0.149$ causal and $0.207$ buffered F1 ($7.2\%$ ER), SWAN reaches $0.578$ buffered PR-AUC ($0.240$ F1 vs.\ $0.106$ for 1D Spec.-Alien), and Genesis, PSM, CreditCard, and Metro reach $66.7\%, 7.7\%, 25.6\%,$ and $4.0\%$ buffered ER.
    \item \emph{Three-stage permutation drift decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across the 19 primary streams under chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits confirms that inter-block regime drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA ($0.0229$ vs.\ $0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.210\text{--}0.230$ vs.\ $0.043\text{--}0.046$), whereas barycenter centering in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($0.0056$ vs.\ $0.0047$) while per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j$ quarantine individual drifting coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
