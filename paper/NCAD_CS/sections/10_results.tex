\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, while \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the highest buffered PR-AUC ($0.375$) and causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.701$ at $f^* = 0.01$).

\noindent\textbf{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring cuts held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three-fold under buffered attribution: $9.3\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $10.8\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, versus $30.0\%$ for TimesNet ($p = 0.049$) and $30.7\%$ for TranAD ($p = 0.007$). Under strictly causal streaming, the false-alarm reduction widens to ten- to eighty-fold: $2.4\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.3\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, compared to $24.3\%$ for TimesNet ($p = 0.010$) and $22.2\%$ for TranAD ($p = 0.010$). This advantage holds across all 33 streams ($0.25\%$ causal stream-macro FPR for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} vs.\ $16.11\%$ and $14.23\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, leaving one dataset out at a time keeps causal FPR at $0.2\%\text{--}2.3\%$ ($3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ buffered) for JEPAs versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ causal ($8.4\%\text{--}22.1\%$ buffered) for reconstructors. In Panel~(c), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the lowest causal held-out FPR on every multi-stream family ($0.2\%$ Daphnet, $0.1\%$ GHL, $0.4\%$ MSL, $0.6\%$ SMAP vs.\ $5.6\%\text{--}36.0\%$ for baselines).

\noindent\textbf{Multi-Horizon Ranking Gains vs.\ Unadjusted Point-F1 Trade-offs:} Under buffered attribution on the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and its $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$ control achieve the highest threshold-free PR-AUC ($0.375$ and $0.374$ vs.\ $0.331$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, $0.329$ for TimesNet, and $0.287$ for TranAD). Buffered Point-F1 ($0.188$ and $0.185$, vs.\ $0.206$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) and event recall ($0.496$ and $0.515$) match TimesNet ($0.197$ F1, $0.583$ recall) and beat TranAD ($0.130$ F1, $0.490$ recall) at one-third the false-alarm rate. Across datasets (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), SMAP exceeds the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ and $0.370$; PR-AUC $0.576$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and Genesis reaches $0.037$ F1 (vs.\ $0.000$). Conversely, on GHL ($\pi=0.013$), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), illustrating that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (\texttt{TS-JEPA} $\lor$ \texttt{lowvar}) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each component of \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} modifies performance relative to \texttt{TS-JEPA}. First, \emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and barycenter centering} remove common-mode baseline shift, cutting KS drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.214 \to 0.163$) and eliminating the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR ($71.3\% \to 0.0\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Second, \emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and Mahalanobis whitening} ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$) capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, raising buffered PR-AUC ($0.331 \to 0.374$), cutting causal FPR ($2.4\% \to 0.4\%$), and lifting causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ ($0.395 \to 0.697$). Third, \emph{post-hoc Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) and manifold gain yield a null aggregate effect on the fixed backbone ($0.188$ vs.\ $0.185$ F1, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0021$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.327$; $0.375$ vs.\ $0.374$ PR-AUC), confirming that the multi-horizon whitened representation drives the gains.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows how zero-lookahead streaming shifts the operating threshold. In causal mode, \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} preserves the highest threshold-free ranking (PR-AUC $0.289$ vs.\ $0.278$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.281$ for TimesNet). Without 64-step window averaging, however, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals have a heavier right tail ($\text{median }\xi = -0.182$, $\gamma_2 = 2.31$) than buffered scores ($\xi = -0.473$, $\gamma_2 = 1.58$) or single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($\xi = -0.307$, $\gamma_2 = 1.33$). When SPOT extrapolates that tail to $q=10^{-3}$ in (\ref{eq:spot_threshold}), it places $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ high on the causal score distribution. Consequently, \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} fires quietly on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.2\%$ of test steps (true-positive rate (TPR) $5.2\%$, held-out FPR $0.3\%$), compared to $10.8\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} (TPR $16.6\%$, held-out FPR $2.4\%$) and $24.2\%$ for TimesNet (TPR $34.0\%$, held-out FPR $24.3\%$). Because it flags only the sharpest peak steps inside each anomaly segment rather than staying on throughout the event, its causal Point-F1 drops ($0.060$ vs.\ $0.188$ buffered, and $0.161$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) even though it catches $50.6\%$ of anomaly events ($\Delta t = 166.2$).

To check whether $50.6\%$ event recall could arise by chance on long anomaly segments ($111$ events with median length $L = 139$ and mean $348.1$ across the 18 non-SWAN streams), Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} (Note) compares each detector against an analytical rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$. At \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}'s held-out nominal rate ($p = \text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} = 0.0033$), random firing catches only $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.288$ of events ($\Delta t_{\text{rand}} = 214.6$ vs.\ $0.506$ and $166.2$ actual). At its overall test firing rate ($\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 0.0220$), unclustered Bernoulli trials reach $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}} = 0.524$ ($\Delta t_{\text{rand}} = 114.7$) because real model alarms cluster inside detected events rather than firing independently. For TimesNet ($p_{\text{hold}} = 0.243$), independent random firing reaches $0.600$ ($0.801$ at $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}}$), exceeding TimesNet's actual $0.588$. Relaxing the static threshold to the empirical $p_{99.5}$ calibration quantile raises \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}'s causal event recall to $0.612$ at $0.76\%$ held-out FPR.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), \texttt{TS-JEPA} holds causal FPR to $0.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ and $3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$ for TimesNet), comparable Point-F1 at $S=1$ ($0.028$ vs.\ $0.025$), and fast detection ($\Delta t = 57.8$ and $85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases across $\{16, 32, 64\}$, TimesNet causal FPR escalates nearly nine-fold ($6.1\% \to 9.8\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Consequently, $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP, MSL, and GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$). Low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, such as hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) and rapid tripwires instead require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.3\%\text{--}11\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we compare models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), \texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.809$) at lower false-alarm rates than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models, at $f^* = 0.01$ \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.395$) and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($0.701$ causal, $0.621$ buffered) substantially increase event recall compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-FPR regime ($f^* \le 0.05$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample across all streams and attribution modes. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($15\times q$) and $0.25\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($3\times q$), compared to $16.11\%$ for TimesNet ($161\times q$) and $14.23\%$ for TranAD ($142\times q$). Replacing parametric SPOT with a non-parametric empirical 99.5th percentile ($p_{99.5}$) yields nearly identical results ($0.7\%$ causal FPR for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $2.3\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} vs.\ $15.4\%\text{--}17.5\%$ for reconstructors), demonstrating that reconstructor false-alarm flooding is not an artifact of GPD tail fitting. Under $S=64$ buffered attribution, however, smearing each window score across 64 consecutive steps dilates every isolated exceedance into a 64-step cluster, inflating buffered FPR to $5.5\%\text{--}6.3\%$ ($55\text{--}63\times q$) for JEPAs and $19.6\%\text{--}19.9\%$ ($196\text{--}199\times q$) for reconstructors under both rules. Static SPOT is therefore near-calibrated for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} only in strictly causal mode. Two diagnostics explain why causal thresholds hold on latent residuals:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} To separate inter-block regime drift from serial correlation and GPD tail-fit error, we pool nominal calibration and evaluation scores $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ across all $19$ non-GHL streams and \emph{refit} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) under three splits: chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, which removes macro-block drift while preserving intra-block serial correlation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$). Once temporal structure is removed by i.i.d.\ shuffling, every model achieves low false-alarm rates near the $0.1\%$ target ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} = 0.0025$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $0.0033\text{--}0.0035$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, TimesNet, and TranAD). In chronological deployment, inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.0229 \to 0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.2103\text{--}0.2302 \to 0.0427\text{--}0.0464$). In barycenter-centered \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, by contrast, inter-block drift vanishes ($0.0\%$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.0033$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.0037$, difference $-0.0004$).
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability, declustering, and rolling adaptation:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median calibration exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$). Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in only $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Applying Ferro--Segers runs declustering ($r=5$) \cite{ferro2003inference} prior to GPD fitting trims stationary causal FPR from $0.25\%$ to $0.20\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and from $0.72\%$ to $0.44\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}. Under discrete step, variance, and regime shifts across 16 streams, adaptive rolling quantiles hold causal \texttt{TS-JEPA} FPR to $0.06\%\text{--}0.40\%$ (vs.\ $0.8\%\text{--}3.0\%$ for TimesNet), whereas continuous monotonic mean ramps defeat trailing rolling windows ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$ FPR across all models).
\end{enumerate}
