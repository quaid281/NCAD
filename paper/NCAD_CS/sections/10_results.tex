\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$). Primary findings:

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076$--$0.086$ versus $0.300$ (TimesNet) and $0.307$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams, the paired reduction is significant: TS-JEPA $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.247, -0.071]$, $p{=}0.002$) and $-0.151$ vs.\ TranAD ($p{=}0.003$); Operator-Entropy shows the same effect ($-0.144$/$-0.142$, $p{=}0.006$). Clustering by dataset ($N{=}7$) yields $p = 0.016\text{--}0.031$, verifying that significance does not depend on within-dataset stream replication. The mechanism is isolated under the same-backbone control (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}).

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Uncertainty:} False-alarm suppression does not translate into statistically distinguishable detection gains. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences are inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$).

\noindent\textbf{Event Recall and Detection Conservatism:} Lower FPR accompanies lower event recall: on non-GHL streams TimesNet captures $58.3\%$ of events and TranAD $49.0\%$, versus $48.1\%$ (TS-JEPA), $43.4\%$ (Reynolds), $30.3\%$ (Potential-Flow). Subtle onsets that do not shift dominant latent coordinates are filtered with the noise.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Delay Trade-Offs ($\Delta t$):} Mean detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) varies widely: TimesNet responds fastest ($\Delta t{=}48$ steps), TS-JEPA averages $80$, and the physics variants are slower still ($133$--$192$); TranAD is slowest ($234$). This raw disparity is largely a detected-set artifact, as delays are averaged across different event sets per model. On shared intersecting detected events at $q^{*}{=}0.05$, the delay gap between TimesNet and TS-JEPA collapses from $31.8$ steps to just $2.3$ steps ($56.9$ vs.\ $59.2$ steps; Table~\ref{tab:trivial_delay}b); vs.\ TCN-ObsPred it is $60$ vs.\ $51$ steps. The apparent latency gap reflects which events each detector catches rather than the intrinsic response delay.

\noindent\textbf{Necessity of Nonlinear Representation Learning (Linear AR Control):} A ridge-regularized linear AR forecaster under identical calibration attains FPR $0.005$ but collapses to Point-F1 $0.051$: observation-space linear dynamics cannot separate faults from background variance. Two training-free references sharpen the point (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:nonparametric}): a PCA-subspace reconstructor matches TimesNet almost exactly ($0.311$/$0.198$/$0.331$ vs.\ $0.300$/$0.197$/$0.329$ FPR/F1/PR-AUC), while a per-channel max-$z$ scorer attains the best ranking ($0.374$) at the worst FPR ($0.339$): ranking quality and threshold conditioning are independent properties.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-Specific Telemetry Dynamics:} Relative performance depends on the physical regime (per-dataset breakdown in Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:dataset_prauc}--\ref{tab:all_datasets}): TimesNet leads on Daphnet periodic kinematics ($0.245$ vs.\ $0.155$ F1); latent prediction leads on spacecraft telemetry (SMAP: OpEntropy $0.591$ PR-AUC; MSL: $0.445$ vs.\ $0.159$). On GHL and Metro, all models struggle ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1). Comparing against the trivial all-positive detector $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ shows that on high-prevalence domains (Daphnet $\pi=14.8\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.255$; MSL $\pi=28.0\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.396$; swan $\pi=28.0\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.438$), all deep detectors achieve Point-F1 near or below a constant alarm, whereas on low-prevalence telemetry (SMAP $\pi=15.5\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.243$ vs.\ TS-JEPA $0.321$; Genesis $\pi=0.3\%$, $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}}=0.006$ vs.\ $0.015$), detectors decisively beat the trivial baseline (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:trivial_delay}a).

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data do not support an aggregate gain:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. TS-JEPA:} paired difference $+0.002$ Point-F1 (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$), $+0.002$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.86$), $+0.009$ FPR ($p{=}0.75$); leads on 9 of 33 streams.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. TS-JEPA:} $-0.015$ Point-F1 ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$), $-0.007$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.13$); leads on 11 of 33.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. TS-JEPA:} Point-F1 differs by $-0.081$ under SPOT ($p{=}0.033$ unadjusted). Its higher event-recall area over nominal causal budgets is descriptive; without matched realized-FPR support and multiplicity-adjusted evidence, neither an intrinsically stronger detector nor a purely operating-point explanation is established.
\end{enumerate}

These findings align with the matched-capacity controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a): the physical regularizers provide no aggregate detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. The dynamical diagnostics on \emph{why} (operator rank preservation without detection gain, covariance-shift insensitivity, and decoupling resolution via Lyapunov invariance) are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

Event-level incident metrics, latency, and rolling-SPOT case studies are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}: rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at a recall cost, and TS-JEPA's causal false-positive rate on the 5-stream operational incident cohort (evaluated across test-split nominal intervals: $4.18\%$, Table~S8) sits an order of magnitude below reconstructive baselines ($20.8$--$30.1\%$). In comparison, on the primary 19-stream held-out calibration benchmark, held-out nominal FPR is $0.0136$ vs.\ $0.2427$/$0.2219$.


Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark} reports the same benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression; on the 14 GHL streams, causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and causal event recall is $46.55\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $58.82\%$ for TimesNet: without a lookahead buffer, latent predictive scoring is an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of detection sensitivity.

\paragraph{Pre-Onset Alarm Attribution}
Buffered attribution is strictly retrospective: scores are painted over $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$, precluding leads beyond $S{=}64$ steps. Median pre-onset ``leads'' of $180$--$204$ steps in empirical lead-time diagnostics are nuisance alarms at the underlying false-positive rate or precursor drift, not prediction: TimesNet emits $206$ such alarms per $1{,}000$ nominal steps versus $24$ for TS-JEPA, reflecting the baseline alarm-rate gap. We do not claim early-warning capability.

\paragraph{Detection Quality at Matched Operating Points}
\label{par:operating_curves}
We fit empirical-quantile thresholds on nominal budgets $q\in[10^{-4},0.9]$ to evaluate event recall and realized FPR on test. Partial-AUC values $0.742$ (TS-JEPA) and $0.618$ (TimesNet) integrate recall over nominal budgets up to $0.10$, not matched realized FPR. At $q^{*}{=}0.05$, causal event recall is $0.774$ vs.\ $0.618$, but realized FPR differs ($0.180$ vs.\ $0.262$). Budget-pooled curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_curves}) obscure stream-specific support and are not paired detector comparisons. Given multi-hundred-step anomalies (mean $402$ in Daphnet, $1{,}102$ in SMAP), alarming at $30\%$ FPR catches $>95\%$ of segments by chance ($1 - (1-\text{FPR})^{L}$); TimesNet's $58\%$ recall at $30\%$ FPR falls within this random expectation.

For an exploratory support-aware check, we interpolate stream--seed curves over common realized-FPR $[0.10,0.50]$ without extrapolation (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:common_support_17models}). The causal TS-JEPA--TimesNet difference is $+0.061$ (95\% dataset-bootstrap CI $[-0.153,0.329]$; dataset-sign-permutation $p{=}0.625$; 20 paired cells across four streams). For TS-JEPA vs.\ PCA-LinPred-LN ($+0.121$), the pre-specified confirmatory test is the stream-level paired Wilcoxon test and 95\% bootstrap CI ($p=0.312$, $[-0.008, 0.271]$, 11 streams); 5-dataset tests are low-powered exploratory checks ($p \ge 0.0625$; permutation $p=0.188$, $p_{\text{BH}}=1.0$; cluster bootstrap CI $[0.015, 0.310]$ reflects $N=5$ undercoverage). TS-JEPA establishes no significant advantage over PCA-LinPred-LN on common support. Bounded-observation TCN matches TS-JEPA causally on nine streams ($-0.018$, CI $[-0.055,0.003]$) and shows higher buffered recall area on five streams ($-0.211$, CI $[-0.349,-0.105]$); neither survives family-wide BH adjustment. Nominal-budget orderings and pooled curves do not establish a causal-regime advantage, and boundedness alone determines neither tail transfer nor matched-FPR detection.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out of sample? Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under both strictly causal trailing and buffered smear inference. The calibrator evaluated here is the \emph{static} peaks-over-threshold step of SPOT --- a single threshold fit once on the calibration block; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}. Four realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Causal vs.\ buffered smear overshoot:} Under causal trailing, TS-JEPA achieves held-out nominal FPR of $0.0101$ ($0.0092$ stream-macro, $\approx$$10\times$ overshoot; median $0.0002$ / $0.02\%$, IQR $[0.0000, 0.0025]$). Buffered inference paints each alarm across $S{=}64$ steps, inflating per-timestep FPR by up to $S \cdot q = 6.4\%$ to reach $0.0526$ ($0.0457$ stream-macro, $53\times$ overshoot) --- alarm smearing explains the bulk of apparent JEPA inflation. In contrast, TimesNet and TranAD suffer causal FPRs of $0.1795$ and $0.1601$ (medians $0.0328$ and $0.0133$; $0.221725$ and $0.221695$ buffered, both rounding to $0.2217$), far exceeding the $S \cdot q$ smear ceiling and confirming genuine distributional breakdown.
    \item \textbf{One-sided overshoot testing:} Rather than two-sided CI exclusion (which counts zero-FPR cells as failures), one-sided overshoot testing ($\text{CI}_{\text{lo}} > 10^{-3}$) reveals TS-JEPA overshoots design risk in only 7\% of causal cells (12\% buffered), versus 46--49\% for reconstructive baselines.
    \item \textbf{Parity with empirical and conformal quantiles:} On TS-JEPA, validation-maximum yields causal FPR $0.0090$ / F1 $0.0722$, empirical p99.5 yields $0.0169$ / $0.0907$, and Split Conformal thresholding ($q_{1-\alpha}, \alpha{=}10^{-3}$) achieves median causal FPR $0.0006$ ($0.0512$ mean) vs.\ $0.1247$ (TimesNet) and $0.1866$ (TranAD). Neither parametric GPD tail modeling nor distribution-free conformal calibration prevents risk overshoot under chronological deployment because non-stationary temporal drift violates exchangeability.
    \item \textbf{Kurtosis-adaptive directional breakdown:} The heuristic multiplier $1+\tanh(\gamma_2)/6$ inflates TS-JEPA buffered FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$. In 33.3\% of cells (47/141) with $\gamma_2 \le 0$, the multiplier lowers the threshold by up to 16.7\%, exploding FPR from 9.17\% to 50.49\%; where $\gamma_2 > 0$ (66.7\%), it raises the threshold and cuts FPR from 3.31\% to 1.28\%.
\end{enumerate}

Empirical tail diagnostics on nine non-GHL streams confirm tail-sample scarcity: median exceedances above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and Grimshaw returns degenerate $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits. Yet KS tests reject in only $6/27$ (TS-JEPA) and $8/27$ (TimesNet) cells, while held-out FPR varies from $0.0$ to $0.67$ across seeds on the same stream under passing fits.

To determine whether drift or scarcity dominates, an exchangeable permutation control uniformly samples calibration and evaluation splits from pooled nominal blocks. Under point exchangeability, causal TS-JEPA FPR drops from $0.0121$ ($12.1\times$ overshoot) to $0.0019$ ($1.9\times$ overshoot), matching design $q{=}10^{-3}$ within sampling variance; TimesNet drops from $0.0872$ to $0.0037$ (a $96\%$ overshoot reduction). Block permutation ($L{=}64$) yields intermediate overshoot ($5.8\times$ for TS-JEPA), demonstrating that inter-block drift dominates calibration failure while autocorrelation is secondary. Across all $N=1080$ dumped cells, KS drift between calibration and test nominal blocks predicts realized SPOT FPR (Spearman $\rho = +0.377$, 95\% CI $[+0.314, +0.433]$, $p = 7.4 \times 10^{-38}$); calibration-block excess kurtosis correlates inversely ($\rho = -0.315$, 95\% CI $[-0.373, -0.260]$, $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-26}$). A controlled-drift study (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:drift_calibration}) shows runs declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ effective exceedances per cell, and rolling calibrators control risk to $1$--$7\%$ under step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33$--$0.39$ FPR).

Computational profiling is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}: JEPAs use $183.8\text{--}185.3$~MB peak memory at $0.60\text{--}0.84$~ms per-window inference.
