\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports the comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams under the held-out calibration protocol of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup} (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$). Primary findings:

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams, latent predictors achieve held-out nominal FPR $0.076$--$0.086$ versus $0.300$ (TimesNet) and $0.307$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired reduction is significant: TS-JEPA $-0.153$ vs.\ TimesNet (CI $[-0.247, -0.071]$, $p{=}0.002$) and $-0.151$ vs.\ TranAD ($p{=}0.003$); Operator-Entropy shows the same effect ($-0.144$/$-0.142$, $p{=}0.006$). The mechanism is isolated under the same-backbone control (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}).

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Parity:} False-alarm suppression does not translate into statistically distinguishable detection gains. On the non-GHL subset TS-JEPA attains $0.234$ Point-F1 / $0.323$ PR-AUC versus $0.197$/$0.329$ (TimesNet) and $0.130$/$0.287$ (TranAD). Across all 33 streams the paired differences are inconclusive: vs.\ TimesNet $+0.012$ F1 (CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$, $p{=}0.54$), $-0.000$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.39$); vs.\ TranAD $+0.051$ F1 ($p{=}0.88$), $+0.023$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.16$).

\noindent\textbf{Event Recall and Detection Conservatism:} Lower FPR accompanies lower event recall: on non-GHL streams TimesNet captures $58.6\%$ of events and TranAD $48.1\%$, versus $49.8\%$ (TS-JEPA), $39.8\%$ (Reynolds), $33.1\%$ (Potential-Flow). Subtle onsets that do not shift dominant latent coordinates are filtered with the noise.

\noindent\textbf{Detection Delay Trade-Offs ($\Delta t$):} Mean detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}) varies widely: TimesNet responds fastest ($\Delta t{=}48$ steps), TS-JEPA averages $80$, and the physics variants are slower still ($133$--$192$); TranAD is slowest ($234$). Because latent encoders smooth observation noise, alarms trigger only once anomalies propagate into dominant latent coordinates --- deployments trade ~4$\times$ fewer nominal false alarms for added initial latency.

\noindent\textbf{Necessity of Nonlinear Representation Learning (Linear AR Control):} A ridge-regularized linear AR forecaster under identical calibration attains FPR $0.006$ but collapses to Point-F1 $0.057$ (precision $4.35\%$): observation-space linear dynamics cannot separate faults from background variance. Nonlinear latent representation is necessary to combine controlled false alarms with usable detection. Two training-free references sharpen the point (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:nonparametric}): a PCA-subspace reconstructor matches TimesNet almost exactly ($0.311$/$0.198$/$0.331$ vs.\ $0.300$/$0.197$/$0.329$ FPR/F1/PR-AUC), while a per-channel max-$z$ scorer attains the best ranking of any baseline ($0.374$) at the worst FPR ($0.339$): ranking quality and threshold conditioning are independent properties.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-Specific Telemetry Dynamics:} Relative performance depends on the physical sensing regime (per-dataset breakdown in Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:all_datasets}--\ref{tab:dataset_prauc}): TimesNet leads on Daphnet periodic kinematics ($0.245$ vs.\ $0.155$ F1), where 2D Fourier decomposition captures gait structure; latent prediction leads on spacecraft telemetry (SMAP: OpEntropy $0.591$ PR-AUC; MSL: $0.445$ vs.\ TimesNet $0.159$). On GHL and Metro all models struggle ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1): subtle actuator drifts and extreme traffic surges evade static EVT bounds.

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

Does adding physics-inspired regularizers (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) improve detection over unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA}? The 33-stream data refute this:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. TS-JEPA:} paired difference $+0.002$ Point-F1 (CI $[-0.025, +0.029]$, $p{=}0.71$), $+0.002$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.86$), $+0.009$ FPR ($p{=}0.75$); leads on only 9 of 33 streams.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. TS-JEPA:} $-0.015$ Point-F1 ($[-0.042, +0.007]$, $p{=}0.61$), $-0.007$ PR-AUC ($p{=}0.13$), $+0.001$ FPR ($p{=}0.92$); leads on 11 of 33.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. TS-JEPA:} significantly worse Point-F1 ($-0.081$, $[-0.160, -0.018]$, $p{=}0.033$) and nominally lower PR-AUC ($-0.067$, $p{=}0.58$); leads on 7 of 33.
\end{enumerate}

These findings align with the matched-capacity regularizer controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) and Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}, where disabling each physical loss term on a fixed backbone produces changes well within seed noise. We conclude that the physical regularizers provide no measurable anomaly detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. Performance differences between models stem from shared representation conditioning rather than the physical loss terms.


The dynamical diagnostics evaluating \emph{why} each regularizer fails (operator rank preservation without detection gain, covariance-shift insensitivity, and curl-free misspecification on cyclic telemetry) are reported in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics} (Tables~\ref{tab:operator_entropy_validation}--\ref{tab:potential_flow_validation}).

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}

Event-level incident metrics, latency, and rolling-SPOT case studies are in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}: rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms up to $21\times$ on drift-prone streams at a recall cost, and TS-JEPA's causal false-positive rate ($4.18\%$) sits an order of magnitude below reconstructive baselines ($20.8$--$30.1\%$).
\input{sections/tab_causal_benchmark.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark} reports the same benchmark under strictly causal ($0$-lookahead) streaming. On the 19 non-GHL streams TS-JEPA reduces held-out nominal FPR to $0.0136$ versus $0.2427$ (TimesNet) and $0.2219$ (TranAD), an $\approx$$18\times$ suppression; on the 14 GHL streams causal FPR falls below $0.009$ for JEPAs, but causal Point-F1 there is near-zero for latent predictors ($\le 0.006$ vs.\ $0.022$ TimesNet). Causal Point-F1 drops to $0.1118 \pm 0.0412$ (TS-JEPA) versus $0.2091 \pm 0.0265$ (TimesNet), and event recall declines from $58.82\%$ to $46.55\%$: without a lookahead buffer, latent predictive scoring is an ultra-conservative filter that prevents false alarms at the price of detection sensitivity.

\paragraph{Buffered Early-Warning Lead Time}
Under buffered inference across five streams, TS-JEPA warns pre-onset for $12.3\%$ of events ($7$ of $57$ pooled; median lead $180$ steps, $23.8$ false warnings per $1{,}000$ nominal steps), while TimesNet warns for $73.7\%$ at a comparable lead ($204$ steps) but emits $206.4$ false warnings per $1{,}000$ --- on GECCO approaching a constant-alarm regime ($873.6$/1k). Pre-onset coverage is confounded by the underlying alarm rate: TS-JEPA trades early-warning coverage for an order-of-magnitude lower nuisance rate.

\paragraph{Detection Quality at Matched False-Alarm Budgets}
Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:matched_fpr} re-evaluates all models at common held-out FPR budgets $q^* \in \{0.001, 0.005, 0.01, 0.05\}$, removing the confound that native SPOT thresholds land on different effective operating points. Under strictly causal streaming the ordering partially reverses: at $q^*{=}0.05$, TS-JEPA attains event recall $0.774$ versus $0.618$ (TimesNet) while emitting $180$ versus $262$ false alarms per $1{,}000$ nominal steps --- the Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark} gap largely reflects TimesNet's miscalibrated threshold admitting a higher realized alarm rate, not an intrinsic sensitivity deficit. Under buffered attribution the ordering remains mixed ($0.486$--$0.560$ vs.\ $0.583$--$0.612$ recall), while TS-JEPA consistently emits fewer nuisance alarms per nominal step; pre-onset conventions are reported per operating point.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

Does automated extreme-value thresholding adhere to its design risk $q$ out-of-sample? Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} compares calibrators on identical score streams across all 33 streams under buffered inference. Four realities emerge:

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Systematic design-risk overshoot:} SPOT ($u{=}0.98, q{=}10^{-3}$) never achieves its design risk out-of-sample --- held-out nominal FPR averages $5.26\%$ (TS-JEPA), $6.17\%$ (OpEntropy), $22.17\%$ (TimesNet), $22.17\%$ (TranAD), a $53$--$222\times$ overshoot.
    \item \textbf{High failure rate:} 95\% bootstrap CIs exclude $q$ in $61\%$/$65\%$/$86\%$/$82\%$ of runs (TS-JEPA/OpEntropy/TimesNet/TranAD); non-stationary drift violates EVT's stationary-tail assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Parity with empirical quantiles:} on TS-JEPA, validation-maximum gives FPR $0.0553$ / F1 $0.1591$ and empirical p99.5 gives $0.0682$ / $0.1598$ --- parametric GPD tail modeling confers no demonstrable benefit.
    \item \textbf{Kurtosis-adaptive failure:} the multiplier inflates TS-JEPA FPR from $0.0526$ to $0.1768$ (CI-exclusion $61\% \to 82\%$) with indistinguishable F1 ($0.1694$ vs.\ $0.1577$); calibration-split kurtosis does not forecast out-of-sample tail shifts.
\end{enumerate}

To separate the causes of calibration failure, we recorded per-stream tail-fit diagnostics on a nine-stream non-GHL subset (TS-JEPA and TimesNet, three seeds each; \texttt{reports/evt\_tail\_diagnostics.csv}). Tail-sample scarcity is genuine --- the median exceedance count above $u = p_{98}$ is only $21$ (range $16$--$70$), and the Grimshaw estimator returns the degenerate boundary shape $\xi = -0.9$ in $33\%$ of TS-JEPA and $52\%$ of TimesNet fits --- yet goodness-of-fit is not the dominant failure: KS tests of the excesses reject at $p < 0.05$ in only $6/27$ (TS-JEPA) and $8/27$ (TimesNet) cells, while realized held-out FPR varies from $0.0$ to $0.67$ across seeds on the \emph{same} stream under passing fits. Inter-block distribution drift, not tail mis-specification, drives the overshoot; exceedances are also strongly clustered ($10$--$22$ per cluster), inflating the effective variance of the FPR estimate. The four-mechanism decomposition and sensitivity sweep in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:evt_detail} corroborate this attribution.

A controlled-drift study (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:drift_calibration}) injects mean shifts, variance rescaling, autocorrelation shifts, regime changes, and continuous ramps into the real dumped residual streams and re-evaluates five calibrators over multiple evaluation blocks. Two results sharpen the failure analysis: runs declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ \emph{effective} exceedances per cell (of a median $21$ raw), so GPD tail fits rest on far fewer independent samples than the raw count suggests; and rolling calibrators control risk to $1$--$7\%$ under step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts but fail under continuous ramps ($0.33$--$0.39$ achieved FPR): adaptation absorbs regime jumps but not drift within the evaluation block itself.

Computational profiling is in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}: the JEPA family uses $183.8\text{--}185.3$~MB peak memory (nearly $7\times$ less than TranAD) at $0.60\text{--}0.84$~ms per-window inference.
