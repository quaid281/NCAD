\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 continuous telemetry streams under the chronological held-out SPOT protocol ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$): Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs. Evaluating realized FPR and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ as primary outcomes shows a large relative reduction in false alarms and calibration error, while \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the highest buffered PR-AUC ($0.375$) and causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.701$ at $f^* = 0.01$).

\noindent\textbf{Lower Realized FPR and Calibration Error on Held-Out Telemetry:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent scoring lowers held-out nominal FPR and calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ three-fold under buffered attribution ($9.3\%$ and $10.8\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} versus $30.0\%$ and $30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD; paired difference for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} $-19.2\%$ vs.\ TimesNet, $p = 0.049$, and $-19.9\%$ vs.\ TranAD, $p = 0.007$) and ten- to eighty-fold under causal streaming ($2.4\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.3\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, calibration error $0.023$ and $0.002$, versus $24.3\%$ and $22.2\%$, calibration error $0.242$ and $0.221$; paired difference $-23.9\%$ vs.\ TimesNet, $p = 0.010$, and $-21.9\%$ vs.\ TranAD, $p = 0.010$). The reduction holds across all 33 streams in paired tests (causal stream-macro FPR $0.25\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} versus $16.11\%$ and $14.23\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)) and across the $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation (where neither hyperparameter selection nor threshold calibration accesses the held-out dataset family), leaving one dataset out at a time keeps causal FPR at $0.2\%\text{--}2.3\%$ and buffered FPR at $3.8\%\text{--}7.0\%$ for JEPAs versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ causal and $8.4\%\text{--}22.1\%$ buffered for reconstructors. In Panel~(c), under strictly causal attribution, \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the lowest held-out nominal FPR on every multi-stream family ($0.2\%$ on Daphnet, $0.1\%$ on GHL, $0.4\%$ on MSL, and $0.6\%$ on SMAP versus $5.6\%\text{--}36.0\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD).

\noindent\textbf{Multi-Horizon Ranking Gains vs.\ Unadjusted Point-F1 Trade-offs:} Under buffered attribution on the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)), \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves the highest threshold-free PR-AUC ($0.375$ vs.\ $0.331$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}, $0.329$ for TimesNet, and $0.287$ for TranAD) while buffered Point-F1 ($0.188$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $0.206$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) and event recall ($0.496$ and $0.477$) remain comparable to TimesNet ($0.197$ Point-F1, $0.583$ event recall) and ahead of TranAD ($0.130$ Point-F1, $0.490$ event recall). Per-dataset standard deviations are as large as the means (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). Only SMAP beats the trivial always-alarm F1 of $0.236$ ($0.379$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $0.370$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}), while \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves leading buffered PR-AUC on SMAP ($0.576$ vs.\ $\pi=0.150$) and Genesis ($0.037$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.000$ for baselines).

\noindent\textbf{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet) with persistent localized transients, where suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration is the primary operational requirement.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (\texttt{TS-JEPA} $\lor$ \texttt{lowvar}) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each component of \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} improves upon single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA}: (i)~\emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and synchronized barycenter centering} eliminate common-mode baseline shift between context and target windows, cutting calibration-to-holdout KS drift from $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.214$ (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) to $0.163$ and reducing the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR from $56.6\%$ to $11.2\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)); (ii)~\emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and Mahalanobis residual whitening} ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, $\alpha_{\text{sol}}=0$) capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, raising buffered PR-AUC from $0.331$ to $0.374$, reducing causal held-out FPR from $2.4\%$ to $0.4\%$, and increasing causal matched-FPR event recall at $f^* = 0.01$ from $0.395$ to $0.697$; and (iii)~\emph{one-sided Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) and nominal manifold distance gain further trim buffered held-out FPR from $12.2\%$ to $10.8\%$ and raise matched-FPR event recall to $0.621$ buffered ($0.701$ causal) at $f^* = 0.01$ while maintaining formal TOST equivalence in unadjusted Point-F1 ($+0.003$, $90\%$ CI $[-0.011, +0.016]$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0021$ at $\Delta = \pm 0.030$).

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} isolates the cost of zero-lookahead streaming. Without retrospective smoothing, lower false-alarm rates ($0.3\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} and $2.4\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}) trade point-level sensitivity for near-zero false alarms: causal Point-F1 is more conservative ($0.060$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} at $0.3\%$ FPR and $0.161$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} at $2.4\%$ FPR, vs.\ $0.209$ for TimesNet at $24.3\%$ FPR), while causal event recall reaches $0.506$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($0.487$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}). Because causal SPOT thresholds on \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} are highly conservative ($0.3\%$ realized FPR), evaluating at the empirical 99.5th calibration percentile ($p_{99.5}$) raises causal event recall to $0.612$ at $0.76\%$ held-out FPR. On five non-stationary incident streams with discrete regime shifts or localized variance steps (including Room-Occupancy, CalIt2, and SMAP \texttt{P-3}), pairing latent scores with dynamic rolling SPOT suppresses false alarms by up to $21\times$. However, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b), this suppression applies specifically to discrete shifts; under continuous monotonic mean ramps, trailing calibration windows continually lag the drifting mean, producing high false-alarm rates ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$) across all models.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), \texttt{TS-JEPA} holds causal FPR to $0.4\%$ and $0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%$ and $3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while achieving higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$ for TimesNet), comparable Point-F1 at $S=1$ ($0.028$ vs.\ $0.025$), and fast detection ($\Delta t = 57.8$ and $85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases across $\{16, 32, 64\}$, TimesNet causal FPR escalates nearly nine-fold ($6.1\% \to 9.8\% \to 21.3\%$), whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Thus $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, e.g., $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$), whereas $S=64$ is unacceptable for low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, e.g., hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) or rapid tripwires, which require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Deployable Calibration Quantiles vs.\ Matched Realized Test-FPR Operating Points}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.3\%\text{--}11\%$ for latent predictors, and long segments ($402$ steps on Daphnet, $1{,}102$ on SMAP) allow a $30\%$ random-alarm detector to touch $>95\%$ of segments via $1-(1-\text{FPR})^L$, we compare models under two operating regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Deployable nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} At causal calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$ (set without test labels), \texttt{TS-JEPA} and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieve higher event recall ($0.730$ and $0.809$) at lower false-alarm rates than TimesNet ($0.618$ recall) and TranAD ($0.602$ recall).
    \item \textbf{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models, at $f^* = 0.01$ \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.395$) and \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($0.701$ causal, $0.621$ buffered) substantially increase event recall compared to TimesNet ($0.217$) and TranAD ($0.149$), whereas at relaxed budgets ($f^* \ge 0.10$) recall curves converge ($p = 0.625$). Latent scoring thus specifically improves sensitivity in the low-FPR regime ($f^* \le 0.05$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Static extreme-value thresholding does not achieve its nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$) out of sample across all streams. Across all 33 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), standard SPOT achieves stream-macro causal FPR of $1.51\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.014$, $15\times$ the $q=10^{-3}$ design risk) and $0.25\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.002$, only $3\times$ the design risk), and buffered FPR of $5.54\%$ and $6.32\%$ ($55\text{--}63\times$). These rates are an order of magnitude below TimesNet ($16.11\%$ causal, $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.160$, $161\times$) and TranAD ($14.23\%$ causal, $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q| = 0.141$, $142\times$), showing that multi-horizon barycenter whitening brings causal static SPOT within $0.15\%$ of the nominal $0.1\%$ target. Comparing static SPOT against runs-declustered GPD fitting (\texttt{Decl.\ SPOT}, run-length $r=5$ via the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}) and adaptive rolling calibrators (\texttt{Roll.\ emp.}, \texttt{Roll.\ SPOT}) across 16 streams and nine drift regimes in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b) shows that: (i)~on stationary nominal streams under causal scoring, \texttt{Decl.\ SPOT} lowers \texttt{TS-JEPA} FPR from $0.72\%$ to $0.44\%$ ($1.36\% \to 1.07\%$ on the 4-stream tail panel, and $24.4\% \to 18.9\%$ for TimesNet), whereas under buffered smoothing declustering reduces $N_u$ exceedances to $N_c \approx 4\text{--}6$ clusters, increasing GPD variance ($0.159$ pooled); and (ii)~adaptive \texttt{Roll.\ emp.} reduces pooled FPR from $6.1\%\text{--}11.3\%$ to $1.1\%\text{--}2.4\%$ under discrete step, variance, and autocorrelation shifts, holding causal \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($0.40\%$) near the $0.1\%$ design risk, whereas continuous monotonic ramps defeat trailing rolling windows ($32.6\%\text{--}38.5\%$). Three diagnostics explain the static behavior:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GPD tail stability and quantile/length sensitivity:} On the 19 non-GHL streams at $u=p_{98}$, median exceedance count is $N_u = 28$ causally ($21$ buffered) with $0\%$ solver fallback and low threshold uncertainty ($\operatorname{SE}(\tau)/\tau = 2.6\%\text{--}3.5\%$ causal). Causally, Grimshaw GPD fits hit the lower shape boundary ($\xi = -0.90$) in $4.4\%$ of JEPA cells ($\text{median }\xi = -0.27$) versus $34.4\%$ for TimesNet and $54.4\%$ for TranAD. Varying $u \in \{p_{95}, p_{98}, p_{99}\}$ leaves causal FPR unchanged ($2.41\%\text{--}2.42\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA}), whereas halving calibration length ($50\%$ $N_{\text{cal}}$) raises causal FPR to $3.72\%$ (\texttt{TS-JEPA}).
    \item \textbf{Platykurtic collapse under total-threshold kurtosis scaling:} Total-threshold scaling $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)]$ reduces causal FPR on leptokurtic \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} cells to $0.11\%$ (matching the $0.1\%$ design risk, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), but $64$-step buffered smoothing renders $31.9\%$ of \texttt{TS-JEPA} cells platykurtic ($\gamma_2 < 0$). Scaling total $\tau$ pulls $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ below $u=p_{98}$ in $28.4\%$ of cells, inflating buffered FPR from $6.40\%$ to $16.30\%$ for \texttt{TS-JEPA} and from $7.57\%$ to $15.18\%$ for \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Excess-only scaling $\tau_{\text{exc}} = u + (\tau - u)[1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)] \ge u$ prevents collapse below $u$ ($9.19\%$ buffered, $2.40\%$ causal).
    \item \textbf{Three-stage refitted permutation decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)):} Pooling nominal calibration and evaluation scores $\mathbf{v}_{\text{pool}} = [v_{\text{cal}}, v_{\text{hold}}]$ and \emph{refitting} SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) on each permuted split separates chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, equalizing macro-block distributions while preserving intra-block autocorrelation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$) rates. Across the $18$ streams with $N_{\text{cal}}, N_{\text{hold}} \ge 100$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(c)), inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $56.6\%$ of excess causal overshoot in single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ for reconstructors) but drops to $11.2\%$ in barycenter-centered \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} ($+0.0013$ $[-0.002, +0.004]$), confirming that synchronized barycenter centering and multi-horizon whitening suppress inter-block regime drift.
\end{enumerate}
