\subsection{Development 33-Stream and Untouched Held-Out 118/162-Stream Benchmark Results}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
Following Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} compares our flagship \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} against 1D latent JEPAs and observation-space baselines across the 33-stream development benchmark (19 non-GHL and 14 GHL streams) under the two-stage protocol: Panel~(a) reports retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) causal streaming, and Panel~(c) per-dataset summaries. Primary independent generalization evidence across the 118 untouched held-out streams (within the 141- and 162-stream archive cohorts) appears in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}; on the 130-stream external baseline screen, TS-JEPA achieves $0.186$ stream and $0.160$ macro Point-F1 versus $0.152$ for TranAD, $0.151$ for TimesNet, $0.097$ for DCdetector, $0.088$ for NCAD, and $0.082$ for Anomaly Transformer.

\noindent\emph{Operational Trade-Offs, Precision, and Recall Across Operating Points:} Comparing \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} against 1D scalar JEPAs and observation-space reconstructors highlights its operational profile across false-alarm rate, point-wise precision ($P$) and recall ($R$), event recall (ER), and onset delay ($\Delta t$). On the 19 development streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 ($0.262$, $P = 0.273$, $R = 0.369$), buffered PR-AUC ($0.406$), buffered ER ($66.4\%$), causal ER ($62.5\%$), fastest causal detection delay ($59.6$ steps vs.\ TimesNet's $62.2$ and TranAD's $241.0$), and highest Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.139$), while keeping held-out causal FPR at $1.6\%$ ($10.7\%$ buffered) versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ causal ($30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ buffered) for TranAD and TimesNet. Under causal streaming, because surgical quenching projects out stationary calibration drift and concentrates causal breaker trips near fault onset transitions rather than extended anomaly plateaus, \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} trades lower causal point-wise recall ($R = 0.088$, $P = 0.214$, causal Point-F1 $= 0.100$) for rapid onset detection ($\Delta t = 59.6$ steps) and high event recall ($62.5\%$), whereas 1D scalar Reynolds suffers severe recall collapse ($R = 0.032$, $P = 0.285$, causal F1 $= 0.050$, $\Delta t = 154.0$ steps) and TimesNet's higher causal Point-F1 ($0.209$) alerts during $24.3\%$ of healthy operation. On the 118 untouched held-out streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), the three-horizon 224-bit panel lifts both causal precision ($P: 0.175 \to 0.241$) and recall ($R: 0.081 \to 0.179$) over 1D scalar SPOT (causal F1: $0.083 \to 0.143$, ER: $51.2\% \to 72.8\%$). Practitioners should deploy \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} to minimize causal onset latency ($\Delta t = 59.6$), maximize event recall ($62.5\%\text{--}66.4\%$) and buffered F1 ($0.262$), and hold causal FPR at $1.6\%$ (or $\le 1.1\%$ via the Strict quorum setting, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\noindent\emph{Reducing Observation-Space False-Alarm Flooding:} On the 19 development streams, predicting spherical latent representations cuts held-out nominal FPR three-fold under buffered attribution ($7.8\%\text{--}10.7\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, $p \le 0.045$) and by an order of magnitude under causal streaming ($0.6\%\text{--}1.6\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$, $p = 0.010$; $N=7$ dataset-clustered $p = 0.016$). Under zero-tuning LODO evaluation, JEPAs maintain $0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR versus $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors. On GHL ($\pi=0.013$), all JEPAs and the linear AR forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1): low FPR without recall is not detection.

\noindent\emph{Scope of Applicability:} The evidence supports \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} under four boundary conditions: (i)~multichannel arrays ($K \ge 9$; SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, SMD, Exathlon, OPPORTUNITY); (ii)~horizon selection $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ matched to the tolerable reporting lag $S-1$ of the sampling rate (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), avoiding $S=64$ on hourly Metro ($63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$); (iii)~calibration histories providing $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ effective independent observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ overlapping windows, Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}), or IQR/Beta-smoothed thresholds when $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$; and (iv)~faults that perturb multi-channel latent trajectories, supplemented by the channel-level branch for isolated single-sensor faults (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Slow process ramps on GHL and Metro ($0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1) remain outside scope.

\subsection{Real-Time Streaming vs.\ Retrospective Buffered Attribution}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Without 64-step averaging, causal whitened residuals have a heavier right tail than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$ vs.\ $1.25$). Under 1D scoring, hijacker dials push the threshold onto that tail, so 1D Reynolds JEPA alerts on $2.4\%$ of test steps and flags isolated peaks ($0.050\text{--}0.052$ causal F1, $47.8\%$ ER, $154.0$-step delay). With per-dial switches, surgical weight-space quenching, and onset relays (\ref{eq:onset_relay}), \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} reaches $62.5\%$ causal ER, $59.6$-step causal delay, and $1.6\%$ causal FPR ($0.262$ buffered F1 and $66.4\%$ buffered ER). A rate-matched random alerter, $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, catches $27.8\%$ of events at \emph{Laminar-JEPA}'s nominal rate, but $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\subsection{Multi-Horizon Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit) Evaluation Across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ introduces a structural reporting lag of $S-1 = 63$ steps before a causal decision is emitted at $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To test whether \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} retains its advantages over 1D scalar scoring and observation-space baselines when the prediction horizon is shortened, we retrain and evaluate the complete multi-horizon backbone, surgical weight-space quenching, and 224-bit panel across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ (structural lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$ steps) on five representative streams across three seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a), partitioning each target window into four nested prefixes $\lceil mS/4 \rceil$ for $S \ge 4$ and four lag-1 difference views at $S=1$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Zero and near-zero reporting lag ($S=1$, lag $0$; $S=4$, lag $3$):} At $S=1$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} achieves $0.037$ causal Point-F1 ($P=0.072$, $R=0.031$), $81.8\%$ event recall, $3.6\%$ FPR, and $63.8$-step onset delay---more than $2.6\times$ the causal Point-F1 of 1D scalar SPOT ($0.014$, ER $= 43.3\%$, FPR $= 1.2\%$). At $S=4$ (3-step lag), \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} reaches $0.050$ causal Point-F1 ($0.082$ buffered F1), $84.4\%$ event recall, $2.9\%$ FPR, and $36.7$-step onset delay, achieving $4.3\times$ higher causal Point-F1 than 1D scalar scoring ($0.012$, ER $= 51.3\%$) while cutting onset delay by nearly two-thirds ($98.5 \to 36.7$ steps).
    \item \emph{Intermediate horizons ($S=16$, lag $15$; $S=32$, lag $31$) vs.\ $S=64$ (lag $63$):} At $S=16$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} reaches $0.055$ causal Point-F1 ($77.3\%$ ER, $2.2\%$ FPR, and $26.6$-step delay) versus $0.032$ for 1D scalar SPOT ($59.1\%$ ER, $\Delta t = 81.7$) and $6.1\%$ FPR for TimesNet. At $S=32$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} reaches $0.074$ causal Point-F1 ($77.8\%$ ER, $2.6\%$ FPR, $\Delta t = 37.1$) and achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 across all horizons ($0.290$ vs.\ $0.182$ for 1D scalar), while at $S=64$ it reaches $0.078$ causal Point-F1 ($0.193$ buffered F1, $80.0\%$ ER, $\Delta t = 35.1$, $3.5\%$ FPR vs.\ $0.044$ F1 for 1D scalar and $21.3\%$ FPR for TimesNet). Thus, \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} consistently outperforms scalar thresholding across all five horizons: $S \in \{4, 16, 32\}$ provides low-lag tripwires ($3\text{--}31$ steps) for minute- or hour-scale telemetry, while $S \in \{32, 64\}$ suits $\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$ streams.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
We evaluate three operating-point comparisons:
(1)~\emph{Autonomous breaker points and calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Without test labels, the Strict ($53.4\%$ ER at $1.1\%$ FPR) and \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} ($62.5\%$ ER at $1.6\%$ FPR) operating points lead the low-FPR frontier; at $q^* = 0.050$, the 224-bit panel reaches $77.4\%$ causal ER versus $61.8\%$ for TimesNet and $60.2\%$ for TranAD.
(2)~\emph{Post-hoc diagnostic controls at matched test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} As noted in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_metrics}, $f^*$ sets thresholds post hoc from test labels as a non-deployable diagnostic check of tail sensitivity at equal alert rates. At $f^* = 0.01$, \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} catches $58.4\%$ of events ($56.2\%\text{--}57.8\%$ for 1D Reynolds and $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA), nearly tripling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and quadrupling TranAD ($14.9\%$), with a similar gap at $f^* = 0.05$ ($72.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$).
(3)~\emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} On the 10 development SMAP/MSL streams stratified by NASA's taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)), reconstructors degrade on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC), whereas \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} and 1D Reynolds reach $0.658$ and $0.652$ ($75.0\%$ autonomous recall). Across all 19 development streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)), \emph{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit)} doubles reconstructor recall on contextual faults ($61.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) and leads on point faults ($79.2\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).

\subsection{Untouched Held-Out Generalization, Calibration Sample-Size Reliability, and Drift Decomposition}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} evaluates the three-horizon Speculative-JEPA panel ($192$ distinct dials, Section~\ref{subsec:triad_formulations}) across 141 streams in 12 datasets at three seeds, foregrounding the 118 untouched held-out streams alongside the 162-stream four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} audit:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Primary generalization on 118 untouched held-out streams and Laminar-JEPA across 162 streams (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)):} On the 118 untouched streams, replacing the scalar score on Speculative-JEPA with the panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.072\text{--}0.083$ to $0.134\text{--}0.143$ ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$), lifts causal event recall from $48.4\%\text{--}51.2\%$ to $66.2\%\text{--}72.8\%$, and cuts causal delay from $84.7\text{--}107.8$ to $55.8\text{--}77.0$ steps, at a higher causal held-out FPR ($7.3\%\text{--}8.3\%$ vs.\ $5.1\%\text{--}5.9\%$; 141-stream causal $\Delta\text{F1} = +0.062$, $N=12$ dataset-clustered $95\%$ CI $[+0.044, +0.072]$, $p = 0.007$). Across $162$ streams ($15$ datasets, including all $118$ untouched streams), surgical quenching in four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} cuts causal held-out FPR from $6.71\%$ to $6.17\%$ ($\Delta = -0.0055$, stream $p = 0.0011$, $N=15$ dataset-clustered $p = 0.027$; $7.93\% \to 7.26\%$ on $n{=}118$, $p = 0.030$) and test FPR from $14.63\%$ to $13.93\%$ ($p < 10^{-4}$, dataset $p = 0.016$), with median causal held-out FPR $1.03\%$ ($1.13\%$ on $n{=}118$) and test FPR $1.59\%$ ($70.4\%$ of streams $\le 2.0\%$).
    \item \emph{Per-dataset consistency (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} The panel leads causal Point-F1 in all six multi-stream held-out datasets, most on SMAP ($0.194$ vs.\ $\le 0.129$) and SMD ($0.090$ vs.\ $\le 0.042$), at a higher causal FPR (most on Exathlon, $23.5\%$ vs.\ $\le 13.7\%$). Under buffered attribution it trails the best 1D JEPA on SMAP, Exathlon, and OPPORTUNITY; on CreditCard, GECCO, and PSM all methods lie within $0.007$ causal F1.
    \item \emph{Calibration sample-size reliability, threshold confidence intervals, and small-sample calibrators:} Because windows overlap by $C-1=255$ steps, the effective independent sample size is $N_{\text{eff}} = N_{\text{cal}}/\tau_{\text{int}} \approx 0.31\,N_{\text{cal}}$ ($\tau_{\text{int}} = 3.23$; median $N_{\text{eff}} = 152$ of $N_{\text{cal}} = 408$ on the 19 development streams). On the 118 untouched streams, mean causal FPR is $19.2\%$ for the 12 streams with $N_{\text{cal}} < 250$ ($N_{\text{eff}} < 78$) versus $1.6\%$ for the 22 streams with $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 2{,}500$ ($N_{\text{eff}} \ge 775$). Auditing calibration blocks across $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100, 250, 500, 1{,}000, 2{,}500, 5{,}000\}$ ($N_{\text{cal}} \approx 323\text{--}16{,}150$) with moving-block bootstrap ($B=200$, block length $64$) shows that the median 95\% CI relative half-width on the 224 per-dial thresholds $\tau_j$ tightens monotonically from $\pm 11.0\%$ (median coefficient of variation (CV) $6.7\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=100$ and $\pm 9.4\%$ (CV $5.7\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=250$ to $\pm 7.8\%$ (CV $4.6\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=500$, $\pm 5.8\%$ (CV $3.4\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=1{,}000$, $\pm 3.8\%$ at $N_{\text{eff}}=2{,}500$, and $\pm 2.8\%$ (CV $1.6\%$) at $N_{\text{eff}}=5{,}000$. Across disjoint calibration segments of length $N_{\text{cal}} \in \{100, 250, 500, 1{,}000\}$, inter-segment test-FPR SD drops from $12.9\%$ ($N_{\text{cal}}=100$) and $10.1\%$ ($N_{\text{cal}}=250$) to $2.8\%$ ($1.7\%$ under Strict quorum) at $N_{\text{cal}}=500$, while quorum $K_{224}$ CV drops from $28.7\%$ to $16.1\%$. Comparing empirical $q_{0.999}$ against four small-sample alternatives---Harrell--Davis Beta-smoothed quantiles, order-statistic-free IQR extrapolation ($\text{med}_j + 4.8\,\text{IQR}_j$), Weibull peaks-over-threshold, and split-conformal calibration---shows that at $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100, 250\}$, where empirical $q_{0.999}$ yields $2.07\%\text{--}2.12\%$ test FPR ($9.2\%$ on $N_{\text{cal}}=100$ segments), Beta-smoothed calibration cuts test FPR to $1.32\%$ and $0.77\%$ ($49.4\%\text{--}41.5\%$ ER) and IQR extrapolation holds test FPR at $0.88\%$ and $0.47\%$ with higher causal F1 ($0.067$ and $0.059$ vs.\ $0.059$ and $0.047$) and lower inter-segment FPR SD ($4.2\%$ vs.\ $6.4\%$). We therefore establish a minimum requirement of $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ independent observations ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows, ensuring expected tail count $N_{\text{eff}}(1 - q_{0.995}) \ge 1.5$, threshold 95\% CI half-width $\le 8\%$, and inter-segment FPR SD $< 3\%$), and recommend IQR or Beta-smoothed thresholds whenever $N_{\text{cal}} < 1{,}000$.
    \item \emph{Three-stage permutation drift decomposition:} Refitting thresholds across the 19 development streams under chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block-permuted ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$), and pointwise i.i.d.\ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$) splits confirms that inter-block regime drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) drives $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in TS-JEPA ($0.0229$ vs.\ $0.0073$) and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors ($0.210\text{--}0.230$ vs.\ $0.043\text{--}0.046$), whereas barycenter centering in Laminar-JEPA cuts the inter-block drift share four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($0.0056$ vs.\ $0.0047$) while per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j$ quarantine individual drifting coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
