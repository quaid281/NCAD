\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} explains why scalar scores degrade when moving from buffered review to live streaming. Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$ and causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $1.2\%$ to $0.7\%$ ($p=0.0025$). In return, \emph{Lite} trades lower causal ER ($62.2\%$ vs.\ $70.1\%$, $p=0.0029$) and causal Point-F1 ($0.142$ vs.\ $0.165$, $p=0.035$) against the $224$-dial reference. A separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.151\text{--}0.152$ for TimesNet and TranAD, and $0.082\text{--}0.097$ for DCdetector, NCAD, and Anomaly Transformer). Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ F1 across $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), because context and target latents shift together and cancel. Across linear ramps ($a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$, duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$), chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$. Across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds), mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ steps on abrupt steps to $433.9$ on exponential $1-e^{-t/512}$ and $586.7$ on $0.001\sigma$/step bias as $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

Pairing \emph{Lite} with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ lifts real GHL causal ER from $0.8\%$ ($0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Post-fault GHL baseline wander, however, drives uncompensated test FPR to $74.4\%$: sliding prediction misses slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift flags benign wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Controlled Perturbation Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned ZCA-anchored latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across block indices ($m\in\{1,2,3\}$; exact horizons under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and cross-block drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $m\in\{2,3\}$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking provides \emph{controlled perturbation attribution} across input channels. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under injected $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Because latent features mix sensors nonlinearly and real field datasets lack ground-truth root-cause labels, this validates synthetic perturbation sensitivity rather than causal fault localization on natural failures, and $1/K$-diluted single-sensor flatlines remain sub-quorum.

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit} evaluate the untouched archive ($n=118$) along four questions:

\noindent\emph{1) Live operating-point shift over 1D JEPA:} Replacing scalar scoring with the $128$-dial quorum expands live event coverage and nearly halves alarm delay (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Causal ER rises from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$, $p=0.0039$, sign-flip BH $p\le 0.016$). End-to-end online latency, including the $63$-step fill lag, drops from $133.5$ steps (cluster CI $[90.4,206.2]$) to $69.0$ steps ($[53.3,102.4]$), with a paired co-detected cut of $-74.6$ steps ($[-156.3,-31.1]$, $p=5.4{\times}10^{-6}$). Mean causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ rises from $4.4\%$ to $6.4\%$ ($0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$ median), and overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) spans zero under dataset clustering ($[-0.026,+0.069]$, stream $p=8.8{\times}10^{-4}$). On $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ streams ($n=59$), \emph{Lite} beats 1D on both ER ($+20.4$ points, cluster CI $[+8.9,+28.7]$, $p=0.016$) and Point-F1 ($0.040\to 0.085$, $[+0.001,+0.091]$, sign-flip $p=0.047$), whereas 1D leads on shorter prefixes ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$ F1).

\noindent\emph{2) Trade-off against the 224-dial reference:} Compared with $224$-dial \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, \emph{Lite} trades peak recall for quieter average operation. The $224$-dial panel reaches higher causal ER ($69.4\%$, $\Delta=-4.8$ points, cluster CI $[-6.6,-1.3]$, $p=0.016$), slightly higher Point-F1 ($0.091$, $\Delta=-0.004$, $[-0.013,+0.006]$, $p=0.15$), and a lower p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($49.3\%$ vs.\ $64.7\%$). In return, \emph{Lite} lowers mean ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $7.0\%$, $p=1.3{\times}10^{-7}$), median ($0.93\%$ vs.\ $1.4\%$), and buffered ($13.3\%$ vs.\ $21.0\%$, $p=9.2{\times}10^{-10}$) $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and lifts the $\le 5\%$ pass rate ($88.1\%$ vs.\ $84.7\%$).

\noindent\emph{3) External reconstructors and matched-budget parity:} Under untouched-archive SPOT, \emph{Lite} beats Linear PCA Forecaster by $+34.5$ ER points and $+0.063$ F1 ($p\le 8.2{\times}10^{-12}$). TimesNet ($0.175$ F1, $0.236$ PR-AUC) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1, $0.236$ PR-AUC) achieve higher raw F1 and ranking only at $25.6\%$ and $24.6\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; at a matched $f=1\%$ test FPR, their ER falls to $25.9\%$ and $25.5\%$ versus $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}. Against scalar JEPAs, archive PR-AUC matches ($0.152$ vs.\ $0.162$ causal; $0.229$ vs.\ $0.230$ buffered); at Lite's $0.25\%$ calibration rate, the quorum catches $65.0\%$ of events versus $52.6\%$ for 1D and $58.1\%\text{--}61.1\%$ for Fisher, top-$k$, and max-$z$, while 1D leads F1 at $f=1\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)).

\noindent\emph{4) Zero-tuning cohorts and nested LODO (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} Across the $118$ untouched streams ($37+1+80$), \emph{Lite} raises causal ER in all $9$ datasets ($p=0.0039$) and cuts latency in $6$ of $8$ multi-step sets. Gains hold on the $4$ zero-tuning external sets ($n=37$: $53.6\%$ vs.\ $38.5\%$ ER, $p=3.8{\times}10^{-4}$; $0.121$ vs.\ $0.057$ F1, $p=2.4{\times}10^{-7}$), when pooling archive-heldout GECCO ($n=38$: $+15.8\%$ ER, $p=2.3{\times}10^{-4}$; $+0.068$ F1, $p=1.9{\times}10^{-7}$), and on the $80$ unseen streams from tuned sets ($70.2\%$ vs.\ $59.6\%$ ER, $p=0.0080$; F1 $p=0.55$). True $9$-fold nested LODO tuning ($s=42$) selects $(0.990, 1.06)$ in every fold ($69.5\%$ vs.\ $52.8\%$ ER, $p=3.3{\times}10^{-4}$; $0.094$ vs.\ $0.076$ F1, $p=1.8{\times}10^{-4}$). Under 9-fold dataset exclusion, ER stays positive in every fold ($+9.7\%\text{--}17.8\%$, $p\le 0.0022$), while unweighted $\Delta\text{Pt-F1}$ dips only when removing Exathlon ($-0.005$, $p=0.065$) due to SMAP.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Chronological distribution shift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration variance vs.\ chronological nonstationarity (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and within-block dependence while removing temporal regime evolution across mean, variance, covariance, and operating modes. Across $n=118$, block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ falls from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ ($\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams), showing that chronological distribution shift explains $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms.
    \item \emph{Associated drift correlates:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) match stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$), whereas stabilized residual-covariance shift is $3.1\times$ larger ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), strongly correlating with quorum inflation ($4.8\times$) and $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In $3$-seed fault-free dev stress tests, terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ amplitude scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and keeps $45^\circ$ basis rotations moderate ($6.8\%$ FPR). Mean steps, duty-cycle shifts, and baseline walks do not self-settle, raising FPR to $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ alongside $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$. Blindly auto-recalibrating on $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, however, absorbs persistent faults: fitting $400$ post-shift windows during a persistent $+2.5\sigma$ fault cuts post-buffer recall to $9.1\%$. Our conditional policy therefore flags $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ under frozen weights, quarantines candidate windows, and permits threshold updates only after operator or control-state confirmation ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall preserved; $4.8\%$ FPR on confirmed shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability and Two-Stage Deployment Funnel}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite calibration histories cannot provide distribution-free tail guarantees. Because sliding windows correlate strongly (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$), $105$ of $118$ untouched streams have $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) tests sensitivity to serial dependence without inventing unobserved extremes: across $n=118$, \texttt{MBB-Median} matches the unbootstrapped quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR; $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1), whereas upper-confidence-bound (UCB) \texttt{MBB-UCB90} and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds cost $8\text{--}17$ ER points and thinning inflates mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

Autonomous deployment therefore uses an empirical two-stage screening funnel rather than a theoretical guarantee (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Requiring $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59$ of $118$ streams (below $2{,}500$ steps, disjoint cross-fitting cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$). Screening frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ then accepts $52$ streams ($35$ also meeting $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$) and routes $7$ drifting streams ($33.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $48.9\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) to fallback. Because selecting on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ introduces selection optimism and finite-sample uncertainty ($0.9\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ vs.\ $5.8\%$ median and $14.5\%$ p90 dependence-adjusted $95\%$ Wilson UCB at median $N_{\text{eff,hold}}=66.7$), healthy $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ on subsequent independent test intervals rises to $11.0\%$ mean and $3.9\%$ median ($53.8\%$ staying $\le 5\%$; $76.9\%$ staying $\le 10\%$) while doubling Point-F1 over 1D ($0.076$ vs.\ $0.034$, $p=3.2{\times}10^{-5}$). Routing all $66$ non-accepted streams to 1D SPOT produces a $118$-stream hybrid ($0.094$ F1, $60.9\%$ ER, $4.6\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $1.9\%$ median $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$) supervised by live $D_{\text{KS}}$ quarantine monitoring.
