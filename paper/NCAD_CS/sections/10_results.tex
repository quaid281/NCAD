\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} reports all 33 primary continuous telemetry streams and the 141-stream archive-wide benchmark under the chronological held-out protocol: Panel~(a) uses retrospective buffered attribution, Panel~(b) strictly causal streaming, Panel~(c) per-dataset breakdowns including anomaly event counts ($E$) and both causal and buffered held-out nominal FPRs, and Panel~(d) the 130-stream and 141-stream archive evaluations. Across both attribution regimes, pairing multi-horizon Soliton-Reynolds JEPA with the 224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel achieves the highest buffered Point-F1 ($0.228$), buffered PR-AUC ($0.411$), causal PR-AUC ($0.312$), causal event recall ($0.607$), and Dataset-Macro Point-F1 ($0.122$), while keeping held-out false alarms an order of magnitude below observation-space reconstructors ($1.1\%\text{--}1.7\%$ causal and $9.8\%\text{--}13.2\%$ buffered vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD).

\noindent\emph{Suppressing False-Alarm Flooding Relative to Reconstructors:} On the 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)), latent prediction cuts held-out nominal FPR three-fold under buffered attribution ($7.8\%\text{--}9.8\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds 1D and 224-Bit Strict vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, $p \le 0.045$) and by more than an order of magnitude under strictly causal streaming ($0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ for Soliton-Reynolds variants and $2.4\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors, $p = 0.010$). This separation holds across all $N=7$ dataset families ($p = 0.016$) and under zero-tuning LODO evaluation ($0.3\%\text{--}2.3\%$ causal FPR for JEPAs vs.\ $12.1\%\text{--}18.4\%$ for reconstructors). In Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit achieves $0.7\%$ causal and $4.7\%$ buffered FPR on Daphnet ($0.223$ buffered F1 vs.\ $0.145$ for TS-JEPA), $0.199$ buffered F1 on MSL (surpassing TS-JEPA at $0.161$ and TimesNet at $0.158$), $0.354$ buffered and $0.351$ causal F1 on SMAP at $38.2$-step causal delay, and $0.063$ buffered F1 on Genesis.

\noindent\emph{Overcoming 1D Score Collapse Across 19 Primary and 141 Archive Streams:} Under legacy 1D scalar scoring, Soliton-Reynolds JEPA trades lower Point-F1 ($0.050$ causal, $0.148$ buffered) for ultra-conservative firing because one or two out-of-sample drifting coordinates (\emph{hijacker dials}) inflate the scalar threshold. Replacing 1D scalar collapse with the 224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel resolves this trade-off on the exact same trained networks (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), \ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b),(d), and~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). On the 19 primary non-GHL streams, \emph{Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Strict} and \emph{224-Bit Breaker} lift buffered PR-AUC from $0.331\text{--}0.370$ to $0.411$, raise buffered Point-F1 to $0.190\text{--}0.228$ ($p = 0.026$), more than double causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.116\text{--}0.138$ ($17$ wins / $0$ losses, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), lift causal event recall to $53.4\%\text{--}60.7\%$ ($63.1\%$ buffered), and cut detection delay from $246.6$ to $105.9$ steps causally ($402.7$ to $71.8$ steps buffered). Across all 141 qualifying multivariate streams in 12 datasets at three seeds (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) and~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)), 3-horizon Speculative-JEPA and Speculative-Alien-JEPA first improve 1D PR-AUC over single-horizon TS-JEPA ($0.184$ vs.\ $0.165$ causally; $0.253\text{--}0.256$ vs.\ $0.213$ buffered, $p \le 0.0042$). Pairing the multi-horizon predictor with the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel (\emph{Speculative-224Bit-Strict} and \emph{Speculative-224Bit-Breaker}) then lifts causal PR-AUC to $0.197$ ($p = 0.014$ vs.\ TS-JEPA), nearly doubles causal Point-F1 from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.125\text{--}0.139$ ($97$ wins / $36$ losses over TS-JEPA, $p = 8.3{\times}10^{-11}$; $114$ wins / $18$ losses over 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$), raises causal event recall from $48.0\%\text{--}50.7\%$ to $64.8\%\text{--}72.7\%$, and halves causal detection delay from $115.4\text{--}125.4$ to $55.3\text{--}78.0$ steps. Under buffered attribution across the 141 streams, the 224-bit panel lifts PR-AUC to $0.261$ ($p = 0.0031$), raises Point-F1 to $0.177\text{--}0.180$ ($p \le 2.6{\times}10^{-4}$), raises event recall to $66.0\%\text{--}68.5\%$, and lowers held-out FPR below 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA ($16.5\%$ vs.\ $18.0\%$) at $46.6\text{--}54.0$-step delay. On GHL ($\pi=0.013$, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)), all JEPAs and the linear autoregressive (AR) forecaster fire near-silently ($0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ FPR, $0.000$ F1), showing that low FPR without recall does not indicate detection.

\noindent\emph{Domain and Operational Applicability Boundaries:} Synthesizing performance across fault classes and sampling rates defines four operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Favorable operating regime:} Multi-channel spacecraft, server, and wearable kinematic arrays ($K \ge 9$, SMAP, MSL, Daphnet, SMD, Exathlon) with localized transients, where the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel maximizes Point-F1 and event recall at low false-alarm rates.
    \item \emph{Latency-constrained telemetry:} Systems where a 63-step emission lag is prohibitive for immediate tripwire response (such as hourly Metro where $63\text{ steps}=63\,\text{h}$), requiring short horizons $S \in \{1, 4\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \emph{Quiet single-channel faults:} Inactive sensor modes (flatlines, dropouts) that LayerNorm embeddings absorb as nominal background, requiring the hybrid union detector (combining TS-JEPA with a rolling variance-collapse monitor) when single-channel coverage is mandatory (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).
    \item \emph{Continuous non-stationary process ramps:} Industrial heating loops and traffic drifts (GHL, Metro; $0.000\text{--}0.022$ F1), which defeat both static and rolling calibration rules.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Component-Level Analysis of Soliton-Reynolds and the 224-Bit Breaker}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}
Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b) isolate how each architectural stage contributes to overall performance. First, \emph{multi-depth TCN tapping and barycenter centering} remove common-mode baseline shift, lowering calibration drift ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.214$ to $0.179$) and cutting the inter-block drift share of excess causal FPR four-fold ($71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). Second, \emph{nested multi-horizon rollout and calibration-stabilized Mahalanobis whitening} capture sub-horizon transients across $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, lifting causal event recall at a $1\%$ FPR budget from $0.395$ to $0.584$. Third, while \emph{post-hoc 1D Lieb--Thirring soliton modulation} ($\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$) leaves 1D Point-F1 and PR-AUC statistically unchanged under TOST ($p_{\text{TOST}} < 10^{-4}$), \emph{expanding the 32 whitened coordinates into the 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel with 32 fast right-edge onset relays} eliminates hijacker-dial threshold inflation, doubling causal Point-F1 and halving detection delay.

\subsection{Operational Incident Evaluation and Causal-vs-Buffered Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
Comparing Panels~(a) and~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} shows why the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel is essential for real-time streaming. Without 64-step window averaging, causal $128$-dimensional whitened residuals have a heavier right tail ($\gamma_2 = 2.30$) than buffered scores ($\gamma_2 = 1.25$). Under legacy 1D scalar scoring, hijacker dials push the scalar threshold high onto that tail so that 1D Soliton-Reynolds JEPA alerts on only $\hat{p}_{\text{alert}} = 2.4\%$ of test steps ($4.0\%$ true-positive rate (TPR) at $0.6\%$ held-out FPR), flagging only isolated peak steps inside each event ($0.050\text{--}0.052$ causal F1). By monitoring each fluid-dynamic dial with its own hijack-guarded switch $b_j(t)$ and triggering immediately when a terminal sub-block and right-edge convective acceleration relay $r_c(t)$ coincide in (\ref{eq:onset_relay}), \emph{224-Bit Breaker} stays active across the true fault duration ($0.138$ causal F1, $60.7\%$ event recall, and $105.9$-step delay at $1.67\%$ held-out FPR). Against an analytical rate-matched i.i.d.\ Bernoulli random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$ (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} Note), random firing at 224-Bit Breaker's held-out nominal rate catches only $28.1\%$ of events versus $60.7\%$ actual, whereas for TimesNet ($24.3\%$ FPR) random firing alone reaches $60.0\%$, exceeding TimesNet's actual $58.8\%$.

\subsection{Horizon Length, Score-Emission Latency, and False-Alarm Control}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
Target horizon $S=64$ imposes a 63-step emission lag before an alarm can trigger (Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). To evaluate detection quality versus latency against baselines at matched emission lag $S-1 \in \{0, 3, 15, 31, 63\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) retrains models across $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five streams (three seeds). At $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag), TS-JEPA keeps causal FPR at $0.4\%\text{--}0.6\%$ (vs.\ $2.5\%\text{--}3.1\%$ for TimesNet) while maintaining higher causal PR-AUC ($0.178\text{--}0.189$ vs.\ $0.150\text{--}0.162$) and short detection delays ($\Delta t = 57.8\text{--}85.2$ steps). As $S$ increases from $16$ to $64$, TimesNet causal FPR climbs from $6.1\%$ to $21.3\%$, whereas latent predictors remain bounded ($\le 2.0\%$). Consequently, $S=64$ suits high-rate sensors ($\ge 1\,\text{Hz}$, such as $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet where $63\text{ steps}=0.98\,\text{s}$) or slow subsystem trend monitoring ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP, MSL, and GHL, $10.5\,\text{min}$). Low-rate infrastructure streams ($\le 1\,\text{min}^{-1}$, such as hourly Metro where $63\,\text{h}$ lag is prohibitive) and rapid tripwires instead require $S \in \{1, 4\}$.

\subsection{Operating Budgets and Contextual vs.\ Point Anomalies}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Because reconstruction baselines calibrate to $22\%\text{--}31\%$ realized FPR versus $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ for latent predictors, we evaluate three operating-point comparisons:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Autonomous circuit-breaker points and nominal calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Without test labels, Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit Strict ($53.4\%$ ER at $1.1\%$ FPR) and 224-Bit Breaker ($60.7\%$ ER at $1.7\%$ FPR) dominate the low-FPR frontier; at calibration quantile $q^* = 0.050$, Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit reaches $77.4\%$ causal event recall (vs.\ $61.8\%$ for TimesNet and $60.2\%$ for TranAD).
    \item \emph{Post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)):} Equalizing realized test nominal FPR across models (vertical dotted guides in Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)) proves that low false-alarm rates are not an artifact of low alert frequency. At a $1\%$ test FPR budget ($f^* = 0.01$), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit detects $58.4\%$ of anomaly events ($56.2\%\text{--}57.8\%$ for 1D Soliton-Reynolds and $39.5\%$ for TS-JEPA),nearly tripling TimesNet ($21.7\%$) and quadrupling TranAD ($14.9\%$). A similar gap holds at $f^* = 0.05$ ($72.8\%$ vs.\ $34.3\%\text{--}40.7\%$).
    \item \emph{Contextual vs.\ point anomaly stratification (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}):} Out-of-range point anomalies (abrupt spikes exceeding $\pm 5\sigma$) can be flagged even by a memoryless marginal $z$-score (\texttt{ZScoreMax}), whereas contextual anomalies stay within normal sensor bounds ($\max|z| \le 5\sigma$) and only violate multi-step trajectory structure. In Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a), stratifying the 10 primary SMAP/MSL streams by NASA's official ground-truth taxonomy \cite{Hundman2018b} shows that observation-space detectors collapse on contextual anomalies ($0.161\text{--}0.317$ buffered PR-AUC and $30\%\text{--}56\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$). Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit leads every contextual metric ($0.658$ buffered PR-AUC, $75.0\%$ autonomous recall, and $82.0\%\text{--}92.0\%$ recall at $f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) while reaching $94.4\%$ recall at $f^* = 0.05$ on NASA point anomalies. Across all 19 non-GHL streams (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(b)), Soliton-Reynolds 224-Bit more than doubles the recall of reconstructors on in-range contextual faults ($61.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ at $f^* = 0.05$) while also leading on point faults ($79.2\%$ vs.\ $56.3\%\text{--}61.1\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why 1D Scalar Calibration Fails vs.\ 224-Bit Stabilization and Drift Decomposition}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} synthesizes why 224-bit multi-perspective circuit breakers outperform 1D scalar calibration across all 141 streams (Panel~(a)) and decomposes the underlying regime-drift mechanisms across the 19 primary streams (Panel~(b)):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Three-stage permutation drift decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)):} To separate inter-block regime drift from serial correlation, we pool nominal calibration and evaluation windows across all $19$ non-GHL streams and refit thresholds under three splits: chronological ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}}$), $L=64$ block permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$, removing macro-block drift while preserving intra-block serial correlation), and pointwise i.i.d.\ permutation ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}}$). Under i.i.d.\ shuffling, every model achieves low false-alarm rates ($\text{FPR}_{\text{pt}} \le 0.35\%$). In chronological deployment, inter-block drift ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} - \text{FPR}_{\text{blk}}$) accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal FPR in single-horizon TS-JEPA and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors, whereas in barycenter-centered Soliton-Reynolds JEPA the inter-block drift share drops four-fold to $18.7\%$ ($\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.56\%$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.47\%$).
    \item \emph{Per-dial hijack isolation and prefix-horizon voting:} Even when overall barycenter drift is small, one or two individual coordinates can shift between training and calibration. In 1D scalar models, those coordinates hijack the scalar tail; in the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, calibration-stabilized whitening $\lambda_{m,c}$ and per-dial hijack guards $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\gamma_j^{\text{tr}\to\text{cal}}$ isolate drifting dials individually, while prefix-horizon voting in buffered mode prevents 64-step window smearing from dilating isolated short-horizon trips (lowering buffered held-out FPR from $18.0\%$ in 1D Speculative-Alien-JEPA to $16.5\%$ in Speculative-224Bit-Strict while lifting Point-F1 from $0.153$ to $0.177$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)).
\end{enumerate}
