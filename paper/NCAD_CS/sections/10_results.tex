\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults that evolve much slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ Point-F1 across all $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Across linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$ ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points). Extending this experiment across six onset profiles ($3$ seeds on clean multivariate segments: abrupt step, $L\in\{64,256,1024\}$ ramps, exponential sensor degradation $1-e^{-t/512}$, and $0.001\sigma$/step accumulating bias) directly tests why slow faults evade short-horizon prediction: as onset slows from abrupt steps to accumulating bias, mean \emph{Lite} detection delay balloons nearly $20\times$ ($29.3 \to 70.7 \to 138.6 \to 361.4 \to 586.7$ steps; exponential degradation $433.9$ steps), context-to-target displacement $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$), and whitened residual RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$) as both windows ride the ramp together.

To test whether a complementary long-term reference closes this blind spot without altering \emph{Lite} weights, we evaluate a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ context features and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. On the $14$ real GHL industrial ramp streams ($3$ seeds), this frozen-reference monitor lifts causal ER from $0.8\%$ (\emph{Lite} alone, $0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$ ($58.5\%$ in a two-timescale union hybrid, $129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$; Point-F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). However, because GHL's nominal baselines keep drifting after fault episodes, uncompensated test FPR rises to $74.4\%$, revealing a fundamental trade-off: sliding-window prediction rejects slow nominal drift at the cost of missing slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift catches slow ramps at the cost of flagging benign baseline wander.

\subsection{Interpretability and Fault Attribution Using the Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Rather than emitting an opaque scalar, the 128-dial panel decomposes active trips $N_{128}(\tau)$ additively across short ($S_1{=}16$), medium ($S_2{=}32$), and long ($S_3{=}64$) horizons plus persistent drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On an abrupt water-chemistry fault in \texttt{GECCO} (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}(a)), all four breaker groups ignite simultaneously, driving $N_{128}(\tau)$ to $4.9{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ whereas 1D Scalar SPOT barely grazes $1.04{\times}$. On an official NASA in-range contextual fault in \texttt{SMAP A-7} (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}(b)), continuous telemetry remains strictly inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$ while its oscillation frequency shifts, stacking medium/long horizons ($S_2, S_3$) and drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ into a sustained plateau up to $1.84{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $1.25{\times}$ on 1D SPOT). Conversely, during an $860$-step thermal ramp on \texttt{GHL-44} (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}(c)), context-target co-movement keeps $N_{128}(\tau)=0$ throughout the climb, whereas the complementary frozen-reference drift statistic rises smoothly across its $1{\times}$ alarm threshold inside the ramp.

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit} evaluate the disjoint five-block archive across four explicit regimes: \emph{autonomous} (healthy-only thresholds), \emph{matched calibration rate}, \emph{post-hoc} test-FPR diagnostic control, and \emph{buffered} review:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operating-point gain vs.\ post-hoc ranking (Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, \ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)):} Across the 118 untouched streams, autonomous \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$, $p=0.0039$) and cuts mean delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps, trading higher mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$). Pooled causal Point-F1 rises from $0.076$ to $0.086$ ($p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$), driven by $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ streams ($0.040\to 0.085$, $n=59$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$, $+20.4$ ER points), whereas 1D SPOT leads Point-F1 when $N_{\text{cal}}<500$ ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$). Under buffered review, \emph{Lite} lifts ER ($59.0\%\to 67.7\%$) and Point-F1 ($0.143\to 0.158$) while lowering $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($19.5\%\to 13.3\%$). At Lite's low $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rate (median $0.25\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), the quorum catches more episodes ($65.0\%$) than 1D ($52.6\%$) and Fisher/top-$k$/max-$z$ ($58.1\%\text{--}61.1\%$); at higher healthy rates ($0.5\%\text{--}5\%$, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)) simpler coordinate pools converge, and at post-hoc matched test FPR ($f=1\%$), 1D leads Point-F1 ($0.084$ vs.\ $0.049$). The panel's value therefore lies strictly in its label-free quorum operating point rather than superior score ranking.
    \item \emph{Cross-dataset and LODO generalization (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} Because weights fit per stream while hyperparameters transfer zero-tune across $K\in[9,55]$, Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a) checks whether gains rely on one archive. \emph{Lite} improves causal ER over 1D in all $9/9$ untouched datasets ($+1.2$ points on SMAP up to $+26.4\text{--}28.9$ on Exathlon, OPPORTUNITY, and Daphnet) and shortens delay on $6/8$ multi-step cohorts ($41$ vs.\ $289$ steps on Exathlon; $56$ vs.\ $147$ on Daphnet). Across all $12$ LODO folds of the 141-stream archive (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(b)), causal ER gain remains strictly positive in $12/12$ folds ($+8.9$ to $+17.9$ points, $\max p=0.0027$), peaking at $+17.9$ ER points and $+0.035$ Point-F1 when all $47$ SMAP streams are dropped. Dropping Exathlon or Daphnet shifts unweighted mean $\Delta\text{Pt-F1}$ slightly negative ($-0.007$ and $-0.002$) because 1D's lower FPR favors step precision on SMAP's long faults ($0.131$ vs.\ $0.088$), even as paired Wilcoxon rank-sum stays favorable ($p=0.055$ and $0.036$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
While $88.1\%$ of untouched streams satisfy $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ (median $0.93\%$; $92.3\%\text{--}100\%$ of streams across SMD, MSL, OPPORTUNITY, GECCO, PSM, and CreditCard at $0.1\%\text{--}1.3\%$ dataset-mean FPR in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), a sharp upper tail pushes the $95$th percentile to $64.7\%$ ($14$ streams $>5\%$, concentrated in Spark workload switches on Exathlon, gait-trial shifts on Daphnet, and three command-mode shifts on SMAP). We distinguish two mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration failure vs.\ concept drift (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Randomly interleaving autocorrelation-preserving blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ keeps finite-sample size $N_{\text{cal}}$ and serial dependence intact while removing chronological drift. Across all 118 untouched streams ($3$-seed audit), stationary block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ collapses from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ ($\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams in Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a), including $1.00 \to 0.00$ on \texttt{SMAP G-6} and $0.75 \to 0.03$ on \texttt{Exathlon 10\_2}), attributing $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms to post-calibration \emph{concept drift} rather than calibration-tail estimation error.
    \item \emph{Internal drift drivers:} Streams with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ share nearly the same median $N_{\text{eff}}$ as stable streams ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$; Spearman $\rho=-0.01$ with $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) and comparable post-LayerNorm latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$), but suffer $3.1\times$ larger stabilized residual-covariance shift ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ relative Frobenius norm), inflating marginal dial trip rates ($13.4\times$ vs.\ $1.5\times$ calibration rate) and quorum count variance ($4.8\times$ vs.\ $1.4\times$), tracked closely by quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Controlled healthy regime-shift stress test:} Injecting fault-free operating changes halfway through clean nominal segments on the 19 development streams ($3$ seeds) maps what breaks and what adapts. Moderate amplitude scaling ($1.5\times, 2.0\times$ on $25\%$ of channels) barely perturbs \emph{Lite} ($2.0\%$ and $3.3\%$ post-shift FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) because terminal LayerNorm absorbs scale changes, while $45^\circ$ cross-channel correlation rotation raises FPR to $6.8\%$ ($D_{\text{KS}}=0.43$). By contrast, persistent nominal mean steps ($+1\sigma, +2\sigma$), alternating duty cycles, and $1.5\sigma$ baseline walks do \emph{not} self-settle after $W=320$ steps because input standardization and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ remain anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$: steady-state FPR stays elevated for both \emph{Lite} ($24.3\%, 32.0\%, 14.6\%, 21.1\%$) and Multi-Horizon 1D ($14.0\%, 33.8\%, 10.7\%, 22.6\%$) as $D_{\text{KS}}$ rises to $0.43\text{--}0.69$. Triggering live recalibration on the first $400$ post-shift windows whenever $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ restores mean-step steady-state FPR from $24.3\%\text{--}32.0\%$ back to $4.8\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability of Coordinate and Quorum Calibration}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ median $7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams below $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)\ge 1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, giving $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Consequently, no distribution-free finite-sample tail guarantee can be claimed from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone. We retain $q_{0.995}$ ($\times 1.12$, floor $3.5$) for two empirical reasons. First, testing Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$ resamples of block length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) across all 118 untouched streams shows that `MBB-Median` yields virtually identical thresholds and false-alarm tails ($0.092$ F1, $65.2\%$ ER, $0.92\%$ median and $62.7\%$ p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ and $60.3\%$ deployed in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), `MBB-UCB90` (and binomial $N_{\text{eff}}$ upper bounds) trims p95 only to $47.7\%$ ($42\%$) while sacrificing $8\text{--}17$ ER points, and thinning $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ by $\tau_{\text{int}}$ worsens mean FPR to $10.4\%$ by starving sample support. Second, the five-step deployment protocol compensates directly for calibration uncertainty without test labels: (1)~fit weights, $\mathbf{\Omega}$, and (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; (2)~require $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ (routing shorter histories to 1D SPOT lifts pooled causal F1 to $0.095$, and two-fold cross-fitting on $T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$ cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$); (3)~audit frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$; (4)~reject autonomous deployment when $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ ($50$ passing streams achieve $0.7\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $0.076$ vs.\ $0.039$ causal F1 over 1D, $p=3.5\times 10^{-4}$); and (5)~monitor live quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ to recalibrate when post-hold regime shifts arise.
