\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark, Attribution Regimes, and Horizons}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} explains why scalar scores degrade when moving from buffered review to live streaming. Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$. A separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.151\text{--}0.152$ for TimesNet and TranAD, and $0.082\text{--}0.097$ for DCdetector, NCAD, and Anomaly Transformer). Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

Across target horizons $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ on five exploratory streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)), both panels exceed 1D Multi-Horizon SPOT at every $S$ while keeping $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}} \le 3.6\%$. Short horizons $S\in\{1,4\}$ yield $2.1\times\text{--}4.3\times$ the scalar causal Point-F1 ($36.7\text{--}44.1$-step delay at $S=4$), whereas buffered Point-F1 peaks at $S\in\{32,64\}$ ($0.290$ and $0.255$). On the 19 development streams (Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)), post-hoc $f^*=1\%$ ER reaches $56.0\%\text{--}63.1\%$ for the panels versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet, though this ranking advantage over scalar JEPAs does not carry over to the untouched archive. On NASA in-range contextual faults \cite{Hundman2018b}, \emph{Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reach $87.5\%$ and $77.5\%$ ER at $1.3\%\text{--}2.2\%$ FPR versus $62.5\%$ at $41.5\%$ FPR for TimesNet. Under Wu and Keogh's benchmark-flaw controls \cite{wu2023flawed} ($19$ dev streams, $3$ seeds, random interior onsets), a $\max_c|x_c|$ one-liner fails on marginal-preserving faults ($0.008\text{--}0.009$ F1). \emph{Lite} catches $50.9\%$ of \emph{cycle-swap} ($0.030$ F1) and $59.6\%$ of \emph{phase-desync} faults ($0.048$ F1) at $1.6\%$ FPR, outperforming Multi-Horizon 1D ($28.1\%$ and $42.1\%$ ER). Adding uncalibrated Gaussian noise ($\sigma\in\{0.15,0.30\}$) preserves \emph{Lite} recall ($61.4\%\text{--}66.7\%$) while raising clean FPR to $6.9\%\text{--}14.3\%$ versus $1.9\%\text{--}6.2\%$ for 1D SPOT.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults that evolve much slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ Point-F1 across all $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). When context and target windows ride a slow ramp together, their latent representations shift in tandem and predictive residuals cancel. Across linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$ ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points). Testing six onset profiles on clean multivariate segments ($3$ seeds: abrupt step, $L\in\{64,256,1024\}$ ramps, exponential degradation $1-e^{-t/512}$, and $0.001\sigma$/step accumulating bias) confirms this mechanism. From abrupt steps to accumulating bias, mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ to $586.7$ steps ($433.9$ steps on exponential degradation) as context-to-target displacement $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened residual RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

To test whether a long-term reference closes this blind spot without altering \emph{Lite} weights, we pair the panel with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ context features and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. On the $14$ real GHL ramp streams ($3$ seeds), this frozen-reference monitor lifts causal ER from $0.8\%$ (\emph{Lite} alone, $0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a two-timescale union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, Point-F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Because GHL's nominal baselines keep wandering after fault episodes, however, uncompensated test FPR rises to $74.4\%$. Sliding-window prediction thus rejects slow nominal drift at the cost of missing slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift catches slow ramps at the cost of flagging benign baseline wander.

Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}--\ref{tab:tail_audit} evaluate the disjoint five-block archive across four explicit regimes: \emph{autonomous} (healthy-only thresholds), \emph{matched calibration rate}, \emph{post-hoc} test-FPR diagnostic control, and \emph{buffered} review:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operating-point gain vs.\ post-hoc ranking (Tables~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, \ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)):} Across the 118 untouched streams, autonomous \emph{Lite} raises live causal ER from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($+12.1$ points, cluster CI $[+3.7,+24.3]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.0039$, sign-flip BH $p\le 0.016$) and cuts mean delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps. It trades higher mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$) while lifting pooled causal Point-F1 from $0.076$ to $0.086$ (BH $p=8.8\times 10^{-4}$, cluster CI $[-0.026,+0.069]$). This gain comes from streams with sufficient calibration history ($N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$, $n=59$), where Point-F1 doubles from $0.040$ to $0.085$ ($p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$, $+20.4$ ER points), whereas 1D SPOT leads when $N_{\text{cal}}<500$ ($0.112$ vs.\ $0.088$). Under buffered review, \emph{Lite} lifts ER ($59.0\%\to 67.7\%$) and Point-F1 ($0.143\to 0.158$) while cutting $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ ($19.5\%\to 13.3\%$). At Lite's low $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alarm rate (median $0.25\%$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)), the quorum catches $65.0\%$ of episodes versus $52.6\%$ for 1D and $58.1\%\text{--}61.1\%$ for Fisher, top-$k$, and max-$z$ pooling. At looser healthy rates ($0.5\%\text{--}5\%$, Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(b)) simpler pools converge, and at post-hoc matched test FPR ($f=1\%$), 1D leads Point-F1 ($0.084$ vs.\ $0.049$). The panel's value therefore lies strictly in its label-free quorum operating point rather than superior score ranking.
    \item \emph{Cross-dataset and LODO generalization (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}):} Because weights fit per stream while hyperparameters transfer zero-tune across $K\in[9,55]$, Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a) tests whether gains rely on one benchmark family. \emph{Lite} improves causal ER over 1D in all $9/9$ untouched datasets ($+1.2$ points on SMAP up to $+26.4\text{--}28.9$ on Exathlon, OPPORTUNITY, and Daphnet) and shortens delay on $6/8$ multi-step cohorts ($41$ vs.\ $289$ steps on Exathlon). Across all $12$ LODO folds of the 141-stream archive (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(b)), causal ER gain stays strictly positive in $12/12$ folds ($+8.9$ to $+17.9$ points, $\max p=0.0027$), peaking when all $47$ SMAP streams are held out ($+17.9$ ER points, $+0.035$ Point-F1). Dropping Exathlon or Daphnet shifts unweighted mean $\Delta\text{Pt-F1}$ slightly negative ($-0.007$ and $-0.002$) because 1D's lower FPR favors step precision on SMAP's long faults ($0.131$ vs.\ $0.088$), even as paired Wilcoxon tests stay favorable ($p=0.055$ and $0.036$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams satisfy $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ (median $0.93\%$; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)). However, $14$ streams exceed $5\%$ and push the $95$th percentile to $64.7\%$, concentrated in Spark workload switches on Exathlon, gait-trial changes on Daphnet, and three command-mode shifts on SMAP. Three mechanisms explain these tail failures:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration failure vs.\ concept drift (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Interleaving autocorrelation-preserving blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and serial dependence while removing chronological drift. Across all 118 untouched streams ($3$-seed audit), block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ drops from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ and stays $\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams in Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a) ($1.00 \to 0.00$ on \texttt{SMAP G-6}; $0.75 \to 0.03$ on \texttt{Exathlon 10\_2}). Excess false alarms are thus driven $92.3\%$ by post-calibration \emph{concept drift} rather than tail estimation error.
    \item \emph{Internal drift drivers:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) have nearly the same median $N_{\text{eff}}$ as stable streams ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$; Spearman $\rho=-0.01$) and similar post-LayerNorm latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$). Instead, they suffer a $3.1\times$ larger shift in stabilized residual covariance ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ relative Frobenius norm). This covariance shift inflates marginal dial trip rates ($13.4\times$ vs.\ $1.5\times$ calibration rate) and quorum variance ($4.8\times$ vs.\ $1.4\times$), which quorum $D_{\text{KS}}$ tracks closely ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Controlled healthy regime-shift stress test:} Injecting fault-free operating changes halfway through clean nominal segments on the 19 development streams ($3$ seeds) shows which shifts self-heal. Moderate amplitude scaling ($1.5\times, 2.0\times$ on $25\%$ of channels) barely perturbs \emph{Lite} ($2.0\%$ and $3.3\%$ post-shift FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) because terminal LayerNorm absorbs scale changes, while $45^\circ$ correlation rotation raises FPR to $6.8\%$ ($D_{\text{KS}}=0.43$). By contrast, persistent mean steps ($+1\sigma, +2\sigma$), alternating duty cycles, and $1.5\sigma$ baseline walks do \emph{not} self-settle after $W=320$ steps because input standardization and $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ remain anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Across those four mean-shift regimes, steady-state FPR stays high at $14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ for \emph{Lite} and $10.7\%\text{--}33.8\%$ for Multi-Horizon 1D ($D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$). Triggering live recalibration on the first $400$ post-shift windows when $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ restores mean-step FPR to $4.8\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability of Coordinate and Quorum Calibration}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Serial dependence ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ median $7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams below $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)\ge 1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). No distribution-free finite-sample tail bound can therefore be claimed from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone. We retain $q_{0.995}$ ($\times 1.12$, floor $3.5$) for two empirical reasons. First, evaluating Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$ resamples of block length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) across all 118 untouched streams shows that `MBB-Median` yields virtually identical thresholds and false-alarm tails ($0.092$ F1, $65.2\%$ ER, $0.92\%$ median and $62.7\%$ p95 $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ and $60.3\%$ deployed in Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). Conservative `MBB-UCB90` and binomial $N_{\text{eff}}$ upper bounds trim p95 only to $42\%\text{--}47.7\%$ while sacrificing $8\text{--}17$ ER points, and thinning $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ by $\tau_{\text{int}}$ worsens mean FPR to $10.4\%$. Second, our five-step protocol mitigates calibration uncertainty without test labels. Step~(1) fits weights, $\mathbf{\Omega}$, and (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$. Step~(2) requires $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$; routing shorter streams to 1D SPOT lifts pooled causal F1 to $0.095$, while two-fold cross-fitting on $T_{\text{nom}}<2{,}500$ cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$. Steps~(3)--(4) audit frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and reject autonomous deployment when $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$ ($50$ passing streams reach $0.7\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ and $0.076$ vs.\ $0.039$ F1 over 1D, $p=3.5\times 10^{-4}$), while Step~(5) monitors live $D_{\text{KS}}$ for recalibration.
