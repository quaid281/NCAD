\subsection{Design-Stage Benchmark, Attribution Regimes, and Horizons}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}
\label{subsec:horizon_latency}
\label{subsec:matched_operating}
Comparing Panels~(a) and (b) of Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} explains why scalar scores degrade when moving from buffered review to live streaming. Buffered evaluation averages overlapping windows across a fault, whereas causal streaming scores a single right-edge step where drifting coordinates inflate a shared SPOT threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). On the 19 non-GHL development streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} leads buffered Point-F1 ($0.253$ vs.\ $0.232$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and dataset-macro F1 ($0.172$ vs.\ $0.130$) while cutting buffered $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ from $11.5\%$ to $4.7\%$. A separate 130-stream single-seed buffered screen provides external context only (TS-JEPA $0.186$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.151\text{--}0.152$ for TimesNet and TranAD, and $0.082\text{--}0.097$ for DCdetector, NCAD, and Anomaly Transformer). Against a rate-matched random alerter $\text{ER}_{\text{rand}}(p) = E^{-1}\sum_{e=1}^E [1-(1-p)^{L_e}]$, the panels' $\le 1.3\%$ causal FPR corresponds to $28.1\%$ chance recall versus $60.0\%$ at TimesNet's $24.3\%$ FPR.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Fault Onset Rate and Progressive Degradation}
\label{subsec:slow_ramps}
A $C=256$-step sliding encoder is structurally blind to faults that evolve much slower than its window ($0.000\text{--}0.001$ Point-F1 across all $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). When context and target windows ride a slow ramp together, their latent representations shift in tandem and predictive residuals cancel. Across linear ramps of amplitude $a\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\sigma$ and duration $L\in\{64,256,1024,4096\}$, chance-corrected recall drops from $+5.7\text{--}7.7$ points at $L=64$ to $+0.5\text{--}2.5$ points at $L=C$ and vanishes for $L\ge 4C$ ($[-4.8,+0.8]$ points). Testing six onset profiles on clean multivariate segments ($3$ seeds: abrupt step, $L\in\{64,256,1024\}$ ramps, exponential degradation $1-e^{-t/512}$, and $0.001\sigma$/step accumulating bias) confirms this mechanism. From abrupt steps to accumulating bias, mean \emph{Lite} delay grows nearly $20\times$ from $29.3$ to $586.7$ steps ($433.9$ steps on exponential degradation) as context-to-target displacement $\|z_{\text{tgt}}-z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2$ contracts ($8.59 \to 8.38$) and whitened residual RMS drops ($2.72 \to 2.60$).

To test whether a long-term reference closes this blind spot without altering \emph{Lite} weights, we pair the panel with a causal exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA, $\alpha=0.02$) Mahalanobis drift monitor anchored to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ context features and calibrated on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. On the $14$ real GHL ramp streams ($3$ seeds), this frozen-reference monitor lifts causal ER from $0.8\%$ (\emph{Lite} alone, $0.25\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) to $57.7\%$, and to $58.5\%$ in a two-timescale union hybrid ($129.7$-step delay, $1.8\%\text{--}2.0\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, Point-F1 $0.0004 \to 0.015$). Because GHL's nominal baselines keep wandering after fault episodes, however, uncompensated test FPR rises to $74.4\%$. Sliding-window prediction thus rejects slow nominal drift at the cost of missing slow ramps, whereas frozen-reference drift catches slow ramps at the cost of flagging benign baseline wander.

\subsection{Latent Alarm Decomposition and Post-Alarm Sensor Attribution}
\label{subsec:interpretability}
Because each breaker indexes a learned latent coordinate rather than a physical sensor, the 128-dial panel provides \emph{latent alarm decomposition} across horizons ($S_1{=}16, S_2{=}32, S_3{=}64$) and drift $|\bar{e}_c|$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:panel_interpretability}). On abrupt \texttt{GECCO} faults, all four groups surge to $4.8{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ while 1D SPOT stays at $\le 0.74{\times}$. On contextual \texttt{SMAP A-7} faults inside $\pm 2.5\sigma$, $S_2, S_3$ and drift stack to $2.02{\times}$ $K_{\text{Lite}}$ (vs.\ $\le 0.89{\times}$). On slow \texttt{GHL-44} ramps ($860$ steps), $N_{128}$ stays $\le 2 < K_{\text{Lite}}$ while frozen-reference drift crosses $1{\times}$ mid-ramp.

Post-alarm masking maps alarms back to physical sensors. Keeping detector weights fixed, replacing channel $c\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ with its healthy mean across $K$ batched forward passes on alarming windows ($\hat{y}(\tau)=1$) ranks channels by dial-count drop $\Delta N_{128}^{(-c)}(\tau)$. Across $14$ multi-channel dev streams under $+2.5\sigma$ sensor-subset shifts, this leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) screen reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 accuracy ($13.9\%$ random) and $92.9\%$ Top-3 accuracy ($38.0\%$ random). Sub-quorum single-sensor flatlines remain limited by $1/K$ latent dilution.

\subsection{Characterization of False-Alarm Failure Modes Under Regime Shifts}
\label{subsec:regime_shift_modes}
Concept drift drives tail false alarms. Across the untouched archive, $88.1\%$ of streams ($104/118$) keep $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($0.93\%$ median; Table~\ref{tab:dataset_lodo_audit}(a)), while $14$ streams with Spark, gait, or command-mode switches push p95 to $64.7\%$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Calibration vs.\ concept drift (Table~\ref{tab:tail_audit}(a)):} Shuffling blocks of length $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$ across $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ preserves $N_{\text{cal}}$ and serial dependence while erasing chronological drift. Across $n=118$, block-permuted $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ falls from $7.44\%$ to $0.57\%$ ($\le 3\%$ on all ten worst streams), blaming post-calibration drift for $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms.
    \item \emph{Internal drift drivers:} High-FPR streams ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}>5\%$) match stable streams in median $N_{\text{eff}}$ ($65.2$ vs.\ $66.5$) and latent mean shift ($0.67$ vs.\ $0.56$). They diverge through a $3.1\times$ larger stabilized residual-covariance shift ($2.05$ vs.\ $0.66$ Frobenius norm), which inflates quorum variance ($4.8\times$) and drives $D_{\text{KS}}$ ($\rho=0.49\text{--}0.63$, $p\le 1.6{\times}10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Regime shifts and quarantined recalibration:} In fault-free dev stress tests ($3$ seeds), terminal LayerNorm absorbs $1.5\times\text{--}2.0\times$ scaling ($2.0\%\text{--}3.3\%$ FPR vs.\ $1.3\%$ clean) and $45^\circ$ rotations stay moderate ($6.8\%$), whereas mean steps, duty cycles, and baseline walks fail to self-settle ($14.6\%\text{--}32.0\%$ FPR; $D_{\text{KS}}\in[0.43,0.69]$). Blindly auto-recalibrating on $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$, however, absorbs persistent faults: fitting $400$ post-shift windows during a persistent $+2.5\sigma$ fault cuts post-buffer recall to $9.1\%$. Our conditional policy therefore flags $D_{\text{KS}}\ge 0.50$ under frozen weights, quarantines candidate windows, and permits threshold updates only after operator or control-state confirmation ($31.9\%$ unconfirmed recall preserved; $4.8\%$ FPR on confirmed shifts).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finite-Sample Reliability and Two-Stage Deployment Funnel}
\label{subsec:finite_sample_calibration}
Finite histories lack distribution-free tail guarantees. Serial dependence (median $\tau_{\text{int}}=7.5$, up to $206$) leaves $105/118$ untouched streams with $N_{\text{eff}}(1-0.995)<1$ (median $N_{\text{eff}}=65$, or $0.325$ expected independent exceedances above $q_{0.995}$). Resampling finite blocks via Moving-Block Bootstrap (MBB, $B=400$, block $\lceil 2\tau_{\text{int}}\rceil$) tests dependence sensitivity rather than generating unobserved extremes. On an independent $3$-seed audit across $n=118$, `MBB-Median` matches that audit's unbootstrapped quorum ($0.92\%$ vs.\ $0.88\%$ median FPR; $62.7\%$ vs.\ $60.3\%$ p95; $0.092$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1), matching the primary run's $0.93\%$ median and $64.7\%$ p95 (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(a)). Conservative `MBB-UCB90` and $N_{\text{eff}}$ bounds cost $8\text{--}17$ ER points, while thinning raises mean FPR to $10.4\%$.

Autonomous operation relies on a two-stage acceptance funnel (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)). After disjoint $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}},\mathbf{X}_q)$ isolation, requiring empirical $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ retains $59/118$ ($50.0\%$) streams (cross-fitting below $2{,}500$ steps cuts $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $18.8\%$ to $4.4\%$). Auditing frozen $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ accepts $52$ streams ($44.1\%$ of $118$; $0.9\%$ mean $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$, $0.076$ vs.\ $0.034$ F1 over 1D, $p=3.2{\times}10^{-5}$) and rejects $7$ ($35$ pass optional $N_{\text{eff}}\ge 50$). Routing all $66$ non-accepted streams to 1D SPOT produces a $118$-stream hybrid ($0.094$ F1, $60.9\%$ ER, $4.6\%$ $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$) supervised by quarantined $D_{\text{KS}}$ monitoring.
