\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports empirical evaluations and dynamical diagnostics across 33 matched continuous telemetry streams spanning seven physical sensing domains under the held-out calibration protocol. We evaluate whether forward prediction in latent representation space produces residual distributions that are better conditioned for automated thresholding, investigate the empirical behavior of physics-inspired regularizers against unconstrained latent controls, analyze operational streaming latency, and quantify the reliability of extreme-value threshold calibration on real telemetry.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance on Continuous Telemetry}
\label{subsec:core_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} presents the empirical comparison across 33 continuous telemetry streams evaluated under the held-out calibration protocol. Models are trained on the first 65\% of each nominal sequence, and extreme-value thresholds (SPOT, $u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$) are calibrated on a strictly held-out 17.5\% nominal segment, with out-of-sample nominal False-Positive Rate (Held-Out FPR) evaluated on the remaining nominal 17.5\% segment (isolated by a 64-step gap). Figure~\ref{fig:hero_sota_comparison} illustrates the comparative profile across strict Point-F1, continuous ranking (PR-AUC), and held-out nominal FPR.

Several primary empirical findings emerge from Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}:

\noindent\textbf{False Alarm Suppression on Held-Out Telemetry:} The primary operational advantage of latent-space predictive architectures is a substantial reduction in nominal false alarms. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA achieves an out-of-sample nominal FPR of $0.066$, Operator-Entropy achieves $0.079$, and Reynolds-Stress achieves $0.076$, compared to $0.297$ for TimesNet and $0.309$ for TranAD. Across all 33 streams (seeds averaged), paired difference tests establish a statistically significant reduction in held-out FPR: TS-JEPA achieves a mean paired FPR reduction of $-0.154$ against TimesNet ($95\%$ bootstrap confidence interval $[-0.249, -0.071]$, Wilcoxon signed-rank $p=0.008$) and $-0.157$ against TranAD ($[-0.247, -0.082]$, $p=0.001$). Similarly, Operator-Entropy achieves a paired FPR reduction of $-0.148$ against TimesNet ($[-0.236, -0.069]$, $p=0.001$) and $-0.151$ against TranAD ($[-0.250, -0.065]$, $p=0.003$). Predicting residuals in contracted latent representations avoids observation-level high-frequency noise, yielding better-conditioned score distributions.

\noindent\textbf{Point-F1 and Continuous Ranking (PR-AUC) Parity:} While latent prediction suppresses nominal false alarms, it does not provide a statistically distinguishable improvement in overall detection fidelity. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TS-JEPA achieves a stream-macro Point-F1 of $0.254$ and PR-AUC of $0.333$ (dataset-macro Point-F1 of $0.153$), compared to $0.207$ Point-F1 and $0.336$ PR-AUC for TimesNet (dataset-macro Point-F1 of $0.104$), and $0.131$ Point-F1 and $0.291$ PR-AUC for TranAD (dataset-macro Point-F1 of $0.070$). Across all 33 streams, paired differences between TS-JEPA and TimesNet are $+0.018$ in Point-F1 ($95\%$ CI $[-0.052, +0.089]$, $p=0.55$) and $+0.002$ in PR-AUC ($[-0.098, +0.097]$, $p=0.30$). Against TranAD, the paired difference is $+0.062$ in Point-F1 ($[-0.012, +0.150]$, $p=0.94$) and $+0.027$ in PR-AUC ($[-0.075, +0.135]$, $p=0.30$). Thus, continuous ranking fidelity and calibrated Point-F1 remain statistically comparable between unconstrained latent prediction and observation-space reconstructive baselines.

\noindent\textbf{Event Recall and Detection Conservatism:} The lower false-positive rate of latent predictive models is accompanied by a reduction in event recall. On the 19 non-GHL streams, TimesNet captures $58.6\%$ of ground-truth anomaly events, and TranAD captures $48.1\%$, whereas TS-JEPA captures $49.8\%$, Reynolds-Stress captures $39.8\%$, and Potential-Flow captures $33.1\%$. Latent-space residuals act as a more conservative detector: by filtering high-frequency variations, subtle anomaly onsets that do not produce large latent coordinate shifts are missed, suppressing false alarms at the expense of lower event sensitivity.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-Specific Telemetry Dynamics:} Tables~\ref{tab:all_datasets} and~\ref{tab:dataset_prauc} report the per-dataset breakdown of unadjusted Point-F1 and PR-AUC across the seven evaluated benchmarks. Relative performance depends on the underlying physical sensing regime:

    \item \textbf{Spacecraft Telemetry (NASA MSL and SMAP):} On spacecraft telemetry, latent prediction leads. On MSL (4 streams), Operator-Entropy ($0.281$ F1, $0.445$ PR-AUC) and TS-JEPA ($0.257$ F1, $0.427$ PR-AUC) outperform TimesNet ($0.159$ F1, $0.310$ PR-AUC).

In GHL, anomalies consist of subtle actuator drifts amidst stationary background telemetry; in Metro, extreme traffic volume surges produce out-of-distribution residuals that evade static EVT bounds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluating Physical Inductive Priors: Hypothesis Testing and Null Results}
\label{subsec:eval_physics_priors}

A central hypothesis evaluated in this investigation is whether incorporating physics-inspired inductive regularizers into latent representations (Koopman spectral entropy, Reynolds stress closure, and potential-flow curl-free dynamics) improves anomaly detection performance over an unconstrained latent predictive architecture (\texttt{TS-JEPA}).

The empirical data across all 33 streams refute this hypothesis:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy vs. TS-JEPA:} Across all 33 streams (seeds averaged), Operator-Entropy yields a mean paired difference of $-0.007$ in Point-F1 ($95\%$ bootstrap CI $[-0.028, +0.012]$, Wilcoxon $p=0.71$) and $+0.008$ in PR-AUC ($[-0.013, +0.034]$, $p=0.28$). Held-out nominal FPR difference is $+0.006$ ($[-0.024, +0.042]$, $p=0.39$). Across 33 streams, Operator-Entropy outperforms TS-JEPA in Point-F1 on only 9 streams.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress vs. TS-JEPA:} Reynolds-Stress yields a paired difference of $-0.026$ in Point-F1 ($[-0.070, +0.004]$, $p=0.33$) and a statistically significant reduction in PR-AUC ($-0.009$, $[-0.024, +0.007]$, $p=0.013$). Held-out FPR difference is $+0.003$ ($[-0.026, +0.040]$, $p=0.94$). Reynolds-Stress leads TS-JEPA in F1 on 11 of 33 streams.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow vs. TS-JEPA:} Potential-Flow performs significantly worse than unconstrained TS-JEPA across both calibrated Point-F1 (paired difference $-0.091$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.178, -0.019]$, $p=0.020$) and continuous ranking PR-AUC (paired difference $-0.076$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.142, -0.019]$, $p=0.039$). Potential-Flow outperforms TS-JEPA on only 5 of 33 streams.
\end{enumerate}

These findings are corroborated by the matched-capacity regularizer controls reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a). When the underlying neural architecture is held fixed on the seven-stream multi-seed subset:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Disabling the spectral entropy loss ($\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$) changes Point-F1 by $+0.001$ ($[-0.006, +0.009]$, $p=1.00$).
    \item Disabling the Reynolds stress loss ($\alpha_{\text{stress}}=0$), or scoring via predictive mean error alone, produces differences that do not exceed seed noise ($p \ge 0.375$).
    \item Disabling Grassmannian regime routing in Potential-Flow changes Point-F1 by $+0.006$ ($[-0.022, +0.032]$, $p=0.58$).
\end{itemize}
We conclude that the physical regularizers do not provide a measurable anomaly detection advantage over unconstrained latent prediction. Whatever performance differences exist between models arise from shared representation conditioning rather than the physical loss terms.

Strictly causal latent scoring therefore represents an operational choice that suppresses false alarms at the cost of lower point-level F1 and delayed event recall.

\subsection{Automated Threshold Calibration Realities on Non-Stationary Telemetry}
\label{subsec:calibration_realities}

A critical requirement for autonomous telemetry monitoring is whether automated extreme-value thresholding adheres to theoretical design risk parameters. When practitioners specify a design risk of $q=10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), the operational expectation is that nominal false alarms will occur at or below this frequency.

\input{sections/tab_calibrator_comparison.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} reports a controlled comparison of threshold calibrators evaluated on identical score streams across the 33 continuous telemetry streams under buffered inference. The empirical results demonstrate several key realities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Systematic Design-Risk Overshoot:} Extreme-value thresholding via SPOT ($u=0.98, q=10^{-3}$) systematically fails to achieve its design risk on out-of-sample nominal telemetry. Across all 33 streams, empirical nominal FPR averages $5.17\%$ for TS-JEPA, $6.15\%$ for Operator-Entropy, $23.88\%$ for TimesNet, and $24.59\%$ for TranAD, overshooting the theoretical $0.1\%$ target by $50$-fold to $245$-fold.
    \item \textbf{High Failure Rate Across Streams:} The 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals for held-out nominal FPR strictly exclude the theoretical design risk $q=10^{-3}$ in $57\%$ of TS-JEPA runs, $65\%$ of Operator-Entropy runs, $87\%$ of TimesNet runs, and $84\%$ of TranAD runs. Real-world telemetry exhibits non-stationary baseline drift and seasonal fluctuations that violate the stationary tail assumptions of Extreme Value Theory.
    \item \textbf{Parity with Simple Empirical Quantiles:} Simple non-parametric quantile rules match or exceed the performance of SPOT. On TS-JEPA, taking the validation maximum yields an empirical held-out FPR of $0.0524$ and Point-F1 of $0.1922$, while an empirical 99.5th percentile rule yields an FPR of $0.0657$ and Point-F1 of $0.1942$. Parametric GPD tail modeling provides no demonstrable benefit over simple empirical percentiles on these benchmarks.
    \item \textbf{Failure of Kurtosis-Adaptive Calibration:} The kurtosis-adaptive multiplier rule ($\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau(1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh\gamma_2)$) substantially degrades nominal calibration. On TS-JEPA, it inflates held-out nominal FPR from $0.0517$ to $0.1880$ (with CI excluding $q$ rising from $57\%$ to $84\%$), while yielding an indistinguishable Point-F1 ($0.1947$ vs.\ $0.1929$). On Operator-Entropy, it raises nominal FPR from $0.0615$ to $0.1098$. Excess kurtosis measured in training splits does not accurately forecast non-stationary tail shifts on out-of-sample telemetry.
\end{enumerate}
