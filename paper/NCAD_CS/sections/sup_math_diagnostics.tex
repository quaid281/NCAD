\section{Mathematical Diagnostics of Physical Regularizers}
\label{sup:math_diagnostics}

To understand why the proposed regularizers enforce their intended mathematical properties yet fail to improve downstream anomaly detection, we conduct targeted dynamical diagnostic experiments.

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy: Anti-Collapse Spectral Rank Preservation}

Table~\ref{tab:operator_entropy_validation} evaluates effective operator rank via the RankMe metric ($\exp(\mathcal{H})$ where $\mathcal{H}$ is the spectral entropy of singular values), representation covariance conditioning, and downstream calibrated Point-F1 across five telemetry streams.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:operator_entropy_validation}, unconstrained Vanilla TS-JEPA and VICReg collapse across $7\text{--}8$ singular modes, yielding an effective operator rank of $24.3\text{--}24.8$. Operator-Entropy JEPA achieves an effective operator rank of $31.996$ out of $32.0$, confirming that the regularizer prevents modal collapse. However, full operator rank alone does not prevent representation collapse: the single-operator penalty acts exclusively on $\mathbf{A}$, allowing the encoder to map observations into a low-dimensional subspace ($\text{Tr}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_Z) = 0.0002$) while $\mathbf{A}$ maintains full rank off-manifold.

\subsubsection{Resolution of Operator-Representation Decoupling via Dual Spectral Entropy and Lyapunov Invariance}
\label{sup:opentropy_ablation}

To resolve this decoupling, we evaluate two structural modifications: (1) dual spectral entropy coupling ($\alpha_Z$), which simultaneously penalizes non-uniformity in the representation covariance eigenspectrum $\mathbf{\Sigma}_Z$, and (2) a Discrete Lyapunov Invariance Defect ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lyapunov}} = \frac{1}{D} \left\| \mathrm{Cov}(z_{\text{pred}}) - \mathrm{Cov}(z_{\text{tgt}}) \right\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
which penalizes rotational drift between predicted and target representation covariance structures. In addition, we evaluate the Cayley orthogonal-dissipative parameterization $W = (I + S + D)^{-1}(I - S - D)$ ($S = -S^T, D \succeq 0$), enforcing contractivity ($\|W\|_2 \le 1.0$) by construction without soft energy penalties.

Coupling the Discrete Lyapunov Invariance Defect aligns operator rotation with representation covariance, reducing nominal FPR by $35\%$ ($0.0212$) and cutting mean event detection latency from $112.0$ to $30.1$ steps. Decoupling representation covariance entropy ($\alpha_Z=0$) while maintaining the Lyapunov defect ($\beta_{\text{Lyap}}=0.02$) and excising inert math layers (OpEntropy-Boosted) yields the highest event recall ($0.4449$, a $+33.3\%$ relative gain over Historical OpEntropy) at reduced nominal FPR ($0.0206$ vs.\ $0.0241$), though Point-F1 ($0.0498$ vs.\ $0.0617$) remains lower than unconstrained TS-JEPA. Table~\ref{tab:potential_flow_validation} confirms that pure curl-free potential flow is mathematically incapable of representing closed circulating orbits, yielding poor detection on Van der Pol ($0.3548$ Point-F1 vs.\ $0.7574$ for Helmholtz JEPA). Because physical telemetry often exhibits non-conservative, cyclic behavior (e.g., diurnal cycles, rotational turbines), enforcing irrotational dynamics severely penalizes normal circulating trajectories, explaining Potential-Flow's poor benchmark performance ($0.065$ dataset-macro F1 in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}). Numerical Jacobian diagnostics show that while the raw spatial gradient field is curl-free (antisymmetric fraction $< 10^{-7}$), enforcing the spherical tangent projection introduces a $15.0\%\text{--}15.7\%$ antisymmetric curl component ($0.150$ on SMAP \texttt{P-3}, $0.157$ on MSL \texttt{M-1}). Tangent projection breaks the curl-free invariant without restoring sufficient circular capacity to track cyclic trajectories.


\subsubsection{Multiscale Front-End: Dyadic-Shell Construction}
\label{sup:frontend_construction}
The encoder front-end constructs $J{=}4$ dyadic shells on an $N_{\text{fft}}=256$ real-FFT grid ($F=129$ non-negative bins $\omega_m = m/N_{\text{fft}}$). Dyadic center frequencies sit at $c_j = 2^{j-J+1}$, $j \in \{0,1,2,3\}$. Unnormalized profiles combine a half-Gaussian anchored at DC ($j=0$), log-frequency octave bells $\tilde{\Psi}_j(\omega_m) = \exp(-(\log_2 \omega_m - \log_2 c_j)^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ with bandwidth $\sigma = 0.6$ ($j \in \{1,2\}$), and a plateau toward Nyquist ($j=J-1$), normalized bin-wise into an exact partition of unity:
\begin{align}
    \Psi_j[\omega_m] &= \frac{\tilde{\Psi}_j(\omega_m)}{\sum_{l=0}^{J-1} \tilde{\Psi}_l(\omega_m)}, \\
    \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} \Psi_j[\omega_m] &\equiv 1.0. \nonumber
\end{align}
The split is applied as $\mathbf{x}_j = \mathrm{irfft}(\Psi_j \odot \mathrm{rfft}(\mathbf{x}))$ (PoU deviation $<1.2\times10^{-7}$, reconstruction error $<5\times10^{-8}$). Each shell feeds a full (non-depthwise) causal 1D convolution with kernels $k \in \{15,9,5,3\}$ and causal padding $p=k-1$, recombined by a softmax router over pooled per-shell energies, a $1{\times}1$ projection, residual scaling $\gamma=0.1$, and LayerNorm. Because shells overlap smoothly, band energies are correlated ($\sum_j \|\mathbf{x}_j\|^2$ recovers $\approx 70\%$ of signal energy) and the finest shell concentrates near Nyquist; per-band in-octave spectral mass is $\{0.50,0.33,0.20,{\approx}1.00\}$ versus $\{0.78,0.50,0.55,0.82\}$ (Haar) and $\{0.86,0.63,0.64,0.87\}$ (DB4) (filterbank frequency diagnostics).
