\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\emph{Multivariate Time-Series Anomaly Detection and Benchmarks:} Most recent deep detectors reconstruct or forecast raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error. Examples include TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, frequency-period TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, graph-based CST-GL \cite{zheng2024cstgl}, and frequency-patching CATCH \cite{wu2025catch}. Time-series foundation models such as MOMENT \cite{goswami2024moment} and DADA \cite{shentu2025dada} extend reconstruction across domains via zero-shot pretraining. Our earlier autoencoder detectors paired reconstruction with LSTM sequence modeling \cite{akbarian2023aelstm} and with K-means clustering of latent codes for space operations \cite{akbarian2024aekmeans}. Across dozens of noisy channels, however, raw sensor jitter and benign baseline shifts still produce heavy-tailed healthy spikes \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}. Benchmark audits \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, liu2024tsbad, zhou2026mtsbench} also show that point-adjusted scores mask heavy false-alarm burdens. We therefore evaluate every step without point adjustment.

\noindent\emph{Self-Supervised Representation Learning and Temporal JEPAs:} Self-supervised encoders skip raw waveform decoding \cite{zhang2023self}. Contrastive detectors such as TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} learn by contrasting real windows against synthetic corruptions or augmented views. JEPAs \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead forecast future latent summaries directly from recent context. Recent temporal JEPAs \cite{ennadir2025tsjepa} adapt this approach to time series while curbing representation collapse through variance-covariance regularization \cite{bardes2021vicreg, garrido2023rankme}. Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse all coordinates into one scalar threshold. One noisy coordinate can then blind the monitor.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamics Machine Learning vs.\ Residual Diagnostics:} Machine learning in fluid mechanics pairs Reynolds-averaged turbulence modeling with reduced-order representations to separate mean flow, coherent structures, and background fluctuations \cite{vinuesa2022enhancing}. Directly imposing physics-informed neural network differential loss penalties during training creates unbalanced convergence rates \cite{wang2022pinns} and ill-conditioned loss landscapes \cite{rathore2024challenges}, and uncalibrated telemetry lacks known governing equations. We omit physics loss penalties during training. Fluid-mechanics diagnostics enter strictly as post-hoc algebra on frozen multi-horizon residuals.

\noindent\emph{Conformal Calibration, Multiple Testing, and Drift:} Recent distribution-free methods adapt thresholds under temporal dependence and distribution shift via weighted quantiles \cite{barber2023conformal}, sequential conditional quantiles \cite{xu2023spci}, and online control loops \cite{angelopoulos2023conformalpid}. Conformal p-value tests also control the false discovery rate (FDR) across independent samples \cite{bates2023testing}, while Multivariate Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (MEWMA) and Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) charts monitor mean shifts. On short, autocorrelated prefixes with $N_{\text{cal}}$ calibration windows, strict per-coordinate multiplicity corrections hit a discrete $(N_{\text{cal}}+1)^{-1}$ floor, and unguarded online adaptation can absorb real faults (Sections~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations} and \ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}). Our coordinate quorum, pre-deployment readiness check, and mode-confirmed quarantine mitigate both issues during label-free deployment.
