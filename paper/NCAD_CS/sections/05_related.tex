\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Observation-Space Forecasting and Reconstructive Detectors:} Deep time-series anomaly detectors predominantly operate in raw observation space by training a network to forecast future sensor readings or reconstruct input windows, flagging timesteps where the point-level observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ exceeds a threshold. Autoregressive long short-term memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding \cite{Hundman2018b} model sequential dependencies directly on raw spacecraft telemetry channels. More recent Transformer and spectral reconstructors introduce specialized attention and frequency modules: TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} combines adversarial two-phase training with self-conditioning sub-series attention; Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} contrasts prior Gaussian kernel associations against series-level self-attention to amplify association discrepancies; and TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} folds 1D sequences into 2D tensors along dominant Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periods so 2D convolutional kernels can capture intra- and inter-period variations.

Despite strong ranking performance on clean benchmarks, observation-space reconstructors share a structural vulnerability under automated thresholding on noisy multi-sensor arrays. Because point-level losses penalize reconstruction error across every channel equally, the network is forced to model high-frequency stochastic noise, quantization jitter, and background environmental fluctuations. In high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), aggregating raw reconstruction errors across channels accumulates heavy-tailed residual spikes, referred to statistically as \emph{leptokurtic} tails (excess kurtosis $\gamma_2 > 0$), which destabilize extreme-value threshold fitting and inflate false alarms whenever baseline operating conditions shift \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}.

\noindent\textbf{Contrastive and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:} To avoid reconstructing raw sensor noise, self-supervised representation methods map windows into a compact latent embedding space. Contrastive detectors construct positive and negative pairs: TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec} maximizes contextual agreement across hierarchical sub-series; Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} contrasts full window embeddings against context-only embeddings using injected synthetic anomalies; and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector} employs dual patch-wise and in-patch attention branches without a reconstruction loss. However, contrastive classifiers depend on whether synthetic perturbations or patch discrepancies match test-time fault morphology, yielding lower calibrated point detection on diverse continuous telemetry ($0.058$ and $0.070$ macro Point-F1 for NCAD and DCdetector vs.\ $0.160$ for latent predictive scoring across 11 datasets, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)).

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} eliminate both raw observation decoding and synthetic negative pairs. Rather than reconstructing inputs, a JEPA encodes a historical context window into a latent vector $z_{\text{ctx}}$ and predicts the target representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$ of a future window produced by a separate target encoder. Because trivial constant embeddings would zero out latent prediction error, non-contrastive architectures prevent representation collapse either through architectural asymmetry (updating the target encoder via an exponential moving average of online weights \cite{grill2020bootstrap}) or through explicit variance-covariance regularization such as VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}, Barlow Twins \cite{zbontar2021barlow}, and batch whitening \cite{ermolov2021whitening}. While prior time-series representation studies evaluate embeddings via post-hoc threshold sweeps, how latent predictive residuals behave under strictly unsupervised extreme-value threshold calibration remains unexplored.

\noindent\textbf{Koopman Operator Theory and Physics-Inspired Priors:} Multi-sensor telemetry reflects the underlying trajectories of physical dynamical systems \cite{brunton2016discovering}. Neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural} parameterize continuous velocity fields in observation space, whereas Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics} and Hamiltonian or Lagrangian networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian, cranmer2020lagrangian} enforce partial differential conservation laws directly on physical state coordinates. However, rigid differential constraints require known governing equations and suffer from stiff competing gradient pathologies on uncalibrated sensor arrays \cite{wang2021understanding}.

Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral} provides a softer dynamical prior: nonlinear dynamics can be lifted into an observable embedding space where time evolution is governed by a linear operator. Data-driven Koopman networks \cite{brunton2016koopman, lusch2018deep, liu2023koopa} learn neural embeddings jointly with a finite-dimensional transition matrix $\mathbf{K}$. Here we regularize the singular value spectrum of $\mathbf{K}$ via Von Neumann spectral entropy and a smooth Bregman energy barrier, maintaining a strict distinction between \emph{physics-inspired} algebraic priors (which shape abstract learned latent coordinates) and \emph{physics-informed} conservation laws, and testing whether operator conditioning yields downstream detection gains or statistical equivalence relative to an unconstrained predictor.

\noindent\textbf{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration and Evaluation Rigor:} Unsupervised streaming deployment requires converting continuous scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to score exceedances above a high calibration quantile $u$ to extrapolate a threshold at design risk $q$. However, classical extreme-value theory assumes independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples. Continuous telemetry violates this assumption through short-range serial correlation, which causes threshold exceedances to occur in multi-step bursts quantified by the Ferro--Segers extremal index \cite{ferro2003inference}, and through non-stationary regime drift between calibration and test blocks. Concurrently, recent benchmark audits \cite{kim2022towards, wu2023flawed, schmidl2022anomaly} have shown that ``point-adjusted'' F1 (which credits an entire anomaly segment if a single timestep triggers an alarm) severely inflates scores; we therefore evaluate all models using unadjusted Point-F1, PR-AUC, causal vs.\ buffered attribution, and matched realized false-alarm operating curves.
