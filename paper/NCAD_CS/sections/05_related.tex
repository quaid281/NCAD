\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Unsupervised time-series anomaly detection has evolved through reconstructive, contrastive, and predictive paradigms. This section reviews these developments and clearly differentiates TS-JEPA from prior art.

\subsection{Reconstructive Anomaly Detection Paradigms}
The predominant paradigm in time-series anomaly detection relies on learning nominal patterns through reconstruction. Early seminal work by Hundman et al. \cite{Hundman2018b} introduced LSTM-based prediction coupled with non-parametric dynamic thresholding for NASA spacecraft telemetry (SMAP/MSL). While effective for simple univariate deviations, recurrent models struggle to capture multi-scale long-range dependencies \cite{hua2019deep}.

To capture complex multi-scale temporal dependencies, modern deep learning architectures incorporate specialized attention and frequency-domain transformations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}:} Introduces Anomaly-Attention to quantify the \textit{Association Discrepancy} between local prior associations (Gaussian kernel attention) and global series associations (self-attention). While effective for isolated point anomalies, it still couples discrepancy with raw waveform reconstruction.
    \item \textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Decomposes 1D multivariate time series into 2D temporal variations using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periodicity discovery and 2D Inception blocks. However, optimizing point-level 2D reconstruction makes it susceptible to over-fitting high-frequency sensor noise.
    \item \textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Employs a two-phase adversarial Transformer with sub-series attention and focus scoring to amplify subtle reconstruction errors, but remains vulnerable to phase-drift in continuous dynamical systems.
\end{itemize}

Despite architectural advancements, all reconstructive methods share a fundamental vulnerability: \textit{signal-level reconstruction memorization}. Because loss functions optimize point-level $L_1$ or $L_2$ differences in raw signal space, reconstructive networks are biased toward fitting high-frequency stochastic noise rather than learning invariant dynamical laws. Furthermore, during sustained anomalies, corrupted observations contaminate the context window, causing the reconstructor to adapt and reconstruct the anomaly as the "new normal."

\subsection{Contrastive Learning and Representation-Based Detection}
To avoid reconstructing raw noise, contrastive methods learn discriminative representations by separating nominal sequences from anomalous perturbations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}:} Uses Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) trained with Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE). Artificial synthetic anomalies (Gaussian noise, point spikes, trend shifts) are injected into training windows to create negative pairs. However, synthetic injections distort the physical dynamical manifold and fail to generalize to subtle real-world physical faults (e.g., gradual thermal dissipation or gait freezing).
    \item \textbf{DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}:} Replaces reconstruction with multi-scale dual-attention (patch-wise and channel-wise) contrastive representation learning, maximizing cross-scale representation consistency. While DCdetector eliminates artificial injection heuristics, contrastive objectives require careful scale selection and representation pooling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Emerging Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)}
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), originally proposed by LeCun \cite{lecun2022path} and demonstrated in computer vision by I-JEPA \cite{assran2023self} and V-JEPA \cite{bardes2024vjepa}, represent a paradigm shift. Rather than reconstructing raw signals, JEPAs predict the representation of a target observation from a context observation entirely within latent space.

Recently, preliminary exploratory works have investigated JEPA concepts in time-series analysis:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{SC-JEPA \cite{he2026scjepa}:} Introduces a Sequential Codebook JEPA for time-series forecasting. However, SC-JEPA relies on discrete Vector Quantization (VQ) codebooks, converting continuous continuous signals into finite discrete tokens. For continuous dynamical systems governed by smooth trajectories, discrete tokenization introduces severe quantization error and loses fine-grained phase-space geometry.
    \item \textbf{T-SAR-JEPA \cite{woldesenbet2026tsarjepa} and CF-JEPA \cite{lee2026cfjepa}:} Apply joint-embedding objectives to multimodal Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) remote sensing or counterfactual causal graphs. However, they rely on heuristic cosine-distance scoring and lack non-contrastive representation regularization and statistical threshold calibration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Positioning and Novelty of TS-JEPA}
TS-JEPA addresses the limitations of both traditional reconstructive/contrastive methods and early JEPA attempts through four distinct architectural innovations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Continuous Non-Contrastive Latent Manifold:} Unlike SC-JEPA's discrete codebooks or NCAD's synthetic injections, TS-JEPA operates directly in continuous latent space, utilizing the non-contrastive VICReg (Invariance, Variance hinge, Covariance decorrelation) loss \cite{bardes2021vicreg} to enforce complete representation diversity without collapse.
    \item \textbf{Covariance-Whitened (Mahalanobis) Discrepancy Metric:} Rather than unweighted Euclidean or cosine distances, TS-JEPA scores target predictions using the empirical precision tensor $\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}$ estimated from nominal transition residuals, normalizing directional variance across multi-sensor axes.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) Tail Calibration:} In contrast to empirical percentile heuristics or ad-hoc elbow floors, TS-JEPA derives decision thresholds by fitting Generalized Pareto Distributions (GPD) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} to nominal predictive residuals under extreme value theory.
    \item \textbf{Limit-Cycle Attractor Stability:} We demonstrate theoretically and visually that TS-JEPA models physical systems as stable limit cycles in latent phase space, providing unambiguous geometric separation between nominal orbits and anomaly breakdown.
\end{enumerate}
