\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Unsupervised time-series anomaly detection has evolved through dynamical equation discovery, reconstructive generative modeling, contrastive representation learning, and predictive world models. This section reviews these developments and clearly differentiates Flow-JEPA from prior art.

\subsection{Dynamical Systems, SINDy, and Neural ODEs}
The physical modeling of multi-sensor time series has long pursued the identification of continuous governing equations $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) \cite{brunton2016discovering}:} Employs sparse regression over candidate non-linear function libraries to discover symbolic ODEs. While transformative for low-dimensional closed systems (e.g., fluid dynamics, celestial mechanics), SINDy fails on real-world industrial telemetry. Calculating numerical derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ on noisy discrete measurements amplifies measurement noise exponentially; unobserved hidden states violate full-state Markov assumptions; and high sensor dimensionality ($K \ge 50$) triggers a combinatorial explosion of candidate terms ($\binom{K+3}{3} > 23,000$).
    \item \textbf{Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs) \cite{chen2018neural}:} Parameterize the continuous velocity field using neural networks $\frac{dx}{dt} = f_\theta(x(t), t)$ and solve trajectories via numerical ODE integrators. While Neural ODEs eliminate hand-crafted function libraries, fitting them directly in raw sensor space forces the integrator to solve stiff equations caused by high-frequency sensor noise, leading to slow training and numerical instability.
    \item \textbf{Flow Matching on Latent Manifolds \cite{lipman2022flow}:} Continuous Normalizing Flows and Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) train neural velocity vector fields $v_\psi(z_t, t)$ along straight Optimal Transport probability paths. Flow-JEPA represents the first architecture to marry Optimal Transport Flow Matching with non-contrastive Joint-Embedding representations, learning continuous dynamical vector fields purely in regularized latent space without raw-space noise integration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reconstructive Anomaly Detection Paradigms}
The predominant deep learning paradigm in time-series anomaly detection relies on learning nominal patterns through raw signal reconstruction. Early seminal work by Hundman et al. \cite{Hundman2018b} introduced LSTM-based prediction coupled with non-parametric dynamic thresholding for NASA spacecraft telemetry (SMAP/MSL). Modern architectures incorporate specialized attention and frequency-domain transformations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}:} Introduces Anomaly-Attention to quantify the \textit{Association Discrepancy} between local prior Gaussian kernel associations and global series self-attention. While effective for isolated point anomalies, it still couples discrepancy with point-level waveform reconstruction.
    \item \textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Decomposes 1D multivariate time series into 2D temporal variations using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) periodicity discovery and 2D Inception blocks. However, optimizing point-level 2D reconstruction makes it susceptible to over-fitting high-frequency sensor noise.
    \item \textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Employs a two-phase adversarial Transformer with sub-series attention and focus scoring to amplify subtle reconstruction errors, but remains vulnerable to phase-drift in continuous dynamical systems.
\end{itemize}

Despite architectural advancements, all reconstructive methods share a fundamental vulnerability: \textit{signal-level reconstruction memorization}. Because loss functions optimize point-level $L_1$ or $L_2$ differences in raw signal space, reconstructive networks are biased toward fitting high-frequency stochastic noise rather than learning invariant dynamical laws. Furthermore, during sustained anomalies, corrupted observations contaminate the context window, causing the reconstructor to adapt and reconstruct the anomaly as the "new normal."

\subsection{Contrastive Learning and Representation-Based Detection}
To avoid reconstructing raw noise, contrastive methods learn discriminative representations by separating nominal sequences from anomalous perturbations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural}:} Uses Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) trained with Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE). Artificial synthetic anomalies (Gaussian noise, point spikes, trend shifts) are injected into training windows to create negative pairs. However, synthetic injections distort the physical dynamical manifold and fail to generalize to subtle real-world physical faults (e.g., gradual thermal dissipation or gait freezing).
    \item \textbf{DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}:} Replaces reconstruction with multi-scale dual-attention (patch-wise and channel-wise) contrastive representation learning, maximizing cross-scale representation consistency. While DCdetector eliminates artificial injection heuristics, contrastive objectives require careful scale selection and representation pooling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Emerging Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)}
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), originally proposed by LeCun \cite{lecun2022path} and demonstrated in computer vision by I-JEPA \cite{assran2023self} and V-JEPA \cite{bardes2024vjepa}, represent a paradigm shift. Rather than reconstructing raw signals, JEPAs predict the representation of a target observation from a context observation entirely within latent space.
Recent preliminary exploratory works have investigated JEPA concepts in time-series analysis:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{SC-JEPA \cite{he2026scjepa}:} Introduces a Sequential Codebook JEPA for time-series forecasting. However, SC-JEPA relies on discrete Vector Quantization (VQ) codebooks, converting continuous signals into finite discrete tokens. For continuous dynamical systems governed by smooth trajectories, discrete tokenization introduces severe quantization error and loses fine-grained phase-space geometry.
    \item \textbf{T-SAR-JEPA \cite{woldesenbet2026tsarjepa} and CF-JEPA \cite{lee2026cfjepa}:} Apply joint-embedding objectives to multimodal Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) remote sensing or counterfactual causal graphs. However, they rely on heuristic cosine-distance scoring and lack continuous velocity field modeling and statistical threshold calibration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Positioning and Novelty of Flow-JEPA}
Flow-JEPA synthesizes the strengths of continuous dynamical modeling and joint-embedding self-supervised learning while resolving their respective limitations through four innovations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Continuous Latent Vector Field Modeling:} Instead of symbolic equation fitting (SINDy) or discrete codebooks (SC-JEPA), Flow-JEPA learns the continuous tangent velocity field of the physical manifold via Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching directly in an abstract latent space.
    \item \textbf{Non-Contrastive VICReg Regularization:} Replaces synthetic negative injection heuristics (NCAD) with non-contrastive variance and covariance penalties, mathematically precluding dimensional collapse while preserving smooth attractor geometry.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Midpoint Velocity Discrepancy:} Eliminates stochastic Monte Carlo inference sampling noise by evaluating the velocity field at the exact Optimal Transport midpoint ($t=0.5$), enabling high-precision Mahalanobis whitening.
    \item \textbf{Asymptotic Extreme Value Tail Calibration:} Applies Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) under the Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem to fit Generalized Pareto tails on nominal velocity residuals, establishing optimal decision boundaries without manual tuning.
\end{enumerate}
