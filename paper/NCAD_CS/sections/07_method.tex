\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} (Stage~1), a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the series at index $t$ into: (i)~a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$), establishing the nominal regime; and (ii)~a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$), inspected for trajectory departures.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit leptokurtic sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro} and~\ref{sec:related}), we shift prediction into a 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$, Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. An online context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ into $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while a target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. To prevent constant-vector collapse, target weights $\phi$ are updated as an EMA of online weights $\theta$, $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$ with cosine momentum $m_k \in [0.995, 0.9995]$ \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures: TS-JEPA and Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
We evaluate two nested latent predictors sharing the same multiscale encoder backbone and coordinate saliency gate: an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (\texttt{TS-JEPA}) and a multi-horizon fluid-dynamic decomposition model (\texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}).

\subsubsection{Unconstrained TS-JEPA Baseline}
In \texttt{TS-JEPA}, a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-horizon prediction $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with standard single-horizon predictive alignment and VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg} ($\gamma_{\text{var}} = 1.0$). While terminal LayerNorm bounds latent representations on the sphere of radius $\sqrt{D}$ ($\|z\|_2 = \sqrt{D}$) and compresses nominal residual kurtosis relative to observation-space reconstructors, single-horizon encoding pools the entire $S=64$ target window into one terminal TCN state, smoothing out short-lived sub-horizon transients and leaving cross-horizon residual correlations unwhitened.

\subsubsection{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA (Multi-Horizon Reynolds Decomposition and Lieb--Thirring Concentration)}
\noindent\textbf{Physical Motivation and Structural Design.} In fluid turbulence, Reynolds decomposition separates a velocity field into its time-averaged mean flow and zero-mean fluctuations, while dispersive wave theory characterizes localized, shape-preserving wavepackets (\emph{solitons}) by spectral concentration of the fluctuation operator. \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} brings both principles into latent time-series prediction through three structural components:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textit{Multi-depth TCN functional hierarchy taps and synchronized barycenter centering:} Standard TCN encoders read only the deepest dilated block, where wide receptive fields attenuate sharp onset transients. In \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, a multi-depth tap head extracts intermediate feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=64$) across all $L=3$ causal dilated TCN stages, concatenating the temporal mean and terminal state of each stage with the base encoder output into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{cat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times (2 L d_{\text{model}} + D)}$. A two-layer residual projection maps $\mathbf{h}_{\text{cat}}$ onto the unit sphere $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. To eliminate common-mode baseline drift between context and target windows, the online context batch barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} = \operatorname{sg}\bigl(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B \mathbf{u}_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}\bigr)$ is subtracted from both online context and EMA target spherical embeddings prior to final LayerNorm projection.
    \item \textit{Nested causal multi-horizon rollout and decoupled horizon VICReg:} Rather than predicting only the full $S=64$ window, we partition $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefix windows $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ with lengths $S_m = (m/M)S \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$. The EMA target encoder encodes each prefix into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. From $z_{\text{ctx}}$, a sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out intermediate predictions $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ ($\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$) using learnable horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the primary predictor $P_\theta$ forecasts the terminal horizon $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ for $m < M$ and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, the training objective is:
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Sol-Rey}} &= \sum_{m=1}^M w_m \Bigl( 2 - \frac{2}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \bigl\langle \bar{z}_{\text{pred}}^{(b,m)}, \operatorname{sg}\bigl(\bar{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(b,m)}\bigr) \bigr\rangle \Bigr) \\
    &\quad + \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \bigl( \mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}) + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}) \bigr) \\
    &\quad + 0.05\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}(z_{\text{ctx}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{z} = z / \|z\|_2$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}(z) = D^{-1}\sum_{d=1}^D \max(0, 1 - \sigma_d(z))$ enforces unit per-coordinate standard deviation, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}(z) = D^{-1}\|\operatorname{Cov}(z) - \operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{Cov}(z))\|_F^2$ decorrelates coordinates within each horizon $m$ independently, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{logdet}}(z_{\text{ctx}}) = -D^{-1}\log\det\bigl(\operatorname{Cov}(\bar{z}_{\text{ctx}})\sqrt{D} + 10^{-2}\mathbf{I}_D\bigr)$ prevents dimensional collapse (achieving $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}}) = 19.8$ vs.\ $11.6$ for single-horizon baselines).

\noindent\textbf{Reynolds Fluctuation Gram Matrix and Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration.}
Given the stacked multi-horizon prediction residuals $\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ with $\mathbf{e}_m = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}$, we fit the empirical shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows to obtain Mahalanobis-whitened multi-horizon residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{e}} = \mathbf{\Omega}(\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{E}) - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$, reshaped into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = [\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_1, \dots, \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_M]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Applying Reynolds decomposition across the $M$ horizons separates $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ into its horizon-mean residual $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean horizon fluctuations $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Let $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ denote the $M \times M$ fluctuation Gram matrix with ordered eigenvalues $0 \le \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$. We define the Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio of order $\gamma = 1$ as:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2} + \epsilon_{\text{LT}}}, \quad \epsilon_{\text{LT}} = 10^{-12}.
    \label{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}
\end{equation}
Physically, nominal background fluctuations disperse energy across multiple uncorrelated sub-horizons, whereas an emerging fault wavepacket concentrates fluctuation energy into a single dominant rank-1 directional mode across horizons. Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} establishes the exact algebraic bounds and invariance of $R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$.

\begin{proposition}[Lieb--Thirring Soliton Concentration Bounds, Centered Gram Rank Deficiency, and Scale Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathbf{E}' = (\mathbf{I}_M - M^{-1}\mathbf{1}_M\mathbf{1}_M^\top)\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ denote the Reynolds-centered multi-horizon fluctuation matrix for $M \ge 2$ and $\gamma > -1/2$. In the exact limit $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$ for any non-zero fluctuation $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~\emph{Centered Gram nullspace and effective rank $M-1$:} Because $\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$, the constant vector $\mathbf{1}_M$ lies in $\ker(\mathbf{G})$, forcing $\lambda_1(\mathbf{G}) \equiv 0$ identically so that $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{G}) \le \min(M-1, D)$.
(ii)~\emph{Sharp isotropy lower bound and soliton upper bound:} The concentration ratio satisfies the tight two-sided inequality
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{M - 1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1,
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
where the lower bound $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) is attained if and only if fluctuation energy is distributed isotropically across $M-1$ orthogonal sub-horizon modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$), and the upper bound $1$ is attained if and only if $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$ ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0 < \lambda_M$), corresponding to a coherent rank-1 soliton wavepacket.
(iii)~\emph{Exact radial scale invariance:} For any scalar $c > 0$, $R_{\text{LT}}(c\mathbf{E}') = R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}')$, rendering $R_{\text{LT}}$ invariant to homogeneous amplitude rescaling.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction, $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{E}'(\mathbf{E}'^\top\mathbf{1}_M) = \mathbf{0}$, so $\lambda_1 = 0$ is always an eigenvalue of $\mathbf{G} \succeq \mathbf{0}$, leaving at most $M-1$ positive eigenvalues $\lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ with $\lambda_M > 0$ since $\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}$. Because $x \mapsto x^{\gamma+1/2}$ is strictly increasing on $x \ge 0$ for $\gamma > -1/2$, $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le \lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$ for all $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$. Summing over the $M-1$ remaining modes gives $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$. Dividing through by $\sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$ yields (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}), with equality on the left iff $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M$ and on the right iff $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{M-1} = 0$. Finally, scaling $\mathbf{E}' \mapsto c\mathbf{E}'$ maps $\mathbf{G} \mapsto c^2\mathbf{G}$ and $\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \mapsto c^{2\gamma+1}\lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2}$, canceling in numerator and denominator of (\ref{eq:lt_soliton_ratio}) as $\epsilon_{\text{LT}} \to 0^+$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
At test time, windows are scored at stride $1$. Let $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denote the dimension-agnostic coordinate saliency gate defined in (\ref{eq:saliency_gate}). Unconstrained \texttt{TS-JEPA} scores the single-horizon residual $\mathbf{e}(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)$ via $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$. In \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, we combine the joint $MD$-dimensional whitened residual gate $g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))$ with the peak sub-horizon gate $\max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))$ into the multi-horizon envelope $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$, and modulate it by a one-sided median absolute deviation (MAD)-standardized soliton concentration gain and nominal manifold distance gain:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(g_{MD}\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t)\bigr)^2 + \max_{1 \le m \le M} g_D\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t)\bigr)^2\Bigr)}, \label{eq:soliton_env} \\
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{Sol-Rey}}(t) &= \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\Bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{sol}}\max\bigl(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t))\bigr)\Bigr) \nonumber \\
    &\quad \times \Gamma_{\text{bank}}(t), \label{eq:soliton_score}
\end{align}
where $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0.12$, $z_{\text{LT}}(t) = (R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}'(t)) - m_{\text{LT}}) / (1.4826\,\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}})$ standardizes $R_{\text{LT}}$ against its nominal training median $m_{\text{LT}}$ and MAD $\text{MAD}_{\text{LT}}$, and $\Gamma_{\text{bank}}(t) = 1 + 0.15\tanh(0.5\,\tilde{d}_{\text{core}}(t)) + 0.10\tanh(0.5\,\tilde{d}_{\text{null}}(t))$ applies a bounded multiplicative gain from MAD-normalized $k$-nearest-neighbor coreset distance $\tilde{d}_{\text{core}}$ ($N_{\text{core}}=64, k=5$) and minor principal-component null-space projection energy $\tilde{d}_{\text{null}}$ ($d_{\text{null}}=8$) fitted on nominal training windows. Because $\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}))$ is strictly one-sided, nominal windows with below-median spectral concentration ($z_{\text{LT}} \le 0$) receive zero soliton amplification, suppressing isolated single-step glitches while amplifying coherent multi-horizon fault wavepackets. Setting $\alpha_{\text{sol}} = 0$ and $\Gamma_{\text{bank}} \equiv 1$ recovers the unmodulated multi-horizon envelope control $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)$ (\texttt{Soliton-Reynolds ($\alpha_{\text{sol}}{=}0$)}).

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
We formalize online evaluation under three criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$, the detector observes only $\mathbf{X}[t-C : \tau, :]$; neither encoder accesses observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps) vs.\ fault-detection delay ($\Delta t$):} Because $z_{\text{tgt}}$ summarizes suspect observations $\mathbf{X}[t : t+S, :]$ spanning sample indices $[t, t+S-1]$, score $s_t$ is emitted at the trailing edge $\tau = t + S - 1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~3), creating a structural emission lag of $S - 1 = 63$ steps ($0.98\,\text{s}$ on $64\,\text{Hz}$ Daphnet, $10.5\,\text{min}$ on $0.1\,\text{Hz}$ SMAP/MSL/GHL, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO, and $63\,\text{h}$ on hourly Metro). This lag is distinct from empirical fault-detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alarm}} - t^*$ from onset $t^*$ to the first alarm $t_{\text{alarm}} \ge t^*$. Shortening the horizon to $S \in \{1, 4\}$ eliminates emission latency for rapid tripwires (Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}).
    \item \textbf{Strictly causal trailing vs.\ retrospective buffered attribution:} \emph{Causal trailing attribution} assigns $s_t$ only to $\tau = t + S - 1$ for zero-lookahead streaming. \emph{Retrospective buffered smear attribution} averages $s_t$ across all suspect timestamps $[t, t+S-1]$, introducing fixed-lag smoothing. Both regimes are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a),(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Architectural Components and Thresholding}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and Scale Equivariance}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
To amplify sparse departures in high-dimensional representations while mitigating channel drowning, we define the coordinate saliency gate $g_d: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ for any vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    g_d(\mathbf{v}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{d \sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}, \\
    w_i &= \frac{\exp\bigl(|v_i| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^d \exp\bigl(|v_j| / (\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}})\bigr)}, \quad \sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:saliency_gate}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau_g = 0.5$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. When $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$, $g_d(\mathbf{0}) = 0$. For non-zero $\mathbf{v}$, rescaling $\mathbf{v} \mapsto c\mathbf{v}$ with $c > 0$ yields $\sigma_{c\mathbf{v}} = c\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}{\sqrt{d}} + \epsilon = c\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} + (1-c)\epsilon$. In the operational regime where $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 \gg \epsilon$, the softmax weights $w_i$ are scale-invariant up to an $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/\|\mathbf{v}\|_2)$ perturbation, satisfying approximate degree-1 homogeneity $g_d(c\mathbf{v}) \approx c\,g_d(\mathbf{v})$ and recovering exact degree-1 homogeneity in the limit $\epsilon \to 0^+$. Input channels use uniform weights in the deployed pipeline (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}).

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
For multivariate streams ($K > 1$), a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Each projected channel is split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point rFFT grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax scale router. Because smooth Fourier shells overlap at band edges ($\sum \Psi_j \equiv 1$ rather than $\sum \Psi_j^2 \equiv 1$), this acts as a multiscale front-end rather than an orthogonal Parseval wavelet bank (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)).

\subsubsection{Extreme-Value Threshold Calibration (SPOT)}
To convert scores $\mathcal{S}(t)$ into binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_{\text{SPOT}})$ from nominal calibration data alone, SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits exceedances $Y = \mathcal{S}(t) - u > 0$ above the 98th calibration percentile $u = p_{98}$ to a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with shape $\xi$ and scale $\sigma$ via Grimshaw maximum-likelihood estimation \cite{grimshaw1993computing} with bounded L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10]$). Inverting the GPD tail at target risk $q = 10^{-3}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{SPOT}} = \begin{cases}
    u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right], & |\xi| \ge 10^{-5}, \\
    u - \sigma \ln\left(\frac{q N_{\text{cal}}}{N_u}\right), & |\xi| < 10^{-5},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:spot_threshold}
\end{equation}
where the lower branch is the continuous exponential-tail limit as $\xi \to 0$, evaluated directly to avoid division by zero. Here $N_{\text{cal}}$ and $N_u$ count post-attribution score timestamps on the calibration block. Primary tables use standard SPOT; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} evaluates runs-declustered GPD fitting, rolling calibrators, and kurtosis-scaled thresholds ($\tau_{\text{eff}}$ and $\tau_{\text{exc}}$).
