\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.77\textwidth]{figures/opentropy_jepa_architecture.pdf}
\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{Four-stage pipeline of \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. \textbf{(1)}~A sliding window ($C{=}256$ context steps, $S{\le}64$ target steps) feeds online and target encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) built on a shared four-block TCN with depthwise frequency shells for $K{\ge}25$, hook-free multi-depth pooling, barycenter centering, and LayerNorm. \textbf{(2)}~A parallel MLP predictor forecasts $M$ nested target prefixes ($M{=}3$ for \emph{Lite}; $M{=}4$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}), and errors are whitened into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 32}$. \textbf{(3)}~The errors expand into $(3+1) \times 32 = 128$ position-plus-drift dials (\emph{Lite}) or $7 \times 32 = 224$ dials (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}). \textbf{(4)}~Each dial has its own tripwire, and a quorum of trips raises a causal ($\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$) or buffered alarm.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-5pt}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} shows the pipeline from raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($K$ channels over $T$ steps) to alarms. In plain terms, it has four stages: (1)~encode each window of recent data into a short summary vector; (2)~predict the summaries of the next few stretches of data; (3)~turn the prediction errors into a set of \emph{dials}, each measuring one aspect of the error; and (4)~give every dial its own threshold and raise an alarm when enough dials trip together. The networks are trained on healthy data only; stabilization weights and alarm thresholds are then fitted on later healthy blocks and frozen.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time. At each position $t$ it splits into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of the past $C = 256$ steps and a \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the next $S = 64$ steps, which ends at the decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features are split into four smooth frequency bands (dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose band weights sum to one at every frequency), filtered by per-shell depthwise causal convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) with dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ \cite{bai2018empirical}. Lower-channel streams ($K < 25$) skip the band split and feed directly into the TCN.

The encoders follow the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}: instead of reconstructing raw waveforms, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and a target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$ channels) pool temporal (last step, max, mean $\in\mathbb{R}^{144}$) and statistical (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, mean absolute first difference $\in\mathbb{R}^{144}$) descriptors through $D=32$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads and a linear bottleneck to $D=32$. $E_\phi$ is an exponential moving average (EMA) of $E_\theta$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on 1D archive controls) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Two native encoder mechanisms preserve fast spikes and center offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping (keeps fast transients):} Both encoders read three intermediate feature maps directly inside the forward pass: the front-end output, block $1$, and final block $3$. Concatenated mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{288}$) pass through a bias-free $288\to 32\to 32$ GELU head with affine-free LayerNorm and blend with the deep output via $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering (removes common offsets):} During training, a running mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, copied into $E_\phi$ at every EMA step) is subtracted from context and target summaries before an affine-free terminal LayerNorm. Centering removes training offsets without guaranteeing invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The simplest JEPA detector, the single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA}, uses a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to predict one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the whole 64-step target window, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant penalty ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Summarizing all 64 steps in one vector can average away a short fault. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} therefore predict $M$ nested prefixes of the target window: $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$ steps) in \emph{Lite}, and $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ steps) in \emph{Laminar-JEPA}. Starting from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ plus learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a single parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP predicts all $M$ summaries $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$ behind one final LayerNorm. The loss gives the full window weight $w_M=1/2$ and each shorter prefix $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$ ($1/4$ in Lite, $1/6$ in 224-Bit), and adds a penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$ that keeps the summaries from collapsing to a constant:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. The penalty acts on each batch of predicted summaries and does two jobs: it keeps every coordinate varying, and it discourages coordinates from copying one another. Unlike log-determinant penalties, which need batches larger than $D$, it works at our batch size $B=16<D=32$. The diagonal entries of $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$, push each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma = 0.75$; the off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize the sample covariance (divisor $B-1$). Hence
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 .
\end{equation}
The penalty raises the effective rank of the target summaries, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}, from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first passed through a \emph{damped whitening} step fitted on healthy training windows, which decorrelates them and brings well-estimated directions close to unit variance. We flatten $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($128$ in full, $96$ in Lite), accumulate its \texttt{float32} sample mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and unbiased covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = (N_{\text{fit}}-1)^{-1}\sum_t (\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})(\cdot)^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$, eigendecompose $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$, clamp rank-deficient or numerical negative modes via $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$, and form $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying $(\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}})$ by the symmetric square root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ whose training covariance has eigenvalues $\lambda_k^2/(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2$: close to $1$ for well-estimated directions, while rank-deficient or near-zero modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$) are attenuated toward $0$ rather than amplified. The whitening is therefore approximate, with damping $10^{-3}$ fixed across all cohorts.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$, never on the block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ that later sets alarm thresholds (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), and blend them toward a simpler per-coordinate scaling, the unwhitened error divided by its root-mean-square on $\mathbf{X}_q$, $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=e_{m,c}(t)/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} An earlier six-block sequential variant also tested gradient-free editing of predictor rows (Steps Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$; because it yielded no reliable archive benefit (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}), the unified backbone omits weight editing entirely.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

The proposition below states two exact properties of this split. Part~(i) says that drift and fluctuation measure non-overlapping parts of the position energy, and that the endpoint dial cannot exceed a multiple of the fluctuation dial. Part~(ii) says the fluctuation pattern lives in at most $M-1$ directions and defines $R_{\text{LT}}$, which is $1$ when one pattern dominates and $1/(M-1)$ when the energy is spread evenly. These are geometric facts about the representation, not accuracy guarantees.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Instead of adding all 224 dials into one score, Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) gives each dial its own threshold, learned from the healthy calibration block. A dial \emph{trips} when it exceeds its threshold, and an alarm fires when enough dials trip at once. Because all dials come from the same $4 \times 32$ error matrix, their trips are correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort; the variance of the trip count is $7.2\times$ that of independent dials). We therefore do not assume independence; the alarm quorums are set from the trip counts observed on healthy calibration data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded directly at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 onset relays:} When a sudden fault first enters the right edge of the target window, the short prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover healthy data, so few position dials trip. To catch this moment, each coordinate $c$ has stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($q_{0.993}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$) and fires a relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when its full-window position dial ($m=4$) trips together with its endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$ dial.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, the trips from window $[t,t+S)$ are reported at its right end $\tau=t+S-1$, the earliest moment all of its data exist; this fixes a reporting lag of $S-1$ steps, separate from the detection delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$ after fault onset $t^*$. Under \emph{buffered attribution}, used for after-the-fact review, each trip is assigned to the steps it describes: position trips to their prefix $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation trips to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint trips to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. $N_{224}(\tau)$, the position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and the relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. Ranking metrics use the plain count $N_{224}(\tau)$ directly. The binary alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with quorums set on calibration counts: $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$. All quantiles use linear interpolation, the safety factor is applied before the floor, quorums stay real-valued, and both trips and alarms use strict inequalities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both tiers share the exact $120.0\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$-parameter backbone ($120.0\text{k}$ at $K < 25$ and $127.7\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$, down from $295.8\text{k}$ in the legacy six-block sequential model) and differ only in horizon count and dial expansion:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Shared streamlined backbone:} Four adaptive TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), $D$-wide pooling and native hook-free tap heads, depthwise rFFT shells gated at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbf{u}_m = z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{32} \to \text{Linear}(32,64) \to \text{GELU} \to \text{LayerNorm}(64) \to \text{Linear}(64,32)$, returning $\operatorname{LN}_{\text{no-aff}}(\mathbf{u}_m + \Delta_m)$).
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and keeps only the $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ under a single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$, removing fluctuation/endpoint dials and onset relays.
    \item \emph{Diagnostic $7D=224$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and expands errors into all $224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA (224 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, attribution mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Per-timestep binary alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 1: Disjoint Training, Whitening \& Adaptation ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q$)}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$ and standardize channels using $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ alone.
\State Set nested prefixes $S_{1:3}=\{16,32,64\}$ (\text{Lite}) or $S_{1:4}=\{16,32,48,64\}$ (\text{224-Bit}) with $K\ge 25$ depthwise rFFT shells.
\For{stride-$16$ training batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ and targets $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$ with hook-free multi-depth pooling and barycenter shift $-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$.
    \State Predict all $M$ prefixes in parallel behind one LayerNorm; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (or fixed epochs) and fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_+^{MD})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\State Fit and freeze stabilization scales and blending weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 2: Untouched Circuit-Breaker Calibration on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$}
\State Compute frozen stabilized residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ via (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).
\If{tier $=$ 224-Bit}
    \State Expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ into $7D=224$ dials (position, drift, fluctuation, endpoint) and $32$ right-edge onset relays.
    \State Fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}) and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\Else \Comment{Operational tier: Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
    \State Extract $(M+1)D=128$ dials ($96$ positions $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}|$ + $32$ drifts $|\bar{e}_c|$), fit $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and set $K_{\text{Lite}}=\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 3: Online Inference on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$}
    \State Compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \to \mathbf{d}(t)$ and binary trips $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(d_j(t)>\tau_j)$ (plus relays $r_c(t)$ if 224-Bit).
    \State Attribute active counts to right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ (\text{causal}) or prefix spans $[t,t+S_m)$ (\text{buffered}).
    \State Emit $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ when active counts exceed $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) or $K_{\text{Lite}}$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} To measure what the breaker panel adds over a single score on the same backbone (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), the 1D controls pool a residual vector into one number: an equal blend of its $\ell_2$ norm and a softmax-weighted coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$). Single-horizon TS-JEPA pools $\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$; 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA combines the pooled full-tensor and peak-horizon scores by root-mean-square (with optional $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$). Both are thresholded with SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.
