Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} traces raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ to binary alarms across four stages: encode recent context into a compact summary, predict multi-horizon target summaries, expand whitened errors into nonnegative \emph{dials}, and fire when a healthy-calibrated quorum of dials trips together.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Stage~1 compresses each raw telemetry window into a bounded $32$-number summary while retaining both slow baselines and fast transients. A length-$320$ window advances one step at a time, splitting at index $t$ into a $256$-step context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and a $64$-step target $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ whose causal decision timestamp is $\tau = t + S - 1$. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise stem before $1{\times}1$ channel mixing. On high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), features split into four smooth dyadic shells of a real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) whose weights sum to one at every frequency, pass through depthwise causal convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombine via a softmax router before a four-block causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN, dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$) \cite{bai2018empirical}. Streams with $K < 25$ feed directly into the TCN.

Following the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and exponential moving average (EMA) target encoder $E_\phi$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$, momentum $m = 0.996$, or $0.995$ on 1D controls) map each window to $D=32$ coordinates without collapsing across $10\text{--}20$ epochs \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. To capture both waveform level and roughness, each encoder summarizes TCN features via three temporal statistics (last step, max, and mean) and three variability statistics (standard deviation, $\ln(1+\text{mean }x^2)$, and mean absolute first difference), projected through $d_{\text{head}}$-wide GELU-LayerNorm heads ($d_{\text{head}}=32$ in canonical models; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)). A final affine-free LayerNorm projects embeddings onto the $\sqrt{D}$-sphere to tame heavy nominal tails ($\gamma_2$), though dividing by total feature variance dilutes isolated single-sensor flatlines by $O(1/\sqrt{KD})$ (Section~\ref{subsec:gating_ablation}). Two mechanisms preserve sharp edges and remove static offsets:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Hook-free multi-depth tapping:} Deep TCN layers smooth out sudden spikes, so both encoders also read the front-end, block $1$, and block $3$ directly, blending mean-centered last-step and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) features ($\mathbb{R}^{288}\to d_{\text{head}}\to 32$) into the output at weight $g = 0.5\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$.
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering:} Subtracting a running training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (rate $0.05$, copied into $E_\phi$) before terminal LayerNorm removes constant training offsets, though post-calibration regime shifts still require live monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Predicting a single $64$-step target summary, as in the baseline \emph{TS-JEPA} (a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a $\beta=0.05$ log-determinant term \cite{bardes2021vicreg}), averages out brief transients. Instead, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} predicts $M=3$ nested target prefixes of $\{16, 32, 64\} = \{S/4, S/2, S\}$ steps from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$ through one parallel two-layer GELU residual MLP behind a single terminal LayerNorm, while the reference \emph{Laminar-JEPA} adds $S_3=48$ ($M=4$). Weighting the full window at $w_M=1/2$ and shorter prefixes at $w_m=1/(2(M-1))$, the training loss pairs MSE with a batch anti-collapse term $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Acting at batch size $B=16<D=32$ without log-determinant inversion, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ pushes each coordinate's standard deviation $\sigma_d$ (biased batch estimate plus $10^{-4}$) above $\gamma=0.75$ via diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d)}$ and penalizes off-diagonal sample covariance $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ (divisor $B-1$):
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac{1}{D}\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\|_F^2 = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d} \max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d) + \tfrac{1}{2D}\sum_{d\neq j}\Sigma_{dj}^2 ,
\end{equation}
raising target effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme} from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Raw prediction errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ ($\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$) have unequal variances and correlate across coordinates. Stage~3 centers and decorrelates them on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ via damped \emph{zero-phase component analysis (ZCA)} symmetric square-root whitening. Flattening $\mathbf{E}(t)$ into $\mathbf{e}_{\text{flat}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{MD}$ ($96$ at $M{=}3$, $128$ at $M{=}4$), we fit its mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ and covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_k)\mathbf{U}^\top$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$. Clamping $\lambda_k=\max(\tilde{\lambda}_k,0)$ defines damped precision $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ and its symmetric square root $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_k}/(\lambda_k + 10^{-3})\bigr)\mathbf{U}^\top$, which brings well-estimated directions near unit variance while attenuating near-null modes ($\lambda_k \ll 10^{-3}$).

Unlike eigenbasis PCA whitening, symmetric ZCA minimizes rotation away from the input $(m,c)$ axes, maintaining strong entrywise correlation with raw errors ($r=0.64\text{--}0.67$ across archive and dev streams vs.\ $r\approx 0$ under eigenbasis PCA). Because nested prefixes overlap in time, $29.2\%\text{--}31.3\%$ of $\|\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}\|_F^2$ still couples across horizon blocks ($m\neq m'$), so reshaped entries $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$ act as \emph{ZCA-anchored coordinates} rather than isolated single-horizon errors. Whenever strict per-horizon separation is needed, replacing $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ with a shared $D{\times}D$ Kronecker ZCA operator $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$ eliminates cross-horizon mixing, commutes identically with horizon averaging, and matches dense ZCA across all $118$ archive streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Index-aligned shrinkage on $\mathbf{X}_q$:} Because ZCA aligns with the input axes ($r=0.64\text{--}0.67$), coordinates with $\text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c)>1.10$ on disjoint $\mathbf{X}_q$ shrink toward index-matched marginals $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)=(e_{m,c}(t)-\bar{\mu}_{\text{res},m,c})/\text{RMS}_q(e_{m,c})$, frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ (strictly within horizon $m$ under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Legacy weight-space quench (ablation only):} Predictor-row editing (Q1--Q4) on $\mathbf{X}_q$ gave no archive gain (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}) and is omitted.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} Splitting reshaped $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t)$ \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} into cross-block mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ ($r=0.63\text{--}0.70$ with pure horizon drift; exact under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$) and fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$ yields $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{ZCA-anchored positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{cross-block drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{block fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} states two algebraic properties of any reshaped $M\times D$ matrix (exact horizon energy under $\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\mathbf{\Omega}_D^{1/2}$): mean and fluctuation partition squared energy orthogonally, and $\mathbf{F}(t)$ spans at most $M-1$ modes.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ (so the RMS horizon error equals $\sqrt{|\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)}$, not $|\bar{e}_c(t)| + d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$) and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Motivation for Coordinate-Level Fault Isolation}
\label{subsec:theoretical_motivation}
In plain terms, multivariate residual scoring faces three design options across $J$ coordinates or dials $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$. Global quadratic aggregation $S_{\text{glob}}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2$ sums all coordinates into one threshold, whereas a marginal union $\max_j \mathbb{I}(|u_j(t)|>\tau_j)$ compounds per-coordinate false alarms across $J$ tests. A correlation-aware coordinate quorum instead gives every dial its own quantile threshold $\tau_j$ and sets a joint trip-count threshold $K_q$ on healthy calibration data. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} states a multiplicative scale-equivariance property motivating this choice: when $k\ll J$ ``hijacker'' coordinates share a persistent post-training scale factor across both $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and operational data, global quadratic thresholds grow unboundedly while per-coordinate quantile tripwires stay invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Threshold Masking vs.\ Matched-Scale Quorum Equivariance]
\label{prop:hijacker_isolation}
Let $\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^J$ denote nominal calibration and operational residual vectors, and assume a matched positive diagonal scaling $\mathbf{S}_\eta = \operatorname{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_J)$ with $s_j=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ ($\eta\ge 0$) on a subset $\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\dots,J\}$ of $k=|\mathcal{H}|<J$ coordinates and $s_j=1$ for $j\notin\mathcal{H}$ acts jointly on both calibration and operational data ($\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}=\mathbf{S}_\eta\mathbf{u}_{\text{cal}}$ and $\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbf{S}_\eta\mathbf{u}(t)$):
(i)~under global scoring $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u})=\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2$, the $(1-\alpha)$-calibration quantile satisfies $\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)\ge (1+\eta^2)\,q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2\bigr)$, growing unboundedly in $\eta^2$ so any live fault $\mathbf{u}(t^*)$ supported on $\mathcal{H}^c$ with $\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2 \le \tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$ goes undetected when $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$; and
(ii)~under scale-homogeneous tripwires $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=q_{1-\beta}(|u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)}|)$, every operational binary trip $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=\mathbb{I}(|u_j^{(\eta)}(t)|>\tau_j^{(\eta)})$ is invariant to $\eta$ for all $\eta\ge 0$ under arbitrary cross-coordinate dependence, leaving $N_{\text{cal}}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J b_{j,\text{cal}}(t)$, $K_q=\kappa\,q_{1-\alpha}(N_{\text{cal}})$, and $N(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J b_j(t)$ unchanged and guaranteeing detection whenever a fault trips $m > K_q$ coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $\sum_{j=1}^J (u_{j,\text{cal}}^{(\eta)})^2 \ge (1+\eta^2)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{H}} u_{j,\text{cal}}^2$ pointwise, monotonicity and positive homogeneity of quantiles give~(i), and $\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}}(t^*)=\mathbf{0}$ yields $S_{\text{glob}}(\mathbf{u}^{(\eta)}(t^*))=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{H}^c}(t^*)\|_2^2\le\tau_{\text{glob}}(\eta)$. For~(ii), positive homogeneity gives $\tau_j^{(\eta)}=s_j\,\tau_j^{(0)}$ for all $j$; dividing $|s_j u_j(t)|>s_j\tau_j^{(0)}$ by $s_j>0$ yields $b_j^{(\eta)}(t)=b_j^{(0)}(t)$ identically on both $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ and operational $t$, preserving $N_{\text{cal}}$, $K_q$, and $N(t)$, while $m$ active coordinates in $\mathcal{H}^c$ yield $N(t^*)\ge m > K_q$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Concrete illustration and scope limits:} For $J=32$ standard normal coordinates at $\alpha=0.005$ and $\beta=0.015$, matched $\eta=5$ scaling on $k=2$ hijackers raises $\tau_{\text{glob}}$ from $56.3$ to $252.4$, masking a $10$-coordinate $3.8\sigma$ fault ($144.4 < 252.4$) while $K_q=3.5$ stays invariant and trips $10 > 3.5$. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} covers \emph{matched multiplicative scaling} only: if operational variance shifts \emph{after} $\tau_j$ are frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, or if additive bias, covariance rotation, or mode changes occur, $b_j(t)$ is not invariant, requiring empirical $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ screening, live $D_{\text{KS}}$ monitoring, and quarantined recalibration (Section~\ref{subsec:regime_shift_modes}). Detection also requires \emph{multi-dial fault coverage ($m > K_q$)}, so diluted single-sensor flatlines miss quorum.

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) implements this quorum on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$. Because dials share the $M \times 32$ error matrix, trips correlate strongly (median effective rank $65/224$; count variance $7.2\times$ binomial independence), so quorums are fitted directly to $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is thresholded at its $98.5$th calibration percentile plus a $10^{-4}$ tie-break floor:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(d_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 separate onset relays:} When a sudden fault enters the target window's right edge while $m \in \{1,2\}$ still see healthy data, coordinate $c$ drives an auxiliary binary relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ (excluded from the $224$-dial vector $\mathbf{d}(t)$) under $q_{0.993} + 10^{-4}$, firing when $m=4$ position trips jointly with endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$.
    \item \emph{Online vs.\ buffered alarm timing:} In live \emph{causal attribution}, every dial from window $[t,t+S)$ maps to its right edge $\tau=t+S-1$, imposing an $S-1=63$-step window-fill lag unless a sharp right-edge onset triggers earlier ($\Delta t < 63$); all reported causal delays $\Delta t = \tau_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ include this lag. In retrospective \emph{buffered attribution}, block trips map back across $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation across $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint across $[t+S_3,t+S)$.
    \item \emph{Quorum decision:} Summing the $224$ dials gives $N_{224}(\tau)$ (with $128$-dial position count $N_{128}(\tau)$, and $N_{224}(\tau)$ used for ranking checks), while summing the $32$ relays gives $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. An alarm fires when any count crosses its healthy quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with calibration quorums $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$ (linear quantiles, strict inequalities).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operational Configuration: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224 Dials)}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
Both archive tiers share the $32$D-head backbone ($119.8\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable, $115.4\text{k}\text{--}124.9\text{k}$ frozen EMA, $235.1\text{k}\text{--}254.2\text{k}$ total or $0.90\text{--}0.97\,\text{MB}$ FP32 across $K\in[5,55]$; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)) with four TCN blocks ($\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$), depthwise rFFT shells at $K \ge 25$, and one parallel residual MLP ($\mathbb{R}^{32} \to 64 \to 32$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Operational $(3+1)D=128$ panel (\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}):} Uses $M=3$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 64\}$, $96\times 96$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$) and monitors $96$ position plus $32$ drift dials under $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$.
    \item \emph{High-recall $7D=224$ reference (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} Uses $M=4$ prefixes ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$, $128\times 128$ $\mathbf{\Omega}$, $+32$ params) with $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}); dev tables also report its $5$-block sequential predecessor ($274.2\text{k}\text{--}276.8\text{k}$ trainable; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128 Dials) and Laminar-JEPA (224+32)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Lite},\text{224-Bit}\}$, mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and active count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\State Partition $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$; standardize on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\For{stride-$16$ batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($S_{1:3}{=}\{16,32,64\}$ or $S_{1:4}{=}\{16,32,48,64\}$)}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}}{=}E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$, $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}{=}E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$; update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$; fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$; freeze $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\State On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), fit tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and freeze $K_{\text{Lite}}{=}\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$ (or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ via (\ref{eq:panel_quorum})).
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
    \State Map trips $b_j(t){=}\mathbb{I}(d_j(t){>}\tau_j)$ to $\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$ (\text{causal}) or $[t,t{+}S_m)$ (\text{buffered}); emit $\hat{y}(\tau)$ when active counts exceed quorum.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} Single-horizon TS-JEPA ($\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) and 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA (RMS of full-tensor and peak-horizon scores, $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$) blend $\ell_2$ norm and softmax coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$), thresholded via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.
