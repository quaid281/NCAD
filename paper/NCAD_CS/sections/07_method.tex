\section{Methodology: Laminar-JEPA and Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering

\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{Overview of the four-stage \emph{Laminar-JEPA} and compact \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} pipeline: \textbf{(1)}~sliding window ($C{=}256, S \in \{1..64\}$) and dual encoders ($E_\theta, E_\phi$) with conditional rFFT splitting ($K{\ge}25$ in Lite), barycenter centering, and LayerNorm; \textbf{(2)}~multi-horizon rollout ($M{=}4$ sequential in full \emph{Laminar-JEPA}; $M{=}3$ parallel single-LayerNorm in \emph{Lite}) under variance-floor and decorrelation loss plus resolvent whitening into $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 32}$; \textbf{(3)}~horizon expansion into $7 \times 32 = 224$ dials (full) or $4 \times 32 = 128$ position-plus-drift dials (\emph{Lite}); and \textbf{(4)}~per-dial Boolean circuit breakers emitting causal ($\tau{=}t{+}S{-}1$) or prefix-buffered alarms.\label{fig:triad_architecture}}
\vspace{-5pt}

Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} shows the pipeline from raw telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ ($K$ channels over $T$ steps) to alarms. In plain terms, it has four stages: (1)~encode each window of recent data into a short summary vector; (2)~predict the summaries of the next few stretches of data; (3)~turn the prediction errors into a set of \emph{dials}, each measuring one aspect of the error; and (4)~give every dial its own threshold and raise an alarm when enough dials trip together. The networks are trained on healthy data only; stabilization weights and alarm thresholds are then fitted on later healthy blocks and frozen.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Encoder and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
A sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances one step at a time. At each position $t$ it splits into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ of the past $C = 256$ steps and a \emph{target window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the next $S = 64$ steps, which ends at the decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$. Telemetry mixes slow trends with fast vibration, so the encoder first separates time scales. Multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal per-channel convolution and are split into four smooth frequency bands (dyadic shells of a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT), forming an amplitude partition of unity). Each band has its own causal convolution (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and a softmax router feeds the bands into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}. Single-channel inputs skip the band split.

The encoders follow the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}: instead of reconstructing raw waveforms, an online encoder $E_\theta$ and a target encoder $E_\phi$ map context and target windows to $D=32$-dimensional summaries, each normalized by LayerNorm to length close to $\sqrt{D}$. The target encoder is a slowly updated copy of the online one, an exponential moving average (EMA) with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ on archive cohorts) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Two problems arise in practice. Deep dilated TCNs blur brief spikes at fault onset, and changes in operating regime shift all summaries together. Two encoder components address them:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth tapping (keeps fast transients):} Each encoder also reads three intermediate feature maps ($d_{\text{model}}=48$): the spectral stem, a middle TCN block, and the final TCN block. For each tap, the last time step and the root-mean-square (RMS) energy over time are projected to $\mathbb{R}^D$ by a two-layer GELU head and blended with the deep TCN output through a bounded gate ($g \le 0.5$).
    \item \emph{Barycenter centering (removes common offsets):} During training, a running mean of the context summaries $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (update rate $0.05$, shared with $E_\phi$) is subtracted from both context and target summaries before the final LayerNorm. Centering removes offsets learned in training; it does not guarantee invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Prediction}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
The simplest JEPA detector, the single-horizon baseline \emph{TS-JEPA}, uses a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to predict one summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the whole 64-step target window, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a VICReg-style log-determinant penalty ($\beta = 0.05$) \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Summarizing all 64 steps in one vector can average away a short fault. \emph{Laminar-JEPA} therefore predicts how the target unfolds: it summarizes $M=4$ nested prefixes of the target window, the first $S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ steps. Starting from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ plus learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a recurrent predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ produces $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(1)}, \dots, \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M-1)}$ in sequence, and an anchor head $P_\theta$ predicts the full-window summary $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)}$. The loss weights the full window by one half and splits the other half equally across the shorter prefixes ($w_m$), and adds a penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$ that keeps the summaries from collapsing to a constant:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Lam}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops gradients through $E_\phi$. Log-determinant penalties need batches larger than $D$; $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ works on small batches. Its diagonal entries $\sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d)}$ push every coordinate's standard deviation above $\gamma = 0.75$, and its off-diagonal entries $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ penalize covariance between coordinates. At batch size $B=16 < D=32$, this raises the effective rank of the target summaries, $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}, from $11.6$ to $19.8$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Residual Whitening and Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Stage~3 turns prediction errors into dials. The signed errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ for the $M=4$ horizons form a $4\times 32$ error matrix $\mathbf{E}(t)$. Its entries have different natural sizes and are correlated, so they are first \emph{whitened}: rescaled to unit variance and decorrelated using statistics from healthy training windows. With the residual covariance $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$, we use the damped precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + 10^{-3})^2\bigr)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. Multiplying centered errors by $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ gives whitened errors $e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t)$; the damping shrinks directions with almost no training variance instead of amplifying their noise.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stabilization on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$:} A coordinate whose error grows between training and later healthy data would be inflated by whitening. We detect such coordinates on a separate healthy block $\mathbf{X}_q$, never on the block $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ that later sets alarm thresholds (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}), and blend them toward a simpler per-coordinate scaling $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t)$. The blend starts when the whitened RMS on $\mathbf{X}_q$ exceeds $1.10$, and all weights are frozen before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is used:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{q}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    The full \emph{Laminar-JEPA} can also run an optional gradient-free \emph{weight-space quench}, which edits the few predictor outputs that drift most on healthy data and keeps the edit only if false alarms fall. It uses only $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_q$ (legacy fixed-epoch development runs without $\mathbf{X}_q$ use $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$; Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}). Coordinates are ranked by a median-absolute-deviation (MAD) standardized instability score that combines mean and scale shifts from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ to $\mathbf{X}_q$, drift between the halves of $\mathbf{X}_q$, and residual autocorrelation, with a negative term for isolated spikes. For the top $k\in\{4,2\}$ coordinates, four steps run in order. \emph{(Q1)}~Damp the selected predictor output rows and LayerNorm gains and shift $\mathbf{p}_m$ toward the $\mathbf{X}_q$ means; through the shared 32D LayerNorm this also moves unselected coordinates (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). \emph{(Q2)}~Move residual centers $75\%$ of the way toward the post-edit $\mathbf{X}_q$ means. \emph{(Q3)}~Scale off-diagonal precision couplings by $0.15$ and rescale whitened $\mathbf{X}_q$ RMS. \emph{(Q4)}~Keep the edit (preferring $k=2$ on ties) only if the $\mathbf{X}_q$ half-split FPR improves while at least $92\%$ of the pre-edit quorum thresholds are preserved; then freeze all parameters.
    \item \emph{Expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} For each of the 32 coordinates, the four horizon errors describe how the prediction error evolves across the target window. Borrowing the mean-plus-fluctuation split of Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} purely as algebra, we split them into a horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and a zero-mean fluctuation $F_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$. Four views of each coordinate give $7D = 224$ nonnegative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~$4D = 128$ \emph{sub-horizon positions} $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$, the error size at each horizon;
    (b)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon-mean drifts} $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$, a persistent offset shared by all horizons;
    (c)~$D = 32$ \emph{horizon fluctuations} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sqrt{M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}^2(t)}$, how much the error changes across horizons; and
    (d)~$D = 32$ \emph{endpoint differences} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$, error that appears late in the window, as at a fault onset.
\end{enumerate}

The proposition below states two exact properties of this split. Part~(i) says that drift and fluctuation measure non-overlapping parts of the position energy, and that the endpoint dial cannot exceed a multiple of the fluctuation dial. Part~(ii) says the fluctuation pattern lives in at most $M-1$ directions and defines $R_{\text{LT}}$, which is $1$ when one pattern dominates and $1/(M-1)$ when the energy is spread evenly. These are geometric facts about the representation, not accuracy guarantees.

\begin{proposition}[Horizon Energy Split and Spectral Concentration Bounds]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)$ and fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{F}(t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}(t)^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$:
(i)~for every coordinate $c$, $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|^2 = |\bar{e}_c(t)|^2 + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$ and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$; and
(ii)~the $M\times M$ horizon Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}(t) = \mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{F}(t)^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ has ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ whose scale-invariant dominant-mode share for any $p > 0$ \cite{lieb1976inequalities} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}\bigl(\mathbf{F}(t)\bigr) = \frac{\lambda_M^p}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^p} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr],
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
reaching $\frac{1}{M-1}$ iff energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ active modes ($\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M > 0$, requiring $D \ge M-1$) and $1$ iff $\mathbf{F}(t)$ has rank one. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ has a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zero column sums ($\sum_{m=1}^M F_{m,c}=0$) cancel the cross term in $M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + F_{m,c})^2$, and $(F_{M,c}-F_{1,c})^2 \le 2(F_{M,c}^2+F_{1,c}^2) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$ gives~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_D$ implies $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}_M$ ($\lambda_1 = 0$), while $\|\mathbf{F}\|_F^2 > 0$ implies $\lambda_M > 0$. Summing $0 \le \lambda_m^p \le \lambda_M^p$ over $m \in \{2,\dots,M\}$ yields $\lambda_M^p \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^p \le (M-1)\lambda_M^p$; inverting and multiplying by $\lambda_M^p$ gives (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}) and its equality conditions, and nonzero scalar rescaling cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Circuit-Breaker Panel}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Instead of adding all 224 dials into one score, Stage~4 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}) gives each dial its own threshold, learned from the healthy calibration block. A dial \emph{trips} when it exceeds its threshold, and an alarm fires when enough dials trip at once. Because all dials come from the same $4 \times 32$ error matrix, their trips are correlated (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort; the variance of the trip count is $7.2\times$ that of independent dials). We therefore do not assume independence; the alarm quorums are set from the trip counts actually observed on healthy calibration data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial thresholds and hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized by its calibration median $m_j$ and scaled MAD $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$, giving a nonnegative score $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. Its tripwire $\tau_j$ is the $98.5$th percentile of $z_j$ on calibration data. Two hijack guards widen the tripwires of suspect dials: $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}} \ge 1$ widens a dial whose calibration tail grows over time (second-half 98th percentile above $1.35\times$ the first half), and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}} \ge 1$ widens a dial much noisier than its peers (scale above $1.875\times$ its perspective-group median):
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}.
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{32 onset relays:} When a sudden fault first enters the right edge of the target window, the short prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still cover healthy data, so few position dials trip. To catch this moment, each coordinate $c$ has stricter $99.3$rd-percentile edge tripwires ($\gamma^{1.25}$ guard) and fires a relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when its full-window position dial ($m=4$) trips together with its endpoint, fluctuation, or $m=3$ dial.
    \item \emph{Alarm timing and quorum voting:} Under \emph{causal attribution}, the trips from window $[t,t+S)$ are reported at its right end $\tau=t+S-1$, the earliest moment all of its data exist; this fixes a reporting lag of $S-1$ steps, separate from the detection delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$ after fault onset $t^*$. Under \emph{buffered attribution}, used for after-the-fact review, each trip is assigned to the steps it describes: position trips to their prefix $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation trips to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint trips to $[t+S_3,t+S)$. Counting active trips at each step gives $N_{224}(\tau)$, the position sub-count $N_{128}(\tau) \subset N_{224}(\tau)$, and the relay count $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$. For ranking metrics, the continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = N_{224}(\tau) + N_{128}(\tau) + 2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ counts position trips and relays twice (fixed on development streams before held-out testing; Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). The binary alarm fires when any count exceeds its quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    with quorums set on calibration counts: $K_{224} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0)$, $K_{128} = \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)$, and $K_{\text{edge}} = \max(1.25\,q_{0.999}, 2.5)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Compact Variant: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}}
\label{subsec:laminar_lite}
\emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} keeps the same four stages but makes three simplifications, each supported by the ablations in Section~\ref{sec:ablations}. Together they cut trainable parameters by $35\%\text{--}52\%$ ($141.9\text{k}$ at $K < 25$ and $222.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$, vs.\ $295.8\text{k}$), speed up windowed GPU inference by $2.43\times$ ($3.14\times$ at $K < 25$), and need no weight editing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Smaller encoder:} A fifth dilated block blurred short-lived anomalies without making the summaries richer, so \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses four TCN blocks (dilations $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$) and $64$D pooling heads. The frequency-band front-end is used only on high-channel streams ($K \ge 25$), where it helped; smaller streams use a direct causal projection.
    \item \emph{Parallel three-horizon predictor:} One residual two-layer GELU MLP predicts $M=3$ prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 64\}$) in parallel from $z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_m$, with a single final LayerNorm. This removes the lag of sequential prediction and the coupling between stacked normalizations, and shrinks $\mathbf{\Omega}$ to $96\times 96$.
    \item \emph{128-dial panel with one quorum:} Only the $96$ position dials $|\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ and the $32$ drift dials $|\bar{e}_c(t)|$ are kept, each calibrated by (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}). Fluctuation and endpoint dials, onset relays, and quenching are removed. Steps are ranked by the plain count $N_{128}(\tau)$, and the alarm uses one quorum, $K_{\text{Lite}} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\,3.5\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Laminar-JEPA (224-Bit) and Laminar-JEPA-Lite (128-Bit)\label{alg:pipeline}}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$, test stream $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$, tier $\in\{\text{Full},\text{Lite}\}$, attribution mode $\in\{\text{causal},\text{buffered}\}$.
\Ensure Per-timestep binary alarms $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ and continuous score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 1: Disjoint Training, Whitening \& Adaptation ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q$)}
\State Split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}$ chronologically into $S$-isolated $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}, \mathbf{X}_q, \mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}})$ and standardize channels using $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ alone.
\State Set nested prefixes $S_{1:4}=\{16,32,48,64\}$ (Full) or $S_{1:3}=\{16,32,64\}$ with $K\ge 25$ rFFT gate (Lite).
\For{stride-$16$ training batches in $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$}
    \State Encode $z_{\text{ctx}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ and targets $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = E_\phi(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)})$ with barycenter shift $-\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$.
    \State Roll out sequential $\{P_{\text{sub}}, P_\theta\}$ (Full) or parallel single-LayerNorm predictor (Lite); update $\theta$ via (\ref{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}) and $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.
\EndFor
\State Select checkpoint on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ (or fixed epochs) and fit $(\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}, \mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_+^{MD})$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$.
\If{tier $=$ Full and $q_{\text{wsp}} = 1$}
    \State Rank drifting coordinates on $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}},\mathbf{X}_q)$; run Steps Q1--Q4 for $k\in\{4,2\}$ and keep edit iff $\mathbf{X}_q$ split and $0.92$-quorum gates pass.
\EndIf
\State Fit and freeze stabilization scales and blending weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) on $\mathbf{X}_q$.
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 2: Untouched Circuit-Breaker Calibration on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$}
\State Compute frozen stabilized residuals $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ on untouched $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ via (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).
\If{tier $=$ Full}
    \State Expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{\text{cal}}$ into $7D=224$ dials (position, drift, fluctuation, endpoint) and $32$ right-edge onset relays.
    \State Fit MAD scales $(m_j,s_j)$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}).
\Else \Comment{Preferred Deployment Tier: Laminar-JEPA-Lite}
    \State Extract $4D=128$ position-plus-drift dials $(|\tilde{e}_{m,c}|, |\bar{e}_c|)$, fit $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and set single quorum $K_{\text{Lite}}=\max(1.12\,q_{0.995},3.5)$.
\EndIf
\Statex \vspace{1.5pt}\textbf{Phase 3: Online Inference on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ or $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$}
\For{each stride-$1$ window $[t-C,\,t+S)$}
    \State Compute $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \to \mathbf{d}(t)$ and binary trips $b_j(t)=\mathbb{I}(z_j(t)>\tau_j)$ (plus relays $r_c(t)$ if Full).
    \State Attribute active counts to right edge $\tau=t+S-1$ (\text{causal}) or prefix spans $[t,t+S_m)$ (\text{buffered}).
    \State Emit $\hat{y}(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ when active counts exceed $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) or $K_{\text{Lite}}$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{1D single-score controls:} To measure what the breaker panel adds over a single score on the same backbone (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), the 1D controls pool a residual vector into one number: an equal blend of its $\ell_2$ norm and a softmax-weighted coordinate RMS ($\alpha=\tau_g=0.5$). Single-horizon TS-JEPA pools $\mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$; 1D Multi-Horizon JEPA combines the pooled full-tensor and peak-horizon scores by root-mean-square (with optional $R_{\text{LT}}$ modulation $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$). Both are thresholded with SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing}.
