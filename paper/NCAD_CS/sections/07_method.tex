This section formalizes the mathematical formulation and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Prediction Paradigm}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a continuous multivariate time-series sequence consisting of $K$ observed sensor variables over $T$ timesteps. The sequence is partitioned into historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect/target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, where $C$ and $S$ denote the respective temporal horizons.

Existing deep learning baselines (e.g., TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) frame anomaly detection as pointwise reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \mathbb{E}\left[ \| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In complex multi-sensor telemetry, ambient high-frequency stochastic jitter dominates the Frobenius norm, causing models to waste representational bandwidth reconstructing sensor noise rather than modeling system state dynamics (leading to noise drowning).

To overcome this, we adopt the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm \cite{lecun2022path}. A context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, updated via:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k, \quad m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995].
\end{equation}
The core objective is to predict target representations in latent space under physical dynamical constraints.

\subsection{The Physics-Informed JEPA Triad}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Rather than relying on unconstrained Euclidean regression, we incorporate physical domain regularizers through three complementary geometric formulations:

\subsubsection{1. Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Dynamics)}
In dynamical systems theory, non-linear trajectories can be analyzed via linear operators acting on observable functions. In our architecture, the encoder maps the observation window to a finite-dimensional latent observable vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^D$. We define the transition between context and target latent states via a learned linear transition matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}.
\end{equation}
To regularize modal stability across latent coordinates, we evaluate the singular value spectrum $\sigma_i(\mathbf{K})$, which governs the energy amplification and contraction of the linear map. We define the normalized spectral entropy as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D \tilde{\sigma}_i \log (\tilde{\sigma}_i + \epsilon), \quad \tilde{\sigma}_i = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sum_{j=1}^D \sigma_j + \epsilon},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ ensures numerical stability. A unitary, energy-conserving linear map distributes energy evenly across all orthogonal modes ($\sigma_i \approx 1$), producing maximal spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{max}} = \log D$. Conversely, severe damping contracts dynamics onto a low-rank subspace (low spectral entropy). To prevent the transition matrix $\mathbf{K}$ from collapsing to a low-rank projection and encourage non-degenerate linear transitions during training, the Operator-Entropy training objective regularizes the predictive transition while maximizing spectral dispersion:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 - \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 10^{-3}$ penalizes dimensional collapse across latent modes. Note that representation collapse of encoder embeddings $z$ is prevented by the asymmetric EMA target encoder with stop-gradient $\bot$, while $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ specifically acts as an inductive regularizer on the operator spectrum during pretraining.

\subsubsection{2. Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Covariance Alignment)}
In multi-channel telemetry representing coupled flows (thermal, convective, hydraulic), interactions across latent state dimensions are governed by transport phenomena. Inspired by hydrodynamic \textit{Reynolds decomposition}, the target encoder $E_\phi$ processes target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ through its convolutional backbone prior to global temporal pooling, outputting an unpooled temporal sequence of feature vectors $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{tgt}} = [z_1, \dots, z_S]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$. We decompose this sequence into a temporal mean trajectory and zero-mean fluctuations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{tgt}}^T + \mathbf{Z}',
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{tgt}} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S z_s \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denotes the window temporal mean, and $\mathbf{Z}' \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$ denotes the dynamic fluctuations.

The inter-coordinate momentum transport across the temporal window is characterized by the second-moment fluctuation covariance matrix across latent coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} = \frac{1}{S} {\mathbf{Z}'}^T \mathbf{Z}' \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}.
\end{equation}
The Reynolds-Stress predictor parameterizes both the mean target transition $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = f_{\text{mean}}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ and a predicted fluctuation covariance $\mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, parameterized via lower-triangular Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}(z_{\text{ctx}})$. The training objective aligns both the mean trajectory and the dynamic covariance across latent dimensions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \| \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_R \| \mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} \|_F^2,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_R = 10^{-2}$. At inference time, the anomaly score $\mathcal{S}(t) = \|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)\|_2^2$ evaluates mean trajectory divergence.

\subsubsection{3. Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
Under the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition theorem, any sufficiently smooth vector field can be uniquely resolved into an irrotational (curl-free) conservative potential gradient and a solenoidal (divergence-free) rotational field. 

In nominal physical equilibria, conservative dynamical systems conserve potential energy along phase trajectories. We parameterize the latent transition dynamics through the gradient of a scalar potential network $\Phi_\psi(z)$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{v}(z) = \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z), \quad \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = z_{\text{ctx}} + \hat{v}(z_{\text{ctx}}).
\end{equation}
Because the curl of the gradient of any $C^2$ scalar field is identically zero ($\nabla \times (\nabla \Phi) \equiv \mathbf{0}$), parameterizing the velocity field as $\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$ guarantees that the predicted trajectory field is \textit{strictly irrotational by construction}. Physical anomalies that introduce non-conservative work, dissipative friction, or unmodeled circulation diverge from any integrable potential field, producing large residual discrepancies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \| \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{ctx}}) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, sliding context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect target windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed with a stride of 1 timestep. For all JEPA architectures, the anomaly score is computed as the latent predictive discrepancy between the predicted target representation $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ and the target encoder representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(t) = \| \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}
While context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ is strictly causal (depending only on the past), the target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ evaluates the upcoming $S=64$ timesteps. Therefore, $\mathcal{S}(t)$ operates with an operational lookahead buffer of $S=64$ timesteps at inference. In a continuous streaming deployment, an anomaly occurring at timestep $t^*$ enters the target window when $t = t^* - S$, causing the model to register elevated discrepancy up to $S=64$ steps prior to the event peak. Under strict unadjusted point-level evaluation, timesteps prior to ground-truth onset are recorded as point-level false positives, establishing an empirical false-positive baseline floor. In our standard benchmark evaluation, we report \textit{Dense} scoring, where sliding windows advance with a stride of 1 timestep and the anomaly score $\mathcal{S}(t)$ is evaluated at each decision time $t$. In operational incident analysis (Section VII.D), we additionally examine \textit{Middle} alignment, where the anomaly score is centered at $t + S/2$ to balance pre-onset and post-onset window overlap. Baseline models (TimesNet and TranAD) are evaluated under the exact identical sliding-window evaluation protocol and standardization to ensure strict comparability.

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{1. Coordinate Saliency Gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 50$), anomalous signals frequently manifest in only a small subset of channels while remaining channels exhibit ambient noise. To prevent dimensional drowning, we introduce a parameter-free \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gate} within the encoder:
\begin{equation}
    s_k = \frac{\text{Var}_t(x_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \text{Var}_t(x_j) + \epsilon}, \quad \tilde{x}_k = x_k \cdot \sigma\left(\frac{s_k - \bar{s}}{\sigma_s + \epsilon}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic sigmoid, $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ ensures numerical stability, and $\bar{s}, \sigma_s$ denote the mean and standard deviation of channel variances across the input window. For multi-channel inputs ($K > 1$), this dynamically prioritizes informative non-stationary coordinates without discarding quiet sensor channels. For single-channel univariate streams ($K = 1$), $s_1 = \bar{s}$ and $\sigma_s = 0$; we define the gate to smoothly reduce to identity scaling ($\tilde{x}_1 = x_1$), ensuring consistent execution across univariate and multivariate streams.

\subsubsection{2. Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Real-world physical anomalies operate across disparate temporal scales, ranging from high-frequency electrical spikes to gradual thermal drifts. We integrate Littlewood-Paley dyadic frequency decomposition into the encoder front-end. The input sequence is decomposed into dyadic octave bands via bandpass filters $\psi_j(\omega) = \psi(2^j \omega)$ satisfying the partition of unity:
\begin{equation}
    |\phi(\omega)|^2 + \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} |\psi(2^j \omega)|^2 = 1, \quad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi].
\end{equation}
This multi-scale frequency decomposition enables the model to isolate high-frequency transient anomalies from low-frequency baseline diurnal oscillations without expanding parameter count.

\subsubsection{3. Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT)}
Following scoring, decision alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT). In standard SPOT, the extreme threshold $\tau$ is determined by fitting a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$ to excesses over an initial threshold $u$ at risk probability $q$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N_u}{N}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right].
\end{equation}
To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via the empirical excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ increases, raising the threshold to suppress false alarms from volatile noise bursts; on light-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 < 0$), the threshold tightens to maximize detection sensitivity.
