\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Windowing, Multi-Depth TCN Hierarchy Tapping, and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to binary alarm decisions. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and partitions the stream at index $t$ into a \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$) and a \emph{suspect target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). For $K > 1$, a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem filters each channel prior to $1 \times 1$ mixing ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass). Channels are decomposed across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid normalized into an exact amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), processed by per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that minimize pointwise observation error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$ and inherit heavy-tailed sensor noise (Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:related}), we map windows into a compact 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ onto the sphere $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, where target weights $\phi$ track online weights $\theta$ via an exponential moving average (EMA), $\phi_k \leftarrow m \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m) \theta_k$ with fixed momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ in Cohorts~3--4) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Because deep dilated TCN blocks smooth out sharp onset spikes while non-stationary streams shift the latent mean, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} introduces two structural operations inside both encoders:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth functional hierarchy tapping:} A tap head extracts $L_{\text{tap}}=3$ intermediate feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow dyadic front-end, the mid-depth TCN block, and the terminal TCN block. For each tap $\ell$, the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy are concatenated into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6 d_{\text{model}}}$, projected into $\mathbb{R}^D$, and fused with the deep representation $\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}}$ via a bounded gate $g = 0.5\,\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} = (1 - g)\,\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}} + g\,\operatorname{LN}\bigl(W_2\,\phi_{\text{GELU}}(\operatorname{LN}(W_1 \mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}}))\bigr).
        \label{eq:multidepth_tap}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} An online running centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ updated at rate $0.05$ on training batches is copied synchronously to the EMA target tap head and subtracted from both context and target fused vectors before terminal LayerNorm projection $z = \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}) \in \sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, canceling common-mode baseline drift between windows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Nested Causal Multi-Horizon Rollout and Variance-Floor Decorrelation Objective}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a single full-window forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$ trained with predictive MSE plus a log-determinant volume term $-\frac{\beta}{D}\ln\det(\mathbf{\Sigma} + \epsilon\mathbf{I})$ on predicted and context batches ($\beta = 0.05$), a smooth relative of VICReg variance-covariance penalties \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. Terminal LayerNorm bounds representations on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ and compresses residual kurtosis, but pooling the $S=64$ target window into one vector averages out short-lived sub-horizon transients.

To resolve sub-window dynamics across target horizon $S$ (default $S=64$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), Stage~2 of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} partitions $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ into $M=4$ nested causal prefixes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ of lengths $S_m = \max(1, \lfloor mS/M \rfloor)$ ($\{16, 32, 48, 64\}$ at $S=64$). The EMA target encoder $E_\phi$ encodes each prefix into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. Starting from $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$, a sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ using learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the full-horizon anchor $P_\theta$ forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ ($m < M$) and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, training minimizes multi-horizon mean-squared error (MSE) plus a variance-floor and decorrelation penalty:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MHR}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr),
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ penalizes low variance and cross-covariance of centered batch predictions $\mathbf{Z}_c = \mathbf{Z} - \mathbf{1}_B \bar{\mathbf{z}}^\top$ with empirical covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{Z}) = (B-1)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_c^\top \mathbf{Z}_c$ and marginal standard deviations $\sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}) = \sqrt{\Sigma_{dd}(\mathbf{Z}) + \epsilon}$ ($\gamma = 0.75$, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$), defined by $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dd} = \sqrt{\max(0, \gamma - \sigma_d(\mathbf{Z}))}$ and $[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})]_{dj} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Sigma_{dj}(\mathbf{Z})$ ($d \neq j$). This enforces a variance floor plus decorrelation (full whitening occurs in Stage~3 via Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage), remaining well-conditioned when $B = 16 < D = 32$ and raising effective representation rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}. Archive-wide evaluations test three-horizon \emph{Speculative-JEPA} across $141$ streams (prefixes $\{16, 32, 64\}$, weights $(0.20, 0.25, 0.55)$, and the 48-step residual interpolated from 32 and 64, giving $192$ distinct statistics) and four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} across $162$ streams ($15$ datasets).

\subsection{Stage 3: Calibration-Stabilized Whitening, Surgical Weight-Space Quenching, and 224-Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Why does collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a single 1D score degrade point-wise F1? Let $\mathbf{E}(t) = [\mathbf{e}_1(t), \dots, \mathbf{e}_M(t)]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ stack the $M=4$ raw sub-horizon prediction residuals $\mathbf{e}_m(t) = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t) - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ across all $MD = 128$ coordinates. Fitting the Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = (\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}} + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I}_{MD})^{-1/2} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ \cite{ledoit2004well} and mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{MD}$ on nominal training windows produces Mahalanobis-whitened residuals $\mathbf{E}^{\text{white}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ with unit training variance per coordinate. On unseen nominal calibration windows, however, one or two whitened directions often experience out-of-sample variance growth (\emph{hijacker dials}) where $\mathbf{\Omega}$ amplifies small covariance shifts. Summing all 128 coordinates into a 1D scalar score allows those one or two drifting coordinates to dominate the calibration tail and inflate the scalar threshold. Stage~3 prevents both precision amplification and 1D collapse through two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Surgical weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and calibration-stabilized whitening:} Inverting $\mathbf{\Omega}$ and predictor Jacobians without data reconstructs the 224-dial coordinate roles at $88.4\%$ accuracy ($162$ streams) and shows that stochastic gradient descent traps $12.7\%$ of horizon-embedding energy in the saw-tooth mode $\boldsymbol{\phi}_3 = [-1, +3, -3, +1]^\top/\sqrt{20}$. A constant baseline shift between training and calibration offsets the mean residual $\bar{\mathbf{e}}_m = \mathbb{E}_{\text{cal}}[\mathbf{e}_m(t)]$ on $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, while covariance rotation inflates variance along steep precision eigenvectors ($\lambda_k \gg 1$). Because terminal LayerNorm maps pre-normalized outputs $\mathbf{u}_m$ onto $\sqrt{D}\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ with first-order Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_{\text{LN}}(\hat{\bm{\mu}}_m) = (\sqrt{D}/\sigma_{\text{pre},m})(\mathbf{I}_D - \hat{\bm{\mu}}_m\hat{\bm{\mu}}_m^\top)$, shifting each predictor's terminal bias by $\Delta\mathbf{b}_m = (\sigma_{\text{pre},m}/\sqrt{D})(\mathbf{I}_D - \hat{\bm{\mu}}_m\hat{\bm{\mu}}_m^\top)\bar{\mathbf{e}}_m$ cancels the stationary tangent-space calibration drift to first order while leaving time-varying anomaly excursions $\mathbf{e}_m(t) - \bar{\mathbf{e}}_m$ invariant. Simultaneously, tempering $\mathbf{\Omega}$ via smooth saturation $\tilde{\lambda}_k = \lambda_k / (1 + \lambda_k/\lambda_{\max})$, damping output rows on the top $k_{\text{rogue}} \le 4$ variance-inflated coordinates, and decoupling their off-diagonal precision entries prevent an unstable direction from contaminating clean coordinates (accepted only when late-half calibration trips do not increase; ablations in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Any coordinate with $\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) > 1.10$ is then blended toward $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t) = e_{m,c}(t) / (\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{raw}}(m,c) + 10^{-4})$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon residual decomposition into $7D = 224$ dials:} By structural analogy to Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} (applied here along the discrete lookahead axis $m \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ rather than a continuous fluid field), we expand $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into $7D = 224$ non-negative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~\emph{sub-horizon position magnitudes ($4D = 128$ dials)} $d_{\text{pos},m,c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ ($m \in \{1..4\}$);
    (b)~\emph{horizon-mean (drift) dials ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{drift},c}(t) = |\bar{e}_c(t)| = |\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$;
    (c)~\emph{horizon-fluctuation dials ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sigma'_c(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t))^2}$; and
    (d)~\emph{endpoint-change (acceleration) dials ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} characterizes this multi-horizon residual representation, showing that the drift and fluctuation dials orthogonally partition sub-horizon energy and that the horizon-fluctuation spectrum lies on a bounded interval (a structural representation property rather than a guarantee of detection accuracy).

\begin{proposition}[Structural Characterization of Multi-Horizon Residual Energy and Spectral Concentration]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) denote the stabilized multi-horizon residual field with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\top \mathbf{1}_M \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Then:
(i)~\emph{Coordinate-wise energy orthogonality and endpoint bound:} For every coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,D\}$, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M d_{\text{pos},m,c}^2(t) = d_{\text{drift},c}^2(t) + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$, and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$.
(ii)~\emph{Spectral concentration bounds for the horizon Gram matrix:} Let $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ have ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ ($\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$). For any $\gamma > -1/2$, the spectral concentration ratio (following Lieb--Thirring power-sum moments \cite{lieb1976inequalities}),
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2}} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr] \quad (\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}),
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant, attaining $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) iff fluctuation energy is isotropic across $M-1$ modes, and $1$ iff $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ yields a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + e'_{m,c})^2$ with $\sum_{m=1}^M e'_{m,c} = 0$ yields the energy identity, and Cauchy--Schwarz on $e'_{M,c} - e'_{1,c}$ gives $d_{\text{accel},c}^2 \le 2(e'^2_{M,c} + e'^2_{1,c}) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$, proving~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$ forces $\lambda_1 = 0$, so $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$, yielding (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Zero-Collapse 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel, Onset Relays, and Temporal Attribution}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
In Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}), each of the 224 dials receives its own binary circuit breaker calibrated on nominal data. Because all 224 dials derive from the same $4 \times 32$ residual field, they are correlated by construction (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the 19 development streams; simultaneous trip counts have $7.2\times$ the variance of an independence model), so the quorums below are calibrated directly on joint nominal trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and nominal hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ (and $j \in \{1,\dots,128\}$ in the 128-bit sub-block panel) is standardized by its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled median absolute deviation (MAD) $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. To quarantine any drifting dial individually, each tripwire $\tau_j$ scales its $98.5$th calibration percentile by a two-factor nominal hijack guard $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}},
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}$ is the ratio of second-half to first-half calibration 98th percentiles whenever $>1.35$ ($1$ otherwise), and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$ is $(m_j + s_j)/(1.5\,s_{\text{group},j})$ whenever $>1.25$ ($1$ otherwise, where $s_{\text{group},j}$ is the perspective-group median of $m_j + s_j$).
    \item \emph{32 fast right-edge onset relays:} Each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines a stricter edge switch $b_{p,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_{p,c}(t) > q_{0.993}(z_{p,c,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_{p,c}^{1.25}\bigr)$ and fires its onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when the terminal sub-block ($m=4$) coincides with endpoint acceleration, fluctuation, or the third sub-block ($m=3$):
    \begin{equation}
        r_c(t) = b_{\text{pos},4,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \wedge \bigl(b_{\text{accel},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{fluc},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{pos},3,c}^{\text{edge}}(t)\bigr).
        \label{eq:onset_relay}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Causal trailing vs.\ prefix-routed buffered attribution and Boolean quorums:} Under \emph{causal trailing attribution}, window switches at index $t$ are emitted solely at the right endpoint $\tau = t + S - 1$ ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable, with structural reporting lag $S-1$ vs.\ empirical detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, each perspective votes only over the steps inside $[t, t+S)$ it physically observes ($[t, t+S_m)$ for sub-block $m$, $[t, t+S)$ for drift and fluctuation, and $[t+S_3, t+S_4)$ for acceleration). Summing active switches at $\tau$ gives counts $N_{224}(\tau)$, $N_{128}(\tau)$, and $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$, with combined count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau) = N_{224}(\tau) + N_{128}(\tau) + 2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ for PR-AUC ranking. Binary alarms trigger when any counter exceeds its calibration quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_{\text{edge}} = \max\bigl(1.25\,q_{0.999}(N_{\text{edge},\text{cal}}),\, 2.5\bigr)$, \emph{224-Bit Breaker} sets $K_{224} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{224,\text{cal}}),\, 5.0\bigr)$ and $K_{128} = \max\bigl(1.12\,q_{0.995}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\, 3.5\bigr)$, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} sets $K_{224} = \max\bigl(1.18\,q_{0.9985}(N_{224,\text{cal}}),\, 5.5\bigr)$ and $K_{128} = \max\bigl(1.20\,q_{0.9985}(N_{128,\text{cal}}),\, 4.0\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{1D Scalar Collapse Controls:} To compare against 1D scalar collapse on identical backbones (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), we evaluate the saliency gate $g_d(\mathbf{v}) = (1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha\sqrt{d\sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}$ with $w_i \propto \exp(|v_i|/(\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}}))$ ($\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2/\sqrt{d} + 10^{-6}$, $\alpha = \tau_g = 0.5$) \label{eq:saliency_gate}. Single-horizon TS-JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$, and 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))^2 + \max_m g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))^2)}$ modulated by $\mathcal{S}_{\text{LT}}(t) = \mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t)\bigl(1 + \alpha_{\text{LT}}\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t)))\bigr)$ ($\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$), calibrated via SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing} ($u = p_{98}, q = 10^{-3}$).
