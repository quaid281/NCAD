\label{sec:proposed_method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a multivariate stream partitioned into context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize pointwise error $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}\| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2$, which can weight channels with different noise scales unequally; whether this consumes model capacity is not tested here. We instead adopt the JEPA framework \cite{lecun2022path}: a context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an EMA momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}}$, with $\phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k$, $m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995]$. The objective forecasts target representations in latent space and tests whether structured priors condition prediction residuals for tail calibration.

\subsection{Latent Predictor Architectures and Dynamical Inductive Priors}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
To examine whether structured dynamical inductive biases stabilize representation forecasting, we evaluate an unconstrained latent predictor alongside three candidate formulations inspired by physical dynamical principles:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
Motivated by Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}, where nonlinear trajectories are mapped via linear operators on observable functions, we parameterize the predictive latent transition via a learned operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}$. To prevent collapse onto a low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or to zero (energy collapse), the regularizer decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ eigenvalues of $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^\top$, and $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i$ total energy. For $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) > 0$, the scale-free modal distribution is $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2$. The Von Neumann spectral entropy is $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i: p_i > 0} p_i \log p_i$ (with $0 \log 0 = 0$). The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} measures Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^\top$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while dimensional collapse to rank 1 yields $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \log D$. Minimizing $\Delta\mathcal{H}$ drives $\mathbf{K}$ toward an isometric modal equipartition rather than low-rank dominant modal truncation as in classical Koopman decompositions, acting directly on learned embeddings without infinite-dimensional lifting.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) without explosive gain, we impose a non-negative Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) = \max(\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 / (\gamma D), \epsilon)$ with numerical floor $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. The floored barrier caps penalty at $\approx 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$ rather than diverging. With $\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$, $r_{\text{safe}} \approx 1$ and training begins with vanishing barrier penalty. The composite regularizer and objective are:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$ and $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$. All shared representation components are held fixed across models and controls. $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts on the operator matrix; it does not analytically prevent encoder representation collapse, which is mitigated empirically by EMA target dynamics and stop-gradient operations \cite{grill2020bootstrap,assran2023self}.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Non-Triviality and Barrier Behavior]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$, and non-degenerate context--target coupling $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} {z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)}}^\top \neq \mathbf{0}$. Then: (i) under the floored barrier ($\epsilon = 10^{-6}$), the energy penalty is bounded ($\le 12.8\,\beta_{\text{norm}}$) yet $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is strictly not a local or global minimizer; and (ii) under unfloored idealization ($\epsilon \to 0^+$), $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.)

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Operator Non-Degeneracy vs. Representation Collapse):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} applies to the transition operator $\mathbf{K}$ and does not analytically prevent encoder collapse: if $E_\theta$ maps all inputs to a constant, $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ attains zero loss. Preventing representation collapse rests empirically on stop-gradient momentum dynamics.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary Quadratic Second-Moment Head}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Inspired by Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence, which separates mean flow from velocity fluctuations, we evaluate an auxiliary quadratic second-moment head in latent space. The observed matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$ is an instantaneous rank-1 projected outer product plus ridge penalty, not a physical turbulent fluctuation covariance.

A two-layer MLP predicts mean target latent $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and linear projection $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$ ($d_s = 16$) yields $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}$. The closure head $h_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})$ outputs lower-triangular $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ with positive diagonal $L_{ii} = \operatorname{softplus}(a_{ii}) + \epsilon$, reconstructing predicted covariance:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}.
\end{equation}
The training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}).
\end{equation}
At test time, the anomaly score adds the second-moment error: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. Downstream performance is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a).

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory and Flow Matching)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, a smooth field splits into a curl-free potential gradient and a solenoidal component: $v = -\nabla \Phi + \nabla \times A$. Dissipative gradient flows provide theoretical motivation:

\begin{proposition}[Idealized Continuous Gradient Flow Motivation (Non-Binding)]
\label{prop:no_limit_cycles}
Let $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$ be an autonomous gradient flow on $\mathbb{R}^D$ with $\Phi \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R})$. There exist no non-constant periodic orbits.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Proof in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:proofs}.)

\paragraph{Velocity Field, Multi-Subspace Routing, and Flow Matching}
The continuous field is $\hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi$, where $\Phi_\psi$ is a three-layer residual MLP conditioned on state $z_t$, diffusion time $t \in [0,1]$, context $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and regime projection $p_{\text{regime}}$, with tangent projection $v_\perp = v - \langle u, v\rangle u$ ($u = z_t/\|z_t\|_2$). Operational regimes use $K_r = 4$ orthonormal subspace projections $\mathbf{U}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d}$ ($d{=}8, D{=}32$), selected by maximal captured context energy $p_{\text{regime}} = \mathbf{U}_{k^*} \mathbf{U}_{k^*}^\top z_{\text{ctx}}$ with separation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{subspace}} = \frac{2}{K_r(K_r-1)} \sum_{i < j} \|\mathbf{U}_i^\top \mathbf{U}_j\|_F^2$.

Training uses conditional flow matching (OT-CFM) along linear paths between noise $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and $z_{\text{tgt}}$: $z_t = (1-t)z_{\text{noise}} + t z_{\text{tgt}}$, with loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \left[ \| \hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2 \right].
\end{equation}
At inference, the detector queries the field at the trajectory midpoint:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}\!\left(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}},\, 0.5,\, z_{\text{ctx}},\, p_{\text{regime}}\right) - z_{\text{tgt}}.
\end{equation}
Discrete midpoint queries on conditional velocity fields do not integrate an autonomous continuous trajectory and do not inherit autonomous non-periodic guarantees; Proposition~\ref{prop:no_limit_cycles} provides idealized continuous motivation and does not bind discrete scoring. Parameterizing $\hat{v}$ as a spatial gradient imposes a curl-free bias ($\nabla \times \hat{v} \equiv \mathbf{0}$) with vanishing circulation $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \hat{v} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$. This constraint is hypothesized to penalize cyclic nominal orbits and degrade detection on periodic kinematics (evaluated empirically in Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors}).

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed at stride 1. For TS-JEPA, Operator-Entropy, and Potential-Flow, the score gates the latent discrepancy through $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}): $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t))$. For the second-moment head (Reynolds-Stress), $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t)) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. All shared representation stages are held fixed across variants and ablation controls (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).

\noindent\textbf{Three-Part Operational Causality Framework:}
To resolve the methodological confounding between data availability, decision latency, and evaluation attribution, we formalize detection under three operational criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:inference_timeline}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Data causality ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability):} At decision time $\tau = t + S - 1$, the model observes only history $\mathbf{X}_{:\tau} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:\tau, :]$. Neither encoder accesses future observations beyond $\tau$.
    \item \textbf{Score-emission latency ($S-1 = 63$ steps):} Because suspect representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$ encodes the horizon $\mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :]$, window score $s_t$ cannot be computed until timestep $\tau = t + S - 1$. This imposes an inherent emission latency of $S - 1 = 63$ steps: $0.98\,\text{s}$ on Daphnet ($64\,\text{Hz}$), $10.5\,\text{min}$ on SMAP, MSL, and GHL under standard 10-s ($0.1\,\text{Hz}$) sampling \cite{Hundman2018b, zhou2026mtsbench}, $63\,\text{min}$ on GECCO ($1\,\text{sample/min}$), and $63\,\text{h}$ on Metro ($1\,\text{sample/h}$).
    \item \textbf{Alarm attribution convention:} We distinguish two attribution regimes: (1)~\emph{strictly causal trailing attribution}, where score $s_t$ is emitted and evaluated strictly at $\tau$, providing zero-lookahead online monitoring; and (2)~\emph{retrospective buffered smear attribution}, where $s_t$ is attributed across the entire suspect window $[t{+}1, t{+}S]$. Under unadjusted point evaluation, buffered alarms in $[t^*-S, t^*]$ register as false alarms: buffering provides fixed-lag detection, not early warning. The two regimes represent distinct operational realities and are reported separately throughout (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, the latent JEPA architectures incorporate shared inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Coordinate Saliency Gating and the Auxiliary Channel-Gate Variant}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 25$), faults frequently originate in a localized subsystem while the remaining channels exhibit ambient noise, diluting localized anomalies in isotropic representations. The deployed detector mitigates this in latent space via an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate $g(\mathbf{e}) \equiv \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e})$ on error vectors $\mathbf{e} = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) &= (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \\
    w_d &= \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)}, \nonumber
\end{align}
amplifying sparse coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$ while remaining exactly $\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$ under isotropic errors. We fix blend parameter $\alpha = 0.5$ and softmax temperature $\tau = 0.5$ across all latent predictors. Reconstructive baselines (TimesNet, TranAD) evaluate unweighted observation errors and do not use the gate. This is the discrepancy gate used in every latent experiment: input channels are encoded unweighted, and the gate acts only on the $D$-dimensional predictive residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary Input-Channel Gate (Evaluated Variant)}
We also evaluate a strictly causal \textit{Causal Channel Saliency Gate} as an auxiliary variant (full specification, boundary-condition verification, and the fault-type study in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:gating}): each channel is weighted by the sigmoid of its standardized context-window variance share, $g_k = \sigma\!\left((s_k - \bar{s})/(\sigma_s + \epsilon)\right)$, applied symmetrically to context and target encoders so that $\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$ (zero target leakage, with an explicit identity short-circuit at $K{=}1$). Two boundary conditions apply: quiet faults (flatlines, stuck values) cannot be isolated by variance amplification --- the fault-type study confirms they are weakly detected under \emph{every} gating regime (recall $0.02$--$0.07$) --- and abrupt regime transitions transiently perturb variance shares until the context window refills.

\paragraph{Zero-Leakage Data Provenance and Temporal Isolation}
We enforce strict temporal isolation across the training, nominal-calibration, and held-out evaluation partitions (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}): standardization statistics come only from the training partition, EVT tail parameters $(u, \sigma, \xi)$ only from the calibration partition, and evaluation proceeds chronologically with frozen weights and thresholds. The auxiliary gate's weights depend only on $x_{\text{ctx}}$; the deployed pipeline weights inputs uniformly.

\subsubsection{Multiscale Frequency Front-End}
Anomalies span temporal scales from sensor spikes to thermal drifts; the encoder front-end therefore splits each channel into $J{=}4$ smooth dyadic shells on an $N_{\text{fft}}=256$ rFFT grid, normalized bin-wise into an exact amplitude partition of unity, $\sum_j \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ (measured deviation $< 1.2\times10^{-7}$; construction details in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:math_diagnostics}). Each shell feeds a causal 1D convolution ($k \in \{15,9,5,3\}$ coarse-to-fine), recombined by learned scale routing. Because the shells overlap smoothly at band edges, the front-end is \emph{less} frequency-localized than standard wavelet banks: per-band in-octave spectral mass is $\{0.50, 0.33, 0.20, {\approx}1.00\}$ versus $\{0.78, 0.50, 0.55, 0.82\}$ (Haar) and $\{0.86, 0.63, 0.64, 0.87\}$ (DB4) (filterbank frequency diagnostics). We therefore describe it as a Fourier-domain multiscale front-end, not an exact Littlewood--Paley octave decomposition, and measure its empirical contribution by matched ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}b).

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Binary alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}: excesses over a high threshold $u$ (98th percentile) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via an empirical heuristic factor governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ rises to suppress volatile-noise false alarms; on light-tailed ones, it tightens for sensitivity.

We emphasize that this is an engineering heuristic for pre-asymptotic finite-sample tails, not an asymptotic EVT theorem. The primary deployed pipeline relies on standard SPOT ($u=p_{98}, q=10^{-3}$) without the multiplier; the kurtosis heuristic is evaluated strictly as an explicit sensitivity arm (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), where it degrades out-of-sample risk control ($5.26\% \to 17.68\%$ FPR) due to threshold lowering on non-positive kurtosis blocks.

More broadly, EVT does not eliminate threshold-calibration difficulty: under $q = 10^{-3}$ (ideal asymptotic FPR $0.1\%$), measured held-out nominal FPR settles at $5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ for the JEPAs and $\approx 22\%$ for TimesNet/TranAD on the 33-stream cell-pooled buffered cohort ($7.6\text{--}8.6\%$ vs.\ $\approx 30\%$ on the 19-stream macro cohort, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}), owing to finite-sample variance, residual autocorrelation, and non-stationary drift between nominal training and deployment. The calibrated finding of this paper is narrower: latent predictive residuals produce substantially fewer false alarms than observation-space residuals under identical calibration, reflecting lighter tails at the cost of lower causal recall. Thresholds use static EVT on held-out nominal splits; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis.
