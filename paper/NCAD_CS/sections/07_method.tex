\label{sec:proposed_method}

This section formalizes the mathematical formulation and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Prediction Paradigm}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a continuous multivariate time-series sequence consisting of $K$ observed sensor variables over $T$ timesteps. The sequence is partitioned into historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect/target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, where $C$ and $S$ denote the respective temporal horizons.

Existing deep learning baselines (e.g., TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) frame anomaly detection as pointwise reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \mathbb{E}\left[ \| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In complex multi-sensor telemetry, ambient high-frequency stochastic jitter dominates the Frobenius norm, causing models to waste representational bandwidth reconstructing sensor noise rather than modeling system state dynamics (leading to noise drowning).

To overcome this, we adopt the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm \cite{lecun2022path}. A context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, updated via:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k, \quad m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995].
\end{equation}
The core objective is to predict target representations in latent space under physical dynamical constraints.

\subsection{The Physics-Informed JEPA Triad}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Rather than relying on unconstrained Euclidean regression, we incorporate physical domain regularizers through three complementary geometric formulations:

\subsubsection{1. Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
In dynamical systems theory, non-linear trajectories can be analyzed via linear operators acting on observable functions. In our architecture, the encoder maps the observation window to a finite-dimensional latent observable vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^D$. We define the predictive transition between context and target latent states via a learned transition matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}.
\end{equation}
To regularize modal energy distribution across latent coordinates and prevent the transition operator from collapsing onto a degenerate low-rank subspace, we define a regularized density operator $\bm{\rho} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ from the operator's Gram matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \bm{\rho} = \frac{\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T}{\text{Tr}(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T) + \epsilon} + \frac{\epsilon}{D}\mathbf{I}_D, \quad \tilde{\bm{\rho}} = \frac{\bm{\rho}}{\text{Tr}(\bm{\rho})},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ provides Tikhonov-style diagonal loading. We evaluate the Von Neumann spectral entropy across the eigenvalues $\lambda_i(\tilde{\bm{\rho}})$ obtained via symmetric eigendecomposition:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i(\tilde{\bm{\rho}}) \log (\lambda_i(\tilde{\bm{\rho}}) + \epsilon).
\end{equation}
The diagonal loading $\frac{\epsilon}{D}\mathbf{I}_D$ strictly bounds eigenvalues away from zero and ensures differentiable, numerically stable gradients throughout backpropagation, avoiding SVD gradient singularities at degenerate or repeated spectra. Maximal spectral entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\text{max}} = \log D$ is achieved when modal energy is dispersed isotropically across all $D$ latent dimensions ($\lambda_i = 1/D$). To penalize dimensional collapse and preserve modal expressivity, the pretraining objective maximizes spectral dispersion:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 - \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 10^{-3}$. Note that representation collapse of the encoder is prevented by the asymmetric EMA target encoder with stop-gradient ($\bot$), while $\mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ specifically acts as an inductive regularizer on the operator spectrum during pretraining.

\subsubsection{2. Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Covariance Alignment)}
In multi-channel telemetry representing coupled flows (thermal, convective, hydraulic), interactions across latent state dimensions are governed by transport phenomena. Inspired by hydrodynamic \textit{Reynolds decomposition}, the target encoder processes the target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ through its convolutional backbone prior to global temporal pooling, outputting an unpooled sequence of feature vectors $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{tgt}} = [z_1, \dots, z_S]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$. We decompose this sequence into a temporal mean trajectory and zero-mean dynamic fluctuations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{tgt}}^T + \mathbf{Z}',
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{tgt}} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S z_s \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denotes the temporal mean, and $\mathbf{Z}' \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$ denotes dynamic fluctuations.

The inter-coordinate momentum transport across the temporal window is characterized by the empirical second-moment fluctuation covariance matrix across latent coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} = \frac{1}{S} {\mathbf{Z}'}^T \mathbf{Z}' \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}.
\end{equation}
Because the $S=64$ steps are generated by a TCN with an effective receptive field of 57 steps, features exhibit temporal autocorrelation; $\mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}}$ functions as an empirical coordinate-covariance penalty rather than an asymptotic sample covariance. The Reynolds-Stress predictor parameterizes both the mean target transition $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = f_{\text{mean}}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ and a predicted fluctuation covariance $\mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T + \epsilon \mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, where $\mathbf{L}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ is a lower-triangular Cholesky factor with positive diagonal entries enforced via softplus. The pretraining objective aligns both the mean trajectory and dynamic covariance across latent dimensions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \| \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_R \| \mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} \|_F^2,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_R = 10^{-2}$. At inference time, the anomaly score $\mathcal{S}(t) = \|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)\|_2^2$ evaluates mean trajectory divergence in the learned metric space.

\subsubsection{3. Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
Under the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition theorem, any sufficiently smooth vector field can be uniquely resolved into an irrotational (curl-free) conservative potential gradient and a solenoidal (divergence-free) rotational field. 

In nominal physical equilibria, conservative dynamical systems conserve potential energy along phase trajectories. We parameterize the latent transition dynamics through the gradient of a scalar potential network $\Phi_\psi(z)$ parameterized with smooth $C^2$ activation functions (e.g., SiLU):
\begin{equation}
    \hat{v}(z) = \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z), \quad \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = z_{\text{ctx}} + \hat{v}(z_{\text{ctx}}).
\end{equation}
Because the curl of the gradient of any $C^2$ scalar field is identically zero ($\nabla \times (\nabla \Phi) \equiv \mathbf{0}$), parameterizing the velocity field as $\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$ guarantees that the predicted trajectory field is \textit{strictly irrotational by construction}. Physical anomalies that introduce non-conservative work, dissipative friction, or unmodeled circulation diverge from any integrable potential field, producing large residual discrepancies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \| \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{ctx}}) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}
We note that conservative gradient flows cannot possess periodic limit cycles; while this inductive prior provides strong false-alarm suppression in stable physical regimes, systems with multi-periodic oscillations (such as human locomotion in Daphnet) benefit from harmonic Fourier formulations (such as TimesNet).

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, sliding context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect target windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed with a stride of 1 timestep. For all JEPA architectures, the anomaly score is computed as the latent predictive discrepancy between the predicted target representation $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ and the target encoder representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(t) = \| \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}
While context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ is strictly causal (depending only on historical observations), evaluating suspect window $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ requires observing $S=64$ upcoming timesteps, establishing an operational lookahead buffer of 64 timesteps at inference. In continuous streaming, an anomaly occurring at timestep $t^*$ enters the target window when $t = t^* - S$, causing the model to register elevated discrepancy up to $S=64$ steps prior to ground-truth peak onset. Under strict unadjusted point-level evaluation, these pre-onset detections are counted as point-level false alarms, establishing an empirical false-alarm baseline floor. In our benchmark evaluation, we report \textit{Dense} scoring, where sliding windows advance with a stride of 1 timestep and the anomaly score $\mathcal{S}(t)$ is evaluated at each decision time $t$. In operational incident analysis (Section VII.D), we additionally evaluate \textit{Middle} alignment, where the anomaly score is centered at $t + S/2$ to balance pre-onset and post-onset window overlap. Baseline models (TimesNet and TranAD) are evaluated under the exact identical sliding-window evaluation protocol, lookahead horizon, and standardization.

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{1. Coordinate Saliency Gating}
On high-dimensional streams ($K \ge 50$), anomalous signals frequently manifest in only a small subset of channels while remaining channels exhibit ambient noise. To prevent dimensional drowning, we introduce a parameter-free \textit{Coordinate Saliency Gate} applied symmetrically to input features in both context and target encoders:
\begin{equation}
    s_k = \frac{\text{Var}_t(x_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \text{Var}_t(x_j) + \epsilon}, \quad \tilde{x}_k = x_k \cdot \sigma\left(\frac{s_k - \bar{s}}{\sigma_s + \epsilon}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic sigmoid, $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ ensures numerical stability, $\bar{s} = 1/K$ represents the uniform variance share expectation across all $K$ channels, and $\sigma_s$ denotes the standard deviation of $\{s_k\}_{k=1}^K$. The term $(s_k - 1/K)/\sigma_s$ represents the standardized spatial z-score of channel variance. For multi-channel inputs ($K > 1$), this dynamically prioritizes informative non-stationary coordinates without discarding quiet sensor channels. For single-channel univariate streams ($K = 1$), $s_1 = \bar{s} = 1$ and $\sigma_s = 0$; the gate smoothly reduces to identity scaling ($\tilde{x}_1 = x_1$). We note that while variance-share gating suppresses background sensor jitter, sensor freeze faults ($\text{Var}(x_k) \to 0$) receive low variance scores; such flatline anomalies must therefore be detected through mean trajectory prediction error rather than variance amplification alone.

\subsubsection{2. Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Real-world physical anomalies operate across disparate temporal scales, ranging from high-frequency electrical spikes to gradual thermal drifts. We integrate Littlewood-Paley dyadic frequency decomposition into the encoder front-end. The input sequence is decomposed into $J=4$ dyadic octave bands via analytic bandpass filters $\psi_j(\omega) = \psi(2^j \omega)$ satisfying the partition of unity:
\begin{equation}
    |\phi(\omega)|^2 + \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} |\psi(2^j \omega)|^2 = 1, \quad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi].
\end{equation}
This multi-scale frequency decomposition enables the model to isolate high-frequency transient anomalies from low-frequency baseline diurnal oscillations without expanding parameter count.

\subsubsection{3. Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT)}
Following scoring, decision alarms $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT). In standard SPOT, the extreme threshold $\tau$ is determined by fitting a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$ to excesses over an initial threshold $u$ at risk probability $q$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right].
\end{equation}
To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via the empirical excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ increases, raising the threshold to suppress false alarms from volatile noise bursts; on light-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 < 0$), the threshold tightens to maximize detection sensitivity. In our primary benchmark (Section VII.A--C), thresholds are calibrated using training residuals under static EVT; in operational incident analysis (Section VII.D), dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated to track streaming baseline drift.
