\subsection{Stage 1: Multiscale Windowing, Multi-Depth TCN Hierarchy Tapping, and Barycenter Centering}
\label{subsec:architecture}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
Consider a multivariate telemetry stream $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ recording $K$ sensor channels over $T$ timesteps. Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture} illustrates the four-stage pipeline from raw telemetry to binary alarm decisions. In Stage~1, a sliding window of length $W = C + S = 320$ advances at stride $1$ and splits the stream at index $t$ into a historical \emph{context window} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ ($C = 256$) and a future \emph{target horizon} $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ ($S = 64$, up to causal decision timestamp $\tau = t + S - 1$). To separate slow drift from high-frequency vibration before cross-channel mixing, multichannel inputs ($K > 1$) pass through a causal depthwise 1D convolutional stem ($K \le 1$ uses an identity bypass) and are split across $J=4$ smooth dyadic shells on a $256$-point real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid normalized into an amplitude partition of unity ($\sum_{j=1}^J \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1$ within $1.2\times 10^{-7}$), filtered by per-shell causal 1D convolutions (kernels $\{15, 9, 5, 3\}$), and recombined via a softmax router into a causal dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}.

Unlike reconstructive detectors that penalize raw waveform error $\|\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|_F^2$, we map windows into a compact 32-dimensional latent space ($D=32$) using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path}. Dual online ($E_\theta$) and target ($E_\phi$) encoders map $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$ and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ to approximately radius-$\sqrt D$ layer-normalized vectors; the normalization epsilon prevents exact sphere membership. Target weights $\phi$ track online weights $\theta$ via an exponential moving average (EMA), $\phi_k \leftarrow m \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m) \theta_k$ with momentum $m = 0.996$ ($0.995$ in archive cohorts) \cite{grill2020bootstrap, assran2023self}. Because deep dilated TCN stacks can smooth sharp onset spikes while non-stationary regimes shift the latent mean, Stage~1 introduces two operations inside both encoders:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Multi-depth functional hierarchy tapping:} To preserve fast transient spikes alongside long-range context, a tap head extracts $L_{\text{tap}}=3$ feature maps $\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T_w \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=48$) from the shallow dyadic stem, mid-depth TCN block, and terminal TCN block. Concatenating the channel-centered terminal state and temporal root-mean-square (RMS) energy of each tap forms $\mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6 d_{\text{model}}}$, which is projected to $\mathbb{R}^D$ and fused with the deep representation $\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}}$ via a bounded gate $g = 0.5\,\sigma(\theta_g) \le 0.5$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} = (1 - g)\,\mathbf{z}_{\text{deep}} + g\,\operatorname{LN}\bigl(W_2\,\phi_{\text{GELU}}(\operatorname{LN}(W_1 \mathbf{h}_{\text{tap}}))\bigr).
        \label{eq:multidepth_tap}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Synchronized barycenter centering:} An online running centroid $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (updated at rate $0.05$ on training batches) is copied synchronously to the EMA target encoder and subtracted from both context and target vectors prior to terminal LayerNorm, $z = \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{fused}} - \bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}})$. This attenuates learned common-mode offsets without guaranteeing invariance to unseen drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Nested Causal Multi-Horizon Rollout and Variance-Floor Decorrelation Objective}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
In the single-horizon baseline (\emph{TS-JEPA}), a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) predictor $P_\theta: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ maps $z_{\text{ctx}}$ to a full-window forecast $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}})$, trained with predictive mean-squared error (MSE) and a log-determinant volume term $-\frac{\beta}{D}\ln\det(\mathbf{\Sigma} + \epsilon\mathbf{I})$ ($\beta = 0.05$) related to VICReg \cite{bardes2021vicreg}. LayerNorm limits each vector's scale but does not bound its temporal tail kurtosis; pooling an $S=64$ window into one vector can average out brief faults.

To resolve how a trajectory evolves across the target window, Stage~2 of \emph{Laminar-JEPA} uses $M=4$ nested prefixes $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[0 : S_m, :]$ with $S_m=\min(S,\max(2,\lfloor mS/M\rfloor))$ for $m<M$ and $S_M=S$ ($\{16,32,48,64\}$ at $S=64$). Each is encoded by $E_\phi$ into $z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. At $S=1$, all four targets contain the same observation; distinct residuals arise from the predictor rollout, not from distinct target horizons. Starting from $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(0)} \equiv z_{\text{ctx}}$, a recurrent sub-horizon predictor $P_{\text{sub}}$ rolls out sequential forecasts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_{\text{sub}}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m-1)} + \mathbf{p}_m)\bigr)$ for $m \in \{1,\dots,M-1\}$ using learned horizon embeddings $\mathbf{p}_m \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while the full-horizon anchor $P_\theta$ predicts $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(M)} = \operatorname{LN}\bigl(P_\theta(z_{\text{ctx}} + \mathbf{p}_M)\bigr)$. With horizon weights $w_m = \frac{1}{2(M-1)}$ ($m < M$) and $w_M = \frac{1}{2}$, training minimizes multi-horizon MSE alongside a non-collapse matrix penalty $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MHR}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{w_m}{D} \Bigl(\bigl\|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)} - \operatorname{sg}\bigl(z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_2^2 + \bigl\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma\bigl(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr\|_F^2\Bigr).
    \label{eq:soliton_reynolds_loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_\gamma(\mathbf{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ penalizes collapse without requiring $B>D$. Its diagonal entries are $\sqrt{\max(0,\gamma-\sigma_d)}$, where $\sigma_d$ uses the batch-biased variance and $10^{-4}$ stabilization ($\gamma=0.75$). Off-diagonal entries are the sample covariances $\Sigma_{dj}/\sqrt{2}$ ($d\ne j$), computed with the $B-1$ divisor. The distinct variance conventions matter for exact correspondence with the objective. At $B=16<D=32$, the measured effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}(z_{\text{tgt}})$ rises from $11.6$ to $19.8$ \cite{garrido2023rankme}. Archive experiments use three-horizon \emph{Speculative-JEPA} on $141$ streams (the fourth prefix is interpolated, $192$ statistics) and four-horizon \emph{Laminar-JEPA} on $162$ streams.

\subsection{Stage 3: Calibration-Stabilized Whitening, Surgical Weight-Space Quenching, and 224-Dial Expansion}
\label{subsec:reynolds_expansion}
Why does collapsing a multi-horizon residual tensor into a 1D scalar score degrade real-time detection? Stacking the $M=4$ prediction errors $\mathbf{e}_m(t)=z_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)-\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}^{(m)}(t)$ forms $\mathbf{E}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$ across $MD=128$ coordinates. On training windows, the sample residual covariance has eigendecomposition $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\text{res}}=\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k)\mathbf{U}^{\top}$. The deployed resolvent precision is
\[
    \mathbf{\Omega}
    =\mathbf{U}\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
    \frac{\lambda_k}{(\lambda_k+10^{-3})^2}
    \right)\mathbf{U}^{\top}.
\]
This $MD\times MD$ precision matrix is positive semidefinite. Applying $\mathbf{\Omega}^{1/2}$ to residuals centered by the training mean $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{res}}$ gives $\mathbf{E}^{\text{white}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}$. This attenuates near-null modes rather than imposing unit training variance in every coordinate. Nominal calibration shifts can still inflate selected coordinates and dominate a scalar norm's tail. Stage~3 addresses this through two mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Weight-space quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}) and calibration-stabilized whitening:} A nominal calibration shift can inflate a few residual coordinates. The implemented post-training intervention does not invert a LayerNorm Jacobian or apply an additive tangent-space bias correction. It ranks coordinates using training-to-calibration residual shifts, variance inflation, within-calibration drift, autocorrelation, and nominal trip frequency. For each of the top $k\in\{4,2\}$ coordinates, it scales the selected output row and bias of both predictor multilayer perceptrons and their output LayerNorm gains by $a_c=\operatorname{clip}((1+0.5\max(0,r_c))^{-1},0.35,0.85)$, where $r_c$ is the standardized coordinate instability score. It also moves the corresponding learned horizon embeddings by $0.35$ times the calibration-minus-training mean residual shift and blends each selected residual-mean buffer entry $75\%$ toward its recalculated calibration mean. For the $MD\times MD$ precision buffer, cross-coordinate entries incident to selected coordinates retain $15\%$ of their value; an eigendecomposition projects the result onto the positive-semidefinite cone before per-coordinate calibration-RMS rescaling. These are direct parameter and buffer edits, not an analytic cancellation of predictor bias. The candidate is accepted only if forward and reversed nominal half-split checks lower internal false alarms or near-threshold excess without increasing forward false alarms, while both main quorums retain at least $92\%$ of their original values; otherwise the original model is restored. This nominal-only check does not guarantee preservation of fault responses (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). Any coordinate with $\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) > 1.10$ is then blended toward its diagonal-standardized residual $\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t) = e_{m,c}(t) / (\text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{raw}}(m,c) + 10^{-4})$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) &= w_{m,c}\,e_{m,c}^{\text{white}}(t) + (1 - w_{m,c})\,\hat{e}_{m,c}^{\text{raw}}(t), \\
        w_{m,c} &= \frac{1}{1 + 2\max\bigl(0, \text{RMS}_{\text{cal}}^{\text{white}}(m,c) - 1.10\bigr)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:stabilized_whitening}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Reynolds-inspired multi-horizon expansion into $7D = 224$ dials:} By structural analogy to Reynolds decomposition in fluid turbulence \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} (applied along the discrete lookahead axis $m \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ of each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$), we decompose $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32}$ into horizon mean $\bar{e}_c(t) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)$ and zero-mean fluctuation $e'_{m,c}(t) = \tilde{e}_{m,c}(t) - \bar{e}_c(t)$, forming $7D = 224$ non-negative dials $\mathbf{d}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{224}$:
    (a)~\emph{sub-horizon position magnitudes ($4D = 128$)} $d_{\text{pos},m,c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{m,c}(t)|$ ($m \in \{1..4\}$);
    (b)~\emph{horizon-mean drift ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{drift},c}(t) = |\bar{e}_c(t)|$;
    (c)~\emph{horizon fluctuation ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{fluc},c}(t) = \sigma'_c(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M e'^2_{m,c}(t)}$; and
    (d)~\emph{endpoint difference ($D = 32$)} $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) = |\tilde{e}_{M,c}(t) - \tilde{e}_{1,c}(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} describes the dials after stabilization: subtracting their horizon mean separates residual energy into a mean and a zero-mean part. It also bounds endpoint difference and spectral concentration for that same residual matrix, not for the raw or independently transformed horizons; these structural bounds do not guarantee detection accuracy.

\begin{proposition}[Structural Characterization of Multi-Horizon Residual Energy and Spectral Concentration]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$ ($M \ge 2$) denote the stabilized multi-horizon residual field with horizon mean $\bar{\mathbf{e}} = M^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\top \mathbf{1}_M \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and zero-mean fluctuation matrix $\mathbf{E}' = \tilde{\mathbf{E}} - \mathbf{1}_M \bar{\mathbf{e}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D}$. Then:
(i)~\emph{Coordinate-wise energy orthogonality and endpoint bound:} For every coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,D\}$, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M d_{\text{pos},m,c}^2(t) = d_{\text{drift},c}^2(t) + d_{\text{fluc},c}^2(t)$, and $d_{\text{accel},c}(t) \le \sqrt{2M}\,d_{\text{fluc},c}(t)$.
(ii)~\emph{Spectral concentration bounds for the horizon Gram matrix:} Let $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ have ordered eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_M$ ($\mathbf{E}'^\top \mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$). For any $\gamma > -1/2$, the spectral concentration ratio (following Lieb--Thirring power-sum moments \cite{lieb1976inequalities}),
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') = \frac{\lambda_M^{\gamma + 1/2}}{\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma + 1/2}} \in \Bigl[\frac{1}{M - 1},\, 1\Bigr] \quad (\mathbf{E}' \neq \mathbf{0}),
    \label{eq:lt_bounds}
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant, attaining $\frac{1}{M-1}$ ($=\frac{1}{3}$ for $M=4$) iff fluctuation energy is isotropic across all $M-1$ modes, and $1$ iff $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{E}') = 1$. Empirically, scalar reweighting by $R_{\text{LT}}$ yields a null effect (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M (\bar{e}_c + e'_{m,c})^2$ with $\sum_{m=1}^M e'_{m,c} = 0$ yields the energy identity, and Cauchy--Schwarz on $e'_{M,c} - e'_{1,c}$ gives $d_{\text{accel},c}^2 \le 2(e'^2_{M,c} + e'^2_{1,c}) \le 2M\,d_{\text{fluc},c}^2$, proving~(i). For~(ii), $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$ forces $\lambda_1 = 0$, so $\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2} \le \sum_{m=2}^M \lambda_m^{\gamma+1/2} \le (M-1)\lambda_M^{\gamma+1/2}$, yielding (\ref{eq:lt_bounds}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 4: Zero-Collapse 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel, Onset Relays, and Temporal Attribution}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
\label{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
Rather than summing the 224 dials into a scalar norm, Stage~4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}) equips each dial with an independent binary circuit breaker calibrated on nominal data. Because all 224 dials derive from the same $4 \times 32$ residual field, their trips are correlated by construction (median effective rank $65$ of $224$ on the development cohort, with joint trip-count variance $7.2\times$ that of independent trials), so alarm quorums are calibrated directly on the empirical distribution of simultaneous nominal trip counts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Per-dial standardization and nominal hijack guards:} Each dial $j \in \{1,\dots,224\}$ is standardized by its nominal calibration median $m_j$ and scaled median absolute deviation (MAD) $s_j = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(d_{j,\text{cal}}) + 10^{-4}$ into $z_j(t) = \max\bigl(0, (d_j(t) - m_j)/s_j\bigr)$. To quarantine any individual coordinate that drifts during calibration, each dial's $98.5$th-percentile tripwire $\tau_j$ is scaled by a two-factor nominal hijack guard $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        b_j(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_j(t) > \tau_j\bigr), \quad \tau_j = q_{0.985}(z_{j,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\,\gamma_j^{\text{sc}},
        \label{eq:dial_tripwire}
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma_j^{\text{cal}}$ is the ratio of second-half to first-half calibration 98th percentiles whenever $>1.35$ ($1$ otherwise), and $\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$ is $(m_j + s_j)/(1.5\,s_{\text{group},j})$ whenever $>1.25$ ($1$ otherwise, relative to perspective-group median $s_{\text{group},j}$).
    \item \emph{32 fast right-edge onset relays:} To trigger rapidly when an abrupt fault enters the right edge of the $S$-step window while earlier prefixes ($m \in \{1, 2\}$) still observe nominal data, each coordinate $c \in \{1,\dots,32\}$ defines a strict edge switch $b_{p,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(z_{p,c}(t) > q_{0.993}(z_{p,c,\text{cal}})\,\gamma_{p,c}^{1.25}\bigr)$ and fires its onset relay $r_c(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ when a terminal-prefix trip ($m=4$) coincides with acceleration, fluctuation, or prefix $m=3$:
    \begin{equation}
        r_c(t) = b_{\text{pos},4,c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \wedge \bigl(b_{\text{accel},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{fluc},c}^{\text{edge}}(t) \vee b_{\text{pos},3,c}^{\text{edge}}(t)\bigr).
        \label{eq:onset_relay}
    \end{equation}
    \item \emph{Causal trailing vs.\ buffered attribution and Boolean quorums:} Under \emph{causal trailing attribution}, switches on window $[t,t+S)$ are emitted at its right endpoint $\tau=t+S-1$ ($\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable, with structural lag $S-1$ and empirical delay $\Delta t=t_{\text{alm}}-t^*$). Under \emph{retrospective buffered attribution}, the 224-dial vector routes position votes to $[t,t+S_m)$, drift and fluctuation votes to $[t,t+S)$, and endpoint-difference votes to $[t+S_3,t+S)$; the separate 128-dial counter and onset relays instead average uniformly over the whole $S$-step window. At $S=1$, the buffered endpoint-difference span is empty. Summing active breakers gives $N_{224}(\tau)$, $N_{128}(\tau)$, and $N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ (ranking score $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)=N_{224}(\tau)+N_{128}(\tau)+2N_{\text{edge}}(\tau)$ for PR-AUC). Binary alarms trigger when any counter exceeds its nominal-calibration quorum:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{y}(\tau) = \mathbb{I}\bigl(&N_{224}(\tau) > K_{224} \;\vee\; N_{128}(\tau) > K_{128} \\
        &\vee\; N_{\text{edge}}(\tau) > K_{\text{edge}}\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:panel_quorum}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_{\text{edge}} = \max\bigl(1.25\,q_{0.999}(N_{\text{edge},\text{cal}}),\, 2.5\bigr)$, \emph{224-Bit Breaker} sets $(K_{224}, K_{128}) = \bigl(\max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 5.0),\allowbreak\, \max(1.12\,q_{0.995}, 3.5)\bigr)$ on nominal trip counts, and \emph{224-Bit Strict} tightens them to $\bigl(\max(1.18\,q_{0.9985}, 5.5),\allowbreak\, \max(1.20\,q_{0.9985}, 4.0)\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{1D Scalar Collapse Controls:} To isolate the gain of the 224-bit panel over 1D scalar scoring on identical backbones (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), we compare against a soft coordinate-saliency pooler $g_d(\mathbf{v}) = (1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \alpha\sqrt{d\sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2}$ with softmax weights $w_i \propto \exp(|v_i|/(\tau_g \sigma_{\mathbf{v}}))$ ($\sigma_{\mathbf{v}} = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2/\sqrt{d} + 10^{-6}$, $\alpha = \tau_g = 0.5$). Single-horizon TS-JEPA scores $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TS}}(t) = g_D(\mathbf{e}(t))$, and 1D Multi-Horizon Reynolds JEPA combines full-field and peak-prefix norms via $\mathcal{S}_{\text{env}}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(g_{MD}(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}(t))^2 + \max_m g_D(\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_m(t))^2)}$ (scaled by $1 + \alpha_{\text{LT}}\max(0, \tanh(0.5\,z_{\text{LT}}(t)))$ for $\alpha_{\text{LT}} \in \{0.12, 0\}$), calibrated via extreme-value SPOT \cite{siffer2017anomaly, balkema1974residual, grimshaw1993computing} ($u = p_{98}, q = 10^{-3}$).
