This section formalizes the mathematical formulation and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a continuous multivariate time-series sequence consisting of $K$ observed sensor variables over $T$ timesteps. The sequence is partitioned into historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect/target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, where $C$ and $S$ denote the respective temporal horizons.

Existing deep learning baselines (e.g., TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) frame anomaly detection as pointwise reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \mathbb{E}\left[ \| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In complex multi-sensor telemetry, ambient high-frequency stochastic jitter dominates the Frobenius norm, causing models to waste representational bandwidth reconstructing sensor noise rather than modeling system state dynamics (leading to noise drowning).

To overcome this, we adopt the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework \cite{lecun2022path}. A context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, updated via:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k, \quad m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995].
\end{equation}
The core objective is to predict target representations in latent space under physical dynamical constraints.

\subsection{The Physics-Inspired JEPA Triad}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Rather than relying on unconstrained Euclidean regression, we incorporate physical domain regularizers through three complementary geometric formulations:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
In dynamical systems theory, non-linear trajectories can be analyzed via linear operators acting on observable functions (Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}). In our architecture, the encoder maps the observation window to a finite-dimensional latent observable vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^D$. We parameterize the predictive transition between context and target latent states via a learned transition operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}.
\end{equation}
To prevent the operator from collapsing onto a degenerate low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or degenerating to zero (energy collapse), we formulate an operator-entropy regularizer that decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote the singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, with corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ of the symmetric Gram matrix $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T$. The total operator Frobenius energy is $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i$. For any non-zero operator ($\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) > 0$), we define the scale-free spectral density distribution $\mathbf{p} = [p_1, \dots, p_D]^T$ over latent modes:
\begin{equation}
    p_i = \frac{\lambda_i(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \lambda_j(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T)} = \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2}, \quad \sum_{i=1}^D p_i = 1, \quad p_i \ge 0.
\end{equation}
We omit isotropic diagonal loading ($\frac{\epsilon}{D}\mathbf{I}$) from the Gram matrix before normalization; adding it would cause $\mathbf{K} \to \mathbf{0}$ to yield an artificial identity spectrum with maximal entropy, spuriously rewarding total collapse. Instead, we evaluate the Von Neumann spectral entropy directly on the scale-free modal probabilities with the standard analytic convention $0 \log 0 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i: p_i > 0} p_i \log p_i.
\end{equation}
The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} quantifies the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform modal equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^T$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Here, $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while dimensional collapse to rank 1 yields maximal deficiency $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \log D$.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) and prevent explosive gain, we introduce a non-negative Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge 0, \quad r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{K}) = \max\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2}{\gamma D}, \epsilon\right),
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ provides a numerical floor. For the identity operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ with $\gamma=1.0$, $\|\mathbf{I}\|_F^2 = D$, yielding $r_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{I}) = 1.0 > \epsilon$ and $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{I}) = 1 - 1 - \log 1 = 0$ exactly. The barrier diverges to $+\infty$ as $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$. In our architecture, the transition operator $\mathbf{K}$ is initialized near identity ($\mathbf{K} \sim \mathbf{I} + \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.01^2 \mathbf{I})$), ensuring $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 \approx D$ and $r_{\text{safe}} \approx 1.0$, which starts training with a vanishing energy penalty ($\Phi_{\text{energy}} \approx 0$). Because latent vectors $z$ are normalized via LayerNorm ($\mathbb{E}[\|z\|_2^2] \approx D$), the isometric reference scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ with $\gamma = 1.0$ matches the natural energy scale of unitary transitions, preserving variance without artificial scaling. In sensitivity checks across $\lambda_{\text{ent}} \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$, the model converged stably without numerical stiffness, with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$ providing a balanced trade-off between spectral spread and predictive regression. The composite spectral regularizer is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$. The total training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 0.5$ (the value used in all experiments). The implementation also applies a radial-heat damping layer, a Cohn--Elkies shell filter and a resolvent purification step to the latent vectors before the loss; these are shared with the $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$ control in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}. We emphasize that $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts directly on the transition operator matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, enforcing a non-collapsed, full-rank operator spectrum; it does not provide an analytical guarantee against representation collapse in the encoder itself. In practice, representation collapse in the encoder is mitigated through the asymmetric Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder dynamics and stop-gradient operations common to self-supervised frameworks (e.g., BYOL \cite{grill2020bootstrap}, I-JEPA \cite{assran2023self}), rather than by $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ directly.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Triviality Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, and non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$. The zero operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is strictly not a local or global minimizer of $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K})$, and $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$, prediction error is finite ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}(\mathbf{0}) = E_{\text{tgt}} < \infty$). Since $\Delta\mathcal{H} \ge 0$, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge \beta_{\text{norm}}(r_{\text{safe}} - 1 - \log r_{\text{safe}})$. As $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$, $r_{\text{safe}} \to \epsilon$ and $-\log r_{\text{safe}} \to -\log \epsilon$, and in the unfloored continuum limit $r \to 0^+$, $-\log r \to +\infty$, driving $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) \to +\infty$. Conversely, the identity operator $\mathbf{K}_0 = \mathbf{I}$ satisfies $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{I}) = 0$ and $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{I}) = 0$, yielding strictly finite loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{I}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 < \infty$. Hence $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ cannot be a minimizer.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Remark (Operator Non-Degeneracy vs. Representation Collapse):} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} establishes that the transition operator $\mathbf{K}$ cannot degenerate to $\mathbf{0}$ or collapse to rank 1 when operating on non-trivial target representations. However, it does not prevent encoder representation collapse. If the encoder $E_\theta$ maps all inputs to a constant representation $z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} = z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)} = \mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, the operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{I}$ achieves zero prediction error and zero spectral penalty while carrying zero information about the input time series. Prevention of constant representation collapse depends empirically on the stop-gradient momentum encoder dynamics rather than Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse}.

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Second-Moment Closure Head)}
\label{subsubsec:reynolds_stress_jepa}
Reynolds decomposition splits a turbulent velocity field into a mean flow and a fluctuation component, where the Reynolds stress tensor captures the second moment of turbulent velocity fluctuations. We adapt the second-moment formulation as an inductive bias in latent representation space. The latent coordinates are learned feature embeddings rather than physical velocity components, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is not a physical momentum-transport tensor.

The context encoder output passes through a Hankel moment filter and a shearing-wavelet pulse block. A two-layer MLP predicts the target latent $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$, followed by a Cohn--Elkies shell filter. The target encoder (EMA, stop-gradient) produces $z_{\text{tgt}}$ through matching filters. A learned linear map $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times D}$ ($d_s = 16$) yields the empirical second-moment matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} = (\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})(\mathbf{P} z_{\text{tgt}})^T + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}.
\end{equation}
Because $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}$ is computed from an instantaneous latent vector rather than averaged over time across the target horizon $S$, it forms a rank-one outer product regularized by a ridge term to enforce membership in the open cone of symmetric positive-definite matrices $\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$. Statistically, it imposes an auxiliary second-moment projection penalty. To ensure that predicted tensors respect this geometry, the closure head $h_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})$ outputs the elements of a lower-triangular matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d_s}$ with positive diagonal elements parameterized as $L_{ii} = \operatorname{softplus}(a_{ii}) + \epsilon$. The predicted covariance is reconstructed via Cholesky factorization:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T + 10^{-3}\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s},
\end{equation}
which strictly confines predictions to $\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d_s \times d_s}$ for all context inputs. The training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \operatorname{MSE}(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.1\, \operatorname{MSE}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}}).
\end{equation}
At inference time, the anomaly score combines the mean latent discrepancy and the fluctuation tensor residual via the coordinate-saliency gate (formalized in Section~\ref{subsec:scoring_function}). The stress term enters both the training loss and the test score. In Section~\ref{subsec:eval_physics_priors} and Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a), controlled ablations that disable the stress loss and score terms show that this auxiliary second-moment objective yields no statistically detectable improvement over standard latent regression ($p \ge 0.375$).

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition theorem, any sufficiently smooth vector field $v \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R}^D)$ can be resolved into an irrotational (curl-free) conservative potential gradient and a solenoidal (divergence-free) rotational field:
\begin{equation}
    v = -\nabla \Phi + \nabla \times A.
\end{equation}
In nominal physical equilibria, dissipative dynamical systems relax toward local minima on potential energy surfaces. Continuous latent trajectory evolution can be modeled as a dissipative gradient flow:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{z}(\tau) = -\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z(\tau)),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_\psi: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}$ is a scalar potential network parameterized with smooth $C^2$ activation functions (e.g., SiLU). Along continuous trajectories of this autonomous system, the potential satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{d\tau} \Phi_\psi(z(\tau)) = \langle \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z), \dot{z}(\tau) \rangle = -\|\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z(\tau))\|_2^2 \le 0,
\end{equation}
rendering $\Phi_\psi$ a strict Lyapunov function. Consequently, continuous-time gradient flows strictly preclude isolated periodic orbits (limit cycles).

\paragraph{Continuous Velocity Field and Harmonic Grassmannian Regimes}
Rather than parameterizing a discrete unconstrained displacement, our implementation constructs the continuous velocity field as the negative gradient of a scalar potential field $\Phi_\psi$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) = -\nabla_{z_t} \Phi_\psi(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_\psi$ is a three-layer residual MLP with LayerNorm and SiLU activations. The network is conditioned on the current state $z_t$, the continuous diffusion time $t \in [0, 1]$ via sinusoidal embeddings, the context representation $z_{\text{ctx}}$, and a discrete regime projection $p_{\text{regime}}$. The raw Euclidean gradient is projected onto the tangent bundle $T_{z_t} \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ via \texttt{MovingTangentProjection}, stabilizing discrete trajectory integration and bounding the effective Lipschitz curvature.

Discrete operational modes are modeled via a Harmonic Grassmannian Codebook maintaining $K=4$ orthonormal subspace frames $\mathbf{U}_k \in \operatorname{Gr}(d, D)$ ($d=8, D=32$, satisfying $\mathbf{U}_k^\top \mathbf{U}_k = \mathbf{I}_d$). The active regime projection $p_{\text{regime}}$ is selected via maximum subspace energy capture:
\begin{equation}
    k^* = \arg\max_{k \in \{1,\dots,K\}} \|\mathbf{U}_k^\top z_{\text{ctx}}\|_2^2, \quad p_{\text{regime}} = \mathbf{U}_{k^*} \mathbf{U}_{k^*}^\top z_{\text{ctx}}.
\end{equation}
During training, the frames are regularized with a Delsarte frame separation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grass}} = \frac{2}{K(K-1)} \sum_{i < j} \|\mathbf{U}_i^\top \mathbf{U}_j\|_F^2$. In Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}, a no-regime control ($K=1$, $p_{\text{regime}} = \mathbf{0}$) evaluates performance when regime routing is disabled.

\paragraph{Flow-Matching Objective, Numerical Midpoint Evaluation, and Dynamical Misspecification}
We distinguish the continuous dynamical system from the empirical learning and inference objective. The theoretical Lyapunov dissipation argument applies to autonomous continuous gradient descent $\dot{z} = -\nabla\Phi(z)$. In contrast, our architecture trains $\hat{v} = -\nabla_{z_t}\Phi_\psi(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}})$ via conditional flow matching (OT-CFM) along linear interpolation paths $z_t = (1-t)z_{\text{noise}} + t z_{\text{tgt}}$, where $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$ and $z_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_{\text{noise}}} \left[ \| \hat{v}(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}) \|_2^2 \right].
\end{equation}
Under conditional flow matching with optimal transport paths, the conditional vector field along the interpolation is $u_t(z_t \mid z_{\text{tgt}}, z_{\text{noise}}) = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{noise}}$. Along the mean deterministic trajectory ($z_{\text{noise}} = \mathbf{0}$), the path simplifies to $z_t = t z_{\text{tgt}}$ and the reference target velocity is constant: $v_t^* = z_{\text{tgt}}$ for all $t \in [0, 1]$. At inference time, rather than numerically integrating an autonomous ODE across $t \in [0, 1]$, the detector queries the velocity field at the trajectory midpoint $t=0.5$ with state $z_{\text{eval}} = 0.5 z_{\text{tgt}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{v}(0.5 z_{\text{tgt}}, 0.5, z_{\text{ctx}}, p_{\text{regime}}) - z_{\text{tgt}}.
\end{equation}
Because the reference target velocity along the mean path is $v_{0.5}^* = z_{\text{tgt}}$, this midpoint evaluation directly measures how closely the learned potential gradient reproduces the nominal target representation. This formulation matches the dimensionality ($D=32$) and Euclidean scale ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ under LayerNorm) of the unconstrained TS-JEPA residual $\mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}$. The anomaly score is computed by passing $\mathbf{e}(t)$ through the latent coordinate-saliency gate: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(\mathbf{e}(t))$.

Consequently, parameterizing $\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi$ guarantees that the predicted velocity field is irrotational ($\nabla \times \hat{v} = \mathbf{0}$) at each instant of diffusion time $t$, but does not imply that the observed physical telemetry is conservative or governed by a potential function. In real industrial and biological telemetry characterized by periodic oscillations or limit cycles (such as gait rhythms in Daphnet), this irrotational restriction causes severe model misspecification, explaining why Potential-Flow JEPA underperforms unconstrained TS-JEPA ($F_1$ difference $-0.091, p=0.020$).

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, sliding context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect target windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed with a stride of 1 timestep. For TS-JEPA, Operator-Entropy JEPA, and Potential-Flow JEPA, the anomaly score is computed by applying the latent coordinate-saliency gate $g(\cdot)$ (Section~\ref{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}) to the latent discrepancy vector $\mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(t) = g(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t)).
\end{equation}
For Reynolds-Stress JEPA, the score incorporates both the gated mean discrepancy and the gated fluctuation tensor residual: $\mathcal{S}(t) = g(z_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}) + 0.5\, g(\operatorname{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{obs}} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{pred}}))$. Context and target representations across all JEPA variants pass through shared filtering stages: Hankel moment filters, Littlewood--Paley dyadic frequency shells, Cohn--Elkies shell filters, and resolvent purification layers. These modules are held fixed across the ablation controls in Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations} to isolate the physical regularizers.

\noindent\textbf{Operational Distinction: Zero-Lookahead Causal Filtering vs. Buffered Predictive Early Warning.}
In operational telemetry pipelines, two distinct deployment regimes exist: (1) zero-lookahead point filtering, which outputs an anomaly label $\hat{y}_t$ using only observations $\mathbf{X}[:t, :]$; and (2) buffered predictive monitoring, where the architecture forecasts representations across an operational lookahead buffer $S$, raising prospective alerts for upcoming failures. TS-JEPA operates under regime (2): given historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:t, :]$, it projects future latent trajectory $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ over $[t, t+S]$. In continuous streaming, an anomaly occurring at timestep $t^*$ first enters the suspect target window at $t = t^* - S$. Consequently, the model registers a sharp increase in predictive discrepancy up to $S=64$ steps before peak physical onset. In our benchmark, we enforce strict unadjusted point-level evaluation at each decision time $t$ ($\text{stride}=1$). Because ground-truth point labels are strictly aligned to the physical anomaly window $[t^*, t^* + L]$, early warnings generated during the pre-onset interval $[t^* - S, t^*]$ are formally scored as point-level false alarms ($FP$). This represents an operational trade-off: predictive foresight provides early mitigation warning for orbital and industrial systems, but incurs a metric penalty under retrospective point-level matching. In operational incident analysis (Section VII.D), we also evaluate \textit{Middle} alignment, where the anomaly score is centered at $t + S/2$ to balance pre-onset and post-onset window overlap. Baseline models (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer, DCdetector, and NCAD) are evaluated under the exact identical sliding-window evaluation protocol, lookahead horizon, and standardization.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Streaming Anomaly Scoring and Decision Calibration}
\label{alg:scoring_protocol}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Streaming telemetry $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$, context horizon $C=256$, suspect horizon $S=64$, deployment mode $\mathcal{M} \in \{\text{Causal}, \text{Buffered}\}$, calibrator $\mathcal{C}_{\text{EVT}}$ fit to nominal residuals.
\Ensure Point-level alarm stream $\hat{y}_t \in \{0, 1\}$ for $t \in [C, T]$.
\State Initialize score buffer $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^T \leftarrow \mathbf{0}$, hit counts $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^T \leftarrow \mathbf{0}$.
\For{$t = C$ \textbf{to} $T - S$}
    \State Extract historical context: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} \leftarrow \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$
    \State Apply causal saliency gating: $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{ctx}} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} \odot \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$ \Comment{Zero target leakage}
    \State Encode latent context: $z_{\text{ctx}} \leftarrow E_\theta(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{ctx}})$
    \State Forecast future latent representation: $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} \leftarrow P_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})$
    \State Extract prospective suspect horizon: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \leftarrow \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$
    \State Encode target representation: $z_{\text{tgt}} \leftarrow E_{\bar{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \odot \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}))$
    \State Compute latent predictive discrepancy: $\mathcal{D}_t \leftarrow \|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}\|_2^2$
    \If{$\mathcal{M} = \text{Causal}$ \textbf{($0$-Lookahead Delay)}}
        \State Assign score strictly to current observed step: $s_t \leftarrow \mathcal{D}_t$
        \State $\hat{y}_t \leftarrow \mathbb{I}(s_t > \tau_{\text{eff}})$ \Comment{Zero future delay}
    \ElsIf{$\mathcal{M} = \text{Buffered}$ \textbf{($S$-Lookahead Delay)}}
        \For{$\tau = t$ \textbf{to} $t + S - 1$}
            \State Accumulate suspect overlap: $s_\tau \leftarrow s_\tau + \mathcal{D}_t$, \quad $c_\tau \leftarrow c_\tau + 1$
        \EndFor
        \State At lookahead finalization step $t$: $\bar{s}_t \leftarrow s_t / \max(c_t, 1)$
        \State $\hat{y}_t \leftarrow \mathbb{I}(\bar{s}_t > \tau_{\text{eff}})$ \Comment{Buffered running average}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return Binary alarm sequence $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Causal Coordinate Saliency Gating and Zero-Leakage Shielding}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional multivariate streams ($K \ge 25$), anomalous faults frequently originate within a localized subsystem (e.g., a single thruster, thermal sensor, or battery cell), while the remaining $K-1$ channels exhibit nominal ambient noise. In isotropic channel representations, localized anomalies are severely diluted by the accumulated background jitter of quiescent dimensions.

To prevent dimensional drowning without introducing target-window leakage, we formulate a strictly causal \textit{Causal Coordinate Saliency Gate}. Given historical context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and prospective target horizon $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, the channel dispersion metrics are computed \textit{strictly and exclusively} from past context observations:
\begin{equation}
    v_k^{\text{ctx}} = \operatorname{Var}_{\tau \in [t-C, t]}(x_{\tau, k}) = \frac{1}{C-1} \sum_{\tau=t-C}^t \left(x_{\tau, k} - \bar{x}_k^{\text{ctx}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    s_k = \frac{v_k^{\text{ctx}}}{\sum_{j=1}^K v_j^{\text{ctx}} + \epsilon}, \quad g_k = \sigma\left(\frac{s_k - \bar{s}}{\sigma_s + \epsilon}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic sigmoid, $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ prevents division by zero, $\bar{s} = 1/K$ represents the uniform variance share expectation across all $K$ channels, and $\sigma_s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K (s_k - \bar{s})^2 + \epsilon}$ denotes the cross-channel dispersion of variance shares. The standardized term $(s_k - 1/K)/\sigma_s$ quantifies the spatial z-score of channel energy. 

The resulting causal modulation vector $\mathbf{g} = [g_1, \dots, g_K]^T \in (0, 1)^K$ is applied symmetrically to scale input sensor channels in both context and target encoders:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{ctx}}[:, k] = \mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}[:, k] \cdot g_k, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{tgt}}[:, k] = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[:, k] \cdot g_k.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{g}$ depends exclusively on $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$, we have:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial g_k}{\partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}} \equiv \mathbf{0}, \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
guaranteeing exact zero-leakage shielding: future transient spikes or anomalies in the suspect target window $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ cannot artificially amplify channel weights prior to encoding. For univariate streams ($K = 1$), $s_1 = \bar{s} = 1$ and $\sigma_s = 0$, causing the gate to smoothly reduce to identity scaling ($\tilde{x}_1 = x_1$).

\paragraph{Zero-Leakage Data Provenance and Temporal Isolation}
To prevent data contamination across the workflow, we enforce strict temporal isolation across three sequential partitions:
1) \textit{Training partition (first 65\%)}: Used exclusively to compute channel standardization statistics ($\bm{\mu}_{\text{train}}, \bm{\sigma}_{\text{train}}$) and optimize model parameters $\theta, \psi$. No test observations or calibration residuals enter training.
2) \textit{Calibration partition (subsequent 17.5\%)}: Composed entirely of nominal operating points, separated from the training split by a buffer of $C=256$ timesteps to prevent context overlap. This partition is used exclusively to evaluate nominal model residuals and fit the EVT/SPOT extreme value distribution parameters ($u, \sigma, \xi$). Model weights and standardization statistics remain strictly frozen during calibration.
3) \textit{Test partition (terminal 17.5\%)}: Contains operational telemetry with labeled anomaly episodes. Scoring proceeds chronologically with frozen model weights and frozen thresholds $\tau_{\text{eff}}$. At each inference step $t$, context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:t, :]$ and suspect horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :]$ maintain strict causal separation: the channel saliency weights $\mathbf{g}$ depend exclusively on $x_{\text{ctx}}$, ensuring zero target leakage ($\partial g_k / \partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$).

\paragraph{Gating Boundary Conditions and Failure Modes}
We note three operational boundary conditions inherent to cross-channel variance gating. First, if a sensor channel exhibits intrinsically small normal fluctuations (such as stable thermal or cryogenic telemetry), its variance share $s_k$ remains low, attenuating its contribution even when an anomaly manifests as an abnormal variance increase that remains small in absolute magnitude relative to noisy channels. To mitigate baseline scale disparity across heterogeneous physical sensors, all raw telemetry streams are standardized via nominal calibration statistics ($\bm{\mu}_{\text{train}}, \bm{\sigma}_{\text{train}}$) prior to gating. Second, quiet faults (such as sensor flatlines, stuck values, or loss-of-signal failures where variance drops toward zero, $v_k^{\text{ctx}} \to 0$) receive low variance scores and cannot be isolated through variance amplification. Instead, flatlines are detected through trajectory predictive discrepancy $\mathbf{e}(t) = \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{tgt}}$, where the predictor fails to anticipate an unnatural constant state. Third, abrupt operational mode transitions (e.g., thruster firings or day-to-night orbital transitions) produce transient cross-channel heteroscedasticity that temporarily perturbs variance shares until the sliding context window $C=256$ is replenished with observations from the new regime.

Complementing input channel gating, an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate is applied to latent error vectors $\mathbf{e} = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{pred}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ during discrepancy scoring:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) = (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \quad w_d = \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)},
\end{equation}
which amplifies sparse latent coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$ while maintaining exact equivalence to $\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$ when errors are isotropic ($e_1 = \dots = e_D$).

\subsubsection{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Physical anomalies manifest across disparate temporal scales, ranging from high-frequency sensor spikes to gradual thermal drifts. To capture multi-scale dynamics without increasing parameter count, the encoder front-end decomposes input sequences into $J=4$ dyadic frequency bands. While continuous wavelet theory defines the Littlewood-Paley partition of unity as:
\begin{equation}
    |\phi(\omega)|^2 + \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} |\psi(2^j \omega)|^2 = 1, \quad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi],
\end{equation}
discrete numerical implementations require exact partition enforcement on a finite frequency grid. Over an $N_{\text{fft}} = 256$ discrete real fast Fourier transform (rFFT) grid yielding $F = 129$ non-negative frequency bins $\omega_m = m / N_{\text{fft}}$ for $m \in \{0, \dots, N_{\text{fft}}/2\}$, dyadic center frequencies are positioned at $c_j = 2^{j - J + 1}$ for $j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. The unnormalized continuous transfer functions follow Gaussian profiles $\tilde{\Psi}_j(\omega_m) = \exp\left(-\frac{(\omega_m - c_j)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$ with bandwidth parameter $\sigma = 0.6$. To strictly preserve signal energy across all discrete frequencies without attenuation or amplification, filter weights are normalized via an exact softmax partition across bands at each bin:
\begin{equation}
    \Psi_j[\omega_m] = \frac{\tilde{\Psi}_j(\omega_m)}{\sum_{l=0}^{J-1} \tilde{\Psi}_l(\omega_m)}, \quad \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} \Psi_j[\omega_m] \equiv 1.0, \quad \forall m \in \{0, \dots, F-1\}.
\end{equation}
In the time domain, these dyadic shells are implemented as 1D depthwise convolutions with kernel lengths $k \in \{11, 7, 5, 3\}$ corresponding to octaves $j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. To preserve causal integrity and prevent temporal leakage across window boundaries, each convolution applies left-side causal padding of $p = k - 1$ steps with zero right-side padding. This multi-scale decomposition isolates transient high-frequency perturbations from low-frequency diurnal baselines before latent projection.

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Following scoring, binary alarm decisions $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}. In classical SPOT, extreme value theory dictates that excesses over a high initial threshold $u$ (set uniformly at the 98th percentile, $u=0.98$) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via an empirical heuristic factor governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ increases, raising the threshold to suppress false alarms from volatile noise bursts; on light-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 < 0$), the threshold tightens to maximize detection sensitivity.

We emphasize that this kurtosis adjustment is an engineering heuristic designed to accommodate pre-asymptotic tail behavior in finite telemetry samples, rather than a formal asymptotic theorem of extreme value theory. In our 16-cell protocol ablation (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities} and Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), isolating the kurtosis multiplier revealed that it does not improve out-of-sample risk control: on held-out data, modulating the threshold by $1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2)$ increased nominal FPR (e.g., for TS-JEPA from $5.17\%$ to $18.80\%$) without improving Point-F1 ($0.1947$ vs. $0.1929$). 

In practical telemetry deployments, EVT cannot eliminate threshold calibration challenges. While the mathematical design risk is set to $q = 10^{-3}$ (which under an ideal asymptotic GPD regime would imply an expected false positive rate of 0.1\%), empirical false positive rates across real-world multi-sensor streams typically settle around $5\text{--}8\%$, representing a substantial calibration gap. This discrepancy stems from finite-sample estimation variance, temporal autocorrelation among consecutive residuals, and non-stationary distribution shifts between nominal training and operational deployment. Under thresholds fit strictly on held-out nominal data (Section~\ref{sec:exp}), the measured nominal FPR at $q=10^{-3}$ is $5.2\text{--}7.9\%$ for the JEPAs and $24\text{--}30\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD, demonstrating that EVT does not deliver its design risk for any model and that the kurtosis adjustment does not close the gap. What our investigation reveals is that latent predictive residuals yield substantially fewer false alarms than raw reconstruction residuals under identical calibration conditions, reflecting lighter residual tails at the cost of lower causal recall. In our benchmark, thresholds are calibrated using held-out nominal splits under static EVT; in operational incident analysis, dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated to examine streaming baseline drift.
