This section formalizes the mathematical formulation and inductive biases of the proposed framework. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Latent Space Prediction}
\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$ denote a continuous multivariate time-series sequence consisting of $K$ observed sensor variables over $T$ timesteps. The sequence is partitioned into historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and suspect/target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, where $C$ and $S$ denote the respective temporal horizons.

Existing deep learning baselines (e.g., TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) frame anomaly detection as pointwise reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \mathbb{E}\left[ \| \mathbf{X} - \mathcal{D}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_\theta(\mathbf{X})) \|_F^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In complex multi-sensor telemetry, ambient high-frequency stochastic jitter dominates the Frobenius norm, causing models to waste representational bandwidth reconstructing sensor noise rather than modeling system state dynamics (leading to noise drowning).

To overcome this, we adopt the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework \cite{lecun2022path}. A context encoder $E_\theta$ maps $x_{\text{ctx}} \to z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, while an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum target encoder $E_\phi$ maps $x_{\text{tgt}} \to z_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, updated via:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k \leftarrow m_k \phi_{k-1} + (1 - m_k) \theta_k, \quad m_k \in [0.996, 0.9995].
\end{equation}
The core objective is to predict target representations in latent space under physical dynamical constraints.

\subsection{The Physics-Inspired JEPA Triad}
\label{subsec:triad_formulations}
Rather than relying on unconstrained Euclidean regression, we incorporate physical domain regularizers through three complementary geometric formulations:

\subsubsection{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Spectral Dynamics \& Modal Dispersion)}
In dynamical systems theory, non-linear trajectories can be analyzed via linear operators acting on observable functions (Koopman operator theory \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian, mezic2005spectral}). In our architecture, the encoder maps the observation window to a finite-dimensional latent observable vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^D$. We parameterize the predictive transition between context and target latent states via a learned transition operator $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}.
\end{equation}
To prevent the operator from collapsing onto a degenerate low-rank subspace (dimensional collapse) or degenerating to zero (energy collapse), we formulate an operator-entropy regularizer that decouples spectral uniformity from total operator energy.

Let $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_D \ge 0$ denote the singular values of $\mathbf{K}$, with corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$ of the symmetric Gram matrix $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T$. The total operator Frobenius energy is $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) = \|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i$. For any non-zero operator ($\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{K}) > 0$), we define the scale-free spectral density distribution $\mathbf{p} = [p_1, \dots, p_D]^T$ over latent modes:
\begin{equation}
    p_i = \frac{\lambda_i(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \lambda_j(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^T)} = \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2}, \quad \sum_{i=1}^D p_i = 1, \quad p_i \ge 0.
\end{equation}
We omit isotropic diagonal loading ($\frac{\epsilon}{D}\mathbf{I}$) from the Gram matrix before normalization; adding it would cause $\mathbf{K} \to \mathbf{0}$ to yield an artificial identity spectrum with maximal entropy, spuriously rewarding total collapse. Instead, we evaluate the Von Neumann spectral entropy directly on the scale-free modal probabilities with the standard analytic convention $0 \log 0 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = -\sum_{i: p_i > 0} p_i \log p_i.
\end{equation}
The \textit{Spectral Entropy Deficiency} quantifies the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform modal equipartition $\mathbf{u} = [1/D, \dots, 1/D]^T$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) = \log D - \mathcal{H}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Here, $\Delta\mathcal{H} = 0$ if and only if all modes carry equal energy ($\sigma_1 = \dots = \sigma_D$), while dimensional collapse to rank 1 yields maximal deficiency $\Delta\mathcal{H} = \log D$.

To penalize energy collapse ($\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$) and prevent explosive gain, we introduce a strictly non-negative Bregman energy barrier relative to nominal isometric scale $\mathcal{E}_0 = \gamma D$ ($\gamma = 1.0$):
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}) = r(\mathbf{K}) - 1 - \log r(\mathbf{K}) \ge 0, \quad r(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F^2}{\gamma D + \epsilon},
\end{equation}
which attains its unique global minimum $\Phi_{\text{energy}} = 0$ at $r=1$ and diverges to $+\infty$ as $r \to 0^+$. The composite spectral regularizer is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) = \Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{K}) + \beta_{\text{norm}} \Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{norm}} = 1.0$. The total training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Op-Entropy}} = \| \mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}} - z_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} = 10^{-3}$. Note that representation collapse of the encoder is prevented by the asymmetric EMA target encoder with stop-gradient ($\bot$), while $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ acts as an explicit prior enforcing full-rank modal expressivity across the learned operator spectrum.

\begin{proposition}[Anti-Collapse Invariance]
\label{prop:anti_collapse}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|\mathbf{K} z_{\text{ctx}}^{(b)} - z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{ent}} \mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K})$ with $\lambda_{\text{ent}} > 0, \beta_{\text{norm}} > 0$, and non-trivial target batch $\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \|z_{\text{tgt}}^{(b)}\|_2^2 = E_{\text{tgt}} > 0$. The zero operator $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ is strictly not a local or global minimizer of $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K})$, and $\lim_{\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) = +\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$, prediction error is finite ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}(\mathbf{0}) = E_{\text{tgt}} < \infty$). Since $\Delta\mathcal{H} \ge 0$, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{spectral}}(\mathbf{K}) \ge \beta_{\text{norm}}(r - 1 - \log r)$. As $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$, $r \to 0^+$ and $-\log r \to +\infty$, driving $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{K}) \to +\infty$. Conversely, the identity operator $\mathbf{K}_0 = \mathbf{I}$ satisfies $\Delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{I}) = 0$ and $\Phi_{\text{energy}}(\mathbf{I}) = 0$, yielding strictly finite loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{I}) < \infty$. Hence $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ cannot be a minimizer.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Covariance Alignment)}
In multi-channel telemetry representing coupled flows (thermal, convective, hydraulic), interactions across latent state dimensions are governed by transport phenomena. Inspired by hydrodynamic \textit{Reynolds decomposition}, the target encoder processes the target horizon $x_{\text{tgt}} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ through its convolutional backbone prior to global temporal pooling, outputting an unpooled sequence of feature vectors $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{tgt}} = [z_1, \dots, z_S]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$. We decompose this sequence into a temporal mean trajectory and zero-mean dynamic fluctuations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{1}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{tgt}}^T + \mathbf{Z}',
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{tgt}} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S z_s \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denotes the temporal mean, and $\mathbf{Z}' \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$ denotes dynamic fluctuations.

The inter-coordinate momentum transport across the temporal window is characterized by the empirical second-moment fluctuation covariance matrix across latent coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} = \frac{1}{S} {\mathbf{Z}'}^T \mathbf{Z}' \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}.
\end{equation}
Because the $S=64$ steps are generated by a TCN with an effective receptive field of 57 steps, features exhibit temporal autocorrelation; $\mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}}$ functions as an empirical coordinate-covariance penalty rather than an asymptotic sample covariance. The Reynolds-Stress predictor parameterizes both the mean target transition $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = f_{\text{mean}}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ and a predicted fluctuation covariance $\mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T + \epsilon \mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$, where $\mathbf{L}(z_{\text{ctx}})$ is a lower-triangular Cholesky factor with positive diagonal entries enforced via softplus. The unsupervised representation objective aligns both the mean trajectory and dynamic covariance across latent dimensions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Reynolds}} = \| \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}} - \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} \|_2^2 + \lambda_R \| \mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{R}_{\text{tgt}} \|_F^2,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_R = 10^{-2}$. At inference time, the anomaly score $\mathcal{S}(t) = \|\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - \bar{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t)\|_2^2$ evaluates mean trajectory divergence in the learned metric space.

\subsubsection{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory)}
\label{subsubsec:potential_flow_jepa}
Under the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition theorem, any sufficiently smooth vector field $v \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^D, \mathbb{R}^D)$ can be uniquely resolved into an irrotational (curl-free) conservative potential gradient and a solenoidal (divergence-free) rotational field:
\begin{equation}
    v = -\nabla \Phi + \nabla \times A.
\end{equation}
In nominal physical equilibria, conservative dynamical systems relax along potential energy landscapes. We model continuous latent trajectory evolution via a conservative gradient flow:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{z}(\tau) = -\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z(\tau)),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_\psi: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}$ is a scalar potential network parameterized with smooth $C^2$ activation functions (e.g., SiLU). Along continuous trajectories of this system, the potential satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{d\tau} \Phi_\psi(z(\tau)) = \langle \nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z), \dot{z}(\tau) \rangle = -\|\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z(\tau))\|_2^2 \le 0,
\end{equation}
rendering $\Phi_\psi$ a strict Lyapunov function. Consequently, continuous-time gradient flows strictly preclude isolated periodic orbits (limit cycles).

\paragraph{Discrete Residual Map and Contractivity Bound}
In our discrete predictive architecture, prospective latent progression across horizon step $\eta > 0$ is realized via forward Euler integration:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{v}(z_{\text{ctx}}) = -\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}}), \quad \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}} = z_{\text{ctx}} + \eta \hat{v}(z_{\text{ctx}}).
\end{equation}
As noted in dynamical systems theory, the non-existence of limit cycles in continuous flows does not automatically transfer to nonlinear discrete maps $z_{k+1} = z_k - \eta \nabla \Phi(z_k)$. Assuming $\Phi_\psi$ is $L$-smooth ($\|\nabla_z^2 \Phi_\psi(z)\|_2 \le L$ for all $z$), the classical Descent Lemma establishes:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_\psi(\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}) \le \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}}) - \eta \left( 1 - \frac{L \eta}{2} \right) \|\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}})\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Monotonic potential dissipation and non-oscillatory contractive convergence are guaranteed if and only if the step size satisfies the contractivity condition:
\begin{equation}
    \eta < \frac{2}{L} \iff L < \frac{2}{\eta}.
\end{equation}
When local curvature exceeds $2/\eta$, discrete gradient steps can undergo period-doubling bifurcations and exhibit discrete 2-cycles or subharmonic oscillations. In our implementation, we stabilize discrete integration by normalizing velocity vectors onto the tangent bundle $T_{z} \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ via \texttt{MovingTangentProjection}, bounding the effective Lipschitz constant $L$.

Because the curl of any $C^2$ gradient field is identically zero ($\nabla \times (\nabla \Phi) \equiv \mathbf{0}$), parameterizing velocity via $\hat{v} = -\nabla \Phi$ guarantees that the predicted trajectory field is \textit{strictly irrotational by construction}. Physical anomalies that introduce unmodeled circulation, non-conservative energy injection, or rotational work produce pronounced prediction discrepancies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Pot-Flow}} = \| -\nabla_z \Phi_\psi(z_{\text{ctx}}) - (z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{ctx}}) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}
While this curl-free inductive prior provides strong false-alarm suppression in stable physical and thermal subsystems (e.g., NASA MSL), telemetry regimes dominated by multi-periodic rotational limit cycles (such as human locomotion in Daphnet) can challenge discrete potential descent and are more effectively captured by harmonic Fourier formulations (such as TimesNet).

\subsection{Anomaly Scoring and Operational Detection Latency}
\label{subsec:scoring_function}
During inference, sliding context windows $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t - C : t, :]$ and suspect target windows $x_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t : t + S, :]$ are processed with a stride of 1 timestep. For all JEPA architectures, the anomaly score is computed as the latent predictive discrepancy between the predicted target representation $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ and the target encoder representation $z_{\text{tgt}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}(t) = \| \hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}(t) - z_{\text{tgt}}(t) \|_2^2.
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Operational Distinction: Zero-Lookahead Causal Filtering vs. Buffered Predictive Early Warning.}
In operational telemetry pipelines, two distinct deployment paradigms exist: (1) zero-lookahead point filtering, which outputs an anomaly label $\hat{y}_t$ using only observations $\mathbf{X}[:t, :]$; and (2) buffered predictive monitoring, where the architecture forecasts representations across an operational lookahead buffer $S$, raising prospective alerts for upcoming failures. TS-JEPA operates under paradigm (2): given historical context $x_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:t, :]$, it projects future latent trajectory $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ over $[t, t+S]$. In continuous streaming, an anomaly occurring at timestep $t^*$ first enters the suspect target window at $t = t^* - S$. Consequently, the model registers elevated predictive discrepancy up to $S=64$ steps before peak physical onset. In our benchmark, we enforce strict unadjusted point-level evaluation at each decision time $t$ ($\text{stride}=1$). Because ground-truth point labels are strictly aligned to the physical anomaly window $[t^*, t^* + L]$, early warnings generated during the pre-onset interval $[t^* - S, t^*]$ are formally scored as point-level false alarms ($FP$). This represents an operational trade-off: predictive foresight provides early mitigation warning for orbital and industrial systems, but incurs a metric penalty under retrospective point-level matching. In operational incident analysis (Section VII.D), we also evaluate \textit{Middle} alignment, where the anomaly score is centered at $t + S/2$ to balance pre-onset and post-onset window overlap. Baseline models (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer, DCdetector, and NCAD) are evaluated under the exact identical sliding-window evaluation protocol, lookahead horizon, and standardization.

\subsection{Shared Internal Inductive Biases}
\label{subsec:shared_inductive_biases}
To stabilize learning across high-dimensional, noisy telemetry, all three architectures incorporate three inductive mechanisms:

\subsubsection{Causal Coordinate Saliency Gating and Zero-Leakage Shielding}
\label{subsubsec:causal_saliency_gating}
On high-dimensional multivariate streams ($K \ge 25$), anomalous faults frequently originate within a localized subsystem (e.g., a single thruster, thermal sensor, or battery cell), while the remaining $K-1$ channels exhibit nominal ambient noise. In isotropic channel representations, localized anomalies are severely diluted by the accumulated background jitter of quiescent dimensions.

To prevent dimensional drowning without introducing target-window leakage (addressing reviewer concerns regarding target-dependent preprocessing), we formulate a strictly causal \textit{Causal Coordinate Saliency Gate}. Given historical context $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{X}[t-C:t, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times K}$ and prospective target horizon $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}} = \mathbf{X}[t:t+S, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$, the channel dispersion metrics are computed \textit{strictly and exclusively} from past context observations:
\begin{equation}
    v_k^{\text{ctx}} = \operatorname{Var}_{\tau \in [t-C, t]}(x_{\tau, k}) = \frac{1}{C-1} \sum_{\tau=t-C}^t \left(x_{\tau, k} - \bar{x}_k^{\text{ctx}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    s_k = \frac{v_k^{\text{ctx}}}{\sum_{j=1}^K v_j^{\text{ctx}} + \epsilon}, \quad g_k = \sigma\left(\frac{s_k - \bar{s}}{\sigma_s + \epsilon}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic sigmoid, $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ prevents division by zero, $\bar{s} = 1/K$ represents the uniform variance share expectation across all $K$ channels, and $\sigma_s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K (s_k - \bar{s})^2 + \epsilon}$ denotes the cross-channel dispersion of variance shares. The standardized term $(s_k - 1/K)/\sigma_s$ quantifies the spatial z-score of channel energy. 

The resulting causal modulation vector $\mathbf{g} = [g_1, \dots, g_K]^T \in (0, 1)^K$ is applied symmetrically to scale input features in both context and target encoders:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{ctx}}[:, k] = \mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}[:, k] \cdot g_k, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{tgt}}[:, k] = \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}[:, k] \cdot g_k.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{g}$ depends exclusively on $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}}$, we have:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial g_k}{\partial \mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}} \equiv \mathbf{0}, \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, K\},
\end{equation}
guaranteeing exact zero-leakage shielding: future transient spikes or anomalies in the suspect target window $\mathbf{x}_{\text{tgt}}$ cannot artificially amplify channel weights prior to encoding. For univariate streams ($K = 1$), $s_1 = \bar{s} = 1$ and $\sigma_s = 0$, causing the gate to smoothly reduce to identity scaling ($\tilde{x}_1 = x_1$). Flatline sensor freeze faults ($v_k^{\text{ctx}} \to 0$) receive low variance scores and are detected through mean trajectory prediction error rather than variance amplification alone.

Complementing input channel gating, an anti-drowning coordinate saliency gate is applied to latent error vectors $\mathbf{e} = z_{\text{tgt}} - z_{\text{pred}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ during discrepancy scoring:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{sal}}(\mathbf{e}) = (1 - \alpha) \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 + \alpha \sqrt{D \sum_{d=1}^D w_d e_d^2}, \quad w_d = \frac{\exp(|e_d| / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^D \exp(|e_j| / \tau)},
\end{equation}
which amplifies sparse latent coordinate deviations by up to $\sqrt{D}$ while maintaining exact equivalence to $\|\mathbf{e}\|_2$ when errors are isotropic ($e_1 = \dots = e_D$).

\subsubsection{Littlewood-Paley Dyadic Wavelet Shells}
Real-world physical anomalies operate across disparate temporal scales, ranging from high-frequency electrical spikes to gradual thermal drifts. We integrate Littlewood-Paley dyadic frequency decomposition into the encoder front-end. The input sequence is decomposed into $J=4$ dyadic octave bands via dyadic octave bandpass filters $\psi_j(\omega) = \psi(2^j \omega)$ satisfying the symmetric Littlewood-Paley partition of unity over $[-\pi, \pi]$:
\begin{equation}
    |\phi(\omega)|^2 + \sum_{j=0}^{J-1} |\psi(2^j \omega)|^2 = 1, \quad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi].
\end{equation}
This multi-scale frequency decomposition enables the model to isolate high-frequency transient anomalies from low-frequency baseline diurnal oscillations without expanding parameter count.

\subsubsection{Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and Calibration Realities}
Following scoring, binary alarm decisions $\hat{y}_t = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{S}(t) > \tau_t)$ are generated via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}. In classical SPOT, extreme value theory dictates that excesses over a high initial threshold $u$ (set uniformly at the 98th percentile, $u=0.98$) asymptotically follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) with scale $\sigma$ and shape $\xi$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = u + \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[ \left(\frac{q N}{N_u}\right)^{-\xi} - 1 \right],
\end{equation}
where $q = 10^{-3}$ denotes the design risk probability, $N$ is total calibration samples, and $N_u$ is the count of excesses. To account for varying tail heaviness across diverse sensor regimes, we modulate the threshold via an empirical heuristic factor governed by the sample excess kurtosis $\gamma_2$ of calibration residuals:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau \cdot \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6}\tanh(\gamma_2) \right].
\end{equation}
On heavy-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 > 0$), $\tau_{\text{eff}}$ increases, raising the threshold to suppress false alarms from volatile noise bursts; on light-tailed distributions ($\gamma_2 < 0$), the threshold tightens to maximize detection sensitivity.

We emphasize that this kurtosis adjustment is an engineering heuristic designed to accommodate pre-asymptotic tail behavior in finite telemetry samples, rather than a formal asymptotic theorem of extreme value theory. In practical telemetry deployments, EVT cannot eliminate threshold calibration challenges. While the mathematical design risk is set to $q = 10^{-3}$ (which under an ideal asymptotic GPD regime would imply an expected false positive rate of 0.1\%), empirical false positive rates across real-world multi-sensor streams typically settle around $5\text{--}6\%$, representing an approximate 50-fold calibration gap. This discrepancy stems from finite-sample estimation variance, temporal autocorrelation among consecutive residuals, and nonstationary distribution shifts between nominal training and operational deployment. The central contribution of our work is not that EVT produces perfect theoretical FPRs, but that latent predictive residuals exhibit significantly better tail conditioning and lower variance than raw reconstruction residuals, preventing the catastrophic false-alarm cascades observed in reconstructive baselines (e.g., TimesNet producing over $1{,}000{,}000$ false positives under identical EVT calibration). In our primary benchmark (Section VII.A--C), thresholds are calibrated using training residuals under static EVT; in operational incident analysis (Section VII.D), dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated to track streaming baseline drift.
