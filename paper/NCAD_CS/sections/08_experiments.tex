\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted using a causal dilated TCN (3 residual blocks, channel depth $F{=}48$, kernel $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4\}$); global temporal average pooling aggregates the full $C{=}256$ context, with LayerNorm and dropout ($p{=}0.20$). The momentum target encoder follows EMA tracking with cosine schedule $m_{\text{base}}{=}0.996 \to m_{\text{max}}{=}0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's nominal telemetry is partitioned into three disjoint chronological blocks: \emph{training} (first 65\%), \emph{nominal calibration} (next 17.5\%), and \emph{nominal evaluation} (final 17.5\%, separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap). The training block optimizes representation weights alone. Withheld from training, the nominal calibration block fits per-stream GPD tail parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}0.98$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$, estimating $\gamma_2$ strictly within this interval. The nominal evaluation block then measures held-out FPR on telemetry unobserved during threshold selection. Thresholds remain fixed during test evaluation to measure static EVT stability under drift; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}).

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment Across Experiments}
To avoid ambiguity, we specify the calibrator per experiment. \textbf{Primary held-out benchmark} (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:causal_benchmark}; all paired statistics): standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier); headline metrics are the ``SPOT'' column of Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}, with calibrator variants compared on identical score streams. \textbf{Ablations and controlled studies} (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}): standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$), matching the primary benchmark; the kurtosis multiplier is evaluated strictly as an explicit sensitivity arm (Section~\ref{subsec:ablation_calibration}, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}). \textbf{Buffered-attribution and EVT mechanism studies}: standard SPOT unless the calibrator is the object of comparison. \textbf{Incident metrics} (Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:operational}): rolling SPOT. No experiment mixes calibrators across models within a table.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Multiplicity Controls}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are trained across five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 large GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) use three seeds (five on the two smallest) for cost, and ablation controls use three. We report held-out nominal FPR, Point-F1, and PR-AUC as distinct outcomes; nonsignificant detection differences are not evidence of parity. The post hoc operating-curve analysis uses only streams with archived score dumps (19 buffered, 18 causal), not the full 33-stream benchmark.

Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. We distinguish two resampling levels: within each stream, the held-out nominal evaluation split uses a moving-block bootstrap ($L{=}64$) over the chronological false-alarm indicator sequence to construct per-cell FPR confidence intervals under serial autocorrelation; across streams, paired model contrasts resample stream differences (2{,}000 stream-level bootstrap iterations with seeds averaged within stream). The independent statistical unit is the telemetry stream ($n{=}19$ non-GHL at five seeds; $n{=}33$ where GHL enters at available coverage), with dataset-clustered checks reported to account for shared sensor environments. Because nonsignificant tests do not establish equivalence, we make no noninferiority claim; instead, we report paired differences with effect sizes and CIs for readers to assess practical margins. $p$-values across comparisons are Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted at $q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$; CIs spanning zero or adjusted $p > 0.05$ are treated as inconclusive, not as equivalence. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:test_map} maps every reported test to its endpoint, cohort, paired-stream count, seed treatment, raw and adjusted $p$, and effect-size interval.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Parameter Capacities}
To isolate algorithmic principles from implementation-specific discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline with identical windowing, standardization, and EVT calibration. TimesNet and TranAD are in-house re-implementations of the published architectures (rather than vendor code), configured to published depths and evaluated under the identical optimization budget (AdamW, $\eta=10^{-3}$, cosine decay, $20$ epochs); no method received per-model hyperparameter tuning beyond the shared configuration, and no threshold, early-stopping, or model-selection step accessed held-out anomaly labels. Hardware-specific throughput, batch, warm-up, and per-timestep latency measurements are reported in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:computational_profile}.

\noindent\textbf{TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}:} Multi-periodic reconstructive architecture mapping 1D series to 2D variation spaces via FFT period discovery and Inception blocks ($648{,}553$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $574{,}529$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}:} Adversarial reconstructive Transformer with two-stage self-conditioning and sub-series attention ($205{,}746$ at $K{=}25$; $201{,}090$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{TS-JEPA (Unconstrained Control):} Standard JEPA predicting $z_{\text{tgt}}$ from $z_{\text{ctx}}$ via a 2-layer MLP ($289{,}061$ parameters at $K{=}25$; $218{,}309$ at $K{=}9$).

\noindent\textbf{Physics-Inspired Formulations:} Operator-Entropy ($285{,}733$ at $K{=}25$, $-1.15\%$ capacity), Reynolds-Stress ($295{,}953$, $+2.4\%$), and Potential-Flow ($338{,}117$, $+17.0\%$): differences do not stem from parameter scaling.

\noindent\textbf{Linear AR Forecaster (Non-Neural Baseline):} Ridge-regularized map from flattened context to target windows, scored by observation-space prediction error --- isolating whether latent representation is necessary at all. \textbf{Score-bound controls:} a matched-backbone observation-space TCN forecaster with LayerNorm on its bottleneck and flattened output, scored by the same saliency gate; PCA-subspace reconstruction; per-channel max-$z$; IsolationForest; $k$NN-distance; observation-space MAD scoring; and the dimensionality-matched PCA$\to$32$\to$LayerNorm gate of Section~\ref{sec:ablations}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Critical surveys \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards} document severe reporting vulnerabilities in this literature --- point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, threshold gaming --- so we report metrics across complementary tiers. \textbf{Exploratory operating characteristics:} event recall integrated over nominal calibration budgets and, separately, over a fixed exploratory realized-test-FPR interval $[0.10,0.50]$ (Section~\ref{sec:results}). For the latter, we interpolate within each stream--seed curve only when both bounds are observed, pair models within identical stream--seed cells, average paired differences by stream, and use dataset-level sign permutations and dataset-clustered bootstrap intervals; Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment covers all tested model, mapping, and endpoint combinations. Missing support is reported, not extrapolated. \textbf{Secondary endpoints:} \textbf{Dataset-Macro Point-F1} (unweighted mean across datasets, preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating); \textbf{channel-macro Point-F1}; \textbf{PR-AUC} (average precision, ranking quality independent of calibration); \textbf{event recall} (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments containing $\ge1$ alarm); \textbf{mean detection delay} $\Delta t$ (onset-to-first-alarm lag over detected events only); and the \textbf{trivial all-positive reference} $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at base rate $\pi$ --- models below it underperform a constant alarm. \textbf{Pooled micro-F1} exposes flood-prone models; contiguous PA-F1 and test-sweep PA-F1 quantify the residual calibration gap.

\subsection{Evaluation Regimes: Zero-Lookahead Causal vs. Buffered Inference}
\label{subsec:causal_vs_buffered_protocol}
To keep the buffered-attribution regime separate from future-observation access, we evaluate two regimes: \textbf{strictly causal online detection} (at decision time $T$ only $\{X_\tau\}_{\tau \le T}$ is accessible; scores use trailing attribution; zero lookahead) and \textbf{buffered window detection} ($S{=}64$ future timesteps are buffered before scoring, capturing full trajectory evolution at a deliberate $64$-step latency). Both record Mean Detection Delay, Event Recall within delay budgets ($\Delta t \le 10, 64$), and false alarms per 1,000 steps ($\text{FAR}_{1\text{k}}$).
