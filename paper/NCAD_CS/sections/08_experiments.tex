\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Two-Stage Nominal Calibration, and Statistical Design}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures use context $C=256$, target horizon $S=64$ ($W=320$, or $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ in Section~\ref{subsec:horizon_latency}), latent dimension $D=32$, nominal training stride $16$, and evaluation stride $1$. Models are trained with AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) and zero per-dataset tuning: batch $16$ for $15\text{--}20$ epochs on development cohorts, and batch $16\text{--}64$ for $10\text{--}12$ epochs with gradient clipping ($1.0$) and cosine annealing on archive cohorts.

\paragraph{Disjoint Nominal Calibration Protocol, Boundary Isolation, and Cross-Fitting}
Each stream's nominal history $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) into a model-fitting prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = T_{\text{nom}} - \lfloor 0.35\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a held-out nominal suffix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$:
(1)~\emph{Stage~A (Disjoint weight fitting, checkpoint selection, and nominal adaptation):} Reusing calibration windows for early stopping, blending weights (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), or weight quenching can make nominal thresholds optimistic. All archive benchmarks ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}) therefore enforce a strictly disjoint \emph{five-block nominal sequence} inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ ($\approx 43\%$ of $T_{\text{nom}}$) fits channel standardization, encoder/predictor weights, running barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and precision $\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{MD}$; $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ ($N_{\text{sel}}\ge 96$ windows) selects the early-stopping checkpoint; and $\mathbf{X}_q$ ($N_q\in[160,2{,}048]$ stride-1 windows separated from $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ by $S=64$-step target-isolation gaps) fits and freezes (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and optional split-gated quenching (Steps Q1--Q4) before $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ is ever touched. Fixed-epoch development suites (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}) omit $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}$: the 19-stream residual cache skips (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}) and quenching altogether, while a paired four-block check ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}\to\mathbf{X}_q\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}\to\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$) on the 19 development streams confirms that disjoint $\mathbf{X}_q$ adaptation holds causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ at $0.96\%$ ($0.58\%$ median; $64.8\%$ ER, $0.233$ buffered F1) for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} and $1.05\%$ ($0.78\%$ median; $64.6\%$ ER) for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, matching Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} ($1.0\%$ and $1.3\%$) while trimming $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fit}}$ from $65\%$ to $50\%$ lowers short-stream causal F1 ($0.133\to 0.071$ and $0.144\to 0.076$). On short histories where carving out five blocks shrinks calibration support, \emph{two-fold chronological cross-fitting} swaps $[0:H)$ and $[H+S:M_{\text{val}})$ under consensus voting ($\hat{y}^{(1)}\wedge\hat{y}^{(2)}$), lowering 19-stream cache $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ from $1.50\%$ to $0.92\%$ (median $1.07\%\to 0.38\%$) at $66.7\%$ median ER.
(2)~\emph{Stage~B (Untouched circuit-breaker calibration and boundary isolation on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Stride-1 windows of length $W = 320$ inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ yield $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window indices. Splitting at $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the untouched \emph{nominal calibration block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}=[0 : H)$ and \emph{held-out nominal evaluation block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}=[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so calibration and evaluation share zero target observations (a full $G=320$ window gap changes causal FPR by $<0.25$ percentage points). On $\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ alone, the frozen model fits dial medians $m_j$, MAD scales $s_j$, hijack guards $\gamma_j$, tripwires $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and quorums ($K_{\text{Lite}}$ or $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ in (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}); $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), frozen on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{test}}$. Raw window count $N_{\text{cal}} = H$ is distinguished from autocorrelation-adjusted \emph{effective sample size} $N_{\text{eff},s} = N_{\text{cal}} / \tau_{\text{int},s}$ via stream- and statistic-specific Sokal initial-positive-sequence estimators ($\tau_{\text{int}}$ up to $206$; Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\paragraph{Hierarchical Statistical Units, Dataset-Clustered Inference, and Hypothesis Testing}
Because streams within multi-stream archives share hardware platforms, we report a two-level inference hierarchy after averaging seeds within each stream (seed standard deviation (SD)):
(1)~\emph{Dataset-clustered and stream-level inference:} Because stream-level $p$-values treat same-spacecraft channels as independent, \emph{dataset-clustered inference} ($95\%$ cluster-bootstrap CIs and exact $2^D$ dataset sign-flip permutation tests) is emphasized alongside stream-level paired Wilcoxon tests and $95\%$ stream-bootstrap CIs ($2{,}000$ resamples). For multiplicity reporting on the 118 untouched streams, the focal causal contrasts comparing \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} against Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D) and TS-JEPA yield Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) \cite{benjamini1995controlling} adjusted stream-level $p=0.014$ and $3.6\times 10^{-4}$ on Point-F1 (raw $p=0.014$ and $1.8\times 10^{-4}$) and adjusted dataset sign-flip $p=0.0078$ and $0.012$ on ER (raw $p=0.0039$ and $0.012$, positive on $9/9$ and $8/9$ datasets); other quoted $p$-values are raw and descriptive, and non-Exathlon subsets ($n=101$, $8$ datasets) verify sensitivity to archive composition.
(2)~\emph{12-fold LODO audit:} Across the 12 LODO folds on the 141-stream archive, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} retains positive gains in all $12/12$ folds on causal ER ($\Delta\in[+0.099,+0.141]$ vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D), buffered Point-F1 ($\Delta\in[+0.002,+0.017]$), and causal Point-F1 vs.\ TS-JEPA ($\Delta\in[+0.013,+0.027]$, $\max p=0.0054$), and in $9/12$ folds on causal Point-F1 vs.\ Multi-Horizon 1D (Section~\ref{subsec:calibration_realities}).

\subsection{Consolidated Cohort Organization, Development vs.\ Held-Out Separation, and Baseline Fairness}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Across all evaluations, we study a two-tier family of multi-horizon coordinate circuit-breaker architectures: \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} is the preferred deployment configuration (parallel three-horizon 128-dial position-plus-drift panel, single quorum, zero weight editing), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} is retained as the expressive four-horizon 224-dial reference variant with optional split-gated quenching (\emph{Laminar-JEPA (w/o Quench)} omits Steps Q1--Q4). Both causal trailing and retrospective buffered attribution are paired on identical splits within each cohort (aside from a single-horizon 130-stream buffered baseline screen in Section~\ref{subsec:core_benchmark}):
(1)~\emph{Development cohorts (design-inspected, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_matrix}):} The 19-stream residual cache ($6$ datasets) evaluates the dial ladder, component ablations, and conformal/statistical-process-control (SPC) regimes on identical 4H unquenched residuals (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a,b), Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}); the 33-stream development benchmark ($7$ datasets: $19$ non-GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) plus $14$ GHL streams in Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, paired with a four-block $\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$ sensitivity run) compares both tiers against 1D JEPAs and observation-space baselines; and tripwire/quenching constants were fixed on $23$ tuning streams ($19$ non-GHL plus \texttt{SMAP T-2}, \texttt{MSL P-10}, \texttt{Daphnet S09R01E2}, and \texttt{OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3}).
(2)~\emph{Untouched held-out cohorts (primary generalization evidence):} Excluding those $23$ design streams from the $141$-stream archive ($12$ datasets, strictly disjoint $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sel}}\perp\mathbf{X}_q\perp\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$) leaves \textbf{$118$ untouched held-out streams} across $9$ datasets ($101$ non-Exathlon streams across $8$ datasets; Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), supplemented by a single-seed 4H Q1--Q4 step-isolation audit on the same $118$ streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}). None of the $118$ held-out streams was inspected during architecture or threshold design.

\noindent\emph{Standardized Baseline Fairness Protocol:} Scalar baselines (TS-JEPA, Multi-Horizon JEPA (1D), TCN Forecaster/Autoencoder, PCA Forecaster, Isolation Forest, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}, NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural}, and DCdetector \cite{yang2023dcdetector}) share the identical chronological splits, training-only standardization, isolation gaps, and SPOT calibration ($u=p_{98}$, $q=10^{-3}$), whereas conformal, SPC, and simpler coordinate detector-bank baselines in Tables~\ref{tab:matched_alert} and \ref{tab:calibrator_comparison} share the identical multi-horizon whitened residuals and nominal calibration alert rate. \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} uses $141.9\text{k}$ trainable parameters at $K < 25$ and $222.3\text{k}$ at $K \ge 25$ versus $295.8\text{k}$ for \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($206\text{k}\text{--}649\text{k}$ across external neural baselines; circuit-breaker panels add zero trainable weights). Runs used PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090 ($0.15\,\text{ms}$/window batched inference for \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} vs.\ $0.38\,\text{ms}$/window for \emph{Laminar-JEPA}, $2.43\times$ speedup; $188\,\text{MB}$ peak memory); code: \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Operational Evaluation Metrics and Aggregation}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To prevent point-adjustment inflation \cite{kim2022towards} and aggregation ambiguity, all binary false-alarm and Point-F1 metrics are computed \emph{per valid timestep $\tau$} (never point-adjusted and never per-window without timeline attribution): $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$ averages $\hat{y}(\tau)$ over valid timesteps in $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}}$ averages $\hat{y}(\tau)$ over nominal test timesteps ($y(\tau)=0$), and Point-F1, Precision, Recall, and PR-AUC (from $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$) score all valid test timesteps against $y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$. Event-level ER measures the fraction of contiguous $y=1$ segments with $\ge 1$ alarm, and delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ is the step lag from segment onset $t^*$ to first in-segment alarm. Unless explicitly labeled \emph{median} or \emph{Dataset-Macro}, cohort headline numbers report \emph{stream-level arithmetic means} of within-stream seed averages, paired with stream medians for skewed FPR distributions and non-deployable post-hoc test-FPR controls ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$) strictly for diagnostic comparison.
