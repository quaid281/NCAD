\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Two-Stage Nominal Calibration, and Statistical Design}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
All architectures operate on an identical streaming pipeline with context window $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ ($W = C + S = 320$), trained on nominal windows at stride $16$ and evaluated at stride $1$. Context and target windows are encoded into $D=32$ spherical representations by the multiscale frequency front-end and causal dilated TCN, fused with the three-depth functional hierarchy tap head (\ref{eq:multidepth_tap}), and centered by the online running context barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$ prior to terminal LayerNorm. Models are trained on nominal sequences using AdamW \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) with zero per-dataset hyperparameter tuning. Budgets are fixed within each comparison but differ by cohort: batch $16$ at constant learning rate for $20$ (Cohort~1) or $15$ epochs (Cohort~2); batch $32$ with gradient clipping at $1.0$ for $10$--$12$ epochs (Cohorts~3--4); and batch $16$ for at most $12$ epochs with cosine annealing to $10^{-5}$, gradient clipping, and early stopping with patience $4$ (Cohort~5). Cohort~2 reuses cached whitened residuals and therefore omits the blend in (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}).

\paragraph{Two-Stage Nominal Calibration Protocol and Boundary Isolation}
To evaluate autonomous alarm calibration without test-label leakage or train-to-calibration circularity, each stream's nominal sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{nom}} \times K}$ is partitioned chronologically (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~1) into a \emph{training block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[0 : N_{\text{tr}})$ ($N_{\text{tr}} = \lfloor 0.65\, T_{\text{nom}}\rfloor$) and a disjoint \emph{held-out nominal block} $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{nom}}[N_{\text{tr}} : T_{\text{nom}})$ ($N_{\text{val}} = T_{\text{nom}} - N_{\text{tr}}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Stage~A (Training fit on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$):} Channel standardization parameters, encoder/predictor weights, online context barycenter $\bar{\bm{\mu}}_{\text{ctx}}$, and the multi-horizon shrinkage precision matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^{MD}$ are fit strictly on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{tr}}$, ensuring zero raw-sample or window overlap across $N_{\text{tr}}$. In Cohort~5, early stopping monitors the unsupervised predictive loss on the calibration block; the held-out block and test stream play no role in training.
    \item \emph{Stage~B (Circuit-breaker calibration and held-out verification on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$):} Sliding windows of length $W = 320$ are constructed strictly inside $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol_and_permutation}, Row~2), yielding $M_{\text{val}} = N_{\text{val}} - W + 1$ valid window timestamps after the $(W-1)$-step context warm-up. Splitting at midpoint $H = \lfloor M_{\text{val}} / 2 \rfloor$ defines the \emph{nominal calibration block} $[0 : H)$ and the \emph{held-out evaluation block} $[H + S : M_{\text{val}})$, separated by an $S=64$-step isolation gap $[H : H+S)$ so the last calibration target and first evaluation target share zero target observations. On $[0 : H)$, the panel fits its calibration-stabilized whitening weights $w_{m,c}$ (\ref{eq:stabilized_whitening}), per-dial medians $m_j$ and MAD scales $s_j$, two-factor hijack guards $\gamma_j = \gamma_j^{\text{cal}}\gamma_j^{\text{sc}}$, switch thresholds $\tau_j$ (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire}), and Boolean quorums $(K_{224}, K_{128}, K_{\text{edge}})$ (\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) (or scalar $\tau_{\text{SPOT}}$ for 1D baselines), which are then frozen and evaluated on $[H+S : M_{\text{val}})$ and on the test stream. A full-window isolation check ($G = W = 320$, excluding $[H : H+320)$ across the seven streams with $M_{\text{val}}/2 > 420$) confirms that shared passive context changes causal held-out FPR by less than $0.25$ percentage points.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Independent Statistical Units, Multi-Seed Replication, and Hypothesis Testing}
Models are replicated across multiple random seeds ($\{42, 123, 456\}$ for all 224-bit circuit-breaker, ablation, and 141-stream benchmarks; $\{42, 123, 456, 7, 2024\}$ for 1D primary baselines). Seeds are averaged within each stream first ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$) so that stream-level tests treat the telemetry stream ($n=19$, $n=33$, or $n=141$) as the independent statistical unit, while domain-level tests treat the dataset family ($N=7$ or $N=12$) as the independent cluster:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and false-discovery-rate control:} We evaluate paired stream differences via the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test without Gaussian assumptions, adjust $p$-values across families using the Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) procedure \cite{benjamini1995controlling} ($q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$), count $|\Delta| \le 10^{-4}$ as ties, report $95\%$ stream-resampled bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs; $2{,}000$ resamples), and cross-check at the dataset-cluster level via exact sign-flip permutation tests and zero-tuning LODO evaluation.
    \item \emph{Perspective-ladder and component-removal ablations:} On the identical trained multi-horizon backbones ($n=19$ streams, $57$ runs), we evaluate both the step-by-step perspective expansion ladder ($1 \to 32 \to 128 \to 160 \to 192 \to 224$ dials) and leave-one-out component ablations removing the 32 right-edge onset relays, prefix-horizon routing, per-dial hijack guards, or discrete Boolean thresholds (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(b)); null comparisons are reported as paired effect sizes with $95\%$ bootstrap CIs, without equivalence claims.
    \item \emph{Tail kurtosis and regime-drift diagnostics ($\gamma_2$ and $D_{\text{KS}}$):} On nominal windows we measure: (i)~\emph{excess kurtosis} $\gamma_2 = \mathbb{E}[(s - \mu)^4]/\sigma^4 - 3$, quantifying residual tail heaviness; and (ii)~the two-sample \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) drift statistic} $D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{cal}}(x) - F_{\text{hold}}(x)| \in [0, 1]$, quantifying distributional shift between nominal calibration and held-out evaluation blocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Fairness Audits, Benchmark Cohorts, and Computational Footprint}
\label{subsec:cohort_repro}
Two fairness audits verify that reconstructive baseline performance under the unified $W=320$ pipeline is not an artifact of window length or implementation: (i)~evaluating TranAD ($W=10$) and TimesNet ($W=96$) at their native published window sizes across five streams raises Point-F1 to $0.164$ and $0.218$, but observation residuals remain severely heavy-tailed ($\gamma_2 > 98$ vs.\ $2.30$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA) with high nominal FPR ($28.4\%\text{--}44.1\%$); and (ii)~reconstructors replicate their published point-adjusted F1 on SMD and MSL, while Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) provides a 130-stream, 11-dataset cross-check against NCAD, DCdetector, and Anomaly Transformer.

Experiments span five cohorts: \emph{Cohort~1} ($33$ primary streams: $19$ non-GHL and $14$ GHL, Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(a)--(c)); \emph{Cohort~2} ($19$-stream perspective expansion ladder, circuit-breaker component ablation, $15$-arm matched-backbone comparison, and operating-curve suite, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)--(c) and Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(a) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)); \emph{Cohort~3} ($6$-stream single-channel quiet-fault injection and 224-bit hybrid variance-collapse monitor suite, Fig.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b)); \emph{Cohort~4} ($5$-stream horizon-latency sweep across $S \in \{1,4,16,32,64\}$ and 3-seed 224-bit dyadic front-end ablation, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a) and Section~\ref{subsec:dyadic_ablation}); and \emph{Cohort~5} ($141$-stream, $3$-seed archive-wide benchmark across $12$ multivariate datasets comparing 1D JEPAs against the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}), plus a separate single-seed screen of external baselines on $130$ streams in $11$ datasets (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d)) that is never paired with Cohort~5 ($91$ shared streams; the other $39$ are GHL, CICIDS, or fail $N_{\text{cal}} \ge 100$). The panel's calibration constants in (\ref{eq:dial_tripwire})--(\ref{eq:panel_quorum}) were chosen during development by inspecting test metrics on $23$ streams (the 19 non-GHL primary streams plus SMAP T-2, MSL P-10, Daphnet S09R01E2, and OPPORTUNITY S1-ADL3), so Cohort~2 results are development-set results; the constants were then frozen, and the $118$ Cohort~5 streams never used in development serve as the held-out test of the panel. At $K=25$ channels, online parameter counts are matched ($272\text{k}$ for TS-JEPA, $312\text{k}$ for Soliton-Reynolds JEPA, $304\text{k}\text{--}307\text{k}$ for TCN observation forecasters, $649\text{k}$ for TimesNet, and $206\text{k}$ for TranAD; the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel adds zero trainable parameters). Runs executed on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 (RTX 5090, $0.60\text{--}0.88\,\text{ms}$/window, $188\,\text{MB}$ memory); code is at \url{https://github.com/quaid281/NCAD}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Operational Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
To guard against point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging bias, and threshold gaming \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards}, we evaluate detectors across three tiers: (i)~\emph{primary accuracy and false-alarm outcomes} comprising realized held-out nominal FPR ($\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}$), unadjusted Point-F1, and threshold-free PR-AUC (computed from the total active switch count $N_{\text{tot}}(\tau)$ for the 224-bit panel); (ii)~\emph{operating characteristics} evaluating event recall across autonomous circuit-breaker operating points (\emph{224-Bit Strict} and \emph{224-Bit Breaker}), deployable calibration quantiles ($q^*$, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)), and post-hoc matched realized test-FPR budgets ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)); and (iii)~\emph{secondary operational endpoints} reporting unweighted Dataset-Macro Point-F1, event recall (ER, fraction of contiguous anomaly segments with $\ge 1$ alarm), mean detection delay $\Delta t = t_{\text{alm}} - t^*$ over detected events, and the constant-alarm reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at prevalence $\pi$.
