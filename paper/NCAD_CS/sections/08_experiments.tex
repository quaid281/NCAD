\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
To ensure rigorous and fair comparison, all architectures are trained and evaluated under an identical streaming time-series pipeline. Telemetry streams are processed using a sliding window with past context duration $C=256$ and suspect target horizon $S=64$ (total window length $L=320$), evaluated with a stride of 1 timestep across all testing streams.

Context representations $z_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=32$) are extracted via a multiscale dyadic front-end and causal dilated TCN (6 residual blocks, $F{=}48$, kernel $5$, dilations $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$); multi-statistic pooling (terminal, mean, max, and channel statistics) projects the $C{=}256$ context through a linear bottleneck recording pre-LayerNorm radial energy before LayerNorm and dropout ($p{=}0.20$). The momentum target encoder uses cosine EMA $m_{\text{base}}{=}0.996 \to m_{\text{max}}{=}0.9995$.

Models are optimized using AdamW with an initial learning rate $\eta=10^{-3}$ decayed to $\eta_{\text{min}}=10^{-5}$ via Cosine Annealing, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size $B=32$ across 20 training epochs on nominal telemetry sequences. 

\paragraph{Held-Out Calibration Protocol}
To evaluate out-of-sample threshold calibration without circularity, each stream's nominal telemetry is partitioned into three disjoint chronological blocks: \emph{training} (first 65\%), \emph{nominal calibration} (next 17.5\%), and \emph{nominal evaluation} (final 17.5\%, separated by an unobserved 64-step isolation gap). The training block optimizes representation weights alone. Withheld from training, the nominal calibration block fits per-stream GPD tail parameters $\sigma, \xi$ above $u{=}0.98$ at $q{=}10^{-3}$, estimating $\gamma_2$ strictly within this interval. The nominal evaluation block then measures held-out FPR on telemetry unobserved during threshold selection. Thresholds remain fixed during test evaluation to measure static EVT stability under drift; dynamic rolling SPOT is evaluated separately in the operational incident analysis (Section~\ref{subsec:causal_vs_buffered}).

\paragraph{Calibrator Assignment}
All experiments use standard SPOT ($u = p_{98}$, $q = 10^{-3}$, no kurtosis multiplier) on held-out nominal splits, except the rolling-SPOT incident analysis and the calibrator comparison itself (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}); no table mixes calibrators across models.

\paragraph{Multi-Seed Replication, Primary Endpoints, and Multiplicity Controls}
Principal models on the 19 non-GHL streams are trained across five seeds (42, 123, 456, 7, 2024); the 14 large GHL streams ($>10^6$ steps) use three seeds (five on the two smallest) for cost, and ablation controls use three. We report held-out nominal FPR, Point-F1, and PR-AUC as distinct outcomes; nonsignificant detection differences are not evidence of parity. 

Inference reports stream-macro means, seed SDs, and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Within each stream, per-cell FPR intervals use a moving-block bootstrap ($L{=}64$) over the false-alarm indicator sequence; across streams, paired contrasts resample stream differences (2{,}000 iterations, seeds averaged within stream). The independent statistical unit is the telemetry stream ($n{=}19$ non-GHL at five seeds; $n{=}33$ where GHL enters at available coverage), with dataset-clustered checks reported to account for shared sensor environments. For general detector contrasts, nonsignificant tests do not establish equivalence; instead, we report paired differences with effect sizes and CIs. Across general contrasts, $p$-values are Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-adjusted at $q_{\text{FDR}} = 0.05$; CIs spanning zero or adjusted $p > 0.05$ are treated as inconclusive. For physical regularizer variants, where hypotheses test whether additions alter baseline performance, we complement Wilcoxon tests with formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) against TS-JEPA: the null hypothesis $H_0: |\mu_1 - \mu_2| \ge \Delta$ tests non-equivalence against $H_1: |\mu_1 - \mu_2| < \Delta$ under an equivalence margin of $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ Point-F1. Every reported test is stated in the text with its endpoint, cohort, number of paired streams, raw and adjusted $p$, and effect-size interval.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures and Parameter Capacities}
To isolate algorithmic principles from implementation-specific discrepancies, all baselines are executed within a unified PyTorch benchmarking pipeline with identical windowing ($C=256, S=64$), standardization, and EVT calibration. TimesNet and TranAD are in-house re-implementations configured to published architectural depths and evaluated under a shared optimization budget (AdamW, $\eta=10^{-3}$, cosine decay, $20$ epochs); no method received per-model hyperparameter tuning beyond the shared configuration, restricting claims to these tested implementations. Three checks bound this protocol: (i)~\emph{Training-budget sensitivity} (7 streams, 3 seeds, held-out SPOT): halving training to 10 epochs shifts JEPA vs.\ reconstructor Point-F1 from $0.048$ vs.\ $0.154$ to $0.038$ vs.\ $0.158$ and held-out FPR from $0.092$ vs.\ $0.333$ to $0.087$ vs.\ $0.333$. (ii)~\emph{Native windows}: at native horizons (TranAD $W{=}10$, TimesNet $W{=}96$), heavy tails persist ($\gamma_2 = 448.25$ and $98.14$, TimesNet FPR $44.09\%$) vs.\ $\gamma_2 = 3.14$ for TS-JEPA. (iii)~\emph{Published-score replication}: baselines replicate published PA-F1 on SMD and MSL, and Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(d) extends comparisons to NCAD ($149{,}634$ parameters at $K{=}25$), DCdetector ($298{,}400$), and Anomaly Transformer ($4{,}785{,}120$) on 130 streams.

\noindent\textbf{Compared Models and Reproducibility Specification:} Operator-Entropy JEPA ($285{,}701$ parameters at $K{=}25$, $-1.16\%$ vs.\ unconstrained TS-JEPA at $289{,}061$); physical controls Reynolds-Stress ($296{,}037$, $+2.4\%$) and Potential-Flow ($338{,}209$, $+17.0\%$); observation baselines TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet} ($648{,}553$) and TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad} ($205{,}746$); Linear AR Forecaster ($\lambda_{\text{ridge}}{=}1.0$); and 12 controlled mechanism arms (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}). Non-finite values are replaced by zero after centering ($\bm{\sigma} \ge 10^{-2}$). SPOT fits GPD parameters $(\xi, \sigma)$ above $u = p_{98}$ via Grimshaw's procedure with L-BFGS-B fallback ($\xi \in [-0.9, 10], \sigma \ge 10^{-6}$); when $N_u < 5$ or $|\xi| < 10^{-6}$, it falls back to exponential tail extrapolation or the $(1-q)$ empirical quantile. All 195 primary runs converged without restarts ($\min \operatorname{SD}(s_{\text{cal}}) = 0.0043$) on PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4 on an RTX 5090 GPU ($0.60$--$0.84$~ms/window, $183.8$--$185.3$~MB peak VRAM for JEPAs).

\subsection{Multi-Tier Statistical Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval_metrics}
Critical surveys \cite{schmidl2022anomaly, kim2022towards} document severe reporting vulnerabilities in this literature (point-adjustment inflation, channel-averaging imbalances, threshold gaming), so we report metrics across complementary tiers. \emph{Exploratory operating characteristics}: event recall over nominal calibration budgets and over a realized-test-FPR interval $[0.10,0.50]$, interpolated within stream--seed curves only where both bounds are observed. \emph{Secondary endpoints}: Dataset-Macro Point-F1 (unweighted mean across datasets, preventing the 14-stream GHL block from dominating); channel-macro Point-F1; PR-AUC (average precision, ranking quality independent of calibration); event recall (fraction of contiguous anomaly segments containing $\ge1$ alarm); mean detection delay $\Delta t$ (onset-to-first-alarm lag over detected events only); and the trivial all-positive reference $\text{F1}_{\text{all-pos}} = 2\pi/(1+\pi)$ at base rate $\pi$; models below it underperform a constant alarm. Pooled micro-F1 exposes flood-prone models; contiguous PA-F1 and test-sweep PA-F1 quantify the residual calibration gap.
