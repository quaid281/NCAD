\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold. When one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise that threshold and mask moderate faults across the other 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also skewed by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace scalar scoring with an integrated multi-horizon circuit-breaker system, like a breaker box in which each circuit trips its own switch regardless of the total load. Across dyadic prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, whitened coordinates and their cross-block mean receive individual healthy tripwires and fire when a correlation-aware quorum trips together. Under matched calibration and operational scaling, higher variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Without claiming superior intrinsic ranking, the panel automatically shifts the label-free operating point toward higher coverage when scalar calibration under-triggers (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational compromise): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $128$ position-plus-drift dials within $0.97$ megabytes (MB) FP32 under one quorum (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224-dial reference): monitors $M=4$ horizons across $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays; \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points against this reference for lower mean and buffered false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our main contribution is a way to choose each stream's alarm operating point at deployment time without fault labels. Each stream is fitted only to its own healthy prefix ($K\in[2,55]$ channels, Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}), and labeled training folds are used only to validate routing thresholds. Two propositions motivate the design, and the evidence falls into four parts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{More faults caught, earlier, at a false-alarm cost:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} shows that, in an idealized setting, inflating a few coordinates rescales only their own tripwires. On $118$ untouched streams, unscreened \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} catches more fault events than 1D JEPA, raising causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$). It also flags paired fault onsets $41.4$ steps earlier. The price is more false alarms, with test FPR rising from $8.8\%$ to $17.0\%$. Point-F1 improves only modestly and drops on three of nine datasets, where cluster confidence intervals (CIs) cross zero. The gains concentrate in streams with at least $500$ calibration windows, and \emph{Lite} trails 1D JEPA when both are held to a strict $1\%$ test FPR.
    \item \emph{Cheap whitening and a gated workflow:} Kronecker-factored zero-phase component analysis (ZCA) performs as well as dense ZCA ($p=0.76$), and a consistent principal component analysis (PCA) basis closes the F1 gap left by causal blending ($p=0.33$). Two gates decide when a stream should use the panel: an archive-informed two-stage gate and a drift-gated three-stage gate. Both select thresholds by nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) validation on labeled training folds and then deploy without labels. When missed faults cost more than false alarms, three-stage routing lifts ER from $52.5\%$ to $59.4\%$ over 1D SPOT for a small rise in test FPR ($8.8\%$ to $10.0\%$). Shuffling healthy blocks to break their time order cuts archive FPR by $92.3\%$, which points to chronological drift as the main source of false alarms.
    \item \emph{External baselines and deployment cost:} Under SPOT, TimesNet and TranAD reach higher raw F1, but only by alarming on about a quarter of held-out healthy steps. When every method is held to a $1\%$ test FPR, \emph{Lite} catches about twice as many events ($51.2\%$ ER versus roughly $26\%$). Streaming on a CPU sustains $93\text{--}141$ windows/s within $128\,\text{KB}$. Leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) masking ranks the injected faulty channel first in $85.7\%$ of synthetic cases.
    \item \emph{Known limits:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds the prediction error energy, but the matching loss reweighting gives no measurable benefit. Serial dependence leaves $105$ of $118$ streams without formal tail guarantees. Two failure modes remain: auxiliary channel guards raise clean FPR to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$, and slow industrial ramps drift away from the frozen healthy reference, pushing test FPR to $74.4\%$.
\end{enumerate}
