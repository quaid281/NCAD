\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments --- NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, wearable biometric arrays --- dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
Multi-sensor telemetry reflects a physical state trajectory $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$, yet modeling dynamics in raw observation space faces four obstacles: estimating derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \Delta x / \Delta t$ on noisy discrete streams amplifies high-frequency noise; telemetry captures only a noisy projection of unmeasured states, violating full-state observability; in large arrays ($K \ge 25$) cross-channel nonlinear interactions resist rigid functional forms; and discrete operational mode transitions (orbital day/night cycling, thruster firings, gait freezing) induce continuous distribution drift.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Reconstructive models score observation-space errors, whereas latent predictors and low-dimensional controls use differently scaled, often bounded scores \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}. Comparing their alarm rates at a common nominal threshold risk does not separate scoring geometry from discriminative quality. Nor does a static threshold fitted on one nominal block guarantee the same false-positive rate on a later block. Whether high-frequency noise specifically consumes reconstruction capacity, or synthetic perturbations in contrastive methods such as NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} distort decisions, remains untested here.

\subsection{Investigating Latent Prediction and Inductive Priors}
We compare score conditioning, detection, and threshold transfer separately. A JEPA predicts target representations from context \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}; observation-space models, a matched-backbone control, a LayerNorm-bounded observation-space TCN, and a 32-dimensional PCA forecast with normalized gated scoring test alternative explanations. We also evaluate spectral entropy, latent second moment, and potential-flow variants as exploratory physics-inspired controls. These experiments do not by themselves isolate a noise-removal mechanism.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\noindent\textbf{1) Disentangling Alarm Rates and Score Quality:} On 19 non-GHL streams, the nominal held-out false-positive rate is $7.6\%$ for TS-JEPA versus $30\%$ for TimesNet, but bounded PCA and bounded observation-space TCN controls also produce light residual tails, and the TCN control does not preserve low calibration FPR. The larger JEPA event-recall area over \emph{nominal design budgets} under causal attribution does not establish dominance at matched realized FPR: few streams span the same realized-FPR range, and paired comparisons are inconclusive. Buffered and causal results are reported separately.

\noindent\textbf{2) Null Results for Physics-Inspired Variants:} Across the 33-stream SPOT cohort, the tested regularizers provide no reliable aggregate detection gain. A Potential-Flow Point-F1 difference has an unadjusted $p{=}0.033$ but does not justify a multiplicity-adjusted superiority or inferiority claim.

\noindent\textbf{3) Conditional Calibration Failure:} Static POT thresholds substantially exceed their nominal design risk under short held-out calibration. Score-distribution differences across blocks correlate with false alarms, but small effective exceedance counts and observational confounding prevent causal attribution to drift alone.

\noindent\textbf{4) Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark:} The SPOT benchmark spans 33 streams from seven datasets (19 non-GHL streams with five seeds; 14 GHL streams with fewer seeds). The score-dump operating-curve cohort is narrower: 19 buffered and 18 causal non-GHL streams. We report paired seed coverage and dataset-level uncertainty for the matched-realized-FPR analysis.
