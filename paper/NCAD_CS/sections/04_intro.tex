\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments --- NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, wearable biometric arrays --- dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs.

\subsection{Challenges in Telemetry Threshold Calibration}
Multi-sensor telemetry monitoring requires automated decision rules that reliably detect physical faults while controlling false alarms. Setting alarm thresholds on continuous telemetry error streams encounters two fundamental difficulties: non-stationary distribution drift between nominal calibration periods and live deployment, and differences in scoring geometry across model families. Reconstructive models score unconstrained observation-space errors, whereas latent predictive architectures and bounded controls produce bounded residuals with lighter tails.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detection Benchmarks}
Comparing detectors at a single nominal threshold probability (such as design risk $q = 10^{-3}$) conflates scoring scale with discriminative detection quality. Retrospective evaluation protocols that paint alarms over multi-step temporal buffers mechanically inflate per-timestep false-alarm rates, confounding operational evaluation. Standard benchmarks often fail to isolate whether low alarm rates stem from predictive dynamics, bounded score geometry, or representation conservatism.

\subsection{Investigating Latent Prediction and Score Conditioning}
We evaluate score conditioning, discriminative detection, and threshold transfer under strict chronological partitions. A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) forecasts target representations from context representations; observation-space models, a matched-backbone control, a LayerNorm-bounded observation-space TCN, and a dimensionality-matched PCA forecast test competing structural explanations. We also evaluate spectral entropy, latent second moment, and potential-flow variants as physics-inspired controls.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Disentangling Alarm Rates and Score Quality:} On 19 non-GHL streams, nominal held-out FPR is $7.6\%$ for TS-JEPA vs.\ $30\%$ for TimesNet, but bounded PCA and TCN controls also yield light residual tails, and the TCN does not preserve low calibration FPR. Larger JEPA event-recall areas over nominal budgets under causal attribution do not establish dominance at matched realized FPR: few streams share realized-FPR support, and paired contrasts are inconclusive. Buffered and causal results are reported separately.
    \item \textbf{Null Results for Physics-Inspired Variants:} Across the 33-stream SPOT cohort, the tested regularizers provide no reliable aggregate detection gain, passing formal two-one-sided equivalence tests within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 ($p \le 0.0054$).
    \item \textbf{Resolving Calibration Failure via Exchangeable Controls:} Static POT thresholds systematically overshoot target risk under chronological deployment ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at $q{=}10^{-3}$). An exchangeable permutation control proves that inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of nominal threshold overshoot, while sample scarcity and autocorrelation are secondary.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark:} The SPOT benchmark spans 33 streams across seven datasets (19 non-GHL at five seeds; 14 GHL at one to three seeds). All experimental logs, stream manifests, and replication scripts are archived in the project repository.
\end{enumerate}
