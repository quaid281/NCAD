\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments such as NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Operational monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains ambient sensor jitter and channel-scale disparities, observation-space residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and strong serial clustering. When paired with automated extreme-value thresholding such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), these heavy-tailed residuals suffer severe calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} A detector that predicts the \emph{summary} of a future window, rather than every raw sample, ignores sensor jitter that no model can forecast. We therefore study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which forecast a compact 32-dimensional latent summary of the next 64 steps from the preceding 256, without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}. We also formulate an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) whose forecasting operator is regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a shifted Bregman energy barrier. We treat these regularizers as conditioning priors on abstract latent coordinates, and we do not claim that they improve detection accuracy. The evaluation asks three separate questions: (i)~how much do latent residuals reduce false alarms under automated calibration, and does calibration reach its nominal $0.1\%$ risk target; (ii)~does detection quality hold across operating points and fault classes; and (iii)~how long does an operator wait between a fault and an alarm.

\subsection{Contributions}
Each contribution below states one finding in words. The supporting numbers sit in the cited tables and figures.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Why latent scoring lowers false alarms:} Across 12 controlled backbone arms, a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) bottleneck bounds heavy residual tails, and a learned latent projection also keeps held-out residuals close to calibration residuals (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)).
    \item \textbf{Fewer false alarms, comparable detection:} On 33 streams from seven datasets, latent scoring cuts held-out false alarms several-fold relative to reconstruction baselines under both retrospective (buffered) and strictly causal evaluation, and it recovers more faults at a fixed low false-alarm budget. Point-wise F1 (Point-F1) and precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) stay comparable to the baselines, and quiet single-channel faults remain weakly detected (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)).
    \item \textbf{The operator regularizer conditions but does not drive detection:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} shows the Koopman operator cannot collapse to zero. Equivalence tests show that removing the entropy term leaves Point-F1 unchanged, while the full OpEntropy architecture shortens detection delay (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)).
    \item \textbf{A measured calibration gap:} Standard extreme-value thresholding still exceeds its $0.1\%$ design risk out of sample. A refitted permutation test attributes most of the excess to distribution shift between calibration and test blocks, and a smaller share to serial dependence (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}
