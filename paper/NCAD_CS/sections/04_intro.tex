\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace scalar scoring with an integrated multi-horizon circuit-breaker system, like a breaker box in which each circuit trips its own switch regardless of the total load. Across dyadic prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, whitened coordinates and their cross-block mean receive individual healthy tripwires and fire when a correlation-aware quorum trips together. Under matched calibration and operational scaling, wider variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), shifting the label-free operating point toward higher coverage at ranking parity within a $256$-step context window (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational compromise): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $128$ position-plus-drift dials within $0.97$ megabytes (MB) FP32 under one quorum (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224-dial reference): monitors $M=4$ horizons across $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays; \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points against this reference for lower mean and buffered false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our primary contribution is the complete streaming detector, label-free operating-point characterization, and gated workflow, supported by two motivating propositions. Each stream fits to its own healthy prefix, testing whether a single frozen architecture and calibration rule transfer without per-dataset tuning. From a 45-stream pool, 33 qualifying streams guide design, and 118 untouched archive streams across nine datasets test generalization under a five-block protocol (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Label-free operating point and paired latency:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} states idealized matched-scale equivariance before upstream coupling and additive floors. Across 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} shifts the operating point over 1D JEPA, lifting causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$). Stream-paired co-detected onset latency drops by $74.6$ steps ($41.4$ on shared segments), and detection-conditioned means fall from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps. Higher coverage raises mean held-out FPR ($4.4\%\to 6.4\%$, $0.93\%$ median) and prospective test FPR ($8.8\%\to 17.0\%$), trading precision ($0.165\to 0.146$) for recall ($0.134\to 0.205$). Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) loses on three of nine datasets with cluster confidence intervals (CIs) crossing zero, gains for $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($p=0.047$), and trails 1D at $1\%$ test FPR ($0.049$ vs.\ $0.084$).
    \item \emph{Whitening structure and prospective screening:} Horizon-preserving Kronecker zero-phase component analysis (ZCA) matches dense ZCA across all $118$ streams ($+0.010$ F1, $95\%$ CI $[-0.012,+0.027]$, $p=0.76$; three-seed $+0.014$, $[-0.006,+0.029]$), while consistent principal component analysis (PCA) recovers the causal raw-axis blending penalty ($0.086$ vs.\ $0.090$ F1, $p=0.33$). Matched-FPR checks confirm ranking parity with scalar JEPAs. Two-stage screening accepts $52$ streams ($0.9\%$ held-out FPR) that hit $11.0\%$ mean ($3.9\%$ median) prospective test FPR and $18.1$ false-alarm episodes per $1{,}000$ healthy test steps, while adding drift screening accepts $46$ streams at $6.8\%$ mean ($2.9\%$ median) test FPR. Healthy-block permutation reduces measured archive FPR by $92.3\%$, motivating quarantined recalibration.
    \item \emph{External baselines and perturbation attribution:} Under untouched-archive SPOT, TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1) reach higher raw F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ held-out FPR, falling to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR vs.\ $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}. CPU streaming sustains $93\text{--}141$ windows/s within $128\,\text{KB}$ of state, and post-alarm leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) masking hits $85.7\%$ Top-1 synthetic subset attribution on injected shifts.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds error energy with a null reweighting effect, while serial dependence leaves $105$ of $118$ streams without tail guarantees. Auxiliary channel guards raise clean FPR ($6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$), and frozen-reference drift on slow industrial ramps yields $74.4\%$ test FPR.
\end{enumerate}
