\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Across spacecraft attitude-control buses, industrial chemical loops, cloud server clusters, and wearable kinematic arrays, dozens of coupled sensor channels stream continuously \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. An autonomous monitor watching these telemetry streams faces a strict operational requirement: it must calibrate its alarm rules using healthy (nominal) history alone, catch subtle multi-sensor faults as they unfold, and never drown operators in nuisance alerts. Yet when standard deep reconstructive detectors (TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}) are deployed under chronological nominal calibration, they trigger false alarms on $22\%\text{ to }31\%$ of healthy operating hours. At that false-alarm rate, an analytical Bernoulli random alerter firing blindly at the same frequency catches as many anomaly events as the neural network itself ($60.0\%$ random vs.\ $58.8\%$ actual for TimesNet).

\noindent\emph{Why Observation-Space Reconstructors Flood the Console:} Reconstructive detectors fail under nominal calibration because they grade themselves by reconstructing raw sensor waveforms point-by-point. Raw telemetry contains unforecastable sensor jitter, thermal drift, and scale disparities across channels. Summing point-wise errors across 25 to 55 channels produces a volatile, heavy-tailed residual stream with high excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 5.5$, Table~\ref{tab:terms}). When an automated tail rule such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} fits a threshold on a finite calibration window, routine operating shifts in later healthy telemetry repeatedly breach that boundary.

\noindent\emph{The Hidden Flaw of Latent Predictors: 1D Scalar Score Collapse and ``Hijacker Dials'':} A natural remedy is to stop reconstructing raw sensor noise and instead forecast a compact \emph{latent summary} of the upcoming window using a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural}. Projecting windows onto a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bounded unit sphere ($\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, $D=32$) compresses residual kurtosis six-fold ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91\text{--}2.30$) and cuts held-out false alarms by an order of magnitude ($0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$ causally). Yet our analysis uncovers a second bottleneck that cripples existing latent detectors: \emph{1D scalar score collapse}. After computing a multi-dimensional prediction residual ($M=4$ nested sub-horizons across $D=32$ latent coordinates, or $128$ values per window), conventional pipelines crush all 128 coordinates into a single 1D scalar anomaly score before setting a threshold. In long-running telemetry, if just one or two of those 32 coordinates experience mild out-of-sample variance drift between training and calibration (\emph{hijacker dials}), they dominate the 1D scalar sum and push the scalar threshold onto the extreme tail. Because the single threshold is inflated to accommodate two noisy dials, genuine faults developing on the remaining 30 coordinates are silenced, collapsing strictly causal Point-F1 to $0.050\text{--}0.052$ and stretching detection delay past $240$ steps.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamic Latent Prediction and the 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel:} Instead of crushing a 32-dimensional multi-horizon trajectory into a single scalar, we wire each coordinate and physical mode of motion to its own independent circuit breaker. We introduce \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} paired with a \emph{224-bit multi-perspective fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel}. At the representation level, the encoder taps multiple depths of a dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN) \cite{bai2018empirical}, subtracts the online running context barycenter to cancel common-mode level shifts, and rolls out predictions across $M=4$ nested prefixes ($S_m \in \{16, 32, 48, 64\}$) regularized by a Frobenius covariance-whitening defect. At the decision level, drawing on Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} and Lieb--Thirring spectral bounds \cite{lieb1976inequalities}, we expand the $D=32$ calibration-whitened coordinates into $7 \times 32 = 224$ fluid-dynamic dials: 128 sub-horizon flow-position dials, 32 Reynolds mean-flow transport dials (steady drift), 32 Reynolds turbulent-fluctuation dials (in-window shear), and 32 convective right-edge acceleration dials (terminal boundary shock). Each dial is governed by a nominal-calibrated binary switch with a two-stage hijacker guard $\gamma_j$ that quarantines noisy calibration dials without touching test data, paired with 32 fast right-edge onset relays that trip immediately when a terminal sub-block and convective acceleration dial fire together.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Zero-collapse 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel:} Replacing 1D scalar score collapse with a 224-bit circuit-breaker panel, hijack guards, prefix-routed voting, and 32 right-edge onset relays doubles strictly causal Point-F1 from $0.052$ to $0.138$ on $n{=}19$ primary streams ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$) and from $0.067\text{--}0.077$ to $0.139$ across $n{=}141$ streams ($114/18$ wins, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$). It also lifts buffered Point-F1 to $0.228$, raises causal event recall to $60.7\%\text{--}72.7\%$, and halves detection delay (Sections~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel} and~\ref{sec:results}, Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}--\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
    \item \emph{Factorial isolation of representation stability and breaker components:} Across matched-backbone models ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ parameters), LayerNorm compresses residual kurtosis from $\gamma_2 = 6.10$ to $0.91$, learned latent targets halve calibration drift from $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.376$ to $0.214$, and multi-horizon barycenter whitening raises effective rank $\operatorname{RankMe}$ from $11.6$ to $19.8$, lowers $D_{\text{KS}}$ to $0.179$, and cuts inter-block drift share four-fold from $71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$. A 6-step perspective ladder and leave-one-out ablations isolate each Reynolds dial family, right-edge relays, prefix routing, and hijack guards (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)--(c)).
    \item \emph{False-alarm suppression, contextual-anomaly dominance, and hybrid quiet-fault coverage:} Across the 19 non-GHL streams, latent prediction cuts causal held-out false-positive rates (FPRs) by an order of magnitude to $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors, while achieving $0.411$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) and $0.312$ causal PR-AUC. At a $1\%$ test-FPR budget, it nearly triples reconstructor recall ($58.4\%$ vs.\ $21.7\%$), leads on NASA contextual anomalies at $0.658$ PR-AUC, and recovers $44.4\%\text{--}52.8\%$ of single-channel freezes via a hybrid variance-collapse monitor (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault} and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Exact wavepacket bounds, equivalence controls, and zero-lag horizons:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that the Reynolds-centered Lieb--Thirring concentration ratio satisfies $\frac{1}{M-1} \le R_{\text{LT}} \le 1$ with exact radial scale invariance, while fixed-backbone ablations show that post-hoc 1D soliton multipliers satisfy Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) \cite{schuirmann1987comparison} equivalence ($p_{\text{TOST}} = 2.7{\times}10^{-5}$) and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step window lag (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
\end{enumerate}
