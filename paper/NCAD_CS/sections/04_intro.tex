\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments (NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT, and biometric arrays), dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, where faults must be identified before hardware failure occurs.

\noindent\textbf{Catastrophic False Alarms in Reconstructive Baselines:} Multi-sensor monitoring requires automated decision rules that detect physical faults while constraining false alarms. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Autoencoders) minimize point-level reconstruction errors in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}. Because raw signals contain ambient jitter, reconstructive objectives allocate capacity to fitting uninformative high-frequency sensor noise. Under automated extreme-value thresholding such as Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, the resulting heavy-tailed residuals trigger false alarms across $20\%\text{--}30\%$ of nominal timesteps, flooding operators and rendering alerting unviable.

\noindent\textbf{The Operator-Entropy JEPA Architecture:} To eliminate observation-space noise accumulation without artificial contrastive perturbations \cite{carmona2021neural}, we propose the \textit{Operator-Entropy Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture} (OpEntropy JEPA). Rather than reconstructing raw waveforms, the architecture forecasts future target representations in abstract latent space \cite{lecun2022path}. To maintain modal expressivity and prevent dimensional collapse, OpEntropy JEPA models latent transitions via a linear Koopman operator regularized by scale-free Von Neumann spectral entropy and a logarithmic Bregman energy barrier. We also evaluate hydrodynamic (Reynolds-stress) and field-theoretic (potential-flow) regularizers as comparative controls. For streaming execution, the framework integrates causal coordinate saliency gating, multiscale dyadic shells, and extreme-value tail calibration.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA Formulation:} We formulate OpEntropy JEPA for multi-sensor telemetry, combining latent representation forecasting with a Koopman transition operator regularized by scale-free Von Neumann spectral entropy and an anti-collapse Bregman energy barrier. We establish theoretical guarantees against operator collapse (Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse}) and characterize its latent coordinate geometry.
    \item \textbf{False-Alarm Suppression Against SOTA Baselines:} Under chronological held-out calibration on 19 non-GHL streams, OpEntropy JEPA suppresses false alarms four-fold against reconstructive baselines, achieving $8.6\%$ nominal held-out FPR versus $30.0\%$ for TimesNet and $30.7\%$ for TranAD (paired reductions of $-0.144$ and $-0.142$, $p=0.006$). Bounded linear and convolutional controls confirm that latent score geometry compresses residual tails while maintaining competitive Point-F1 and PR-AUC.
    \item \textbf{Threshold Calibration Innovations and Drift Decomposition:} We formalize operational thresholding under chronological streaming, showing that static extreme-value calibration systematically overshoots design risk ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at $q{=}10^{-3}$). Under an exchangeable permutation protocol, inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of risk overshoot, dominating sample scarcity and autocorrelation.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark and Null Results:} Across 33 continuous streams from seven physical datasets (19 non-GHL at five seeds; 14 GHL at one to three seeds), we evaluate causal and buffered streaming regimes. Across fixed-backbone controls, physical regularizers confirm formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence to unconstrained latent prediction within $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 ($p \le 0.0054$). All code, data, and scripts are released open-source.
\end{enumerate}
