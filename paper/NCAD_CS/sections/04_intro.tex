\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains sensor jitter and scale disparities, observation residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), heavy-tailed residuals suffer calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} Predicting the \emph{summary} of a future window, rather than every raw sample, ignores unforecastable sensor jitter. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which forecast a 32-dimensional latent summary of the next $S$ steps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for low-latency tripwires) from the preceding 256, without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}. Beyond unconstrained predictors (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formulate physically grounded variants: an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a Bregman energy barrier, and a \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} enforcing Cayley-accretive Crouzeix contractivity and Stiefel positive semidefinite second-moment closure. We evaluate these structures as geometric conditioning priors via equivalence tests rather than claims of detection superiority. Our contribution is relative false-alarm suppression and reduced calibration-to-holdout drift rather than solved absolute calibration at $q=10^{-3}$. We evaluate: (i)~how much latent residuals reduce realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ under static, declustered, and rolling calibrators; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent projection, and horizon isolate the mechanism of improvement; (iii)~how detection varies across abrupt, gradual, and quiet single-channel faults (and the false-alarm cost of hybrid sensor monitors); and (iv)~the trade-off between score-emission latency ($S-1$) and detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Factorial isolation of tail compression vs.\ drift reduction:} Across 14 matched-encoder and non-parametric arms ($269\text{k}\text{--}307\text{k}$ online parameters), a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) bottleneck bounds heavy residual tails ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), whereas a learned latent projection halves calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.193\text{--}0.214$) relative to normalized observation forecasters (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \textbf{Fewer false alarms, comparable detection, and hybrid quiet-fault recovery:} On 33 streams from seven datasets, latent scoring cuts held-out FPRs and calibration error three-fold under retrospective (buffered) attribution and eight- to ten-fold under strictly causal streaming, and nearly doubles event recall at a $1\%$ realized FPR budget. Point-wise F1 (Point-F1) and precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) remain comparable to baselines, and pairing latent prediction with a sensor variance monitor raises quiet single-channel event recall from $0.19\text{--}0.28$ to $0.42\text{--}0.50$ at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c), Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)).
    \item \textbf{Verified operator conditioning and short-horizon latency control:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves Koopman non-degeneracy across the full objective (including the discrete Lyapunov covariance defect, repulsive origin curvature at $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{0}$, and finite-$\epsilon$ energy bounds), while the Cayley-accretive Crouzeix linear core and sign-stabilized QR Stiefel retractions enforce contractive linear transport and interior covariance closure. Equivalence tests confirm that Koopman spectral regularization leaves Point-F1 unchanged within $\Delta = \pm 0.015\text{--}0.030$ while shortening detection delay, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and~\ref{tab:ablations}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Decomposed calibration error across static, declustered, and adaptive rules:} A refitted permutation decomposition estimates point-estimate shares of $56.6\%\text{--}64.1\%$ for inter-block drift in pooled excess JEPA risk above $q=10^{-3}$ ($77.5\%\text{--}92.3\%$ with resolved positive confidence intervals (CIs) for reconstructors, whereas per-stream JEPA CIs include zero across 11 zero-FPR streams), and runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles narrow the gap on step and variance shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}
