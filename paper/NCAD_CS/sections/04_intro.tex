\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, chemical plants, server farms, and wearable medical devices report health through dozens of continuous sensor channels \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. When a valve sticks, a reaction wheel drifts, or a patient's gait freezes, an automated monitor must detect the fault promptly while learning healthy behavior from normal history alone, because labeled faults are rare. Equally important, the monitor must keep false alarms low during nominal operation so operators can trust its alerts.

\noindent\emph{Why Observation-Space Reconstructors Suffer High False-Alarm Rates:} Deep detectors such as TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} reconstruct the raw sensor signal and alert when reconstruction error is large. Raw telemetry, however, contains unforecastable sensor jitter, thermal drift, and disparate channel scales. Summed across 25 to 55 channels, these misses form a heavy-tailed residual with frequent spikes (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Calibrated on healthy data via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} and evaluated on later healthy data, reconstructors raise false alarms during $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of normal operation; a random alerter firing as often would catch $60.0\%$ of anomaly events, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\noindent\emph{The Bottleneck of Scalar Score Aggregation:} Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoid fitting raw noise by compressing each telemetry window into a 32-dimensional unit-sphere summary and predicting future summaries in latent space, cutting false alarms to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$. Yet existing latent detectors still sum all coordinate errors into one scalar score compared against one threshold. If one or two of the 32 coordinates shift slightly between training and calibration---variance-dominating coordinates we term \emph{hijacker dials}---they dominate the sum and inflate the threshold, so faults on the remaining 30 coordinates go undetected: causal Point-F1 falls to $0.050\text{--}0.052$ and average delay exceeds 240 steps.

\noindent\emph{Four-Stage Architecture (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} We replace scalar score aggregation with a four-stage pipeline, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}). First, a causal dilated encoder maps context and target windows onto a 32-dimensional spherical latent space via layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and forecasts four nested sub-horizons with Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis whitening. Second, by structural analogy to Reynolds averaging in fluid mechanics \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} (without physical Navier--Stokes assumptions), we decompose each of the 32 coordinate error trajectories across the four nested horizons into seven kinematic views: four sub-horizon errors plus their horizon mean (drift), standard deviation (fluctuation), and endpoint difference (acceleration). Third, these $7 \times 32 = 224$ statistics feed an independently calibrated \emph{224-bit circuit-breaker panel} with per-dial calibration drift guards and 32 fast right-edge coincidence relays (\emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} when unquenched). Fourth, post-training \emph{surgical weight-space quenching} on healthy calibration windows removes mean predictor-bias drift along the LayerNorm tangent space and tempers top precision-matrix eigendirections before panel calibration.

\subsection{Contributions}
We separate our evaluation into a 19-stream (and 33-stream) development cohort for ablations and a disjoint 118-stream untouched held-out cohort (within a 141- to 162-stream archive across 15 datasets) providing primary generalization evidence (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{224-bit circuit-breaker panel and surgical quenching across development and untouched held-out cohorts:} Replacing scalar thresholding with the 224-bit panel and surgical weight-space quenching (Sections~\ref{subsec:reynolds_expansion}--\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel}) raises causal Point-F1 on the 19 development streams from $0.052$ to $0.138$ ($17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$), while \emph{Laminar-JEPA} reaches $0.262$ buffered Point-F1, $1.6\%$ causal false-positive rate (FPR), and $59.6$-step causal delay, trading sustained causal point coverage ($0.138$ vs.\ $0.100$ causal F1) for faster onset detection ($59.6$ vs.\ $105.9$ steps). On the 118 untouched held-out streams, a three-horizon panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.072\text{--}0.083$ (1D JEPAs) to $0.143$ ($p = 1.6{\times}10^{-13}$, dataset-clustered $p = 0.007$) and event recall from $51.2\%$ to $72.8\%$; across 162 streams (15 datasets), surgical quenching cuts causal held-out FPR by $8.0\%$ relative ($p = 0.0011$, dataset $p = 0.027$; median held-out FPR $1.03\%$, test FPR $1.59\%$).
    \item \emph{Mechanism isolation, quenching ablations, and multi-horizon latency scaling:} Matched-backbone ablations ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ parameters, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) isolate LayerNorm kurtosis compression ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$), learned latent targets (drift $D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$), barycenter centering (regime-drift FPR share $71.3\% \to 18.7\%$), the 1-to-224 dial ladder, and bias recentering vs.\ precision tempering across $k_{\text{rogue}} \in \{0,\dots,12\}$. Retraining the 224-bit panel and \emph{Laminar-JEPA} across target horizons $S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$ shows $1.9\times\text{--}4.3\times$ higher causal Point-F1 and $+26\%\text{--}38\%$ higher event recall than 1D scalar scoring down to $S=1$ (zero lag) and $S=4$ (3-step lag).
    \item \emph{False-alarm reduction and diagnostic fixed-budget recall:} On the 19 development streams, latent prediction holds autonomous causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($N=7$ dataset $p = 0.016$). Under a non-deployable post-hoc diagnostic control at $f^* = 1\%$ test FPR, the panel catches $58.4\%$ of anomaly events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet and reaches $0.658$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) on NASA contextual faults (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}--\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Calibration sample-size theory, single-channel branch, and null results:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds the horizon-fluctuation concentration ratio $R_{\text{LT}} \in [\frac{1}{M-1}, 1]$ (a structural representation property; scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ reweighting is null, $\Delta\text{F1} = -0.005$). Auditing effective sample sizes $N_{\text{eff}} \in \{100,\dots,5{,}000\}$ with moving-block bootstrap confidence intervals establishes a minimum requirement of $N_{\text{eff}} \ge 300$ independent samples ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 1{,}000$ windows) for empirical quantiles, while interquartile-range (IQR) and Beta-smoothed calibrators hold test FPR at $0.47\%\text{--}1.32\%$ when $N_{\text{eff}} \le 250$. Across 17 multivariate streams and six single-sensor fault classes, a complementary channel-level branch raises single-channel event recall from $61.4\%\text{--}80.7\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}98.2\%$, whereas slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{enumerate}
