\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains sensor jitter and scale disparities, observation residuals exhibit heavy-tailed excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), heavy-tailed residuals suffer calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} Predicting the \emph{summary} of a future window, rather than every raw sample, filters out unforecastable sensor jitter. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which forecast a 32-dimensional latent summary of the next $S$ steps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for low-latency tripwires) from the preceding 256 steps without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}. Alongside an unconstrained single-horizon baseline (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formulate a multi-horizon fluid-dynamic decomposition model, \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}. To capture fast onset transients while removing slow baseline drift, \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} taps intermediate temporal convolutional stages, subtracts online context batch barycenters, and whitens prediction residuals across four nested sub-horizons. It then separates the whitened residuals into mean transport and centered sub-horizon fluctuations, scoring wavepacket coherence via a Lieb--Thirring spectral concentration ratio. Our central contribution is relative false-alarm suppression, reduced calibration-to-holdout drift, and multi-horizon ranking gains rather than solved absolute calibration at $q=10^{-3}$. We evaluate: (i)~how much latent residuals reduce realized false-positive rates (FPRs) and out-of-sample calibration error $|\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}} - q|$ under static, declustered, and rolling calibrators; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent projection, multi-horizon whitening, and soliton concentration isolate the mechanism of improvement; (iii)~how detection varies across abrupt, gradual, and quiet single-channel faults; and (iv)~the trade-off between score-emission latency ($S-1$) and detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Factorial isolation of tail compression vs.\ drift reduction:} Across 12 matched-backbone and non-parametric arms ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ online parameters), a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) bottleneck bounds heavy residual tails ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$). Moving prediction into a learned latent space halves calibration-to-holdout drift ($D_{\text{KS}}: 0.376 \to 0.214$ in \texttt{TS-JEPA}), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.163$ (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)).
    \item \textbf{Fewer false alarms, leading multi-horizon ranking, and hybrid quiet-fault recovery:} Across the 19 non-GHL streams, latent scoring cuts held-out FPRs three-fold under retrospective buffered attribution ($9.3\%\text{--}10.8\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for TimesNet and TranAD) and ten- to eighty-fold under strictly causal streaming ($0.3\%\text{--}2.4\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA} achieves leading buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC $0.375$), point-wise F1 (Point-F1 $0.188$), and causal matched-FPR event recall ($0.701$ at $1\%$ FPR), and pairing latent prediction with a sensor variance monitor raises quiet single-channel event recall from $0.19\text{--}0.28$ to $0.42\text{--}0.50$ at a $+15.1\%$ nominal FPR cost (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault}(b) and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(b)).
    \item \textbf{Lieb--Thirring soliton concentration bounds and short-horizon latency control:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that Reynolds-centered multi-horizon fluctuation Gram matrices $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{E}'{\mathbf{E}'}^\top \in \mathcal{S}_+^M$ have an exact rank deficiency ($\lambda_1 \equiv 0$) and obey the tight scale-invariant bound $\frac{1}{M-1} \le R_{\text{LT}}(\mathbf{E}') \le 1$, separating isotropic sub-horizon noise ($R_{\text{LT}} = \frac{1}{M-1}$) from rank-1 soliton wavepackets ($R_{\text{LT}} = 1$). Fixed-backbone ablations show that the multi-horizon whitened envelope drives the ranking and drift gains while the post-hoc soliton multiplier satisfies Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence without altering Point-F1, and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step emission lag (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations} and~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
    \item \textbf{Decomposed calibration error across static, declustered, and adaptive rules:} A refitted permutation decomposition across all 19 non-GHL streams shows that inter-block regime drift accounts for $71.3\%$ of excess causal risk in single-horizon \texttt{TS-JEPA} and $78.3\%\text{--}81.8\%$ in reconstructors (with resolved positive confidence intervals (CIs)). In contrast, inter-block drift vanishes ($0.0\%$, $\text{FPR}_{\text{chr}} = 0.0033$ vs.\ $\text{FPR}_{\text{blk}} = 0.0037$) in barycenter-centered \texttt{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, and runs-declustered SPOT and rolling quantiles narrow the gap on step and variance shifts (Table~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
\end{enumerate}
