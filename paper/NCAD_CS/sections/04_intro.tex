\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In complex deployments (NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT, and biometric arrays), dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, where faults must be identified without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\textbf{False-Alarm Flooding Under Automated Threshold Calibration:} Operational monitoring requires automated decision rules that convert continuous anomaly scores into binary alarms using nominal calibration data alone. Prevailing deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) minimize point-level reconstruction error in raw observation space \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains ambient sensor jitter and channel-scale disparities, observation-space residuals exhibit severe excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$) and serial clustering. When paired with automated extreme-value thresholding such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} at nominal design risk $q = 10^{-3}$ ($0.1\%$), these heavy-tailed residuals suffer severe calibration breakdown on unseen nominal data, triggering false alarms across $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of nominal timesteps.

\noindent\textbf{Latent Residual Conditioning and Scope of Claims:} To suppress false-alarm flooding without synthetic anomaly injection \cite{carmona2021neural}, we study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) that score predictive discrepancies in a low-dimensional latent space \cite{lecun2022path}, alongside an \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (OpEntropy JEPA) variant regularized by Koopman spectral entropy and a shifted Bregman energy barrier. Physics-inspired regularizers (operator entropy, Reynolds-stress closure, curl-free potential flow) are evaluated strictly as algebraic priors on abstract latent coordinates, not as physical conservation laws, and our experiments separate three distinct operational objectives: (i)~suppressing nuisance false alarms under automated calibration, (ii)~detecting anomalies reliably across operating points, and (iii)~emitting scores early enough to support intervention.

\subsection{Contributions}
This study makes four focused contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Operational False-Alarm Reduction vs.\ Detection Sensitivity:} Across 33 continuous telemetry streams from seven physical datasets (19 non-GHL at five seeds; 14 GHL at three to five seeds), latent predictive scoring via TS-JEPA and OpEntropy JEPA reduces held-out nominal false-positive rates three- to four-fold under retrospective buffered attribution ($7.4\%\text{--}10.1\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$ TimesNet and $30.7\%$ TranAD; paired reductions $-0.142$ to $-0.153$, $p \le 0.006$) and $8\times\text{--}20\times$ under strictly causal trailing streaming ($1.1\%\text{--}2.8\%$ vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$). Evaluating both matched calibration quantiles ($q^* \in \{0.001, 0.050\}$) and direct matched realized test-FPR operating points ($f^* \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(b)) across all 19 non-GHL streams shows that latent models improve low-FPR causal event recall ($0.395\text{--}0.431$ vs.\ $0.217$ at $f^*{=}0.01$), whereas buffered recall, Point-F1, and PR-AUC remain statistically comparable to reconstructive baselines.
    \item \textbf{Mechanism Isolation (Compression vs.\ Representation Drift):} Across 12 controlled backbone arms on all 19 non-GHL streams (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_backbone}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(a), Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(e)), we separate score-distribution compression from representation learning: adding a $D{=}32$ LayerNorm bottleneck to an observation forecaster (\texttt{TCN-ObsPred-LN}) mechanically reduces calibration kurtosis ($\gamma_2: 6.10 \to 0.91$ causal) and halves causal FPR ($0.247 \to 0.117$), while learned latent projection in TS-JEPA halves calibration-to-holdout Kolmogorov--Smirnov drift ($0.376 \to 0.214$, $p=0.0010$; $0.376 \to 0.209$, $p=0.0004$ for \texttt{OpEntropy}) and lowers causal FPR to $0.009\text{--}0.028$.
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy Theory and Factorial Regularizer Null Results:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} distinguishes two regimes: at finite $\epsilon > 0$, the smooth Bregman barrier has vanishing matrix gradient at $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{0}$ and relies on non-degenerate cross-covariance ($\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{0}$) to exclude stationarity at zero, whereas in the $\epsilon \to 0^+$ limit the barrier alone diverges as $\|\mathbf{K}\|_F \to 0$. Factorial ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a),(c)) show that spectral regularization drives operator effective rank to $31.97\text{--}32.00/32$ and condition number to $\kappa(\mathbf{K}) = 1.03\text{--}1.08$, but yields no aggregate detection gain over unconstrained TS-JEPA, confirming Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence within $\pm 0.05$ F1 (Reynolds-Stress $p = 0.0008$; isolating the spectral regularizer vs.\ $\lambda_{\text{ent}}=0$ confirms equivalence within $\pm 0.03$ with $p < 0.0001$).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Risk Calibration Diagnostics, Latency, and Fault Boundaries:} Evaluating SPOT and empirical quantiles across target risks $q \in [10^{-4}, 10^{-1}]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_operating_calibration}(c)) shows that static calibration overshoots nominal design risk across all tested detectors ($1.0\%\text{--}1.5\%$ causal and $5.3\%\text{--}6.2\%$ buffered FPR at $q{=}10^{-3}$ for JEPAs vs.\ $16\%\text{--}22\%$ for reconstructors), with inter-block drift accounting for $84\%\text{--}96\%$ of causal overshoot under our exchangeable permutation protocol. Horizon sweeps ($S \in \{1, 4, 16, 32, 64\}$) and fault injections further establish where zero-lag ($S{=}1$) monitoring succeeds and where quiet flatline faults ($0.02\text{--}0.07$ recall) require complementary variance monitors.
\end{enumerate}
