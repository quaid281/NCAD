\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet stochastic jitter, benign thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ heterogeneous channels allows one noisy sensor to produce heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibration via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) on later healthy data across our 19 development streams. Because faults span many steps, random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes, matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next window's summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, still collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and one threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} drift during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1 and $138\text{--}154$-step delay on development streams). Published benchmarks are further inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial amplitude spikes, high anomaly density, and run-to-failure label bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint nominal blocks, benchmark against random-rate bounds, and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace the single scalar alarm with a panel of circuit breakers, like a building breaker box where a short in one circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across dyadic target prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, each of the 32 coordinates receives separate \emph{dials} tracking horizon error size and persistent drift, each with its own healthy-calibrated threshold, and the system alarms when a quorum of dials trips together. Drifting dials widen only their own thresholds instead of blinding the rest of the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Unlike a bank of independent detectors, the dials are structured views of one error matrix, and the quorum is fitted directly to joint trip counts on healthy data so cross-dial correlation is measured rather than assumed away. The panel does not act as a universally superior anomaly ranking score; instead, correlation-aware coordinate thresholding improves the label-free operating-point trade-off between live event coverage and detection delay without test-label threshold optimization. Targeting abrupt to intermediate-onset faults (within roughly one $256$-step context window), we establish a clear architectural hierarchy (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (primary operational architecture): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $(3+1)\times 32=128$ position-plus-drift dials under a single quorum ($120\text{k}\text{--}129\text{k}$ trainable parameters).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (diagnostic and ablation reference): shares the exact same encoder and parallel predictor across $M=4$ horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays) solely to isolate which error views aid live causal versus retrospective buffered attribution; adding fluctuation and relay dials inflates false alarms without improving live operational F1, making \emph{Lite} the preferred detector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Thirty-three development streams guide design-stage comparisons, while 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets (within a 141-stream, 12-dataset archive) under a disjoint five-block protocol test generalization and calibration reliability (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}), separating live \emph{causal} streaming from \emph{buffered} retrospective attribution:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Theory and live operating-point gain:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} proves that per-dial tripwires and quorums stay invariant under coordinate-scale drift that inflates global scalar thresholds. On the 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ across all $9$ datasets ($p=0.0039$) and cuts delay from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps at higher mean causal FPR ($6.4\%$ vs.\ $4.4\%$; median $0.93\%$ vs.\ $0.39\%$), doubling causal Point-F1 when $N_{\text{cal}}\ge 500$ ($0.040\to 0.085$, $p=3.2\times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \emph{Operating-point attribution and regime-shift failure modes:} Matched test-FPR controls confirm no score-ranking gain over 1D scoring; the benefit comes from where the label-free quorum sets the operating point. While $88.1\%$ of untouched streams hold causal $\text{FPR}_{\text{hold}}\le 5\%$ ($92\%\text{--}100\%$ across six datasets), post-calibration concept drift rather than finite-sample quantile variance drives the $64.7\%$ $95$th percentile (verified via moving-block bootstrap and healthy regime-shift stress tests), motivating a five-step acceptance and live $D_{\text{KS}}$ protocol.
    \item \emph{Baselines, interpretability, and deployment:} Latent prediction cuts development causal FPR from $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ (reconstructors) to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$; full-pipeline benchmarks verify single-core CPU streaming ($93\text{--}141$ windows/s, $\le 128\,\text{KB}$ state) and multi-horizon coordinate attribution across abrupt, contextual, and slow-ramp events.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds horizon energy (null scalar reweighting); $105/118$ streams lack independent tail support for standalone guarantees; synthetic channel-branch gains cost $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$ FPR without proving natural sensor-fault coverage; and slow ramps ($L\ge 4C$, including GHL) require a frozen-reference long-horizon drift monitor.
\end{enumerate}
