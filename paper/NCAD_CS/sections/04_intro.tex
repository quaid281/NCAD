\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in complex multi-sensor time series is essential for safeguarding mission-critical systems across aerospace, cyber-physical infrastructure, and industrial Internet-of-Things (IoT) \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In modern spacecraft telemetry—such as the Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) satellite and the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rover—thousands of sensors continuously record electrical currents, thermal gradients, and actuator kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. When unexpected operational deviations occur, automated systems must rapidly identify them before irreversible hardware degradation or mission failure takes place \cite{cuellar2024explainable}.

Despite extensive research, unsupervised time-series anomaly detection remains fundamentally constrained by three architectural dilemmas:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{The Reconstruction Dilemma:} Traditional generative and autoencoding models (e.g., LSTMs, VAEs, Masked Transformers) rely on minimizing pixel- or point-level reconstruction error in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, hoh2025rethinking, wen2025lgat}. However, raw sensor telemetry is inherently corrupted by high-frequency stochastic measurement noise and ambient jitter. Because reconstruction loss penalizes every point discrepancy equally, autoencoders expend substantial capacity memorizing uninformative high-frequency noise rather than learning the macro-scale physical dynamics governing system states.
    \item \textbf{The Synthetic Contrastive Trap:} To bypass signal reconstruction, contrastive frameworks like Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} train encoders via Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE). These methods inject artificial perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise spikes, sequence cut-and-paste) to generate negative samples. However, real physical anomalies (e.g., gradual thermal drift, actuator degradation, freezing of gait) manifest as subtle topological shifts along the system's dynamical manifold. Artificial synthetic injections distort the learned embedding geometry, inducing artificial decision boundaries that produce excessive false alarms or miss complex physical failure modes.
    \item \textbf{Discrete Codebook Quantization Losses:} While self-supervised Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) have achieved remarkable success in computer vision \cite{assran2023self}, recent attempts to adapt JEPA to time series (e.g., SC-JEPA \cite{he2026scjepa}) rely on Vector-Quantized (VQ) codebooks that discretize continuous trajectories into finite token vocabularies. For physical systems governed by smooth differential equations, discrete quantization introduces destructive discretization error and obscures smooth trajectory transitions.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges, we introduce \textbf{TS-JEPA} (Time-Series Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture), a non-contrastive self-supervised framework designed for unsupervised multivariate anomaly detection in continuous representation space. Instead of reconstructing raw input waveforms or synthesizing artificial negatives, TS-JEPA learns the true physical dynamical manifold of the system by predicting future latent representations directly.

As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:phase_space_attractor}, TS-JEPA maps nominal system behavior into stable, low-dimensional limit-cycle attractors in representation space. A causal dilated Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) context encoder processes historical context $x_{\text{ctx}}$, while a latent predictor network $P_\psi$ forecasts the latent representation $\hat{z}_{\text{tgt}}$ of the upcoming target window $x_{\text{tgt}}$. Target embeddings are generated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) momentum target encoder and regularized via a joint VICReg objective (Invariance, Variance hinge, Covariance decorrelation) \cite{bardes2021vicreg} to strictly prevent representation collapse without contrastive negative sampling.

During inference, anomalous system behavior is detected by measuring the \textbf{Covariance-Whitened (Mahalanobis) Discrepancy} between the observed target embedding and the predictor's forecast. By whitening the prediction residual with the empirical precision tensor $\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}$ estimated from nominal transitions, TS-JEPA normalizes directional variance and captures true dynamical violations. Finally, Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) tail calibration \cite{siffer2017anomaly} establishes an asymptotically sound decision threshold under the Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem, eliminating ad-hoc heuristic threshold collapse.

The primary contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{A Continuous Non-Contrastive World Model for Time Series:} We propose TS-JEPA, eliminating both signal-level reconstruction loss and synthetic anomaly injection biases by predicting future physical states in continuous latent space regularized with VICReg.
    \item \textbf{Covariance-Whitened Latent Discrepancy Scoring:} We formulate a Mahalanobis precision-weighted discrepancy metric that normalizes directional variance across multi-sensor latent axes, resolving zero-F1 detection collapse on complex telemetry channels.
    \item \textbf{Geometric Phase-Space Attractor Formulation:} We demonstrate through 2D/3D phase-space analysis that TS-JEPA maps nominal periodic dynamics to stable limit cycles, providing clear geometric interpretability for anomaly breakout.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Multi-Domain Benchmark Validation:} Across 26 benchmark channels spanning 5 diverse real-world domains (spacecraft telemetry, cloud streaming logs, environmental IoT, biomedical gait, and wearable activity), TS-JEPA achieves a \textbf{+35.6\% grand macro PA-F1 lift} over baseline contrastive models and demonstrates universal domain-agnostic superiority.
\end{itemize}
