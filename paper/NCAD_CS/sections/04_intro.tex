\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable}. In NASA SMAP and MSL rovers, industrial process loops, and biometric sensor arrays, dozens of channels track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. Monitors must identify subsystem faults rapidly without overwhelming operators with nuisance alerts.

\noindent\emph{Twin Bottlenecks: Observation-Space Noise Flooding and 1D Scalar Score Collapse:} In deployed monitoring, an automated detector must trigger binary alarms using healthy (nominal) calibration data alone. Standard deep detectors (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer) reconstruct raw sensor readings and flag timestamps with large point-by-point error \cite{xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet, tuli2022tranad}. Because raw telemetry contains sensor jitter and scale differences across channels, point-level errors produce frequent, clustered spikes and a heavy-tailed residual distribution with high excess kurtosis ($\gamma_2 > 8$, Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Under automated calibration rules such as Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly}, normal background shifts easily cross the threshold, triggering false alarms on $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of healthy timesteps. Shifting prediction into a compact latent space suppresses sensor jitter, but existing latent detectors still collapse their multi-dimensional prediction residuals into a single 1D scalar anomaly score before calibration. In multi-horizon latent spaces ($4 \times 32 = 128$ whitened coordinates), if even one or two coordinates experience mild out-of-sample variance shift between training and calibration (\emph{hijacker dials}), summing or gating all coordinates into a 1D scalar inflates the calibration tail and pushes the scalar threshold to the extreme tip of the distribution, causing the detector to fire only on isolated peak steps inside an anomaly.

\noindent\emph{Fluid-Dynamic Latent Prediction and the 224-Bit Circuit-Breaker Panel:} Predicting a compact \emph{summary} of a future time window rather than every noisy sensor sample filters out random jitter before the prediction error is computed. We study Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path}, which compress the preceding 256 timesteps into a 32-dimensional summary vector and forecast the summary of the next $S$ timesteps ($S=64$ by default, or $S \in \{1, 4\}$ for fast tripwires) using healthy training data alone \cite{carmona2021neural}. Alongside a single-horizon baseline (TS-JEPA), we introduce \textit{Soliton-Reynolds JEPA}, which taps multiple depths of its temporal convolutional network \cite{bai2018empirical}, subtracts the running context barycenter to cancel slow level shifts, and whitens prediction errors across $M=4$ nested sub-horizons. Drawing on Reynolds decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} and Lieb--Thirring spectral bounds \cite{lieb1976inequalities}, it separates multi-horizon errors into mean transport and zero-mean fluctuations. To eliminate 1D scalar score collapse and parametric tail extrapolation altogether, we pair the multi-horizon predictor with a \emph{224-bit multi-perspective circuit-breaker panel} that expands the $32$ latent coordinates into $7 \times 32 = 224$ fluid-dynamic dials (128 sub-horizon flow positions, 32 Reynolds slow-drift transport dials, 32 Reynolds turbulent-fluctuation dials, and 32 convective right-edge acceleration dials) monitored by independent hijack-guarded binary switches and 32 fast right-edge onset relays. We evaluate four questions: (i)~how much latent representations and the 224-bit circuit-breaker panel lower realized false-positive rates (FPRs) while improving point-wise F1, event recall, and detection delay; (ii)~how normalization, learned latent targets, multi-horizon whitening, and multi-perspective Boolean tripwires each contribute; (iii)~how detection varies across point spikes, contextual faults, and quiet single-sensor freezes; and (iv)~how shortening $S$ trades off alarm lag ($S-1$) against detection quality.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Zero-collapse 224-bit fluid-dynamic circuit-breaker panel:} We show that collapsing 128 whitened sub-horizon residuals into a 1D scalar score allows isolated drifting coordinates to inflate scalar thresholds and suppress causal Point-F1 ($0.050\text{--}0.052$). Replacing 1D score collapse with a parameter-free 224-bit circuit-breaker panel and 32 fast right-edge onset relays more than doubles causal Point-F1 ($0.052 \to 0.116\text{--}0.138$ on $n{=}19$ primary streams, $17/0$ wins, $p = 1.5{\times}10^{-5}$; $0.067\text{--}0.077 \to 0.125\text{--}0.139$ across $n{=}141$ archive streams, $114/18$ wins, $p = 1.2{\times}10^{-16}$), lifts causal event recall to $60.7\%\text{--}72.7\%$, and cuts detection delay in half (Sections~\ref{subsubsec:circuit_breaker_panel} and~\ref{sec:results}, Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}--\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}).
    \item \emph{Why latent predictors stabilize alarm decisions:} Across matched-backbone models ($272\text{k}\text{--}312\text{k}$ online parameters), a layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} bottleneck compresses residual kurtosis from $6.10$ to $0.91$, predicting a learned latent summary cuts calibration-to-holdout drift in half ($D_{\text{KS}}$ from $0.376$ to $0.214$), and multi-horizon barycenter whitening in Soliton-Reynolds JEPA lowers drift further to $D_{\text{KS}} = 0.179$ while cutting the inter-block drift share of excess causal false alarms four-fold from $71.3\%$ to $18.7\%$ (Tables~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) and~\ref{tab:calibrator_comparison}(b)).
    \item \emph{Fewer false alarms, contextual-anomaly dominance, and quiet-fault recovery:} Across the 19 non-GHL streams, latent prediction cuts held-out FPRs dramatically relative to TimesNet and TranAD ($0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ causal and $7.8\%\text{--}9.8\%$ buffered vs.\ $22.2\%\text{--}30.7\%$ for reconstructors) while achieving $0.411$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC; $0.312$ causal), leading on NASA contextual anomalies, and recovering quiet single-channel freezes when paired with a rolling sensor-variance monitor (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark} and~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Figs.~\ref{fig:regularizer_and_quiet_fault} and~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Multi-horizon wavepacket bounds, ablation controls, and fast response:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} proves that the Reynolds-centered Lieb--Thirring concentration ratio satisfies $\frac{1}{M-1} \le R_{\text{LT}} \le 1$ with exact radial scale invariance, while fixed-backbone ablations show that multi-horizon whitened perspectives drive the primary gains (with post-hoc scalar wavepacket multipliers satisfying Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) \cite{schuirmann1987comparison} equivalence) and short horizons ($S \in \{1, 4\}$) eliminate the 63-step window lag (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}(a)).
\end{enumerate}
