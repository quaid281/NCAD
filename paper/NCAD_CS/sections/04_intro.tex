\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold: when one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise the shared threshold and mask moderate faults across the remaining 30 coordinates ($0.050\text{--}0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also inflated by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

We replace scalar scoring with an integrated multi-horizon circuit-breaker system, like a breaker box where each circuit trips its own switch regardless of total load. Across dyadic prefixes of 16, 32, and 64 steps, whitened coordinates and their cross-block mean receive individual healthy tripwires and fire when a correlation-aware quorum trips together. Under matched calibration and operational scaling, wider variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}), shifting the label-free operating point toward higher coverage within a $256$-step context window (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational compromise): predicts $M=3$ dyadic horizons in parallel behind one layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and monitors $128$ position-plus-drift dials under one quorum, using $119.8\text{k}\text{--}129.3\text{k}$ trainable parameters and $0.90\text{--}0.97$ megabytes (MB) of FP32 weights with the target encoder.
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (224-dial reference): monitors $M=4$ horizons across $224$ continuous dials plus $32$ separate onset relays; \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive causal recall points against this reference for lower mean, median, and buffered false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference, though with a higher 95th-percentile tail.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our primary contribution is the complete streaming detector and gated deployment workflow rather than the supporting algebraic identities alone. Each stream fits on its own healthy prefix, testing whether one frozen architecture and calibration rule transfer across sensor suites without per-dataset tuning. From a 45-stream development pool, 33 qualifying streams guide design, and 118 untouched archive streams across 9 datasets evaluate generalization under a disjoint five-block protocol (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Coordinate quorum and live operating point:} Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} shows that per-coordinate quantile tripwires stay invariant when a subset of coordinates shares wider variance across calibration and operation. Across 118 untouched streams, \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ ($p=0.0039$) over a single-score multi-horizon JEPA and cuts end-to-end alarm latency from $133.5$ to $69.0$ steps at $6.4\%$ mean healthy FPR ($0.93\%$ median). Overall Point-F1 ($0.076\to 0.086$) spans zero across datasets, while doubling on streams with at least $500$ calibration windows ($p=0.047$).
    \item \emph{Whitening structure, drift, and gated screening:} Replacing dense whitening with strictly horizon-preserving Kronecker whitening matches accuracy across all $118$ streams ($0.101$ vs.\ $0.091$ F1, $p=0.76$), and matched-FPR checks confirm ranking parity with scalar JEPAs. Empirical two-stage screening accepts $52$ of $118$ streams ($3.9\%$ median FPR on subsequent healthy test intervals) and routes $66$ to a 1D fallback. Shuffling healthy blocks links $92.3\%$ of excess false alarms to chronological nonstationarity, motivating quarantined drift monitoring that awaits confirmed mode switches.
    \item \emph{External baselines and perturbation attribution:} Under untouched-archive SPOT calibration, TimesNet ($0.175$ F1) and TranAD ($0.157$ F1) reach higher raw F1 only at $24.6\%\text{--}25.6\%$ mean healthy FPR, dropping to $25.5\%\text{--}25.9\%$ ER at $1\%$ test FPR versus $51.2\%$ for \emph{Lite}. Single-core CPU streaming sustains $93\text{--}141$ windows/s within $128$ kilobytes (KB) of state, and post-alarm leave-one-channel-out (LOCO) masking reaches $85.7\%$ Top-1 synthetic sensor-subset attribution on injected shifts.
    \item \emph{Operational boundaries:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds multi-block error energy with a null reweighting effect, while serial dependence leaves $105$ of $118$ streams without distribution-free tail guarantees. Auxiliary single-channel guards inflate clean FPR to $6.8\%\text{--}19.4\%$, and frozen-reference slow-ramp recovery on industrial heaters incurs $74.4\%$ test FPR from baseline wander.
\end{enumerate}
