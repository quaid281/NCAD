\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments (such as the NASA Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) satellite, Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rovers, industrial IoT facilities, and wearable biometric sensor arrays), dozens to hundreds of continuous telemetry channels concurrently track electrical power buses, thermal gradients, mechanical vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. When sensor faults or operational breakdowns emerge at the edge, monitoring systems must identify dynamical anomalies before hardware damage occurs \cite{cuellar2024explainable}.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
From a physical perspective, multi-sensor telemetry reflects the state trajectory of an underlying continuous physical system $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$. However, attempting to model dynamical equations directly in raw observation space presents four main challenges on edge devices:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Numerical Instability in Empirical Differentiation:} Estimating instantaneous time derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ directly on discrete, noise-corrupted sensor streams amplifies high-frequency measurement noise, destabilizing numerical trajectory tracking.
    \item \textit{Partial Observability \& Unmeasured Internal States:} Remote sensing telemetry captures only a noisy surface projection of unmeasured thermodynamic, electrical, and environmental states, violating full-state observability.
    \item \textit{High-Dimensional Cross-Sensor Coupling:} In multi-sensor arrays ($K \ge 50$), cross-channel dependencies and non-linear interactions cannot be adequately represented by rigid functional forms in raw signal space.
    \item \textit{Non-Stationary Downlink \& Operational Drift:} Edge IoT and satellite systems undergo discrete operational mode transitions (e.g., orbital day/night thermal cycling, thruster firings, Parkinsonian gait freezing, cloud server garbage collection) that cause continuous distribution drift.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Deep learning architectures provide data-driven alternatives to explicit equation modeling, yet they face three primary limitations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Signal-Level Noise Memorization:} Generative models (e.g., autoencoders, VAEs, masked Transformers) minimize point-level $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}. Because reconstruction loss penalizes every discrepancy equally, autoencoders allocate capacity to fitting high-frequency sensor jitter rather than learning nominal dynamical transitions.
    \item \textit{Limitations of Synthetic Contrastive Perturbations:} Contrastive frameworks such as Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} train encoders via Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE) using heuristic synthetic perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise spikes, sequence splicing). However, real physical faults manifest as subtle deviations from nominal dynamics. Synthetic injections distort embedding geometry, inducing artificial decision boundaries that generate false alarms on continuous telemetry.
    \item \textit{Scale Distortion and Threshold Fragility in Observation Space:} In high-dimensional observation space, reconstruction residual magnitude is dominated by high-variance background sensors. Under automated streaming thresholding such as Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT), this distortion causes extreme-value calibrators to fit to ambient sensor noise rather than anomalous state changes, triggering false alarm flooding in operational telemetry.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Approach: Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures}
To eliminate signal-space noise memorization without relying on synthetic contrastive perturbations, we formulate a family of \textit{Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures} (PI-JEPA) for multi-sensor telemetry. Instead of reconstructing raw, jitter-corrupted sensor observations $\hat{x} \approx x$, a JEPA maps past observations $x_{\text{ctx}}$ into an abstract representation space and predicts target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ directly \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}. By operating in latent space, the architecture discards high-frequency stochastic sensor noise that lacks long-range predictability.

However, unconstrained latent prediction alone can be underspecified in multi-sensor telemetry. To incorporate physical domain priors into self-supervised representation learning, we introduce three physics-informed JEPA architectures featuring inductive dynamical regularizers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Operator-Entropy JEPA (Koopman Spectral Mechanics):} Parameterizes a latent linear Koopman transition matrix and monitors spectral stability via normalized dynamic singular-value entropy. Discrepancies between predicted and target spectral profiles isolate anomalous damping and spectral decoherence.
    \item \textbf{Reynolds-Stress JEPA (Continuum Mechanics \& Covariance Alignment):} Inspired by hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition ($z = \bar{z} + z'$), this architecture aligns the second-order cross-channel fluctuation covariance tensors $\mathbf{R}_{ij} = \mathbb{E}[z'_i z'_j]$. Structural anomalies disrupt multi-sensor covariance balance.
    \item \textbf{Potential-Flow JEPA (Conservative Field Theory):} Enforces conservative latent trajectory transitions by parameterizing the transition field as the gradient of a scalar potential ($\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$). Because gradient fields are irrotational by construction, non-conservative dynamical anomalies produce large predictive discrepancies.
\end{enumerate}

To stabilize learning across heterogeneous high-dimensional sensor arrays, all three architectures incorporate Coordinate Saliency Gating to isolate active channels, Dyadic Wavelet Shells to decouple high-frequency transients from baseline drift, and a Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) calibrator that modulates tail thresholds against non-Gaussian kurtosis.

\subsection{Contributions}
The contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Physics-Informed Joint-Embedding Framework:} We formulate an unsupervised anomaly detection framework that embeds physical regularizers (spectral dissipation, turbulent momentum transfer, conservative flow) directly into latent representations, avoiding raw-space noise reconstruction.
    \item \textit{Physics-Informed JEPA Formulations:} We derive and implement three physics-informed architectures (\texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, and \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA}) and benchmark them alongside an unconstrained control (\texttt{TS-JEPA}).
    \item \textit{Inductive Architectural Components:} We introduce Coordinate Saliency Gating for cross-channel fault localization, Dyadic Wavelet decomposition for multi-resolution temporal analysis, and Kurtosis-Adaptive EVT tail calibration to provide automated streaming alarms without transductive test tuning.
    \item \textit{Multi-Tier Benchmark Evaluation:} We evaluate performance across 11 telemetry datasets (45 matched channels), reporting Dataset-Macro, Channel-Macro, Pooled Micro-F1, and Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) alongside an analytical All-Positive reference baseline. We show that latent prediction resolves threshold calibration collapse, achieving up to \textbf{+43.2\% relative improvement} in Dataset-Macro F1 and a \textbf{3.2$\times$ to 5.3$\times$ gain} in Pooled Micro-F1 while reducing false positives by an order of magnitude compared to reconstructive baselines.
\end{itemize}
