\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams. At that false-alarm rate, even random alarms would catch $60.0\%$ of fault episodes, about as many as TimesNet ($58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 prediction errors into one scalar score with one threshold. When one or two noisy coordinates (\emph{hijackers}) inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise that shared threshold and mask moderate faults on the other 30 coordinates, leaving live Point-F1 at only $0.055$. Published benchmarks are also skewed by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed partial differential equation loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we borrow fluid-mechanics diagnostics purely as post-hoc residual algebra. In latent space, normal operation behaves like a steady \emph{laminar} baseline, whereas an isolated noisy sensor acts like a local disturbance and a real fault behaves like a coherent multi-scale instability. Across nested horizons of $16$, $32$, and $64$ steps, we apply Reynolds mean-fluctuation decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} to separate sustained drift from brief errors. Each whitened coordinate and mean-drift \emph{dial} then receives its own healthy tripwire, much like a circuit-breaker panel in which each breaker tracks its own branch rather than total load. To keep isolated noise spikes from sounding false alarms, the monitor fires only when a \emph{quorum} of dials trips at the same time, meaning the count of simultaneously active dials exceeds a minimum voting threshold learned directly from healthy history. Because normal correlated bursts set that count threshold automatically, capping each dial to a single binary vote stops noisy coordinates from inflating a global score. We study two configurations (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational model): predicts three nested horizons in parallel behind a single layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization}, monitors $128$ position-and-drift dials under one quorum, and fits within $1$ megabyte (MB; Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($224$-dial reference): extends the panel to four horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays); \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points for lower false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our central contribution is moving away from single-scalar anomaly thresholding in label-free time-series monitoring by pairing fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics with per-coordinate quorum voting, fitted solely to each stream's own healthy history ($K\in[2,55]$ channels, Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics:} \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} is trained with an unconstrained predictive objective, and Reynolds decomposition of its multi-horizon latent residuals separates sustained cross-horizon drift from brief errors. This separation drives our largest live-detection and latency gains (Section~\ref{subsec:controlled_ablations}).
    \item \emph{Coordinate quorum voting in place of single-scalar thresholding:} Each dial carries its own healthy tripwire, so a noisy coordinate moves only its own threshold, and the alarm requires a healthy-calibrated quorum of simultaneous trips. The gain comes from a stronger label-free operating point when scalar calibration under-triggers, not from better anomaly ranking.
    \item \emph{Strict multi-domain evaluation and operational readiness:} On $118$ untouched streams from nine spacecraft, server, and industrial datasets, standalone \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} raises causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ over single-score multi-horizon JEPA and detects shared faults $41.4$ steps earlier, at the cost of a higher prospective test FPR ($17.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$). Because $128$ separate tripwires need enough stable history to calibrate, a label-free readiness check routes short or drifting streams back to scalar calibration ($59.4\%$ ER at $10.0\%$ test FPR overall), and a quarantined recalibration policy handles mode switches. We also document where the panel fails: slow ramps, single-sensor flatlines, and regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}
