\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in complex multi-sensor time series is essential for safeguarding mission-critical systems across aerospace, cyber-physical infrastructure, industrial Internet-of-Things (IoT), and digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In modern cyber-physical deployments---such as the NASA Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) spacecraft, Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rovers, and wearable biomedical monitors---dozens to hundreds of continuous sensors concurrently track mechanical vibrations, electrical telemetry, thermal gradients, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. When unexpected operational faults develop, autonomous monitoring systems must identify subtle dynamical breakdowns before catastrophic hardware failure occurs \cite{cuellar2024explainable}.

\subsection{The Dilemma of Modeling Complex Physical Telemetry}
From a classical physics perspective, multi-sensor telemetry reflects the state trajectory of an underlying continuous dynamical system $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$. However, attempting to model continuous dynamical laws directly in raw observation space faces four fundamental roadblocks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{The Numerical Derivative Explosion:} Estimating instantaneous time derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ on noisy, discrete sensor streams amplifies high-frequency measurement noise, catastrophically destabilizing continuous trajectory tracking.
    \item \textit{Partial Observability \& Hidden States:} Physical telemetry captures only a low-dimensional, noisy surface projection of unmeasured internal thermodynamic, electrical, and environmental states, violating full-state observability.
    \item \textit{High-Dimensional Non-Linear Coupling:} In multi-channel systems ($K \ge 50$), complex cross-channel dependencies and non-linear interactions cannot be adequately represented by rigid, hand-crafted functional forms in raw signal space.
    \item \textit{Stochastic Regime Transitions:} Real-world systems undergo discrete operational mode shifts (e.g., spacecraft thruster firings, Parkinsonian gait freezing, cloud server garbage collection) that violate smooth, single-mode differential equations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
To bypass explicit equation modeling, deep learning architectures have emerged as data-driven alternatives, yet they remain constrained by three fundamental dilemmas:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{The Reconstruction Memorization Dilemma:} Traditional generative models (e.g., Autoencoders, VAEs, Masked Transformers) minimize point-level $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}. Because reconstruction loss penalizes every point discrepancy equally, autoencoders expend substantial capacity memorizing high-frequency stochastic sensor jitter rather than learning the macro-scale dynamical laws governing nominal transitions.
    \item \textit{The Synthetic Contrastive Trap:} Contrastive frameworks like Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} train encoders via Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE) using heuristic synthetic perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise spikes, sequence splicing). However, real physical faults manifest as subtle topological breakouts from nominal dynamical attractors. Synthetic injections distort the learned embedding geometry, inducing artificial decision boundaries that produce excessive false alarms.
    \item \textit{Conditional Mean Collapse in Multi-Branch Dynamics:} Deterministic predictive encoders trained with MSE loss $\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{z} - z\|^2]$ are mathematically constrained to predict the \textit{conditional mean} $\mathbb{E}[z_{\text{tgt}} \mid z_{\text{ctx}}]$. When physical systems possess multiple valid operational branches, the conditional mean lands in unphysical, empty latent space, triggering false alarms during legitimate state transitions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Approach: Flow-Matching Joint-Embedding Architectures}
To resolve raw-space numerical noise bottlenecks and the reconstruction/collapse traps of deep baselines, we introduce TS-JEPA and its continuous generalization Flow-JEPA (Time-Series Flow-Matching Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture). Rather than attempting to integrate stiff trajectories in raw sensor space or reconstructing raw waveforms, our framework embeds the system into an abstract representation space and directly learns the continuous velocity vector field of the nominal physical manifold via Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) \cite{lipman2022flow}.

As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:phase_space_attractor}, TS-JEPA maps nominal system behavior into stable limit-cycle attractors in representation space. A causal dilated Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) or Patch Transformer context encoder processes historical context $x_{\text{ctx}}$, while a flow velocity network $v_\psi(z_t, t, z_{\text{ctx}})$ models the continuous probability paths leading to target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$. The representation space is regularized via non-contrastive VICReg (Invariance, Variance hinge, Covariance decorrelation) \cite{bardes2021vicreg} to strictly preclude dimensional collapse.

During inference, anomalous system behavior is detected by measuring the Deterministic Midpoint Velocity Discrepancy whitened by an empirical Mahalanobis precision tensor $\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}$. Finally, Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) tail calibration \cite{siffer2017anomaly} establishes an asymptotically sound decision threshold under the Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem, completely eliminating ad-hoc heuristic threshold tuning.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
The primary contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Continuous Dynamical Flow Matching on Latent Manifolds:} We reframe unsupervised time-series anomaly detection as continuous tangent velocity field learning on a joint-embedding manifold, bypassing stiff raw-space numerical integration and eliminating deep autoencoder reconstruction traps.
    \item \textit{Optimal Transport Flow-Matching in JEPA Space (Flow-JEPA):} We formulate Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching within an EMA-regularized latent space, modeling complex multi-branching operational regimes without conditional-mean collapse.
    \item \textit{Deterministic Midpoint Evaluation \& Mahalanobis Whitening:} We derive a zero-variance deterministic velocity discrepancy metric evaluated at the Optimal Transport midpoint ($t=0.5$), enabling Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) extreme value calibration to lock onto true tail risks with mathematical precision.
    \item \textit{Comprehensive Multi-Domain Benchmark Validation:} Evaluated across 11 diverse benchmark datasets comprising 130 continuous telemetry channels (and extended distributed streaming ablations on the 30-channel Exathlon suite, totaling over 1,300 model-channel runs), our framework consistently surpasses modern deep baselines (TimesNet, TranAD, Anomaly Transformer, DCdetector, NCAD) under both channel-weighted and unweighted dataset macro metrics.
\end{itemize}
