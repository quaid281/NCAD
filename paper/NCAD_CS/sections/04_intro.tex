\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, chemical plants, server farms, and wearable medical devices report health through dozens of continuous sensor channels \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. When a valve sticks, a reaction wheel drifts, or a patient's gait freezes, an automated monitor must detect the fault promptly while learning healthy behavior from normal history alone, because labeled faults are rare. Equally important, the monitor must keep false alarms low during nominal operation so operators can trust its alerts.

\noindent\emph{Why Observation-Space Reconstructors Suffer High False-Alarm Rates:} Deep detectors such as TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly} reconstruct the raw sensor signal and alert when reconstruction error is large. Raw telemetry, however, contains unforecastable sensor jitter, thermal drift, and disparate channel scales. Summed across 25 to 55 channels, these misses form a heavy-tailed residual with frequent spikes (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Calibrated on healthy data via Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} and evaluated on later healthy data, reconstructors raise false alarms during $22\%\text{--}31\%$ of normal operation; a random alerter firing as often would catch $60.0\%$ of anomaly events, exceeding TimesNet's $58.8\%$.

\noindent\emph{The Bottleneck of Scalar Score Aggregation:} Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) \cite{lecun2022path, carmona2021neural} avoid fitting raw noise by compressing each telemetry window into a 32-dimensional unit-sphere summary and predicting future summaries in latent space, cutting false alarms to $0.6\%\text{--}2.4\%$. Yet existing latent detectors still sum all coordinate errors into one scalar score compared against one threshold. If one or two of the 32 coordinates shift slightly between training and calibration---variance-dominating coordinates we term \emph{hijacker dials}---they dominate the sum and inflate the threshold, so faults on the remaining 30 coordinates go undetected: causal Point-F1 falls to $0.050\text{--}0.052$ and average delay exceeds 240 steps.

\noindent\emph{Four-Stage Architecture (\emph{Laminar-JEPA}):} We replace scalar score aggregation with a four-stage pipeline, \emph{Laminar-JEPA} (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}). First, a causal dilated encoder maps context and target windows onto a 32-dimensional spherical latent space via layer-normalization (LayerNorm) \cite{ba2016layernormalization} and forecasts nested target prefixes; training-residual covariance is filtered by a resolvent precision transform. Second, by structural analogy to Reynolds averaging \cite{reynolds1895dynamical}, we decompose 32 coordinate error trajectories into four horizon errors, their mean, fluctuation, and endpoint difference. Third, these $7\times32=224$ statistics feed independently calibrated circuit-breaker dials and 32 right-edge coincidence relays (\emph{Reynolds 224-Bit} when unquenched). Fourth, optional post-training \emph{weight-space quenching} scales selected predictor rows and adjusts calibration buffers before panel calibration.

\subsection{Contributions}
We separate our evaluation into a 19-stream (and 33-stream) development cohort for ablations and a disjoint 118-stream untouched held-out cohort (within a 141- to 162-stream archive across 15 datasets) providing primary generalization evidence (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Panel, quenching, and held-out cohorts:} On the reproducible 19-stream ablation cache, the unquenched panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.013$ to $0.071$ versus scalar scoring ($p=0.0023$), while the 160-dial subset reaches $0.098$. The separately trained 33-stream benchmark yields $0.262$ buffered Point-F1 and $59.6$-step causal delay for quenched \emph{Laminar-JEPA}; these are not paired measurements on the rechecked cache. On 118 untouched held-out streams, the unquenched three-horizon panel raises causal Point-F1 from $0.083$ to $0.143$ and event recall from $51.2\%$ to $72.8\%$; four-horizon Laminar-JEPA reaches $62.2\%$ event recall. In a matched four-horizon 162-stream comparison, quenching lowers causal held-out false-positive rate (FPR) by $8.2\%$ relative but slightly lowers causal Point-F1.
    \item \emph{Mechanism and latency limits:} Matched-backbone controls in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) measure latent bottleneck and calibration-drift effects; scalar spectral reweighting offers no reliable benefit. The rechecked dial ladder in Panel~(a) and a separate residual-mean-buffer recentering study test scoring alternatives without identifying one universally best configuration. An exploratory five-stream sweep over $S\in\{1,4,16,32,64\}$ finds $1.9$--$4.3$ times the scalar causal Point-F1 at $S=1,4$; it cannot establish cross-domain latency generalization.
    \item \emph{False-alarm reduction and diagnostic fixed-budget recall:} On the 19 development streams, latent prediction holds autonomous causal FPR at $0.6\%\text{--}1.7\%$ versus $22.2\%\text{--}24.3\%$ for reconstructors ($N=7$ dataset $p = 0.016$). Under a non-deployable post-hoc diagnostic control at $f^* = 1\%$ test FPR, the panel catches $58.4\%$ of anomaly events versus $21.7\%$ for TimesNet and reaches $0.658$ buffered precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) on NASA contextual faults (Tables~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}--\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}, Fig.~\ref{fig:operating_and_horizon}).
    \item \emph{Calibration sample size, single-channel branch, and null results:} Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_collapse} bounds the horizon-fluctuation concentration ratio $R_{\text{LT}} \in [\frac{1}{M-1}, 1]$ (a structural representation property; scalar $R_{\text{LT}}$ reweighting is null, $\Delta\text{F1} = -0.005$). Moving-block bootstrap analyses suggest that longer nominal histories stabilize empirical thresholds, but $N_{\text{eff}}=300$ does not establish reliability for the $99.9$th percentile. Interquartile-range (IQR) and Beta-smoothed calibrators yielded test FPR of $0.47\%\text{--}1.32\%$ in the evaluated short-history settings. Across 17 multivariate streams and six injected single-sensor fault classes, a complementary channel-level branch raises event recall from $61.4\%\text{--}80.7\%$ to $93.0\%\text{--}98.2\%$; these controlled injections do not establish performance on naturally occurring sensor faults, and slow ramps remain undetected.
\end{enumerate}
