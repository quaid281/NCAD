\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments (such as the NASA Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) satellite, Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rovers, industrial IoT facilities, and wearable biometric sensor arrays), dozens to hundreds of continuous telemetry channels concurrently track electrical power buses, thermal gradients, mechanical vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}. When sensor faults or operational breakdowns emerge at the edge, monitoring systems must identify dynamical anomalies before hardware damage occurs \cite{cuellar2024explainable}.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
From a physical perspective, multi-sensor telemetry reflects the state trajectory of an underlying continuous physical system $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$. However, attempting to model dynamical equations directly in raw observation space faces four core challenges on edge devices. First, estimating instantaneous time derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ directly on discrete, noise-corrupted sensor streams amplifies high-frequency measurement noise, destabilizing numerical trajectory tracking. Second, remote sensing telemetry captures only a noisy surface projection of unmeasured thermodynamic, electrical, and environmental states, violating full-state observability. Third, in large sensor arrays ($K \ge 50$), cross-channel dependencies and non-linear interactions cannot be adequately represented by rigid functional forms in raw signal space. Finally, edge IoT and satellite systems undergo discrete operational mode transitions (e.g., orbital day/night thermal cycling, thruster firings, Parkinsonian gait freezing, cloud server garbage collection) that cause continuous distribution drift.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Deep learning architectures provide data-driven alternatives to explicit equation modeling, yet they face three primary limitations. First, generative models (e.g., autoencoders, VAEs, masked Transformers) minimize point-level $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}. Because reconstruction loss penalizes every discrepancy equally, autoencoders allocate capacity to fitting high-frequency sensor jitter rather than learning nominal dynamical transitions. Second, contrastive frameworks such as Neural Contextual Anomaly Detection (NCAD) \cite{carmona2021neural} train encoders via Contextual Outlier Exposure (COE) using heuristic synthetic perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise spikes, sequence splicing). However, real physical faults manifest as subtle deviations from nominal dynamics; synthetic injections distort embedding geometry, inducing artificial decision boundaries that generate false alarms on continuous telemetry. Third, in high-dimensional observation space, even after per-channel standardization, Euclidean reconstruction errors ($\|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2 = \sum_k (x_k - \hat{x}_k)^2$) accumulate stochastic residual variance across dozens of irrelevant or noisy sensors. Under automated streaming thresholding such as Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT), this residual accumulation yields heavy, unstable tail distributions that distort extreme-value calibrators, triggering false alarm flooding in operational telemetry.

\subsection{Our Approach: Physics-Inspired Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures}
To substantially suppress sensitivity to observation-space noise without relying on synthetic contrastive perturbations, we formulate a family of \textit{Physics-Inspired Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures} (PI-JEPA) for multi-sensor telemetry. Instead of reconstructing raw, jitter-corrupted sensor observations $\hat{x} \approx x$, a JEPA maps past observations $x_{\text{ctx}}$ into an abstract representation space and predicts target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ directly \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}. By operating in latent representation space, the architecture filters out high-frequency stochastic sensor noise that lacks long-range dynamical predictability.

However, unconstrained latent prediction alone can be underspecified in complex multi-sensor telemetry. To incorporate physical domain priors into self-supervised representation learning, we introduce three physics-inspired JEPA architectures featuring inductive dynamical regularizers. The first formulation, \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, parameterizes a latent transition matrix regularized by Von Neumann spectral entropy during pretraining to penalize dimensional collapse and preserve linear modal expressivity in multi-sensor dynamics. The second, \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, adapts hydrodynamic Reynolds decomposition ($z = \bar{z} + z'$) to align second-order fluctuation covariance across latent coordinates, capturing structural anomalies that disrupt multi-coordinate covariance balance. The third, \textit{Potential-Flow JEPA}, enforces conservative latent trajectory transitions by parameterizing the transition field as the gradient of a scalar potential ($\hat{v} = \nabla \Phi$); because gradient fields are irrotational by construction, non-conservative dynamical anomalies produce pronounced predictive discrepancies.

To stabilize learning across heterogeneous high-dimensional sensor arrays, all three architectures incorporate Coordinate Saliency Gating to isolate active channels, Dyadic Wavelet Shells to decouple high-frequency transients from baseline drift, and a Kurtosis-Adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT / SPOT) calibrator that modulates tail thresholds against non-Gaussian kurtosis.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four contributions:

\noindent\textbf{1) Physics-Inspired Joint-Embedding Framework:} We formulate an unsupervised anomaly detection framework that embeds physical inductive regularizers (spectral dissipation, turbulent momentum transfer, conservative flow) directly into latent representations, avoiding raw-space noise reconstruction.

\noindent\textbf{2) Flagship Inductive Formulations:} We derive and implement three physics-inspired architectures (\texttt{Operator-Entropy JEPA}, \texttt{Reynolds-Stress JEPA}, and \texttt{Potential-Flow JEPA}) and benchmark them systematically alongside an unconstrained control (\texttt{TS-JEPA}).

\noindent\textbf{3) Inductive Architectural Components:} We introduce Coordinate Saliency Gating for cross-channel fault localization, Dyadic Wavelet decomposition for multi-resolution temporal analysis, and Kurtosis-Adaptive EVT tail calibration to provide automated streaming alarms without transductive test tuning.

\noindent\textbf{4) Multi-Tier Benchmark Evaluation:} We evaluate performance across 11 telemetry datasets (45 matched telemetry streams), reporting Dataset-Macro, Channel-Macro, Pooled Micro-F1, and Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) alongside an analytical All-Positive reference baseline. We demonstrate that latent prediction mitigates threshold calibration collapse, achieving up to 43.2\% relative improvement in Dataset-Macro F1 (where Operator-Entropy JEPA is the sole model to exceed the analytical All-Positive baseline) and a 3.2$\times$ to 5.3$\times$ gain in Pooled Micro-F1 while reducing false positives by 8.0-fold against TimesNet ($130{,}237$ vs $1{,}047{,}144$) and 3.5-fold against TranAD ($452{,}621$).
