\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In multi-sensor monitoring deployments (NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, and wearable biometric arrays), dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs.

\noindent\textbf{Challenges in Telemetry Threshold Calibration:} Multi-sensor telemetry monitoring requires automated decision rules that reliably detect physical faults while controlling false alarms. Setting alarm thresholds on continuous telemetry error streams encounters two fundamental difficulties: non-stationary distribution drift between nominal calibration periods and live deployment, and differences in scoring geometry across model families. Reconstructive models score unconstrained observation-space errors, whereas latent predictive architectures and bounded controls produce bounded residuals with lighter tails.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detection Benchmarks:} Comparing detectors at a single nominal threshold probability (such as design risk $q = 10^{-3}$) conflates scoring scale with discriminative detection quality. Retrospective evaluation protocols that paint alarms over multi-step temporal buffers mechanically inflate per-timestep false-alarm rates, confounding operational evaluation. Standard benchmarks often fail to isolate whether low alarm rates stem from predictive dynamics, bounded score geometry, or representation conservatism.

\noindent\textbf{Investigating Latent Prediction and Score Conditioning:} We evaluate score conditioning, discriminative detection, and threshold transfer under strict chronological partitions. A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) forecasts target representations from context representations; observation-space models, a matched-backbone control, a LayerNorm-bounded observation-space TCN, and a dimensionality-matched PCA forecast test competing structural explanations. We also evaluate spectral entropy, latent second moment, and potential-flow variants as physics-inspired controls.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{False-Alarm Suppression vs.\ Detection Superiority:} On 19 non-GHL streams, static SPOT calibration yields $7.6\%$ nominal held-out FPR for TS-JEPA versus $30.0\%$ for TimesNet and $30.7\%$ for TranAD. However, this conservatism does not establish superior anomaly detection: paired differences in Point-F1 ($+0.012$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.053, +0.075]$) and PR-AUC ($-0.000$) are statistically inconclusive. Bounded PCA and TCN controls show that bounded score geometry compresses residual tails, yet tail shape alone does not ensure out-of-sample threshold transfer. Event-recall comparisons at matched realized false-positive rates cover sparse common support across streams and show no aggregate advantage.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Equivalence of Physics-Inspired Regularizers:} Across the 19-stream multi-seed suite (and mirrored on all 33 streams), Koopman spectral entropy and Reynolds-stress regularizers provide no reliable aggregate detection benefit over unconstrained TS-JEPA: both confirm formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence within a practical $\pm 0.05$ Point-F1 margin ($p \le 0.0054$; $95\%$ CIs $[-0.014, +0.021]$ and $[-0.042, +0.007]$).
    \item \textbf{Explaining Calibration Failure under Distribution Drift:} Static POT thresholds systematically overshoot nominal design risk under chronological deployment ($5.3\text{--}6.2\%$ vs.\ $\approx 22\%$ buffered; $1.0\%$ vs.\ $16\text{--}18\%$ causal at nominal $q{=}10^{-3}$, or $0.1\%$). An exchangeable permutation protocol shows that inter-block distribution drift accounts for $84\text{--}96\%$ of this calibration overshoot, whereas serial autocorrelation and sample scarcity are secondary factors.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark and Baselines:} The benchmark spans 33 continuous telemetry streams across seven datasets (19 non-GHL at five seeds; 14 GHL at one to three seeds). In-house baseline configurations for TimesNet and TranAD are justified against published benchmarks, and all experimental logs, stream manifests, and replication scripts are archived in the repository.
\end{enumerate}
