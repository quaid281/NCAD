\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous anomaly detection in multi-sensor telemetry monitors operational health across aerospace systems, Earth observation, cyber-physical infrastructure, and wearable digital healthcare \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep}. In remote sensing deployments --- NASA SMAP, MSL rovers, industrial IoT facilities, wearable biometric arrays --- dozens to hundreds of channels concurrently track power buses, thermal gradients, vibrations, and kinematics \cite{Hundman2018b}, and faults must be identified before hardware damage occurs.

\subsection{Challenges in Modeling Multi-Sensor Telemetry}
Multi-sensor telemetry reflects a physical state trajectory $\dot{x}(t) = \mathbf{f}(x(t))$, yet modeling dynamics in raw observation space faces four obstacles: estimating derivatives $\dot{x} \approx \Delta x / \Delta t$ on noisy discrete streams amplifies high-frequency noise; telemetry captures only a noisy projection of unmeasured states, violating full-state observability; in large arrays ($K \ge 25$) cross-channel nonlinear interactions resist rigid functional forms; and discrete operational mode transitions (orbital day/night cycling, thruster firings, gait freezing) induce continuous distribution drift.

\subsection{Limitations of Deep Anomaly Detectors}
Deep detectors face three limitations. First, generative models (autoencoders, VAEs, masked Transformers) minimize pointwise $L_1/L_2$ errors in raw signal space \cite{Hundman2018b, xu2022anomaly, wu2023timesnet}, so reconstruction loss allocates capacity to high-frequency jitter rather than nominal dynamics. Second, contrastive frameworks such as NCAD \cite{carmona2021neural} train on synthetic perturbations whose geometry distorts the embedding space, inducing artificial decision boundaries and false alarms. Third, reconstruction errors $\|x - \hat{x}\|_2^2$ accumulate residual variance across dozens of irrelevant channels; under automated thresholding, heavy-tailed and non-stationary residuals defeat static extreme-value calibrators and trigger false-alarm flooding --- although, as we show, inter-block drift rather than tail mass is the dominant failure mechanism.

\subsection{Investigating Latent Prediction and Inductive Priors}
We study whether latent-space prediction mitigates observation-space noise drowning without synthetic contrastive perturbations. A JEPA maps context $x_{\text{ctx}}$ into a representation space and predicts target representations $z_{\text{tgt}}$ directly \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}, discarding unpredictable stochastic jitter and potentially conditioning residuals for tail calibration. Alongside an unconstrained predictor (\texttt{TS-JEPA}), we formalize and test three physics-inspired regularizers: \textit{Operator-Entropy JEPA} (spectral-entropy regularization of a latent Koopman transition matrix), \textit{Reynolds-Stress JEPA} (second-moment fluctuation-covariance alignment), and \textit{Potential-Flow JEPA} (curl-free gradient dynamics $\hat{v} = -\nabla\Phi$). All share a coordinate-saliency scoring gate and a dyadic frequency front-end under SPOT calibration; a strictly causal input-channel gating variant is evaluated separately in a fault-type study.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work makes four principal contributions:

\noindent\textbf{1) Latent Predictive Residuals Shift the Operating Curve, Not Just the Operating Point:} Latent prediction produces lighter-tailed residuals and lower nominal FPR ($7.6\%$ vs.\ $30\%$). Re-evaluating all models on a common alarm-budget grid with realized test FPR as the comparison axis removes the operating-point confound: under strictly causal scoring the JEPA family occupies the upper recall--FPR envelope (partial AUC $0.74$--$0.75$ vs.\ $0.59$--$0.63$), a heterogeneous advantage that disappears under buffered attribution. A dimensionality-matched observation-space control shows that bounded low-dimensional scoring suffices for the tail conditioning, while the learned embedding supplies the separation.

\noindent\textbf{2) Rigorous Evaluation of Physics-Inspired Inductive Priors:} Across 33 streams, neither Operator-Entropy ($+0.002$ Point-F1, $p{=}0.71$) nor Reynolds-Stress ($-0.015$, $p{=}0.61$) beats unconstrained TS-JEPA. Potential-Flow is F1-weakest under SPOT ($-0.081$, $p{=}0.033$ unadjusted) yet strongest on the causal operating curve --- an operating-point artifact, not weak scores. Fixed-backbone ablations show no significant change when the physical terms are disabled.

\noindent\textbf{3) Empirical Analysis of Threshold Calibration Failures:} Static POT thresholds miss their design risk ($q{=}10^{-3}$) on non-stationary telemetry by $50$--$200\times$. Stream-level diagnostics attribute the failure to inter-block drift (KS distance vs.\ realized FPR, Spearman $\rho{=}+0.38$), not tail mis-fit --- calibration-block kurtosis is mildly \emph{protective} ($\rho{=}-0.32$) --- and runs-declustering leaves only $5$--$9$ effective exceedances per cell.

\noindent\textbf{4) Reproducible Multi-Seed Benchmark:} 33 matched streams across seven datasets under a strict held-out calibration protocol (65/17.5/17.5 chronological split with a 64-step isolation gap, up to five seeds, dataset-clustered bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon tests with BH-adjusted $p$-values); all code, checkpoints, and logs released.

Supplementary material contains proofs, extended diagnostics, and case-study detail.
