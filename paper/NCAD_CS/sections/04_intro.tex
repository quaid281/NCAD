\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spacecraft, power plants, and industrial machines stream dozens of sensor channels at once \cite{meng2019spacecraft, chalapathy2019deep, cuellar2024explainable, Hundman2018b}. A monitor must flag faults quickly while staying quiet on normal operation, learning nominal behavior from unlabeled history alone because faults are rare. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust until the monitor is ignored.

Deep detectors typically reconstruct raw waveforms and alarm on high reproduction error, including TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, TranAD \cite{tuli2022tranad}, and Anomaly Transformer \cite{xu2022anomaly}. Yet jitter, thermal drift, and duty-cycle shifts leave irreducible observation errors; summing across $25\text{--}55$ channels allows one noisy sensor to create heavy-tailed healthy spikes. Calibrating Streaming Peaks-Over-Threshold (SPOT) \cite{siffer2017anomaly} on healthy windows still leaves $22\%\text{--}24\%$ causal false-positive rates (FPR) across our 19 development streams, where random alarming at $24\%$ already hits $60.0\%$ of episodes (matching TimesNet's $58.8\%$).

A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self, bardes2024vjepa} instead compresses each window into a 32-number summary and forecasts the next summary, discarding unpredictable jitter and cutting development false alarms tenfold. Standard JEPA detectors, however, collapse all 32 errors into one scalar score and threshold. When one or two \emph{hijacker dials} inflate during warm-up or load changes, they raise that threshold and mask moderate faults across the other 30 coordinates ($0.055$ causal Point-F1). Published benchmarks are also skewed by point adjustment \cite{kim2022towards}, trivial spikes, and run-to-failure bias \cite{wu2023flawed}; we score every step without point adjustment across disjoint blocks and reserve 118 untouched archive streams.

Rather than imposing brittle physics-informed partial differential equation (PDE) loss penalties on uncalibrated sensors, we borrow fluid-mechanics diagnostics purely as post-hoc residual algebra. In latent space, normal operation behaves like a steady \emph{laminar} baseline, whereas an isolated noisy sensor acts like a local disturbance and a real fault behaves like a coherent multi-scale instability. Across nested horizons of $16$, $32$, and $64$ steps, we apply Reynolds mean-fluctuation decomposition \cite{reynolds1895dynamical} to separate sustained drift from brief errors. Each whitened coordinate and mean-drift \emph{dial} then receives its own healthy tripwire, much like a circuit-breaker panel in which each breaker tracks its own branch rather than total load. To keep isolated noise spikes from sounding false alarms, the monitor fires only when a \emph{quorum} of dials trips at the same time, meaning the count of simultaneously active dials exceeds a minimum voting threshold learned directly from healthy history. Because normal correlated bursts set that count threshold automatically, higher variance on a few coordinates scales only their own tripwires instead of blinding the rest of the panel (Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation}). Without claiming superior intrinsic ranking, the quorum shifts the label-free operating point toward higher fault coverage when scalar calibration under-triggers (Fig.~\ref{fig:triad_architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} (operational model): predicts three nested horizons in parallel behind a single layer-normalization (LayerNorm) step \cite{ba2016layernormalization}, monitors $128$ position-and-drift dials under one quorum, and fits within $1$ megabyte (MB) (Table~\ref{tab:matched_alert}(b)).
    \item \emph{Laminar-JEPA} ($224$-dial reference): extends the panel to four horizons ($224$ dials plus $32$ onset relays); \emph{Lite} trades $4.8$ archive recall points for lower false alarms and $25.5\%$ faster inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
Our central contribution is moving away from single-scalar anomaly thresholding in label-free time-series monitoring, pairing fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics with per-coordinate quorum voting. Each stream fits solely to its own healthy history ($K\in[2,55]$ channels, Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}), and the work rests on three pillars:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Fluid-inspired multi-horizon residual diagnostics (no physics loss):} We introduce \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite}, a compact ($<1\,\text{MB}$) multi-horizon latent predictor trained with an unconstrained predictive objective. Treating healthy latent dynamics as a steady laminar baseline, we apply Reynolds mean-fluctuation decomposition strictly as post-hoc residual algebra across nested horizons. Isolating sustained cross-horizon drift from brief errors drives our sharpest live-detection and latency gains without stiff PDE penalties.
    \item \emph{Replacing single-scalar thresholding with coordinate quorum voting:} Conventional detectors collapse multivariate prediction errors into one scalar score and apply one global threshold, allowing a few noisy coordinates to inflate that threshold and hide faults elsewhere. We replace single-scalar thresholding with a coordinate circuit-breaker panel: every dial carries its own healthy tripwire, and an alarm sounds only when enough dials fire simultaneously to clear a healthy-calibrated quorum count. Proposition~\ref{prop:hijacker_isolation} formalizes why this stops high-variance coordinates from blinding the monitor. Rather than claiming better intrinsic score ranking, we show that departing from scalar thresholding succeeds by choosing a stronger label-free operating point when scalar calibration under-triggers.
    \item \emph{Strict multi-domain evaluation and operational readiness:} Evaluated step-by-step without point adjustment across $118$ untouched streams from nine spacecraft, server, and industrial datasets, standalone \emph{Laminar-JEPA-Lite} lifts causal event recall (ER) from $52.5\%$ to $64.6\%$ over single-score multi-horizon JEPA and detects shared faults $41.4$ steps earlier, at higher prospective test FPR ($17.0\%$ vs.\ $8.8\%$). Because calibrating $128$ per-coordinate tripwires requires enough stable history, we pair the panel with a label-free pre-deployment readiness check that switches short or drifting streams to scalar calibration ($59.4\%$ ER at $10.0\%$ test FPR) and a quarantined recalibration policy, while documenting operational limits on slow ramps, single-sensor flatlines, and regime shifts.
\end{enumerate}
