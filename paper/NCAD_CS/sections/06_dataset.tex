\section{Multi-Sensor Telemetry Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:dataset}

Each test case in this paper is a \emph{stream}: one recording from one system, with $K$ sensor channels sampled over time. Every stream has a healthy (nominal) history, used for training and calibration, followed by a labeled test segment that contains the faults. We use two collections of streams. A 45-stream development suite drawn from 11 datasets guided every design decision and supplies the main development benchmark. A larger 141-stream archive drawn from 12 datasets supplies the final evaluation, and 118 of its streams were never inspected during design.

\noindent\emph{Development suite:} The 45 streams come from NASA spacecraft archives \cite{Hundman2018b}, the University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository \cite{bachlin2010wearable, asuncion2007uci}, TimeEval \cite{schmidl2022anomaly}, and mTSBench \cite{zhou2026mtsbench}. They cover three kinds of systems with channel counts from 2 to 72 and very different fault types:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \emph{Spacecraft and solar telemetry:} six SMAP streams ($K{=}25$), six MSL rover streams ($K{=}55$), and SWAN-SF solar magnetograms \cite{angryk2020multivariate} ($K{=}38$). NASA labels each SMAP and MSL fault as either an out-of-range \emph{point} fault, where a value leaves its normal range, or an in-range \emph{contextual} fault, where values stay in range but behave abnormally together \cite{Hundman2018b} (Table~\ref{tab:point_vs_contextual}(a)).
    \item \emph{Wearable motion sensing:} six Daphnet Freezing-of-Gait (FoG) streams \cite{bachlin2010wearable} recorded at $64\,\text{Hz}$ ($K{=}9$, $72$ episodes), so the $S{=}64$-step target window spans one second.
    \item \emph{Industrial processes and infrastructure:} 14 Gasoil Heating Loop (GHL) streams ($K{=}16$) whose $26$ faults are slow ramps, the Genesis pick-and-place testbed ($K{=}18$), GECCO water chemistry ($K{=}9$), Metro I-94 traffic ($K{=}5$), Room-Occupancy sensing \cite{candanedo2016accurate} ($K{=}5$), CalIt2 building flow \cite{asuncion2007uci} ($K{=}2$), and six CICIDS network streams ($K{=}72$).
\end{enumerate}
Thirty-three of these streams form the development benchmark: 19 non-GHL streams and the 14 GHL streams, spanning 7 datasets. Inclusion required enough healthy data for calibration ($N_{\text{cal}} \ge 500$ windows), at most 55 channels, and at most $1.2{\times}10^5$ test steps (Table~\ref{tab:core_benchmark}(c)). The other 12 streams are reserved: five have very short healthy histories ($764$--$1{,}492$ steps; CalIt2, MSL C-2, MSL D-16, and two Room-Occupancy streams), the six CICIDS streams exceed the channel limit, and GECCO is evaluated in the archive instead.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation archive:} The archive contains every qualifying stream from 12 datasets, requiring at least $100$ calibration windows and at least one labeled anomaly: 47 SMAP, 22 Daphnet, 18 MSL, 18 SMD, 17 Exathlon, and 13 OPPORTUNITY streams, plus single streams from GECCO, Genesis, Pooled Server Metrics (PSM), CreditCard, Metro, and SWAN. Healthy histories range from $1{,}920$ to $132{,}481$ steps and test segments from $2{,}158$ to $114{,}001$ steps. Removing the 23 streams used to tune the method leaves 118 untouched streams from 9 datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:cohort_repro}).

\noindent\emph{How each stream is split:} Every healthy history is divided in time order. The first $65\%$ fits the model, and its first part also sets the per-channel standardization ($\bm{\sigma}$ floored at $10^{-2}$). The next $17.5\%$ calibrates alarm thresholds ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{cal}}$), and after a 64-step gap the last $17.5\%$ measures false alarms on healthy data the thresholds have never seen ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{hold}}$). Archive runs further divide the model-fitting block (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).

\noindent\emph{Caveats:} SMAP and MSL contain documented labeling flaws \cite{wu2023flawed}, and streams from the same archive often share hardware, so they are not fully independent. We therefore also treat whole datasets as units of analysis (7 for the development benchmark, 9 for the untouched streams, 12 for the full archive), using Dataset-Macro means, cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), exact dataset sign-flip permutation tests, and leave-one-dataset-out audits (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}).
